# 21.2 Circle theorems — Learn

**Prerequisites:** angles on a straight line total $180^\circ$; angles in a triangle total $180^\circ$; identify a radius, chord and circumference.

**Objectives:** apply angle at centre, semicircle, same segment and cyclic quadrilateral theorems. This page covers these G10 angle results.

## Angles at the centre and circumference

An angle at the **centre** has its vertex at the circle's centre. An angle at the **circumference** has its vertex on the circle. When both angles stand on the same arc, the centre angle is twice the circumference angle.

**Example 1.** The angle at the circumference is $38^\circ$. Work out the angle at the centre standing on the same arc.

The centre angle is twice the circumference angle.

Substitute: $2\times38^\circ=76^\circ$.

So the centre angle is $76^\circ$.

An angle in a semicircle is always $90^\circ$. This follows because a straight angle at the centre is $180^\circ$, and the circumference angle is half of it.

Angles in the same segment are equal. They stand on the same chord and lie in the same part of the circle.

**Example 2.** Two angles stand on the same chord in the same segment. One is $51^\circ$. Find the other.

Angles in the same segment are equal.

Therefore the other angle is $51^\circ$.

## Cyclic quadrilaterals

A **cyclic quadrilateral** has all four vertices on one circle. Its opposite angles add to $180^\circ$.

**Example 3.** Opposite angles are $x^\circ$ and $112^\circ$. Find $x$.

Opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral total $180^\circ$.

Write the equation: $x+112=180$.

Subtract $112$: $x=68^\circ$.

> **Watch out.** Do not double the circumference angle when the angles stand on different arcs. Check that both angles stand on the same arc before using the centre theorem.
