# 21.3 Tangents and chords — Learn

**Prerequisites:** identify a radius, chord and tangent; use right angles and isosceles triangles.

**Objectives:** apply tangent-radius, equal-tangent and perpendicular-bisector chord facts, and the alternate segment theorem. This page covers these G10 results.

## Tangents and radii

A radius drawn to the point where a tangent touches the circle is perpendicular to the tangent. It makes a right angle of $90^\circ$.

**Example 1.** A radius meets a tangent at $T$. One angle at $T$ is $90^\circ$. Find the adjacent angle on the straight tangent line.

Angles on a straight line total $180^\circ$.

Subtract the right angle: $180^\circ-90^\circ=90^\circ$.

Tangents drawn from the same point outside a circle have equal lengths.

**Example 2.** Two tangents from point $P$ touch a circle at $A$ and $B$. If $PA=5.4$ cm, find $PB$.

Tangents from the same external point are equal.

Therefore $PB=PA=5.4$ cm.

## Chords and the alternate segment

A perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord. **Bisects** means cuts into two equal parts.

**Example 3.** A perpendicular from the centre meets a chord of length $12$ cm. Find each part of the chord.

The perpendicular bisects the chord.

Divide the length by two: $12\div2=6$ cm.

The **alternate segment theorem** says the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the opposite segment of the circle.

**Example 4.** The angle between a tangent and a chord is $47^\circ$. Find the angle in the alternate segment.

The alternate segment theorem gives equal angles.

So the angle is $47^\circ$.

> **Watch out.** The perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord only when it meets the chord at $90^\circ$. Check for the right-angle mark or a stated perpendicular.
