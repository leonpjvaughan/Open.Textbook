# 4.1 Equivalent fractions and ordering — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication and place value.

**Objectives:** A fraction names equal parts of a whole. The top number is the numerator; the bottom number is the denominator. Equivalent fractions have the same value.

## Learn the method

Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same non-zero number. This keeps the fraction value unchanged. To compare fractions, use a common denominator or convert to decimals.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Make $\frac{3}{4}$ have denominator $20$.

$4\times5=20$, so multiply the numerator by $5$ too.

$\frac{3}{4}=\frac{3\times5}{4\times5}=\frac{15}{20}$.

**Example 2.** Order $\frac{5}{6}$ and $\frac{7}{9}$.

The lowest common denominator is $18$.

$\frac{5}{6}=\frac{15}{18}$ and $\frac{7}{9}=\frac{14}{18}$.

Therefore $\frac{7}{9}<\frac{5}{6}$.

> **Watch out.** Do not compare fractions by looking only at their denominators. A larger denominator can mean smaller parts, but the numerator matters too. Compare using a common denominator: $\frac{3}{5}=\frac{12}{20}$ and $\frac{7}{10}=\frac{14}{20}$, so $\frac{3}{5}<\frac{7}{10}$.
