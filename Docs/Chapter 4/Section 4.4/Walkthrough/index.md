# 4.4 Fractions and terminating decimals — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication and place value.

**Objectives:** A terminating decimal has a finite number of digits after the decimal point. Fractions with denominators that reduce to factors of $2$ and $5$ terminate.

## Learn the method

To convert a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator by the denominator. To convert a terminating decimal, write it over $10$, $100$, or another power of $10$, then simplify.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Write $\frac{7}{20}$ as a decimal.

Make the denominator $100$ by multiplying by $5$.

$\frac{7}{20}=\frac{35}{100}=0.35$.

**Example 2.** Write $0.125$ as a fraction.

There are three decimal places, so write it over $1000$.

$0.125=\frac{125}{1000}$.

Divide top and bottom by $125$.

$\frac{125}{1000}=\frac{1}{8}$.

> **Watch out.** The decimal places set the denominator: $0.07=\frac{7}{100}$, not $\frac{7}{10}$. Count the places, then simplify and check by division.
