# 4.5 Recurring decimals — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication and place value.

**Objectives:** A recurring decimal has a digit or block of digits that repeats forever. A dot above a digit marks the repeating block. This section is Higher only.

## Learn the method

Set the recurring decimal equal to $x$. Multiply by a power of $10$ that moves one repeating block past the decimal point. Subtract the equations to remove the recurring tail.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Convert $0.\dot{6}$ to a fraction.

Let $x=0.666\ldots$.

$10x=6.666\ldots$.

Subtract the first equation from the second.

$9x=6$.

Divide by $9$.

$x=\frac{6}{9}=\frac{2}{3}$.

**Example 2.** Convert $0.1\dot{2}$ to a fraction.

Let $x=0.1222\ldots$.

$10x=1.222\ldots$ and $100x=12.222\ldots$.

Subtract to remove the recurring part.

$90x=11$, so $x=\frac{11}{90}$.

> **Watch out.** For $0.1\dot{2}$, only the $2$ repeats. The number is $0.1222\ldots$, not $0.121212\ldots$. Check by expanding the decimal before setting up the equations.
