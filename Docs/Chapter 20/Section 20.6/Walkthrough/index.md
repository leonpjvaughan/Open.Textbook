# 20.6 The sine rule — Learn

**Prerequisites:** sine ratios, angle sum in a triangle, and calculator degree mode.

**Objectives:** use the sine rule to find a missing side or angle in a non-right-angled triangle. This teaches DfE G22.

A side and its opposite angle form a pair. The **sine rule** is $\frac{a}{\sin A}=\frac{b}{\sin B}=\frac{c}{\sin C}$, where lower-case letters are opposite their matching capital angles.

**Example 1.** In a triangle, $A=30^\circ$, $B=45^\circ$, and $a=8$ cm. Find $b$.

$$\frac{b}{\sin45^\circ}=\frac{8}{\sin30^\circ}$$

$$b=\frac{8\sin45^\circ}{\sin30^\circ}$$

$$b\approx11.3\text{ cm}$$

**Example 2.** Find angle $A$ if $a=6$ cm, $b=10$ cm and $B=90^\circ$.

$$\frac{6}{\sin A}=\frac{10}{\sin90^\circ}$$

$$\sin A=0.6$$

$$A=\sin^{-1}(0.6)\approx36.9^\circ$$

> **Watch out.** Pair each side with its opposite angle. A wrong pairing changes the equation; mark opposite pairs on the diagram first.

## Progress check

For the garden diagonal, the right-triangle ratios still work. The sine rule is useful when the triangle has no right angle.
