# 10.5 Geometric sequences — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication, division, and substitution into a rule.

**Objectives:** recognise a geometric sequence and find its term-to-term
multiplier. This section develops the Higher content in A24.

## A constant multiplier

A **geometric sequence** is formed by multiplying each term by the same number.
That number is the common multiplier.

In $3,6,12,24,\ldots$, each term is multiplied by $2$. The common multiplier is
$2$.

**Example 1.** Find the next two terms of $5,15,45,\ldots$.

$$
45\times3=135
$$

$$
135\times3=405
$$

The common multiplier is $3$, and the next terms are $135$ and $405$.

## A multiplier below one

A multiplier can be a fraction or decimal. Multiplying by a number between zero
and one makes positive terms smaller.

**Example 2.** Continue $80,40,20,\ldots$.

$$
20\times\frac12=10
$$

$$
10\times\frac12=5
$$

The common multiplier is $\frac12$, so the next terms are $10$ and $5$.

**Progress check.** Pattern B starts with $2$ tiles and doubles each time. By
design 5 it has $2,4,8,16,32$ tiles.

> **Explain:** What happens to later terms when the multiplier is above $1$,
> equal to $1$, or between $0$ and $1$?

> **Watch out.** Do not add the multiplier. In $3,6,12,\ldots$, the change is
> not a fixed amount. Check each term by dividing it by the previous term.

## You should now be able to

- recognise a geometric sequence by its constant multiplier;
- find the multiplier by dividing consecutive terms;
- continue a geometric sequence with integer or fractional multipliers;
- describe how the multiplier affects later positive terms.
