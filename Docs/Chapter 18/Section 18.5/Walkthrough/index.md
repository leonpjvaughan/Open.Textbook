# 18.5 Similarity and similar lengths — Learn

**Prerequisites:** ratio, fractions, multiplication and division.

**Objectives:** identify similar shapes and find corresponding lengths using a common scale factor. Covers: G19 and R12.

**Similar** shapes have equal corresponding angles and proportional corresponding lengths. The **scale factor** is the multiplier from one shape to the other.

**Example.** Similar triangles have corresponding sides $4$ cm and $10$ cm.

Scale factor from small to large is $10\div4$.
$10\div4=2.5$.
A side of $6$ cm on the smaller triangle becomes $6\times2.5=15$ cm.

To test if shapes are similar, compare ratios of corresponding lengths. All ratios must match.

**Example.** Compare side lengths $6,9,12$ with $10,15,20$.

$10\div6=\frac53$.
$15\div9=\frac53$.
$20\div12=\frac53$.
The ratios match, so the side lengths are proportional.

> **Watch out.** Keep corresponding sides in the same order. A ratio of small-to-large must not be compared with a ratio of large-to-small. Check that each multiplier matches.

### Progress check

A mural is reproduced at a scale factor of $1.5$. A tile edge of $8$ cm becomes $12$ cm. Explain how the ratio confirms the scale.

## You should now be able to

- explain what makes two shapes similar;
- calculate a scale factor using corresponding lengths;
- find missing lengths in similar shapes;
- test similarity by comparing corresponding ratios.
