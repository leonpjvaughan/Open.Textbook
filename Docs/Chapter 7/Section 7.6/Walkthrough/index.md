# 7.6 Surds and exact calculations — Learn

**Prerequisites:** square numbers, factors and simplifying fractions.

## Before you begin

You should be confident with square numbers, factors and simplifying fractions.

## Objectives

- Simplify surds by taking square factors outside the root.
- Add and subtract like surds.
- Multiply and divide surds.
- Rationalise a denominator in a simple exact calculation.

## Simplifying surds

A surd is an exact root that cannot be written as a whole number. Split a number under a square root into a square factor and the remaining factor.

**Example 1.** Simplify $\sqrt{72}$.

$$
\sqrt{72}=\sqrt{36\times2}=6\sqrt2
$$

**Example 2.** Simplify $3\sqrt8+\sqrt{18}$.

$$
3\sqrt8+\sqrt{18}=3(2\sqrt2)+3\sqrt2
$$

$$
=9\sqrt2
$$

Only like surds can be added, as with matching variables.

## Products and denominators

Multiply surds by multiplying the numbers under the roots. Rationalising a denominator rewrites a fraction so its denominator has no root.

**Example 3.** Simplify $\sqrt6\times\sqrt{15}$.

$$
\sqrt6\times\sqrt{15}=\sqrt{90}=3\sqrt{10}
$$

**Example 4.** Rationalise $\frac{5}{\sqrt3}$.

$$
\frac5{\sqrt3}\times\frac{\sqrt3}{\sqrt3}=\frac{5\sqrt3}{3}
$$

> **Watch out.** A common error is to write $\sqrt a+\sqrt b=\sqrt{a+b}$. For example, $\sqrt9+\sqrt{16}=7$, but $\sqrt{25}=5$. Simplify each root first, then combine only matching surds.

> **Progress check.** Before simplifying $\sqrt{50}+\sqrt8$, identify the square factor in each radicand.

## You should now be able to

- simplify square roots by taking out square factors;
- combine and multiply surds exactly;
- rationalise a simple denominator.
