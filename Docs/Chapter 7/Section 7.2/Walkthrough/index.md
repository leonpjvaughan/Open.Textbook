# 7.2 Compound measures: speed, density and pressure — Learn

## Before you begin

You should be able to convert units and rearrange a formula by using inverse operations.

## Objectives

- Calculate speed, distance and time.
- Calculate density, mass and volume.
- Calculate pressure, force and area.
- Choose units that match the quantities.

## Speed

Speed tells you the distance travelled per unit of time. The formula is $\text{speed}=\text{distance}\div\text{time}$.

**Example 1.** A train travels $150$ km in $2.5$ hours. Find its average speed.

$$
150\div2.5=60
$$

The average speed is $60$ km/h. Rearranging gives distance $=\text{speed}\times\text{time}$ and time $=\text{distance}\div\text{speed}$.

**Example 2.** A cyclist travels at $8$ m/s for $45$ seconds.

$$
8\times45=360\text{ m}
$$

## Density and pressure

Density is mass per unit volume: $\text{density}=\text{mass}\div\text{volume}$. Pressure is force per unit area: $\text{pressure}=\text{force}\div\text{area}$.

**Example 3.** A block has mass $540$ g and volume $200\text{ cm}^3$.

$$
540\div200=2.7\text{ g/cm}^3
$$

**Example 4.** A force of $360$ N acts on an area of $0.12\text{ m}^2$.

$$
360\div0.12=3000\text{ Pa}
$$

One pascal, written Pa, is one newton per square metre. Check that all units agree before substituting.

> **Watch out.** Dividing by time gives speed, but multiplying gives distance. Check the formula triangle or units before choosing an operation.

> **Progress check.** A runner covers $400$ m in $80$ seconds. Which formula gives the speed, and what units will the answer use?

## You should now be able to

- calculate speed, distance and time from the formula;
- calculate density, mass and volume from the formula;
- calculate pressure, force and area from the formula;
- convert units first when the quantities do not match.
