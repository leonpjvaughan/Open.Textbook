# 1.3 Negative numbers — Walkthrough

**Prerequisites:** ordering on a number line; the four operations (section 1.2).

**By the end of this section you can:** order negative numbers; add, subtract,
multiply and divide with negative numbers; and interpret a negative result as a
loss, a debt or a fall.

**Delivers DfE:** N1 (ordering), N2 (the four operations with negative numbers).

## 1. The number line

Negative numbers are to the left of zero. The further left, the smaller.

$$
-7 < -3 < -0.5 < 0 < 2
$$

**Example 1.** The temperature falls from $3\,^\circ\text{C}$ to $-4\,^\circ\text{C}$.
How many degrees did it fall?

The gap from $3$ down to $0$ is $3$ degrees, and from $0$ down to $-4$ is another
$4$ degrees, so the fall is $3 + 4 = 7\,^\circ\text{C}$.

## 2. Adding and subtracting

Think of a number line, or of money and debt.

$$
-3 + 5 = 2 \qquad 2 - 6 = -4 \qquad -3 - 5 = -8
$$

Subtracting a negative is the same as adding:

$$
4 - (-2) = 4 + 2 = 6
$$

> **Common error.** "Two negatives make a positive" applies to **multiplying and
> dividing**, not to adding. $(-3) + (-5) = -8$, not $+8$. And $-3 - 5 = -8$: you
> move 5 further left, not right.

## 3. Multiplying and dividing

For multiplication and division, decide the sign first, then work with the sizes:

| Signs | Result |
|---|---|
| $+ \times +$ | $+$ |
| $+ \times -$ | $-$ |
| $- \times -$ | $+$ |

**Example 2.** Work out $(-6) \times 4$ and $(-6) \times (-4)$.

$(-6) \times 4 = -24$ (different signs, so negative).
$(-6) \times (-4) = 24$ (same signs, so positive).

The same rules apply to division: $(-20) \div 5 = -4$ and $(-20) \div (-5) = 4$.

## 4. Negative numbers in context

A **profit** is positive; a **loss** is negative. A balance below zero is a debt.
If takings are less than costs, the profit is negative.

### Investigation — profit and loss

**Predict first:** in the anchor problem the stall spent £92.80. If the stall only
sold **60 cans** (at £1.20) and 6 boxes (at £3.00), will the result be a profit or
a loss? Decide before you move anything.

Then use the tool: set "Bought" to 120, "Cost each" to 0.65, "Price each" to 1.20
and "Sold" to 60 to see this case, and try your own values.

**Explain:** when the readout says the profit is negative, what does that mean in
words? Why is a negative profit called a loss?

## 5. Anchor checkpoint

**Checkpoint.** With 60 cans sold:

$$
\text{revenue} = 60 \times 1.20 + 6 \times 3.00 = 72 + 18 = £90
$$

$$
\text{profit} = 90 - 92.80 = -£2.80
$$

The stall would have made a **loss of £2.80**. A negative profit is a loss.

## You should now be able to

- order positive and negative numbers;
- add and subtract with negatives by moving along a number line;
- multiply and divide using the sign rules;
- explain what a negative answer means in a money context.
