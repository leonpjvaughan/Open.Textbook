# Chapter 1: Number skills

**By the end of this chapter you will be able to:**

- read, write and order numbers, including decimals and negative numbers;
- add, subtract, multiply and divide with integers and decimals using reliable written methods;
- work with negative numbers in context, including profit and loss;
- use the correct order of operations and check a calculation with an inverse operation;
- round to a required degree of accuracy, estimate, and use estimation to check an answer.

## The anchor problem: the school-fair stall

You are running the drinks-and-cakes stall at the school fair. The cash box
starts with a **£50 float** — money you must give back at the end.

- You buy **120 cans** of drink at **65p** each.
- You buy **8 boxes** of cakes at **£1.85** each.
- You sell **95 cans** at **£1.20** each.
- You sell **6 boxes** of cakes at **£3.00** each.

By the end of the chapter you will work out **exactly** whether the stall made a
profit or a loss, how much, and how much money should be in the cash box.

> **First contact — no working yet.** Before any exact calculation, *estimate*.
> Do you expect a profit or a loss? Roughly how much?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>If you are not sure where to start</summary>

Round each price to something easy and think about the totals. For example, 120
cans at about 65p costs a little less than $120 \times 70\text{p} = £84$.
</details>

## Where it is used

Cash handling, budgets, profit and loss, and estimating before you commit money.
The same skills underpin wages, bills, savings and unit pricing in a shop.

## Your turn to connect it
