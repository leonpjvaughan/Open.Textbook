# 1.2 The four operations — Walkthrough

**Prerequisites:** place value and ordering (section 1.1).

**By the end of this section you can:** add, subtract, multiply and divide
integers and decimals using written methods; and check an answer with an inverse
operation.

**Delivers DfE:** N2 (four operations, formal written methods, place value), N3
(inverse operations).

## 1. Addition and subtraction

Line up the digits by **place value** — ones under ones, tenths under tenths.
Then work column by column.

**Example 1.** Work out $78 + 14.8$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
78.00 \\
+\ 14.80 \\
\hline
92.80
\end{aligned}
$$

So $78 + 14.8 = 92.80$.

> **Common error.** $78 + 14.8$ is **not** $78 + 14.8 \to 92.8$ by lining up the
> last digits. The $8$ in $14.8$ is eight tenths, so it belongs under the tenths
> column, not under the $8$ ones.

## 2. Multiplication

**Short multiplication** multiplies by a single digit. **Long multiplication**
multiplies by a number with more than one digit, working one digit at a time and
adding the partial products.

**Example 2.** Work out $120 \times 0.65$.

First work out $120 \times 65 = 7\,800$. Then place the decimal point: $0.65$ has
two decimal places, so the answer also has two:

$$
0.65 \times 120 = 7\,800 \div 100 = 78
$$

So the drinks cost $£78$.

**Example 3.** Work out $8 \times 1.85$.

$8 \times 185 = 1\,480$, and $1.85$ has two decimal places, so the answer has two:
$8 \times 1.85 = £14.80$.

## 3. Division

Division is the inverse of multiplication. Use short division for a single-digit
divisor.

**Example 4.** Work out $132 \div 6$.

$$
132 \div 6 = 22
$$

**Example 5.** Work out $95 \times 1.20$ to find the drinks revenue.

$95 \times 120 = 11\,400$, and $1.20$ has two decimal places, so
$95 \times 1.20 = £114$.

Where a division does not come out exactly, either give the remainder
($29 \div 4 = 7$ remainder $1$) or continue past the decimal point
($29 \div 4 = 7.25$).

## 4. Checking with inverse operations

Every operation has an inverse that undoes it:

| Operation | Inverse |
|---|---|
| add | subtract |
| multiply | divide |

**Example 6.** You work out $78 + 14.8 = 92.8$. Check it: $92.8 - 14.8 = 78$. ✓

Estimation gives a second, independent check: $120 \times 0.65$ is about
$120 \times 0.7 = 84$, and $78$ is plausible.

> **Common error.** In long multiplication, forgetting that the second partial
> product represents tens (not ones) shifts every following column and makes the
> answer ten times too small.

## 5. Anchor checkpoint

Work out the two costs exactly and add them:

$$
120 \times 0.65 = 78 \qquad 8 \times 1.85 = 14.80 \qquad 78 + 14.80 = 92.80
$$

The stall spent **£92.80** on stock. How does this compare with your estimate
from the chapter opener?

## You should now be able to

- add and subtract integers and decimals with the digits correctly aligned;
- multiply by one- and two-digit numbers using written methods;
- divide, giving a remainder or a decimal answer as required;
- check any answer with an inverse operation.
