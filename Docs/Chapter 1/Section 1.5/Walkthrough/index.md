# 1.5 Rounding and estimation — Walkthrough

**Prerequisites:** place value (1.1); the four operations (1.2).

**By the end of this section you can:** round to the nearest 10, 100 or whole
number; round to a given number of decimal places or significant figures; and use
estimation to check an answer.

**Delivers DfE:** N14 (estimate answers, check calculations), N15 (round to an
appropriate degree of accuracy).

## 1. Rounding to the nearest 10, 100 or whole number

Find the place value you are rounding to. Look at the digit to its right: if it
is 5 or more, round up; otherwise round down.

**Example 1.** Round $4\,367$ to the nearest $100$.

The hundreds digit is $3$. The digit to its right is $6 \geq 5$, so round up:
$4\,367 \approx 4\,400$. (The symbol $\approx$ means "is approximately equal to".)

**Example 2.** Round $2.7$ to the nearest whole number. The tenths digit is
$7 \geq 5$, so $2.7 \approx 3$.

## 2. Decimal places

To round to $n$ decimal places, keep $n$ digits after the point and look at the
next one.

**Example 3.** Round $3.14159$ to 2 decimal places.

Keep $3.14$; the next digit is $1 < 5$, so round down: $3.14159 \approx 3.14$.

## 3. Significant figures

Significant figures count from the first non-zero digit. Rounding to significant
figures is useful when a number is large or small.

**Example 4.** Round $4\,367$ to 2 significant figures.

The first two significant figures are $4$ and $3$. The next digit is $6 \geq 5$,
so round up: $4\,367 \approx 4\,400$.

**Example 5.** Round $0.004\,367$ to 2 significant figures.

Leading zeros are **not** significant. The first significant figure is $4$, so the
first two are $4$ and $3$, giving $0.004\,4$.

> **Common error.** Treating leading zeros as significant figures.
> $0.004\,367$ to 2 s.f. is $0.004\,4$, not $0.004\,37$.

## 4. Estimation

Estimating means rounding each number first, so the arithmetic is quick. It is a
**check**, not an exact answer.

**Example 6.** Estimate $19.7 \times 6.2$.

Round: $20 \times 6 = 120$. The exact answer is $122.14$, so the estimate is
close — the calculation is plausible.

## 5. Anchor checkpoint

The stall's profit was $£39.20$. Round it to the nearest pound for a poster:
$£39.20 \approx £39$.

Now estimate as a check: revenue is about $95 \times 1.20 + 6 \times 3 \approx
100 \times 1.2 + 18 = 138$; cost is about $120 \times 0.65 + 8 \times 2 = 78 + 16 =
94$; so the profit should be about $138 - 94 = £44$. The exact $£39.20$ is a
little lower, but the same size — no arithmetic blunder.

## You should now be able to

- round to the nearest 10, 100 or whole number;
- round to a given number of decimal places or significant figures;
- estimate a calculation by rounding first;
- use an estimate to judge whether an exact answer is reasonable.
