# 3.4 Negative and fractional indices — Learn

**Prerequisites:** roots and integer index laws; fractions and reciprocals.

**Objectives:** interpret fractional indices as roots; combine roots and powers; evaluate negative fractional indices.

This Higher section extends N7. A fractional index connects powers with roots.

## Unit fractions as roots

For a positive number $a$, the index law shows that squaring $a^{1/2}$ gives
$a$. So $a^{1/2}=\sqrt a$. In the same way, $a^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]a$.

$$a^{1/2}=\sqrt a \qquad a^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]a$$

**Example 1.** Work out $64^{1/3}$.

$$64^{1/3}=\sqrt[3]{64}$$

$$64^{1/3}=4$$

## Other fractional indices

The denominator gives the root. The numerator gives the power. For example,
$a^{2/3}$ means take the cube root, then square.

$$a^{2/3}=(\sqrt[3]a)^2$$

**Example 2.** Work out $81^{3/4}$.

$$81^{3/4}=(\sqrt[4]{81})^3$$

$$81^{3/4}=3^3$$

$$81^{3/4}=27$$

## Negative fractional indices

A negative index means take the reciprocal. So $a^{-m/n}=1/a^{m/n}$ when
$a$ is positive and non-zero.

**Example 3.** Work out $32^{-2/5}$.

$$32^{-2/5}=\frac{1}{32^{2/5}}$$

$$32^{2/5}=(\sqrt[5]{32})^2=2^2=4$$

$$32^{-2/5}=\frac14$$

> **Watch out.** The denominator tells you the root and the numerator tells
> you the power. In $a^{2/3}$, taking the square root would swap these roles.
> Check by rewriting the expression with a radical.

## You should now be able to

- rewrite a unit fractional index as a root;
- interpret $a^{m/n}$ as an nth root raised to the mth power;
- use a negative index to form a reciprocal;
- evaluate positive numbers with negative fractional indices.
