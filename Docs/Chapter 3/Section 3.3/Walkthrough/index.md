# 3.3 Laws of indices — Learn

**Prerequisites:** powers as repeated multiplication; multiplication and division of algebraic terms.

**Objectives:** simplify products and quotients of powers; simplify a power raised to a power; use zero and negative integer indices.

This section covers the laws of indices in A4 and N7. An index is the small
number that tells how many equal factors a power contains.

## Multiply and divide powers

When the base stays the same, multiplying powers joins their factors. Dividing
powers removes matching factors.

$$x^3x^2=(xxx)(xx)=x^5$$

$$\frac{x^5}{x^2}=\frac{xxxxx}{xx}=x^3 \quad (x\ne0)$$

So $x^a x^b=x^{a+b}$ and $x^a\div x^b=x^{a-b}$ for non-zero $x$.

**Example 1.** Simplify $a^4a^3$.

$$a^4a^3=a^{4+3}$$

$$a^4a^3=a^7$$

## A power raised to a power

$(x^a)^b$ means repeat the whole group $x^a$ a total of $b$ times. Therefore
the indices multiply.

$$ (x^2)^3=(x^2)(x^2)(x^2)=x^6 $$

**Example 2.** Simplify $(y^3)^4$.

$$ (y^3)^4=y^{3\times4} $$

$$ (y^3)^4=y^{12} $$

## Zero and negative indices

For non-zero $x$, $x^0=1$. The quotient law explains this: $x^3\div x^3$
equals both 1 and $x^{3-3}=x^0$.

$$x^0=1 \quad (x\ne0)$$

The same law gives $x^{-2}=1/x^2$, because $x^3\div x^5=x^{-2}$ and also
$x^3/x^5=1/x^2$.

> **Watch out.** In $x^3x^4$, add the indices to get $x^7$. In $(x^3)^4$,
> multiply them to get $x^{12}$. The brackets show that the whole power repeats.
> Check by writing out the factors.

## Progress check

Rewrite $2^{20}$ as $(2^{10})^2$. Use $2^{10}=1024$ to find the exact size
after 20 doublings. Compare the result with your estimate from 3.2.
