# 12.2 Factorising harder quadratics — Learn

**Prerequisites:** expanding brackets and factorising monic quadratics.

**Objectives:** factorise $ax^2+bx+c$ when $a$ is not $1$, including cases where the leading coefficient is negative. This extends A4.

## Split the middle term

For $ax^2+bx+c$, find two numbers with product $ac$ and sum $b$. Split $bx$, then factor by grouping.

**Example 1.** Factorise $6x^2+11x+3$.

The product $ac$ is $18$. The numbers $9$ and $2$ have product $18$ and sum $11$.

$$
6x^2+11x+3=6x^2+9x+2x+3
$$

$$
=3x(2x+3)+1(2x+3)
$$

$$
=(3x+1)(2x+3)
$$

## Negative leading coefficient

Take out a negative factor first. Then use the same method.

**Example 2.** Factorise $-2x^2+5x+3$.

$$
-2x^2+5x+3=-(2x^2-5x-3)
$$
The product is $-6$ and the sum is $-5$, so use $-6$ and $1$.

$$
=-(2x^2-6x+x-3)
$$

$$
=-(2x(x-3)+1(x-3))
$$

$$
=-(2x+1)(x-3)
$$

> **Watch out.** Choosing numbers with the right product is not enough. Their sum must match the middle coefficient too. Expand your final factors to check both.

## You should now be able to

- use the product $ac$ and sum $b$ to split a middle term;
- factorise by grouping matching brackets;
- handle a negative leading coefficient by taking out $-1$ first.
