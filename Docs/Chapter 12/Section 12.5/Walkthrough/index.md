# 12.5 The quadratic formula — Learn

**Prerequisites:** rearranging equations, square roots, and substituting values into a formula.

**Objectives:** solve a quadratic equation using the quadratic formula and interpret its discriminant. This covers A18 at Higher level.

## The formula

For $ax^2+bx+c=0$, where $a\ne 0$,

$$
x=
\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}
$$

The expression $b^2-4ac$ is the **discriminant**. A positive discriminant gives two real roots, zero gives one repeated root, and a negative discriminant gives no real roots.

**Example 1.** Solve $2x^2+3x-2=0$.

Here $a=2$, $b=3$, and $c=-2$.

$$
x=
\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{3^2-4(2)(-2)}}{2(2)}
$$

$$
=
\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{25}}{4}
$$

$$
x=
\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{or}\quad x=-2
$$

> **Watch out.** Keep the negative sign in $c=-2$ when substituting. The discriminant is $9-4(2)(-2)=25$, not $9-16$.

## You should now be able to

- identify $a$, $b$, and $c$ after writing the equation in standard form;
- substitute carefully into the quadratic formula;
- use the discriminant to describe the number of real roots.
