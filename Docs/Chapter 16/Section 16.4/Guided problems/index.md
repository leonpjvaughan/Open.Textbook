# 16.4 Angles in polygons — Practise with help

Try each question before opening a hint. Open the next part only when you need it.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the missing angle in a triangle with angles $45^\circ$ and $65^\circ$.
2. What is a quadrilateral angle sum?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Check your answers</summary>

1. $70^\circ$. 2. $360^\circ$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Work out the missing angle in a basic question from this section.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the angle fact that fits the diagram or shape.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>One step</summary>

Set up an equation using the known angle total.

$(5-2)\times180=540^\circ$ for a pentagon.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Use the relevant angle sum and subtract the known angles. State the angle fact in your answer.

</details>

**2.** A courtyard design uses the angle fact from this section. Find an unknown angle and explain your choice of fact.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Mark the known angles and identify their relationship before calculating.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>One step</summary>

Write an equation for the known angles and the unknown.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Substitute the known values, solve, then check that the angles fit the shape.

$540\div5=108^\circ$ for each angle of a regular pentagon.

</details>
</details>

**3. Earlier learning.** Work out: $7+5\times3$. Explain which operation you do first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the order of operations.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>One step</summary>

Calculate $5\times3$ before adding.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$7+5\times3=7+15=22$. Multiplication comes before addition.
