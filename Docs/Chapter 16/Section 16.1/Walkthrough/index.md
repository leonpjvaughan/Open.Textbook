# 16.1 Angle facts at a point and on a line — Learn

**Prerequisites:** recognise a right angle and measure an angle in degrees. An angle measures a turn between two lines.

**Objectives:** use angles on a straight line, angles around a point, and vertically opposite angles. These are the G3 facts for this section.

## Angles on lines and at points

Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$. Angles around a point add to $360^\circ$. Vertically opposite angles are the pair opposite each other where two straight lines cross; they are equal.

**Example 1.** A straight line is split into angles of $124^\circ$ and $x^\circ$. Find $x$.

$$
124 + x = 180
$$

$$
x = 180 - 124 = 56^\circ
$$

The two angles make a straight turn, so their sum is $180^\circ$.

**Example 2.** Three angles around a point are $85^\circ$, $140^\circ$ and $y^\circ$. Find $y$.

$$
85 + 140 + y = 360
$$

$$
y = 360 - 225 = 135^\circ
$$

The three angles complete one full turn.

**Example 3.** Two straight lines cross. One angle is $47^\circ$. Find the other three.

The opposite angle is $47^\circ$ because vertically opposite angles are equal. Each adjacent angle is $180-47=133^\circ$ because it lies on a straight line.

> **Watch out.** The two angles next to $47^\circ$ are not equal to it. Each makes a straight line with it, so each is $133^\circ$. Check that opposite angles match.
