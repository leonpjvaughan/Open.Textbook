# 15.2 Simplifying algebraic fractions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** factorise quadratics and cancel common numerical factors.

**Objectives:** factorise a numerator or denominator, cancel common factors, and state excluded values. This section teaches how to simplify algebraic fractions.

An algebraic fraction has an algebraic expression in its numerator, denominator, or both. Factorising writes an expression as a product. You can cancel common factors, but you cannot cancel separate terms joined by addition or subtraction.

**Example 1.** Simplify $
rac{x^2-9}{x^2+6x+9}$.

Factorise the numerator: $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$.
Factorise the denominator: $x^2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3)$.
Cancel the common factor $x+3$: $
rac{x-3}{x+3}$.
The original denominator is zero at $x=-3$, so $x
eq-3$.

**Example 2.** Simplify $
rac{6x^2}{9x}$.

Write numerator and denominator as products: $
rac{2\cdot3\cdot x\cdot x}{3\cdot3\cdot x}$.
Cancel common factors: $
rac{2x}{3}$.
The original denominator requires $x
eq0$.

> **Watch out.** You cannot cancel the $x$ terms in $
rac{x+4}{x+2}$. They are parts of sums, not common factors. Factor first, then cancel whole factors.
