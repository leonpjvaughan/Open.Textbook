# 24.2 Tables, bar charts, pie charts and pictograms — Learn

**Prerequisites:** tally charts, fractions, percentages and angle facts.

**Objectives:** organise categorical data in a frequency table; choose and construct a suitable chart; interpret charts. This section covers S2.

## Tables and chart choice

A **frequency** is the number of times a category occurs. A frequency table lists each category and its frequency. Use a bar chart for separate categories: leave gaps between bars. A pictogram uses symbols, each worth a stated number. A pie chart shows parts of a whole; its sectors add to $360^\circ$.

For a pie chart, calculate each sector angle with

$$	ext{angle}=
rac{	ext{category frequency}}{	ext{total frequency}}	imes360^\circ$$

**Example.** In a survey of 40 pupils, 10 walk, 20 take a bus and 10 travel by car. Find the bus sector angle.

The bus fraction is $20/40=1/2$.

The angle is $1/2	imes360^\circ=180^\circ$.

The bus sector is half the circle.

**Predict:** Which display makes it easiest to compare category counts? **Test:** Change the sample size control and watch the chart update. **Explain:** What stays the same when the proportions stay the same? Write one sentence before reading on.

> **Watch out.** A pictogram must state the value of one symbol. If one symbol means four pupils, a half-symbol means two pupils.
