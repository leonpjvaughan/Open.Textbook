# 24.3 Averages and range — Learn

**Prerequisites:** ordering numbers and dividing exactly or approximately.

**Objectives:** calculate and interpret mean, median, mode and range; compare what each measure describes. This section covers S4.

The **mean** is total divided by count. The **median** is the middle value after ordering. The **mode** is the most frequent value. The **range** is maximum minus minimum. These are measures of a data set; an unusual value, called an **outlier**, can affect the mean and range strongly.

**Example.** Find the mean, median, mode and range of $3, 4, 4, 7, 12$.

Add the values: $3+4+4+7+12=30$.

Divide by five: mean $=30/5=6$.

The ordered middle value is 4, so median $=4$.

The most frequent value is 4, so mode $=4$.

Subtract the extremes: range $=12-3=9$.

**Example.** Compare the mean and median after adding 30 to the list.

The new total is $30+30=60$ for six values.

The new mean is $60/6=10$.

The middle pair is 4 and 7, so median $=(4+7)/2=5.5$.

The large value raises the mean more than the median.

> **Watch out.** The median is not always one of the data values. With an even count, average the two middle values.
