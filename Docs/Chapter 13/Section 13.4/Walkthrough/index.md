# 13.4 The equation of a circle and tangents — Learn

**Prerequisites:** You can use coordinates, Pythagoras' theorem and gradients of straight lines.

**Objectives:** You will use circle equations, test points and find tangent equations using perpendicular gradients.

## Circle equations

A circle with centre $(a,b)$ and radius $r$ has equation

$$
(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2
$$

The two squared distances from the centre add to the squared radius.

**Example 1.** State the centre and radius of $(x-2)^2+(y+3)^2=25$.

Compare the equation with standard form:

$$
a=2,\quad b=-3,\quad r^2=25
$$

Take the positive square root because a radius is a length:

$$
\text{centre }(2,-3),\quad r=5
$$

**Example 2.** Check whether $(3,4)$ lies on $x^2+y^2=25$.

Substitute both coordinates:

$$
3^2+4^2=9+16=25
$$

The equation is true, so the point lies on the circle.

## Tangents

A **tangent** touches a circle at one point. At that point, the tangent is perpendicular to the radius. For non-vertical lines, perpendicular gradients multiply to $-1$.

**Example 3.** Find the tangent equation at $(3,4)$ to the circle $x^2+y^2=25$.

The radius gradient from $(0,0)$ to $(3,4)$ is $4/3$:

$$
m_{\text{radius}}=\frac{4-0}{3-0}=\frac43
$$

Use the negative reciprocal for the tangent gradient:

$$
m_{\text{tangent}}=-\frac34
$$

Use $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$ through $(3,4)$:

$$
y-4=-\frac34(x-3)
$$

> **Watch out.** In $(x-a)^2+(y-b)^2=r^2$, the centre is $(a,b)$. The signs inside the brackets look opposite; substitute the centre to check.

## Progress check

A circle has centre $(1,2)$ and a radius with gradient $2$. Predict the tangent gradient and check that their product is $-1$.

## You should now be able to

- find the centre and radius from a circle equation;
- write the equation from a centre and radius;
- test whether a point lies on a circle;
- find a tangent gradient and equation using perpendicular lines.
