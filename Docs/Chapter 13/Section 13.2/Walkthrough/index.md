# 13.2 Trigonometric graphs — Learn

**Prerequisites:** You know sine, cosine and tangent ratios in right-angled triangles. You can plot coordinates.

**Objectives:** You will sketch sine, cosine and tangent graphs in degrees, and read values, periods, asymptotes and intersections.

For these graphs, $x$ is an angle in degrees. A **period** is the length of one repeating cycle.

## Sine and cosine

The graph $y=\sin x$ passes through $(0,0)$, reaches $1$ at $90^\circ$, returns to zero at $180^\circ$, reaches $-1$ at $270^\circ$, then returns to zero at $360^\circ$. Its period is $360^\circ$.

The graph $y=\cos x$ starts at $1$. It reaches zero at $90^\circ$, $-1$ at $180^\circ$, zero at $270^\circ$, then $1$ at $360^\circ$. Its period is also $360^\circ$.

**Example 1.** Solve $\sin x=\frac12$ for $0^\circ\leq x\leq360^\circ$.

The equation asks where the sine graph meets the horizontal line $y=\frac12$:

$$
x=30^\circ\quad\text{or}\quad x=150^\circ
$$

## Tangent

The graph $y=\tan x$ repeats every $180^\circ$. It crosses the horizontal axis at multiples of $180^\circ$. It has vertical asymptotes where cosine is zero, because $\tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$.

**Example 2.** Sketch $y=\tan x$ from $-180^\circ$ to $180^\circ$.

Mark zeros at $-180^\circ,0^\circ,180^\circ$:

$$
x=-180^\circ,\quad 0^\circ,\quad 180^\circ
$$

Mark asymptotes at $-90^\circ$ and $90^\circ$, then draw increasing branches between them.

## Guided graph investigation

:::figure ch13-trig

**Predict:** Before moving the control, predict where $y=a\sin x$ reaches its maximum when $a=1$.

**Test:** Change the amplitude control and compare the highest and lowest points.

**Explain:** What changes as $a$ changes? What stays fixed? Use one value from the graph.

> **Watch out.** Sine and cosine repeat after $360^\circ$, while tangent repeats after $180^\circ$. Check the cycle length before drawing another cycle.
