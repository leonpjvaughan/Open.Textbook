# 14.2 Simultaneous equations by substitution — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Solve linear equations and substitute values into expressions.

**Objectives:** Solve pairs of linear equations by replacing a variable with an equal expression. This page covers A19.

## Replace a variable

**Substitution** means replacing a variable with an expression equal to it. Use this method when one equation already gives a variable on its own.

**Example 1.** Solve $y=2x-1$ and $x+y=8$.

Replace $y$ in the second equation with $2x-1$:

$$
x+(2x-1)=8
$$

$$
3x-1=8
$$

$$
3x=9
$$

$$
x=3
$$

Substitute $x=3$ into $y=2x-1$:

$$
y=2(3)-1=5
$$

The solution is $(3,5)$. Check: $3+5=8$.

**Example 2.** Solve $x=3y+2$ and $2x+y=19$.

Replace $x$ with $3y+2$ in the second equation:

$$
2(3y+2)+y=19
$$

$$
7y+4=19
$$

$$
y=\frac{15}{7}
$$

Then $x=3(15/7)+2=59/7$. Check by substitution in both original equations.

> **Watch out.** If $y=2x-1$, substitute the whole expression using brackets: $x+(2x-1)$, not $x+2x-1$ without tracking the signs. Expand and check the result in both equations.

## Progress check

For Company B, the fares give $f+4r=16$ and $f+8r=24$. Use the first equation to write $f$ in terms of $r$. What equation do you get after substitution?

- rearrange a linear equation to make one variable the subject;
- substitute an expression into the other equation;
- find both values and check both equations.
