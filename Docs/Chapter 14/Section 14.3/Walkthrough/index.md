# 14.3 Solving simultaneous equations graphically — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Plot coordinates and draw straight-line graphs from tables or equations.

**Objectives:** Solve a pair of linear equations by finding the intersection of their graphs. This page covers A19.

## The intersection is the solution

A point $(x,y)$ lies on a graph when its coordinates satisfy that graph's equation. The intersection of two graphs satisfies both equations, so it gives their simultaneous solution.

**Example.** Find the intersection of $y=2x+1$ and $y=-x+7$.

At the intersection, the two expressions for $y$ are equal:

$$
2x+1=-x+7
$$

$$
3x=6
$$

$$
x=2
$$

Substitute $x=2$ into $y=2x+1$:

$$
y=5
$$

The lines meet at $(2,5)$. On a graph, read both coordinates from the crossing. A drawn graph may give an approximate answer; check by substitution when possible.

**Explain:** What do equal $y$ values tell you about the point? Enter your explanation in your notes.

> **Watch out.** The intersection is an ordered pair $(x,y)$, not one number. Read across for $x$ and up for $y$, then check that the pair fits both equations.
