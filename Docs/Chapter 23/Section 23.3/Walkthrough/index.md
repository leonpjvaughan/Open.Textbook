# 23.3 Frequency trees, tables and possibility spaces — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication, fractions and systematic listing.

**Objectives:** organise data in a frequency tree or table; list outcomes systematically; calculate probabilities by counting outcomes.

A frequency tree splits a total into groups and subgroups. Each branch count tells how many observations are in that group. A two-way table displays counts for two categories. A possibility space lists every possible outcome of an experiment.

**Example 1.** A survey asks 30 students whether they walk to school. Of 18 who walk, 11 are girls. Of 12 who do not walk, 5 are girls. Complete the counts.

Walking girls: 11; walking boys: $18-11=7$.

Non-walking girls: 5; non-walking boys: $12-5=7$.

Girls total: $11+5=16$; boys total: $7+7=14$.

The table entries total $16+14=30$, which checks the survey total.

|  | Girls | Boys | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Walk | 11 | 7 | 18 |
| Do not walk | 5 | 7 | 12 |
| Total | 16 | 14 | 30 |

**Example 2.** Two fair coins are tossed. List the outcomes and find the probability of one head.

List the first coin then the second: HH, HT, TH, TT.

Two of four outcomes contain exactly one head: HT and TH.

$$P(\text{exactly one head})=\frac24=\frac12$$

> **Watch out.** In a possibility space, HT and TH are different outcomes because the first and second tosses are distinct.

## Progress check

A frequency tree starts with 50 people: 28 use a bus and 22 do not. If 16 bus users are adults, then 12 are not adults. Check that each pair of branches adds to its parent count.

## You should now be able to

- read and complete frequency trees and two-way tables;
- list a possibility space systematically and use it to find probability.
