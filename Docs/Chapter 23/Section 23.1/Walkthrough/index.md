# 23.1 The probability scale and equally likely outcomes — Learn

**Prerequisites:** fractions, equivalent fractions and simple ratios.

**Objectives:** describe chance on a scale from impossible to certain; calculate probabilities when outcomes are equally likely; use complements.

A probability is a number from 0 to 1. Zero means impossible, and 1 means certain. A probability of one half means the event and its opposite are equally likely. Probabilities may be written as fractions, decimals or percentages.

For equally likely outcomes, count the outcomes that match the event and divide by the total number of outcomes:

$$P(\text{event})=\frac{\text{number of matching outcomes}}{\text{total number of outcomes}}$$

The complement is the event that does not happen. Its probability is $1-P(\text{event})$.

**Example 1 — P2 and P3.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of rolling a number greater than 4.

The outcomes are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, each equally likely.

Two outcomes, 5 and 6, are greater than 4.

$$P(\text{greater than 4})=\frac{2}{6}=\frac13$$

**Example 2 — P4.** A bag contains 4 green counters and 1 yellow counter. Find the probability of not choosing green.

There are 5 counters, so the probability of green is $\frac45$.

$$P(\text{not green})=1-\frac45=\frac15$$

> **Watch out.** Do not count colours as equally likely when there are different numbers of counters. One yellow counter among five gives probability $\frac15$, not $\frac12$.

## Progress check

The fair die has probability $\frac13$ of showing more than 4. The opposite event is showing 4 or less, so its probability is $\frac23$.
