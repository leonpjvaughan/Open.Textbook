# 19.3 Arc length and sector area — Learn

**Prerequisites:** use circle circumference and area formulae; calculate fractions of amounts.

**Objectives:** calculate arc lengths, sector areas and sector perimeters from an angle and radius. This covers G18.

## Fractions of a circle

A sector is a slice of a circle bounded by two radii and an arc. Its angle is a fraction of $360^\circ$. The arc length and area use that same fraction.

$$\text{arc length}=\frac{\theta}{360}\times2\pi r$$

$$\text{sector area}=\frac{\theta}{360}\times\pi r^2$$

**Example 1.** Find the arc length for radius 12 cm and angle $60^\circ$.

Find the fraction of the full turn: $60\div360=\frac16$.

Find one sixth of the circumference: $\frac16\times2\pi\times12=4\pi$.

The arc length is $4\pi\text{ cm}$.

**Example 2.** Find the area of a $90^\circ$ sector with radius 8 cm.

The sector is one quarter of a circle: $90\div360=\frac14$.

Take one quarter of the circle area: $\frac14\times\pi\times8^2=16\pi$.

So its area is $16\pi\text{ cm}^2$. Add both radii to the arc when finding sector perimeter.

> **Watch out.** The angle fraction must use degrees out of $360^\circ$. Do not use the angle alone as a multiplier.

Progress check: suppose the pond path covers one quarter of the $4$ m radius pond boundary. Work out that arc length in terms of $\pi$.

## You should now be able to

- identify the fraction $\theta/360$ of a full circle;
- calculate an arc length and sector area;
- include both radii when finding sector perimeter;
- keep exact answers in terms of $\pi$.
