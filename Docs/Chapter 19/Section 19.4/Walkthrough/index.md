# 19.4 Surface area and volume of prisms and cylinders — Learn

**Prerequisites:** calculate areas of 2D shapes and multiply lengths; use circle formulae.

**Objectives:** calculate volumes and surface areas of prisms and cylinders. This covers G16 and G17.

## Prisms and cylinders

A prism has the same cross-section all along its length. Its volume is cross-section area times length. Its surface area is the sum of all exposed face areas. A cylinder is a circular prism.

For a cylinder, $V=\pi r^2h$. Its curved surface unwraps to a rectangle with sides $2\pi r$ and $h$, so curved area is $2\pi rh$. Add two circular ends for total surface area.

**Example 1.** A triangular prism has cross-section area $15\text{ cm}^2$ and length 8 cm.

Multiply the cross-section area by the length: $15\times8=120$.

Its volume is $120\text{ cm}^3$.

**Example 2.** A cylinder has radius 3 cm and height 7 cm. Find its volume and total surface area.

Use $V=\pi r^2h$: $V=\pi\times3^2\times7=63\pi$.

Add the curved area and two ends: $2\pi\times3\times7+2\pi\times3^2=60\pi$.

So volume is $63\pi\text{ cm}^3$ and surface area is $60\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

> **Watch out.** Surface area uses square units and volume uses cubic units. A missing face often means you counted an internal join.

Progress check: name the cross-section of a cylinder and the dimensions of its curved rectangle.

## You should now be able to

- find volume by multiplying cross-section area by prism length;
- calculate cylinder volume using $\pi r^2h$;
- find exposed face areas for total surface area;
- distinguish square units from cubic units.
