# 19.6 Composite shapes and solids — Learn

**Prerequisites:** use area, circle and solid formulae; split a figure into known shapes.

**Objectives:** find areas, perimeters, volumes and exposed surface areas of composite figures. This covers G17.

## Split, calculate, combine

A composite shape joins familiar shapes. Draw or imagine the joins, then add areas or volumes. For an opening or cut-out, subtract its area. For surface area, count exposed faces only.

**Example 1.** A $12$ cm by $8$ cm rectangle has a $4$ cm by $3$ cm corner removed.

Find the large area: $12\times8=96\text{ cm}^2$.

Find and subtract the cut-out: $4\times3=12$, then $96-12=84$.

The remaining area is $84\text{ cm}^2$.

**Example 2.** Join cuboids $4\times3\times2$ cm and $5\times3\times2$ cm along a $3\times2$ cm face.

Add their volumes: $4\times3\times2+5\times3\times2=54\text{ cm}^3$.

For outer surface area, subtract both hidden join faces from the two separate surface areas.

> **Watch out.** Adding the surface areas counts the joined faces twice. Remove both copies of every internal face.

Progress check: which measurements would you need to find the volume of a cylinder joined to a hemisphere?

## You should now be able to

- split a composite area into familiar shapes or subtract a cut-out;
- add volumes of joined solids;
- remove internal faces when finding outside surface area;
- explain which dimensions and formulae you chose.
