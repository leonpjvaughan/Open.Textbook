# 19.5 Surface area and volume of spheres, cones and pyramids — Learn

**Prerequisites:** use circle area; substitute into formulae and handle square and cube units.

**Objectives:** calculate surface area or volume of spheres, cones and pyramids. This covers G17.

## Curved solids and pyramids

For a sphere, $V=\frac43\pi r^3$ and $A=4\pi r^2$. A cone has $V=\frac13\pi r^2h$ and curved area $\pi rl$, where $l$ is slant height. A pyramid has $V=\frac13Ah$, where $A$ is its base area. To find its surface area, add the base area and the areas of its triangular faces.

**Example 1.** Find the volume of a cone with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm.

Substitute radius and perpendicular height: $V=\frac13\pi\times3^2\times10$.

Calculate: $V=30\pi\text{ cm}^3$.

**Example 2.** A square-based pyramid has base side 8 cm and height 9 cm.

Find its base area: $A=8\times8=64\text{ cm}^2$.

Use one third of base area times height: $V=\frac13\times64\times9=192\text{ cm}^3$.

> **Watch out.** Cone surface area uses slant height; cone volume uses perpendicular height. Label the diagram before substituting.

Progress check: state which height belongs in a cone volume formula.

## You should now be able to

- calculate sphere surface area and volume;
- calculate cone volume and curved surface area using the correct heights;
- calculate pyramid volume from base area and perpendicular height;
- find pyramid surface area by adding the base and triangular face areas;
- give square or cubic units as required.
