# 10.4 The nth term of a quadratic sequence — Practise with help

Try each item before opening its hints. Reveal the full working when you want to check your reasoning.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the first difference in $3,8,13$.
2. What is $2^2$?
3. Evaluate $n^2+n$ when $n=3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Check your warm-up</summary>

1. $5$.
2. $4$.
3. $12$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the nth term of $2,6,12,20,\ldots$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First differences are $4,6,8$; second differences are $2,2$. Start with $n^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract square numbers: $1,2,3,4$ remain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The remainder is $n$, so the rule is $n^2+n$.

</details>
</details>
</details>


**2.** Find the nth term of $3,10,21,36,\ldots$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Second differences are $4$, so start with $2n^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $2,8,18,32$: the remainders are $1,2,3,4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The rule is $2n^2+n+1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>


**3.** Check whether $n^2+2n$ gives $3,8,15,24$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute positions 1 through 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The values are $3,8,15,24$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

It matches all four terms.

</details>
</details>
</details>
