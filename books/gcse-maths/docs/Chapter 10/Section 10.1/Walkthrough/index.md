# 10.1 Term-to-term and position-to-term rules — Learn

**Prerequisites:** addition, subtraction, multiplication, and substitution into
an expression.

**Objectives:** describe how terms change and use a position number to find a
term directly.

## Term-to-term rules

A **term** is one number in a sequence. A term-to-term rule tells you how to get
from one term to the next.

For $4, 7, 10, 13,\ldots$, add $3$ each time. The dots mean that the pattern
continues.

**Example 1.** Write the next two terms.

$$
13+3=16
$$

$$
16+3=19
$$

The next terms are $16$ and $19$.

## Position-to-term rules

The **position** is a term's place in the list. A position-to-term rule uses the
position number to find a term without listing every earlier term.

In $3,6,9,12,\ldots$, each term is $3$ times its position. So the rule is
$3n$, where $n$ is the position.

**Example 2.** Find the 20th term.

$$
3n=3\times20
$$

$$
3\times20=60
$$

The 20th term is $60$.

For $5,8,11,14,\ldots$, each term is $3$ times its position, then $2$ more.
The rule is $3n+2$. At position $4$:

$$
3(4)+2=12+2=14
$$

**Progress check.** Pattern A in the chapter problem follows $3n+2$. Its
position 5 term is $3(5)+2=17$ tiles.

> **Watch out.** The first term is not always the change between terms. For
> $5,8,11,\ldots$, the first term is $5$ and the change is $3$. Check a rule
> by substituting positions $1$ and $2$.

## You should now be able to

- state the term-to-term change in a sequence;
- continue a sequence using its rule;
- calculate a term from its position number;
- check a position-to-term rule against the first terms.
