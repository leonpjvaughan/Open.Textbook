# 8.4 Factorising with common factors — Learn

**Prerequisites:** expanding a single bracket and identifying common factors in integers.

**By the end of this section you can:** factorise an expression by taking out its common factor and check by expanding.

## Learn the method

Factorising reverses expanding. It writes a sum as a product.

A common factor divides every term. Find the greatest common numerical factor and any variable shared by all terms.

For $12x+18$, the greatest common factor is $6$: $12x+18=6(2x+3)$.

Check by expanding the bracket. The result must match the original expression.

**Worked example.** Factorise $15x+20$.

$15x+20=5(3x+4)$ — take out the common factor $5$.

Check: $5(3x+4)=15x+20$.

> **Watch out.** Factorising only the first term leaves the expression unchanged incorrectly. Divide every term by the common factor, then expand your answer to check.

- factorise an expression by taking out its common factor and check by expanding.
