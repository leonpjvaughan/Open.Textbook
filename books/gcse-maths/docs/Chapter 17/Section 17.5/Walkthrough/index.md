# 17.5 Bearings and scale drawings — Learn

**Prerequisites:** use a protractor, measure a line segment and multiply or divide with decimals.

**Objectives:** measure lengths and angles, interpret and draw scale plans, use compass directions, and write three-figure bearings.

## Bearings

A **bearing** gives a direction as an angle measured clockwise from north. Write it with three digits, adding zeros at the front when needed. For example, east is $090^\circ$ and south-west is $225^\circ$.

**Worked example.** A path goes from the south-west corner of a rectangle to its north-east corner. Find its bearing from the start if the rectangle is $24$ m east-west and $18$ m north-south.

- The path's eastward component is $24$ m and its northward component is $18$ m.
- The angle east of north is $\tan^{-1}(24/18)$, about $53.1^\circ$.
- A bearing turns clockwise from north, so the bearing is about $053^\circ$.

For a hand-drawn direction, place the protractor centre at the start and align zero with north. Measure clockwise.

## Investigate a bearing and its back bearing

**Predict:** The bearing of B from A is measured at A, turning clockwise from the
north line. Before you move the slider, predict the bearing of A from B when the
bearing of B from A is $090^\circ$.

**Test:** Move the bearing slider and watch the arc at A. Then look at the second
north line, which is drawn at B, and compare the two bearings.

> **Explain:** The north line at A and the north line at B point the same way. Use
> that to explain why the two bearings always differ by $180^\circ$.

## Scale drawings

A **scale** compares a drawing length with a real length. At scale $1$ cm represents $3$ m, divide real lengths by $3$ to find centimetres on the plan.

**Worked example.** Draw the $24$ m by $18$ m garden at $1$ cm to $3$ m.

- $24 \div 3 = 8$, so draw the east-west side as $8$ cm.
- $18 \div 3 = 6$, so draw the north-south side as $6$ cm.
- Measure the diagonal: $\sqrt{8^2+6^2}=10$ cm, representing $30$ m.

> **Watch out.** A bearing is not measured from the nearest compass direction. Start at north and turn clockwise; write three digits, such as $053^\circ$.
> **Re-check:** read the bearing clockwise from north and confirm that it has three digits.

## Progress check

The plan is drawn at $1$ cm for $3$ m, and its diagonal measures $10$ cm. Work out the real path length. Compare it with your first estimate, and explain what the bearing $053^\circ$ tells you.

## You should now be able to

- measure line segments and angles accurately;
- use compass points and three-figure bearings;
- convert between lengths on a scale drawing and real lengths;
- check a direction angle from north, clockwise.
