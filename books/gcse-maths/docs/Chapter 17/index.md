# Chapter 17: Properties, constructions, loci and bearings

## The chapter problem: plan a safe route

A community garden is a rectangular site, $24$ m east to west and $18$ m north
to south. A water point lies at the centre. A straight path runs from the
south-west corner to the north-east corner. A circular planting bed has its
centre at the water point and radius $4$ m.

**First contact:** predict whether the path is longer or shorter than $30$ m.
Estimate the path's bearing from the south-west corner. No exact answer is
needed yet.

At the end, you will draw the plan to scale, describe the path using a bearing,
construct the centre lines, and show where points exactly $4$ m from the water
point can lie.

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- name and use the parts, properties and symmetries of 2D and 3D shapes;
- read and draw plans and elevations of 3D shapes;
- use a ruler and compasses to construct perpendiculars and bisect angles;
- describe and solve problems about loci, including shortest distance to a line;
- measure angles and lengths, read scale drawings, and use three-figure bearings.

## Where it is used

Drawings and directions are part of design and navigation. An architect
draws plans and elevations, a gardener sets out a centre with compasses, and a
pilot or a walker follows a bearing. Constructions build accurate figures with
only a straight edge and compasses.
