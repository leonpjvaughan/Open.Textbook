# 15.2 Simplifying algebraic fractions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** factorise quadratics and cancel common numerical factors.

**Objectives:** factorise a numerator or denominator, cancel common factors, and state excluded values.

An algebraic fraction has an algebraic expression in its numerator, denominator, or both. Factorising writes an expression as a product. You can cancel common factors, but you cannot cancel separate terms joined by addition or subtraction.

**Example 1.** Simplify $\frac{x^2-9}{x^2+6x+9}$.

Factorise the numerator: $x^2-9=(x-3)(x+3)$.
Factorise the denominator: $x^2+6x+9=(x+3)(x+3)$.
Cancel the common factor $x+3$: $\frac{x-3}{x+3}$.
The original denominator is zero at $x=-3$, so $x\neq-3$.

**Example 2.** Simplify $\frac{6x^2}{9x}$.

Write numerator and denominator as products: $\frac{2\cdot3\cdot x\cdot x}{3\cdot3\cdot x}$.
Cancel common factors: $\frac{2x}{3}$.
The original denominator requires $x\neq0$.

> **Watch out.** You cannot cancel the $x$ terms in $\frac{x+4}{x+2}$. They are parts of sums, not common factors. At $x=1$, the fraction is $5/3$, not $1$. Cancel whole common factors only.

## Progress check

Explain why the simplified expression can look defined at an excluded input. The restriction comes from the original denominator.

## You should now be able to

- factorise the numerator and denominator;
- cancel whole common factors;
- state values excluded by the original denominator.
