# 15.5 Algebraic proof — Learn

**Prerequisites:** expand brackets, collect like terms, and use algebraic notation for integers.

**Objectives:** represent numbers with variables, build a chain of valid equalities, and prove divisibility or an identity. This section teaches algebraic proof.

A proof uses general reasoning to show that a statement is true for every value in its stated set. Consecutive integers differ by one. An even integer can be written as $2n$, where $n$ is an integer.

**Example 1.** Prove that the sum of three consecutive integers is divisible by $3$.

Let the integers be $n$, $n+1$, and $n+2$.
Add them: $n+(n+1)+(n+2)$.
Collect terms: $3n+3$.
Factorise: $3(n+1)$.
Since $n+1$ is an integer, the sum is a multiple of $3$.

**Example 2.** Prove that the difference between the squares of consecutive integers is odd.

Let the integers be $n$ and $n+1$.
Subtract the squares: $(n+1)^2-n^2$.
Expand: $n^2+2n+1-n^2$.
Simplify: $2n+1$.
This is twice an integer plus one, so it is odd.

> **Watch out.** Testing examples can suggest a result, but it does not prove a statement for all integers. Use a general integer and explain why the final form has the required property.
