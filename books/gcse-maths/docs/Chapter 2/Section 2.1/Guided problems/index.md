# 2.1 Factors, multiples and prime numbers — Practise with help

Try each question first. Open a hint if needed, then check the full working.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $6\times7$.
2. List the first four positive multiples of 5.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $42$.
2. $5,10,15,20$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find all the factors of 24.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Find factor pairs, starting with 1.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The pairs are $1\times24$, $2\times12$, $3\times8$, and $4\times6$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The factors are $1,2,3,4,6,8,12,24$.
</details>

**2.** Is 37 prime? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Test prime divisors no greater than $\sqrt{37}$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

$6^2=36<37<49=7^2$, so test 2, 3 and 5.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

37 is not divisible by 2, 3 or 5. It has no prime factor up to its square root, so 37 is prime.
</details>

**3.** Write the first five positive multiples of 8.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply 8 by 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The products are 8, 16, 24, 32 and 40.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The multiples are $8,16,24,32,40$.
</details>
