# 2.1 Factors, multiples and prime numbers — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication facts and division with whole numbers.

**Objectives:** find factors and multiples; recognise prime and composite numbers; explain why 1 is not prime.

## Factors and multiples

A **factor** of a number divides it exactly, with no remainder. A **multiple** is the result of multiplying a number by a whole number.

For 18, factor pairs are $1\times18$, $2\times9$ and $3\times6$. Therefore its factors are $1,2,3,6,9,18$.

Multiples of 6 are $6,12,18,24,30,\ldots$. The list continues by adding 6 each time.

**Worked example.** Find all factors of 20 and the first four positive multiples of 7.

Pair factors until the pairs reverse: $1\times20$, $2\times10$, $4\times5$.

So the factors are $1,2,4,5,10,20$.

Multiply 7 by $1,2,3,4$: $7,14,21,28$.

## Prime numbers

A **prime number** is a whole number greater than 1 with exactly two factors: 1 and itself. A **composite number** has more than two factors. The number 1 is neither prime nor composite because it has one factor.

**Worked example.** Is 29 prime?

Check possible divisors up to 5, since $5^2<29<6^2$. Neither 2, 3 nor 5 divides 29 exactly.

No prime divisor at most $\sqrt{29}$ divides it, so 29 is prime.

**Worked example.** Explain why 21 is composite.

$21=3\times7$, so 3 and 7 divide it exactly.

It has factors besides 1 and itself, so it is composite.

> **Watch out.** You may call 1 prime because it has no factor pair. A prime must have exactly two factors; 1 has only one. Check the number of factors.
