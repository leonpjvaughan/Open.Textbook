# 2.2 Prime factorisation, HCF and LCM — Practise with help

Try each question first. Open a hint if needed, then check the full working.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Is 13 prime? State its factors.
2. Find two factors shared by 18 and 30.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. Yes; 1 and 13.
2. For example, 1 and 6.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Write 48 as a product of prime factors.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Divide by 2 while the result is even.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

$48=2\times24=2\times2\times12=2\times2\times2\times6$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$48=2\times2\times2\times2\times3=2^4\times3$.
</details>

**2.** Find the HCF of 18 and 30.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Write each number as prime factors and take shared primes with smaller powers.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

$18=2\times3^2$ and $30=2\times3\times5$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The shared factors are $2\times3=6$, so the HCF is 6.
</details>

**3.** Find the LCM of 8 and 12.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Use each prime with its greater power.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

$8=2^3$ and $12=2^2\times3$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$2^3\times3=24$, so the LCM is 24.
</details>
