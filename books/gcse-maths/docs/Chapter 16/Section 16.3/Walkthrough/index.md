# 16.3 Angles in triangles and quadrilaterals — Learn

**Prerequisites:** use straight-line and parallel-line facts, and solve one-step equations.

**Objectives:** use triangle and quadrilateral angle sums, including the exterior angle of a triangle.

## Triangles

The interior angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$. An exterior angle is formed by extending one side. It equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles.

:::prove angles-in-a-triangle

**Example 1.** A triangle has angles $48^\circ$, $73^\circ$ and $x^\circ$.

$$
48 + 73 + x = 180
$$

$$
x = 180 - 121 = 59^\circ
$$

**Example 2.** A triangle has two opposite interior angles of $42^\circ$ and $69^\circ$. Find the exterior angle.

$$
42 + 69 = 111^\circ
$$

The exterior angle is $111^\circ$, the sum of the opposite interior angles.

## Quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is a four-sided polygon. Its interior angles add to $360^\circ$. You can split it into two triangles, each with angle sum $180^\circ$.

**Example 3.** Three angles in a quadrilateral are $90^\circ$, $105^\circ$ and $83^\circ$. Find the fourth.

$$
90 + 105 + 83 + x = 360
$$

$$
x = 360 - 278 = 82^\circ
$$

> **Watch out.** A quadrilateral does not have angle sum $180^\circ$. That is the sum for one triangle; a diagonal makes two triangles, so $180+180=360^\circ$.
