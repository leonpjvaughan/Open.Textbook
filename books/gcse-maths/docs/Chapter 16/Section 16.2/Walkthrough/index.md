# 16.2 Angles in parallel lines — Learn

**Prerequisites:** use straight-line and vertically opposite angle facts. Parallel lines never meet, however far they extend.

**Objectives:** identify corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles and use their facts to calculate unknown angles.

## A transversal crosses parallel lines

A transversal is a line that crosses two other lines. When those lines are parallel, matching angles have useful relationships:

- corresponding angles are equal;
- alternate angles are equal;
- co-interior angles on the same side of the transversal add to $180^\circ$.

Before you move the sliders, predict which angles at the lower crossing stay equal to the angle marked a. Write your prediction down first.

:::figure parallel-line-angles

Now slide and tilt the transversal and check. Then explain why the corresponding and alternate angles stay equal, and why the co-interior angles add to $180^\circ$.

**Example 1.** A corresponding angle is $71^\circ$. Find its matching angle.

Corresponding angles are equal for parallel lines, so the angle is $71^\circ$.

**Example 2.** An alternate angle is $113^\circ$. Find the unknown alternate angle.

Alternate angles are equal for parallel lines, so the unknown angle is $113^\circ$.

**Example 3.** Co-interior angles are $x^\circ$ and $68^\circ$. Find $x$.

$$
x + 68 = 180
$$

$$
x = 180 - 68 = 112^\circ
$$

Co-interior angles on the same side of a transversal sum to $180^\circ$.

> **Watch out.** Equal-looking positions do not prove lines are parallel. Use the parallel-line fact only when the diagram states the lines are parallel or you have proved it.

## Progress check

A straight path crosses two parallel tile borders. One acute angle is $68^\circ$. What is the co-interior angle on its side? Give the angle fact.

## You should now be able to

- recognise corresponding, alternate and co-interior angle pairs;
- use equality for corresponding and alternate angles;
- use a sum of $180^\circ$ for co-interior angles.
