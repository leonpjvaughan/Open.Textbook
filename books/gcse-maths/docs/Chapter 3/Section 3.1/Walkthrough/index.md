# 3.1 Powers and roots — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication, division and square numbers.

**Objectives:** calculate integer powers; find square, cube and higher roots;
connect a root to the power that reverses it.

By the end of this section, you can calculate integer powers, find roots, and
check a root by using its matching power.

## Powers

A power is a short way to write repeated multiplication. In $3^4$, the base is
$3$ and the index is $4$. It means multiply four copies of 3.

$$3^4=3\times3\times3\times3=81$$

**Example 1.** Work out $2^5$.

$$
2^5=2\times2\times2\times2\times2
$$

$$2^5=32$$

The index tells you how many factors to multiply. It does not tell you to
multiply the base by the index.

## Roots

A square root reverses squaring. The square root of 49 is 7 because $7^2=49$.
The symbol $\sqrt{\ }$ means the positive square root.

$$\sqrt{49}=7 \qquad 7^2=49$$

A cube root reverses cubing. The cube root of 27 is 3 because $3^3=27$.

$$\sqrt[3]{27}=3 \qquad 3^3=27$$

**Example 2.** Work out $\sqrt{144}$ and $\sqrt[3]{125}$.

$$12^2=144 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \sqrt{144}=12$$

$$5^3=125 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \sqrt[3]{125}=5$$

To check a root, raise your answer to the matching power.

> **Watch out.** $\sqrt{49}$ means the positive root, 7. The equation
> $x^2=49$ has two solutions, $x=7$ and $x=-7$, because both square to 49.
> Check whether the question asks for a root or for solutions to an equation.

## Progress check

The file doubles once per round. After 3 rounds it has $2^3=8$ units. Predict
how the power will represent the size after 20 rounds.

## You should now be able to

- write repeated multiplication as a power and calculate it;
- find square, cube and higher roots of known powers;
- check a root by raising it to the matching power;
- distinguish a positive square root from the two solutions of a square equation.
