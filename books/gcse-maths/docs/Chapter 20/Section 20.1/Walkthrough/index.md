# 20.1 Pythagoras' theorem — Learn

**Prerequisites:** square numbers, square roots, and identifying a right angle.

**Objectives:** use Pythagoras' theorem to find a missing side in a right-angled triangle, including the hypotenuse.

A **hypotenuse** is the longest side, opposite the right angle. If the shorter sides are $a$ and $b$, and the hypotenuse is $c$, then $a^2+b^2=c^2$.

:::prove pythagoras

**Example 1.** A right-angled triangle has shorter sides $6$ cm and $8$ cm. Find its hypotenuse.

$$c^2=6^2+8^2$$

$$c^2=36+64$$

$$c^2=100$$

$$c=10\text{ cm}$$

The positive root is used because a length cannot be negative.

**Example 2.** The hypotenuse is $13$ cm and one shorter side is $5$ cm. Find the other side $x$.

$$x^2+5^2=13^2$$

$$x^2=169-25$$

$$x^2=144$$

$$x=12\text{ cm}$$

> **Watch out.** Do not subtract when finding the hypotenuse. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle, so its square equals the sum of the other two squares. Check that it is the longest side.
