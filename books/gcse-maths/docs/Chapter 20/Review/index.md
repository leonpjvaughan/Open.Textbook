# Chapter 20: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The garden is $9$ m by $12$ m. The path is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.

$$d^2=9^2+12^2$$

$$d^2=81+144=225$$

$$d=15\text{ m}$$

The estimate should have been near $15$ m. The result is longer than either side and shorter than going around two edges, so it is plausible.

## Mixed practice: choose a method and explain why

For each question, name the method and explain why it fits before calculating.

1. A ladder is 13 m long and reaches 12 m up a wall. Find its distance from the wall. Which right-triangle method applies?
2. A 10 m pole is viewed from 10 m away. Find the angle of elevation. Which ratio connects the measurements?
3. Two sides of a triangle are 7 cm and 9 cm, with included angle 60°. Find the third side. Which rule fits?
4. Sides 8 cm and 11 cm include angle 30°. Find the area. Which area formula fits?
5. A cuboid measures 3 cm by 4 cm by 12 cm. Find its space diagonal. How many right triangles do you need?
6. In a triangle, $a=6$ cm, $A=30^\circ$, and $B=45^\circ$. Find $b$.
7. Work out exactly $\sin60^\circ\cos30^\circ$.
8. A triangle has sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the angle opposite the longest side.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answers and checks</summary>

1. Pythagoras: $5$ m. 2. Tangent: $45^\circ$. 3. Cosine rule: $\sqrt{67}$ cm. 4. $22$ cm$^2$. 5. Two triangles; $13$ cm. 6. $6\sqrt2$ cm. 7. $3/4$. 8. Cosine rule: about $92.9^\circ$.

**Reflection:** Which measurements help you choose between Pythagoras, a trigonometric ratio, the sine rule, the cosine rule, and the area formula? Which method can you now explain without notes?
