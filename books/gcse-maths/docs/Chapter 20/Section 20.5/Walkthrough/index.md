# 20.5 3D Pythagoras and trigonometry — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometric ratios in a flat triangle.

**Objectives:** find a diagonal or angle in a three-dimensional shape by identifying right-angled triangles.

A **3D diagonal** joins two vertices that do not lie on one face. Work in stages: find a face diagonal first, then use it with the remaining perpendicular side.

**Example 1.** A cuboid measures $3$ cm by $4$ cm by $12$ cm. Find its space diagonal $d$.

First find the diagonal across the $3$ by $4$ base.

$$b^2=3^2+4^2$$

$$b=5\text{ cm}$$

Now use $b$ and the height as the two perpendicular sides.

$$d^2=5^2+12^2$$

$$d=13\text{ cm}$$

**Example 2.** In the same cuboid, find the angle between the space diagonal and the base. The diagonal's vertical rise is $12$ cm and its horizontal projection is $5$ cm.

$$\tan\theta=\frac{12}{5}$$

$$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{12}{5}\right)$$

$$\theta\approx67.4^\circ$$

> **Watch out.** A face diagonal is not the space diagonal. Draw the second right-angled triangle and use the face diagonal as one of its sides.
> **Re-check:** name the triangle you used and confirm that its hypotenuse is the space diagonal.

## Progress check

The garden diagonal in 20.1 is a flat version of the same method: identify perpendicular sides before applying the theorem.

## You should now be able to

- identify the face diagonal and use it to find a space diagonal;
- model the angle between a space diagonal and a face using a right-angled triangle;
- select Pythagoras' theorem or a trigonometric ratio and check the units.
