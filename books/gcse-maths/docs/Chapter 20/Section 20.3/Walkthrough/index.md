# 20.3 Angles of elevation and depression — Learn

**Prerequisites:** trigonometric ratios and drawing right-angled triangles.

**Objectives:** model an angle of elevation or depression and solve for a distance or angle.

An **angle of elevation** is measured upwards from a horizontal line. An **angle of depression** is measured downwards from a horizontal line. The horizontal lines are parallel, so the angle of depression equals the matching angle inside the triangle.

**Example 1.** A person stands $20$ m from a tower. The angle of elevation to its top is $35^\circ$. Find the height above the person's eye level.

Draw a right-angled triangle. The height is opposite and $20$ m is adjacent.

$$\tan 35^\circ=\frac{h}{20}$$

$$h=20\tan 35^\circ$$

$$h\approx14.0\text{ m}$$

**Example 2.** A boat is $50$ m below a cliff top. The angle of depression is $25^\circ$. Find the horizontal distance $d$.

The angle inside the boat triangle is also $25^\circ$ because the horizontal lines are parallel.

$$\tan25^\circ=\frac{50}{d}$$

$$d=\frac{50}{\tan25^\circ}$$

$$d\approx107.2\text{ m}$$

> **Watch out.** The angle of depression starts at the observer's horizontal line. Copying it into the triangle uses parallel lines; check the matching angle before choosing a ratio.
