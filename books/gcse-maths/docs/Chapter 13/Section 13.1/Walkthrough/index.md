# 13.1 Cubic, reciprocal and exponential graphs — Learn

**Prerequisites:** You can plot coordinates, recognise quadratic graphs and substitute values into expressions.

**Objectives:** You will sketch cubic, reciprocal and exponential graphs and interpret their intercepts, asymptotes and growth.

## Cubic graphs

A cubic rule has highest power three. The graph of $y=x^3$ falls to the left and rises to the right. To find an $x$-intercept, set $y=0$ and solve.

**Example 1.** Find the intercepts of $y=x^3-4x$.

Set $y=0$:

$$
x^3-4x=0
$$

Factor out $x$:

$$
x(x^2-4)=0
$$

Factor the difference of squares:

$$
x(x-2)(x+2)=0
$$

So the intercepts are $(-2,0),(0,0),(2,0)$. The $y$-intercept is found by setting $x=0$.

## Reciprocal graphs

A reciprocal rule has the form $y=\frac{k}{x}$. The curve has two branches and approaches both axes. An **asymptote** is a line that a curve approaches without reaching.

**Example 2.** Find values for $y=\frac1x$ at $x=-2,-1,1,2$.

Substitute each input:

$$
y=-\frac12,-1,1,\frac12
$$

The rule has no value when $x=0$, so its branches do not join across that line.

## Exponential graphs

An exponential rule has the variable in the power, such as $y=2^x$. For each unit increase in $x$, the output is multiplied by the same factor.

**Example 3.** Find values of $y=2^x$ for $x=-1,0,1,2$.

Substitute each input:

$$
2^{-1}=\frac12,\quad 2^0=1,\quad 2^1=2,\quad 2^2=4
$$

The graph passes through $(0,1)$ and increases by a factor of two each step right.

> **Watch out.** The reciprocal rule is undefined at $x=0$. Leave a gap there; joining the branches would include points that do not satisfy the rule.
> **Re-check:** put $x=0$ into the rule and ask whether the expression can be worked out.
