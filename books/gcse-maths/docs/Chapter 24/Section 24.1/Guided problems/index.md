# 24.1 Collecting data, sampling and questionnaires — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

**Closed book:** Write $\frac{18}{30}$ in its simplest form, then work out $20\%$ of 150.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Check your answers</summary>

$\frac{18}{30}=\frac35$, and $20\%$ of 150 is 30. If either answer differs, revisit equivalent fractions and percentages before continuing.
</details>

### Question 1

Estimate the number of pupils who prefer football in a group of 240, if 18 of 30 sampled pupils do.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Use the sample fraction.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the sample fraction by the population.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The sample fraction is $18/30=0.6$. Estimate $0.6\times240=144$ pupils.
</details>

### Question 2

Improve this question: “You like the new canteen menu, do you not?”

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Look for wording that suggests an answer.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Ask for an opinion without suggesting a preferred answer.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

For example: “What do you think of the new canteen menu?”
</details>

### Question 3

A sample of 50 pupils has 15 who walk. Estimate the percentage.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the count by the sample size.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the fraction of the sample by 100.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The fraction is $15/50=0.3$. Convert it to a percentage: $0.3\times100=30\%$.
</details>
