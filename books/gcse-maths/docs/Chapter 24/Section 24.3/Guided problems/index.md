# 24.3 Averages and range — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

**Closed book:** Put $7, 2, 9, 4$ in order from smallest to largest, then work out $7+9+4$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Check your answers</summary>

The order is 2, 4, 7, 9, and $7+9+4=20$. If either answer differs, revisit ordering numbers and addition before continuing.
</details>

### Question 1

Order $4, 1, 3$ from smallest to largest and write the middle value.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Put the values in order first, then look at the one in the middle.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

In order the values are $1, 3, 4$.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The middle value is 3, so the median is 3.
</details>

### Question 2

Find the mean of 2, 4, 6.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Add the values, then divide by how many values there are.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The total is $2+4+6=12$, and there are 3 values.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

Mean $=12\div3=4$.
</details>

### Question 3

Find the median and range of 2, 3, 3, 8.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

For the median, find the middle of the ordered list. For the range, subtract the smallest value from the largest.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The middle pair is the 2nd and 3rd values, which are both 3.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

Median $=(3+3)\div2=3$; range $=8-2=6$.
</details>
