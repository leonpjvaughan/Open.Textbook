# 24.4 Averages from frequency tables — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

**Closed book:** Work out $6\times4$, then work out $12+15+18$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Check your answers</summary>

$6\times4=24$, and $12+15+18=45$. If either answer differs, revisit multiplication and addition before continuing.
</details>

### Question 1

Values 1 and 3 have frequencies 2 and 4. Work out the total frequency.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

The total frequency is the sum of the frequency column.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Add the two frequencies: $2+4$.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

Total frequency $=2+4=6$.
</details>

### Question 2

For the same values and frequencies, work out $1\times2$ and $3\times4$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply each value by its own frequency.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The first product is $1\times2=2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$1\times2=2$ and $3\times4=12$.
</details>

### Question 3

Use those values and frequencies to find the mean.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the total of value $\times$ frequency by the total frequency.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The total of value $\times$ frequency is $2+12=14$, and the total frequency is 6.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

Mean $=14\div6=\frac{7}{3}$.
</details>
