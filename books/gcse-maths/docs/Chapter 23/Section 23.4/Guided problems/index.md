# 23.4 Combined events and tree diagrams — Practise with help

Work each question first. Open the hint if you need it, then reveal the working to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Try these from memory before opening the answers.

1. What is $\frac12\times\frac12$?
2. List the outcomes of two coin tosses.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Check</summary>

1. $\frac14$. 2. HH, HT, TH, TT.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of exactly two tails.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

List the four equally likely outcomes. Which one has two tails?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Only TT has two tails. Its probability is $\frac12\times\frac12=\frac14$.

</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair die is rolled twice. Find the probability of two numbers less than 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The results less than 3 are 1 and 2. The two rolls are independent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

For each roll the probability is $\frac26=\frac13$. Multiply: $\frac13\times\frac13=\frac19$.

</details>
</details>

**3.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of at least one head.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the probability of no heads, then subtract it from 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

 Use the complement: $1-P(\text{no heads})=1-\frac14=\frac34$.

</details>
</details>

**4.** A bag contains 4 red and 3 blue counters. One counter is drawn and not replaced. Find the probability of red then blue.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

After a red counter is removed, count the blue counters and the total counters left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The probabilities are $\frac47$ for red first and $\frac36$ for blue next.
Multiply: $\frac47\times\frac36=\frac{12}{42}=\frac27$.
</details>
</details>
