## Practise with help

**1.** A fair four-sided spinner has outcomes 1, 2, 3 and 4. Find the probability of an even number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count matching outcomes and divide by all equally likely outcomes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Even outcomes are 2 and 4, so $P(\text{even})=\frac24=\frac12$.

</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of a result less than 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

List the die results that meet the condition, then divide by six.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The matching results are 1 and 2, so the probability is $\frac26=\frac13$.

</details>
</details>

**3.** A bag has 2 white and 6 black counters. Find the probability of not choosing black.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the total number of counters. The white counters are the outcomes that are not black.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Not black means white, so $P=\frac28=\frac14$.

</details>
</details>

**4.** A fair six-sided die is rolled 60 times. Find the expected number of fives. Then compare the expected relative frequency with the theoretical probability of a five.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the probability of a five first. Multiply it by 60 for the expected count.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The probability of a five is $\frac16$. The expected number is
$60\times\frac16=10$. The expected relative frequency is $\frac{10}{60}=\frac16$,
which matches the theoretical probability. An actual set of 60 rolls may differ.
</details>
</details>
