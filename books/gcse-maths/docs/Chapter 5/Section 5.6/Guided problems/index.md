# 5.6 Comparing lengths, areas and volumes — Practise with help

Try each question first. Open a hint if you are stuck, the next step for one
concrete move, and the full working only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. Do not look back at the section.

1. Work out $6^2$.
2. Work out $2^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $6^2=36$.
2. $2^3=8$.

If an answer differs, revisit Section 3.1 for squares and cubes.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Similar shapes have length scale factor 2.5. The smaller area is
12 cm$^2$. Find the larger area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square the length scale factor to get the area scale factor.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The area factor is $2.5^2=6.25$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The larger area is $12\times6.25=75$ cm$^2$.
</details>

**2.** Similar solids have length scale factor 3. The smaller volume is
20 cm$^3$. Find the larger volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Cube the length scale factor to get the volume scale factor.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The volume factor is $3^3=27$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The larger volume is $20\times27=540$ cm$^3$.
</details>

**3.** Two similar solids have volumes $40$ cm$^3$ and $320$ cm$^3$. Find the
length scale factor from small to large.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the volume factor first, then take its cube root.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$320\div40=8$, so the volume factor is 8.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The length factor is $\sqrt[3]{8}=2$. Check: $2^3=8$.
</details>

**4. Earlier learning.** Work out $4^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A cube multiplies the number by itself three times.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4^3=4\times4\times4$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$4^3=64$. This section cubes the length factor to find the volume factor.
</details>
