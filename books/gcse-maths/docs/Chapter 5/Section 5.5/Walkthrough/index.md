# 5.5 Scale factors, scale diagrams and maps — Learn

## Prerequisites

You should be able to multiply and divide whole numbers and fractions. Check units before comparing quantities.

## Key terms

A **scale** compares a drawing length with a real length. The **scale factor** is the multiplier between corresponding lengths.

## Objectives

- Convert units and use scales to find map, drawing and real lengths.
- Check that the result matches the units and direction of change.

## Learn the method

A scale compares drawing and real lengths in the same units. Scale $1:50$ means one drawing unit represents 50 real units. Convert units before applying the scale.

**Worked example. A 6 cm drawing represents a 9 m wall. Find drawing:real scale.**

- Convert: $9\times100=900$ cm.
- Form the ratio: $6:900$.
- Divide both parts by 6: $1:150$.
- Check: 1 cm represents 150 cm, or 1.5 m.

**Progress check:** at 1 cm for 1 m, an 18 m path needs 18 cm.

> **Watch out.** Do not compare 9 m with 6 cm before converting units. Write both lengths in centimetres, then simplify the ratio.

## Try it: read a map

**Predict first.** The map uses a scale of 1 cm for 1 m. Without moving the
slider, write down the real distance for a map length of 7 cm. Then move the
slider and check your prediction.

:::figure ch5-map-scale

**Test.** Add 1 cm at a time and watch the readout. By how much does the real
distance change each time?

**Explain.** Write one sentence saying why the real distance is found by
multiplying the map length rather than adding to it, and what would change if the
scale were 1 cm for 5 m.

## You should now be able to

- I can convert lengths into matching units before using a scale.
- I can find drawing lengths and real distances from a scale.
- I can check units and the size of the result.
