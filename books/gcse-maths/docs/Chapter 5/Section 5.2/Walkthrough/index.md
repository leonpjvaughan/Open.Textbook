# 5.2 Ratio, fractions and linear functions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiply and divide whole numbers and fractions. Check units before comparing quantities.

## Key terms

A **linear relationship** has a constant rate of change. A **constant of proportionality** is the multiplier in a relationship $y=kx$.

**Objectives:** express ratio parts as fractions of a total and identify proportional linear relationships; check that the result matches the units and direction of change.

## Learn the method

A ratio can describe a fraction of a total. For $a:b$, the first amount is $a/(a+b)$ of the total. Proportion means equality of ratios. A linear relationship has form $y=mx+c$. Direct proportion is $y=kx$, whose graph passes through $(0,0)$.

**Worked example. The ratio of red to blue counters is $2:3$. If the ratio stays the same, how many blue counters go with 8 red counters?**

- Set the ratios equal: $\frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{b}$.
- The first ratio has been multiplied by 4, so multiply the second part by 4: $b=12$.
- The equivalent ratio is $8:12$.

**Worked example. 4 cakes use 300 g of flour. Find the flour for 10 cakes.**

- Find flour for one cake: $300\div4=75$ g.
- Multiply by 10 cakes: $75\times10=750$ g.
- Check the rate: $750\div10=75$ g per cake.

The amounts are in direct proportion because their ratio stays constant: $300:4=750:10$.
If $n$ is the number of cakes and $f$ is flour in grams, the relationship is $f=75n$.

> **Watch out.** A straight line is not always direct proportion. Check its $y$-intercept: direct proportion passes through $(0,0)$.

## You should now be able to

- write each ratio part as a fraction of the total.
- use a constant multiplier to complete a proportional relationship.
- explain why a non-zero intercept is not direct proportion.
