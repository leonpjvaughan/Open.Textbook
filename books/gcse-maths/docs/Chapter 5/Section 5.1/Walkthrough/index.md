# 5.1 Ratio notation, simplifying and sharing — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiply and divide whole numbers and fractions. Check units before comparing quantities.

## Key terms

A **ratio** compares quantities by division. The **unitary method** finds the value of one equal part.

**Objectives:** write equivalent ratios, simplify them and share a total in a given ratio; check that the result matches the units and direction of change.

## Learn the method

A ratio compares amounts by division. In $3:5$, there are 3 parts of the first amount for every 5 parts of the second. Multiply or divide every part by the same non-zero number to make an equivalent ratio. To share a total, add the parts, divide the total by their sum, then multiply by each part count.

**Worked example. The ratio of red counters to blue counters is $3:5$. What fraction of the blue count is the red count?**

- Compare red with blue: $\frac{3}{5}$.
- The red count is $\frac{3}{5}$ of the blue count.

**Worked example. A class has 8 pupils who walk and 12 who travel by bus. Express the number who travel by bus as a fraction of the number who walk.**

- Write bus:walk as a fraction: $\frac{12}{8}$.
- Simplify: $\frac{12}{8}=\frac{3}{2}$.
- The fraction is greater than 1 because more pupils travel by bus than walk.

**Worked example. Share £64 in the ratio $3:5$.**

- Add the parts: $3+5=8$.
- Find one part: $64\div8=8$.
- First share: $3\times8=24$.
- Second share: $5\times8=40$.
- Check: $24+40=64$.

**Worked example. Share 40 counters in the ratio 3:5, where 3 parts are red and 5 parts are the total.**

- The ratio is part:whole, so red is $\frac{3}{5}$ of the total.
- Red counters: $40\times\frac{3}{5}=24$.
- Blue counters: $40-24=16$.
- Check: $24:40=3:5$.

A **unitary method** finds the value of one part first. For example, 24 counters in ratio $3:5$ have 8 total parts. One part is $24\div8=3$, so the second amount is $5\times3=15$.

> **Watch out.** Do not add the same number to both ratio parts. That changes the quotient. Divide each part by the same factor, then check that the simplified ratio has the same quotient.

## You should now be able to

- simplify ratios by multiplying or dividing each part by the same factor.
- find one part and share a total in a given ratio.
- check that my shares add to the original total.
