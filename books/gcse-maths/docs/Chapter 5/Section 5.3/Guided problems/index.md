# 5.3 Direct proportion — Practise with help

Try each question first. Open a hint if you are stuck, the next step for one
concrete move, and the full working only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. Do not look back at the section.

1. Work out $5^2$.
2. Find the value of $k$ when $12=4k$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $5^2=25$.
2. Since $12\div4=3$, $k=3$.

If an answer differs, revisit Section 3.1 for squares and Section 1.4 for
inverse operations.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** $y$ is proportional to $x^2$. When $x=3$, $y=45$. Find $y$ when $x=5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the model $y=kx^2$ and substitute the known pair.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$45=9k$, so $k=5$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Substitute $x=5$: $y=5\times25=125$. Check with the scale factor: $45\times(25/9)=125$.
</details>

**2.** $y$ is proportional to $x$. When $x=7$, $y=35$. Find $y$ when $x=12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write $y=kx$ and substitute the known pair.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$k=35\div7=5$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$y=5\times12=60$. Check: $35\div7=60\div12=5$.
</details>

**3.** The number of pens is directly proportional to the number of packs. 3
packs hold 36 pens. How many pens do 8 packs hold?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Direct proportion means a constant number of pens per pack.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

One pack holds $36\div3=12$ pens.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$12\times8=96$ pens. Check: $96\div8=12$.
</details>

**4. Earlier learning.** Work out $6^2$ and $(-2)^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A square multiplies a number by itself. A cube multiplies it three times.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6^2=6\times6$, and $(-2)^3=(-2)\times(-2)\times(-2)$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$6^2=36$. Also, $(-2)^3=-8$, because three negative factors give a negative answer.
</details>
