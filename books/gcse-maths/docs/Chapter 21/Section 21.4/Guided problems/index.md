# 21.4 Proof of circle theorems — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. What do equal sides in an isosceles triangle tell you about the opposite angles?
2. What is the sum of the angles in a quadrilateral?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answers</summary>

1. The opposite angles are equal.
2. $360^\circ$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A triangle stands on a diameter. Prove its angle at the circumference is $90^\circ$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Start with the angle at the centre on a diameter.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The central angle is $180^\circ$. The circumference angle is half of it.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The diameter subtends a straight angle of $180^\circ$ at the centre. The angle at the circumference is half the centre angle, so it is $90^\circ$.
</details>

**2.** A perpendicular from centre $O$ meets chord $AB$ at $M$. Prove $AM=MB$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Compare triangles $OMA$ and $OMB$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Use equal radii, the shared side $OM$, and the right angles at $M$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$OA=OB$ because both are radii. $OM$ is shared, and both triangles are right-angled at $M$. The triangles are congruent by RHS, so corresponding sides $AM$ and $MB$ are equal.
</details>

**3.** Choose the correct method and explain why: opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are $x^\circ$ and $116^\circ$. Find $x$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Choose between the triangle angle sum and the cyclic quadrilateral theorem.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Opposite angles total $180^\circ$, so write $x+116=180$.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$x=180-116=64^\circ$. The cyclic quadrilateral theorem applies because the angles are opposite and all four vertices lie on one circle.
</details>
