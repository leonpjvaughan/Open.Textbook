# 21.1 Parts of a circle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. What is a line segment?
2. Two lines meet at $90^\circ$. What word describes them?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. A straight part of a line with two endpoints.
2. Perpendicular.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A circle has radius $3.8$ cm. Work out its diameter.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

The diameter is two radii.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Write $d=2r$, then substitute $r=3.8$.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$d=2(3.8)=7.6$ cm.
</details>

**2.** Name the part: (a) the circle's boundary; (b) a part of that boundary; (c) the region between a chord and an arc.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Think of circumference, arc and segment.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Match each description to the definition from the Learn page.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

(a) circumference; (b) arc; (c) segment.
</details>

**3.** A chord has length $11$ cm. A circle has radius $6$ cm. Is this possible?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the chord length with the diameter.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The diameter is $2(6)=12$ cm.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

Yes. A chord can be shorter than the $12$ cm diameter, and $11<12$.
</details>
