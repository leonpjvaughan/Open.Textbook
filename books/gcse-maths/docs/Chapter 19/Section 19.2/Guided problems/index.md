# 19.2 Circumference and area of circles — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $8\times7$. 2. Find half of 18.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Check your answers</summary>

1. $56$. 2. $9$.
</details>

## Practise with help

Attempt each question first. Open the hint, then the next step, when needed.

**1.** A circular clock face has radius 15 cm. Work out its circumference in terms of π.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Circumference uses the radius in $C=2\pi r$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $r=15$ cm and simplify $2\pi\times15$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$C=2\pi\times15=30\pi\text{ cm}$.
</details></details></details>

**2.** A circular pond cover has diameter 18 cm. Work out its area in terms of π.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Area needs the radius, not the diameter.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Halve the diameter first: $18\div2=9$ cm. Then use $A=\pi r^2$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$A=\pi\times9^2=81\pi\text{ cm}^2$.
</details></details></details>

**3.** A circle has circumference 20π cm. Work out its radius.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Work backwards from $C=2\pi r$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the circumference by $2\pi$: $20\pi\div2\pi$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

$r=20\pi\div2\pi=10\text{ cm}$.
</details></details></details>

Try the remaining questions without opening the full working.
