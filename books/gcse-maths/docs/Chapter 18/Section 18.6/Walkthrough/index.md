# 18.6 Similarity: areas and volumes — Learn

**Prerequisites:** similarity and scale factors, area of rectangles, and volume of cuboids.

**Objectives:** relate length, area and volume scale factors for similar shapes and solids.

When a shape is enlarged by length scale factor $k$, each length is multiplied by $k$. An area uses two lengths, so its scale factor is $k^2$. A volume uses three lengths, so its scale factor is $k^3$.

**Example.** A similar shape has length scale factor $3$.

Area scale factor $=3^2$.
$3^2=9$.
An area of $7$ cm$^2$ becomes $7\times9=63$ cm$^2$.

> **Explain:** Why does the area multiplier grow faster than the length multiplier,
> and the volume multiplier faster still? Write about how many lengths each
> quantity uses.

**Example.** A similar solid has volume scale factor $125$.

Length scale factor $=\sqrt[3]{125}$.
$\sqrt[3]{125}=5$.
Each matching length is multiplied by $5$.

> **Watch out.** Do not use the length scale factor directly on area or volume. Check the power: square for area, cube for volume.

## You should now be able to

- find area and volume scale factors from a length scale factor;
- find a length scale factor from an area or volume ratio;
- solve problems involving similar areas and solids;
- explain why area is squared and volume is cubed.
