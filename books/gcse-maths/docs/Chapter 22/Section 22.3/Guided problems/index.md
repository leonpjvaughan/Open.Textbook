# 22.3 Vectors in geometric problems and proofs — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\begin{pmatrix}2\\-1\end{pmatrix}+\begin{pmatrix}-3\\4\end{pmatrix}$.
2. What vector reverses $\begin{pmatrix}5\\-2\end{pmatrix}$?

<details markdown="1"><summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\begin{pmatrix}-1\\3\end{pmatrix}$; add matching components.
2. $\begin{pmatrix}-5\\2\end{pmatrix}$; reversing a vector changes both signs.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** In triangle $PQR$, $\overrightarrow{PQ}=\mathbf{a}$ and $\overrightarrow{QR}=\mathbf{b}$. Find $\overrightarrow{PR}$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>Follow the route from $P$ to $Q$ to $R$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>$\overrightarrow{PR}=\overrightarrow{PQ}+\overrightarrow{QR}$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>$\overrightarrow{PR}=\mathbf{a}+\mathbf{b}$.</details>

**2.** In parallelogram $ABCD$, $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{u}$ and $\overrightarrow{AD}=\mathbf{v}$. Find $\overrightarrow{AC}$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>Use the route $A$ to $B$ to $C$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>$\overrightarrow{BC}=\overrightarrow{AD}=\mathbf{v}$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>$\overrightarrow{AC}=\overrightarrow{AB}+\overrightarrow{BC}=\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}$.</details>

**3.** In the same parallelogram, find $\overrightarrow{DB}$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>Use the route $D$ to $A$ to $B$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>$\overrightarrow{DA}=-\mathbf{v}$ and $\overrightarrow{AB}=\mathbf{u}$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>$\overrightarrow{DB}=-\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}$.</details>

**4.** A point $M$ is the midpoint of a segment with endpoint position vectors $\mathbf{p}$ and $\mathbf{q}$. Write the position vector of $M$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>A midpoint is halfway between the two endpoints.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>Add the endpoint vectors, then multiply by $\frac12$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>$\overrightarrow{OM}=\frac12(\mathbf{p}+\mathbf{q})$.</details>

**5.** Use position vectors to show that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>Set one vertex at the origin and label the two side vectors $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>The other vertices have position vectors $\mathbf{u}$, $\mathbf{v}$ and $\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}$.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>Both diagonal midpoints have position vector $\frac12(\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v})$. Therefore they are the same point and the diagonals bisect each other.</details>

**6.** Explain why a vector proof can use any chosen origin.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>Think about whether the final equality depends on the labels or on the point locations.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>A different origin changes all position vectors by the same displacement.</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>The proof compares positions using consistent vectors. Choosing a different origin shifts each position vector equally, so the geometric equality remains true.</details>
