# 22.1 Vector notation and column vectors — Learn

**Prerequisites:** coordinates, directed movement, and the horizontal and vertical axes.

**Objectives:** describe translations as two-dimensional vectors; read and draw column vectors on a grid.

## Describe a translation

A **translation** moves every point of a shape the same distance in the same
direction. A **vector** records that movement. Its direction matters as well as
its length.

We write a column vector with the horizontal change above the vertical change.
Right and up are positive; left and down are negative.

$$
\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}
$$

This vector means move 3 units right and 2 units down.

**Example 1.** Point $P$ moves from $(1,2)$ to $(5,-1)$. Describe the translation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{horizontal change} &= 5-1=4 \\
\text{vertical change} &= -1-2=-3 \\
\overrightarrow{PP'} &= \begin{pmatrix}4\\-3\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The movement is 4 units right and 3 units down.

**Predict:** before moving the controls, predict where the point will move when
the horizontal entry is negative. Write your prediction down first.

**Explain:** how do the signs show the direction? Write your explanation before

## Column vectors

The same vector can be shown by an arrow on a square grid or by a column. Count
the horizontal and vertical steps from the arrow's start to its end.

**Example 2.** An arrow starts at $(2,-1)$ and ends at $(-1,3)$. Write its column vector.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{horizontal change} &= -1-2=-3 \\
\text{vertical change} &= 3-(-1)=4 \\
\text{vector} &= \begin{pmatrix}-3\\4\end{pmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the arrow moves 3 units left and 4 units up. The grid representation and the
column representation describe the same movement.

> **Watch out.** Reversing the column gives the opposite movement. Check the start
> and end points: reversing an arrow changes both signs.
