# 25.5 Comparing distributions and outliers — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the IQR when $Q_1=5$ and $Q_3=14$.
2. Find the median of $4,7,9,12,15$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Feedback</summary>

1. $9$. 2. $9$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Group A has median 12 and IQR 4. Group B has median 15 and IQR 7. Compare typical values and middle-half spread.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

Compare medians for typical values and IQRs for middle-half spread.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

Compare $15$ with $12$, then compare $7$ with $4$.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

Group B has the higher median and the larger IQR. Its typical value is higher, and its middle half is more spread out.
</details>

**2.** A data set has $Q_1=10$, $Q_3=18$. Find both IQR fences.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

First calculate the IQR. Then subtract or add 1.5 IQR.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

IQR $=8$; $1.5\times8=12$.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

Lower fence $=10-12=-2$. Upper fence $=18+12=30$.
</details>

**3.** Earlier work: find the median of $5,8,12,14,21$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>

The list is ordered. Choose the middle value.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Next step</summary>

The third value is in the middle.
</details>

<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>

The median is $12$.
</details>
