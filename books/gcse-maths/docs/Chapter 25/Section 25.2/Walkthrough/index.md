# 25.2 Cumulative frequency — Learn

**Prerequisites:** read a frequency table, plot coordinates, and find medians and quartiles.

**Objectives:** form cumulative frequencies, draw and read a cumulative frequency graph, and estimate quartiles.

**Cumulative frequency** is a running total. It gives the number of observations up to and including a boundary.

| Time $t$ (minutes) | $0<t\leq10$ | $10<t\leq20$ | $20<t\leq30$ | $30<t\leq40$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Frequency | 4 | 9 | 5 | 2 |
| Cumulative frequency | 4 | 13 | 18 | 20 |

**Example 1.** Plot the cumulative frequency graph for the table.

Use the upper class boundaries and cumulative totals: $(10,4),(20,13),(30,18),(40,20)$. Include $(0,0)$, label both axes, and join the points with a smooth increasing curve.

For 20 observations, estimate the median at cumulative frequency $20/2=10$.

Estimate the lower and upper quartiles at cumulative frequencies $20/4=5$ and $3(20)/4=15$.

Read across from each cumulative frequency to the curve, then down to the time axis. The graph gives approximate values because the original data are grouped.

> **Watch out.** Plot the cumulative total at the upper boundary of each class. Plotting ordinary frequencies instead gives a different graph; check that the curve never decreases.
