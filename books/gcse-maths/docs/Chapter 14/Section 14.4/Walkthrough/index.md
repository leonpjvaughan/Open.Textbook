# 14.4 Linear and quadratic simultaneous equations — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Expand and factorise quadratics, solve quadratic equations, and substitute into expressions.

**Objectives:** Combine a linear equation and a quadratic equation by substitution, then find every solution pair.

## Substitute, then solve the quadratic

A **quadratic equation** has a squared variable, such as $x^2$. When one equation is linear, substitute its expression into the quadratic. The resulting quadratic can have two solutions.

**Example.** Solve $y=x+1$ and $y=x^2-3x+1$.

The equations both equal $y$, so set their right sides equal:

$$
x+1=x^2-3x+1
$$

Collect terms on one side:

$$
x^2-4x=0
$$

Factorise:

$$
x(x-4)=0
$$

So $x=0$ or $x=4$. Use $y=x+1$: when $x=0$, $y=1$; when $x=4$, $y=5$. The solutions are $(0,1)$ and $(4,5)$. Check each pair in both equations.

> **Watch out.** A quadratic can give two values of $x$. Do not stop after the first root. Substitute every root to find its matching $y$ value, then check each pair.

## Progress check

In the example, the two intersections are separate points. Which equation is quickest for finding $y$ once you know $x$? Explain why.

## You should now be able to

- set two expressions equal when both equal the same variable;
- solve the resulting quadratic equation;
- find and check every coordinate pair.
