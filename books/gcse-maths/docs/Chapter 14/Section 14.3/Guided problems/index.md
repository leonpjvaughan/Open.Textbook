# 14.3 Solving simultaneous equations graphically — Practise with help

Work each question first. Open a hint if you need one, then reveal the working to check your method.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. Do not look back at the section.

1. How far right and how far down is the point $(3,-2)$ from the origin?
2. For $y=2x+1$, find $y$ when $x=0$ and when $x=2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. 3 right and 2 down.
2. $1$ and $5$.

If an answer differs, revisit Section 11.1 for plotting coordinates and drawing straight-line graphs.

</details>
## Practise with help

**1.** Complete a table for $y=x+1$ when $x=-1,0,1$.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>
Substitute each $x$ value into the right side of the equation.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>
When $x=-1$, $y=0$; when $x=0$, $y=1$; when $x=1$, $y=2$. Plot $(-1,0)$, $(0,1)$, and $(1,2)$, then draw a straight line through them.
</details>

**2.** Find the intersection of $y=x+1$ and $y=-2x+7$ using a table or graph.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>
Choose $x$ values, calculate both $y$ values, and look for a matching pair.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>
At $x=2$, both equations give $y=3$. The lines meet at $(2,3)$. Check: $2+1=3$ and $-2(2)+7=3$.
</details>

**3.** The two lines are $y=2x+1$ and $y=-x+7$. Which method could find their crossing exactly? Work it out.

<details markdown="1"><summary>Hint</summary>
At the crossing the two expressions for $y$ have the same value.
</details>
<details markdown="1"><summary>Full working</summary>
Set $2x+1=-x+7$. Then $3x=6$, so $x=2$. Substitute to get $y=5$. The intersection is $(2,5)$.
</details>
