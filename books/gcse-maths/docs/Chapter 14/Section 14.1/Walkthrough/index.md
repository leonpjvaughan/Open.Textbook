# 14.1 Simultaneous equations by elimination — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Solve linear equations and collect like terms.

**Objectives:** Solve two linear equations by adding or subtracting them to remove a variable.

## Remove one variable

A simultaneous solution makes both equations true. **Elimination** combines equations so one variable disappears. Add equations when opposite coefficients cancel; subtract when matching coefficients cancel.

**Example 1.** Solve $2x+y=11$ and $x+y=7$.

Subtract the second equation from the first:

$$
(2x+y)-(x+y)=11-7
$$

$$
x=4
$$

Substitute $x=4$ into $x+y=7$:

$$
4+y=7
$$

$$
y=3
$$

The solution is $(x,y)=(4,3)$. Check: $2(4)+3=11$ and $4+3=7$.

**Example 2.** Solve $3x+2y=16$ and $3x-y=7$.

Subtract the second equation from the first. The $x$ terms cancel:

$$
(3x+2y)-(3x-y)=16-7
$$

Collect the remaining terms:

$$
3y=9
$$

$$
y=3
$$

Substitute $y=3$ into $3x-y=7$ to find $x$:

$$
3x-3=7
$$

$$
3x=10
$$

$$
x=\frac{10}{3}
$$

Check the pair in both original equations: $3(10/3)+2(3)=16$ and $3(10/3)-3=7$.

> **Watch out.** When subtracting an equation, subtract every term. In $(2x+y)-(x+y)$ the $y$ terms cancel because $y-y=0$. Check the final pair in both equations.

## Progress check

The two taxi fares for Company A are $f+4r=14$ and $f+8r=22$. Subtract the first equation from the second. What does the result tell you about $r$?

## You should now be able to

- decide whether to add or subtract equations to eliminate a variable;
- solve the remaining linear equation;
- substitute back and check both original equations.
