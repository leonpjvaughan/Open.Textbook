# 12.6 Completing the square — Learn

**Prerequisites:** expanding brackets, collecting terms, and solving equations by square roots.

**Objectives:** rewrite a quadratic by completing the square, solve it, and use the form to find the turning point. This covers A11 and A18 at Higher level.

## Build a square

For $x^2+bx+c$, take half of $b$, square it, and add and subtract that value.

**Example 1.** Complete the square for $x^2+6x+2$.

Half of $6$ is $3$, and $3^2=9$.

$$
x^2+6x+2=x^2+6x+9-9+2
$$

$$
=(x+3)^2-7
$$

The turning point of $y=(x+3)^2-7$ is $(-3,-7)$ because the square is smallest at $x=-3$.

**Example 2.** Solve $x^2+6x+2=0$.

$$
(x+3)^2-7=0
$$

$$
(x+3)^2=7
$$

$$
x=-3\pm\sqrt7
$$

> **Watch out.** The half coefficient is squared: half of $6$ is $3$, and the added value is $9$. Check by expanding the completed form.

## You should now be able to

- rewrite a monic quadratic in completed-square form;
- solve an equation by isolating the square and taking both square roots;
- read a turning point from completed-square form.
