# 12.4 Solving quadratic equations by factorising — Learn

**Prerequisites:** factorising monic quadratics and solving linear equations.

**Objectives:** solve quadratic equations by factorising and using the zero-product rule.

## Use the zero-product rule

If two factors multiply to zero, at least one factor must be zero. Factorise the equation, set each factor to zero, and solve.

**Example 1.** Solve $x^2+5x+6=0$.

$$
x^2+5x+6=0
$$

$$
(x+2)(x+3)=0
$$
So $x+2=0$ or $x+3=0$.

$$
x=-2 \quad\text{or}\quad x=-3
$$

**Example 2.** Solve $x^2=5x+14$.

$$
x^2-5x-14=0
$$

$$
(x-7)(x+2)=0
$$
Therefore $x=7$ or $x=-2$.

## A guided product investigation

**Predict:** for $(x+2)(x+3)=0$, predict the two solutions before you move a control. Then predict what happens to each solution when you change its bracket number. **Test:** change the two bracket numbers and watch where the factor lines and the product curve meet the axis. **Explain:** why is each solution the opposite sign to the number inside its bracket?

> **Watch out.** From $ab=0$, do not divide by $a$ or $b$. Either factor could be zero, so set each factor equal to zero.

## You should now be able to

- rearrange a quadratic equation so one side is zero;
- factorise and apply the zero-product rule;
- give both solutions and check them in the original equation.
