# 3.3 The highest common factor and the lowest common multiple — Learn

**Prerequisites:** listing factors and multiples, and writing a number as a product of
primes.

**Objectives:** find the highest common factor and the lowest common multiple of two
or more numbers; decide which of the two a word problem is asking for.

By the end of this section, you can answer the chapter problem and say why it needed
the idea it needed.

## The highest common factor

The **highest common factor** of two numbers is the largest number that divides both
of them exactly.

**Example 1.** Find the highest common factor of 24 and 36.

In prime factors:

$$24 = 2^3\times3 \qquad 36 = 2^2\times3^2$$

A common factor can only use primes that appear in **both**. And it cannot use more
copies of a prime than the smaller of the two has. So:

- 2 appears in both. The smaller power is $2^2$, so take $2^2$.
- 3 appears in both. The smaller power is $3^1$, so take $3$.

$$\text{HCF} = 2^2\times3 = 12$$

## The lowest common multiple

The **lowest common multiple** of two numbers is the smallest number that both divide
into exactly.

**Example 2.** Find the lowest common multiple of 24 and 36, using the same
factorisations.

A common multiple has to contain **enough** of each prime to build either number. So
it needs the *larger* power of each prime that appears:

- 2: the larger power is $2^3$.
- 3: the larger power is $3^2$.

$$\text{LCM} = 2^3\times3^2 = 72$$

The two rules are the same rule with the comparison flipped.

| | Which power of each prime |
|---|---|
| Highest common factor | the **smaller** |
| Lowest common multiple | the **larger** |

**Example 3.** Find the highest common factor and the lowest common multiple of 84
and 210.

$$84 = 2^2\times3\times7 \qquad 210 = 2\times3\times5\times7$$

Both contain 2, 3 and 7. Only 210 contains 5.

$$\text{HCF} = 2\times3\times7 = 42 \qquad \text{LCM} = 2^2\times3\times5\times7 = 420$$

> **Watch out.** The highest common factor is never bigger than the smaller of the two
> numbers, and the lowest common multiple is never smaller than the larger of them.
> Those two facts are free checks. If you are asked for the highest common factor of
> 84 and 210 and you get 420, you have found the lowest common multiple — or you have
> used the larger powers where you needed the smaller ones.

## Which one does the question want?

This is the part that decides the mark. Read what the question is *doing* with the
numbers.

**Sharing or dividing into equal groups** means the highest common factor. The answer
has to divide both numbers, so it is a factor.

**Repeating events lining up** means the lowest common multiple. The answer has to be
a multiple of both, so it is a multiple.

**Example 4.** Two lighthouses flash every 12 seconds and every 18 seconds. They flash
together. How long until they next flash together?

The question asks when two repeating events line up again. That is a multiple of both
12 and 18, and "next" makes it the lowest one.

$$12 = 2^2\times3 \qquad 18 = 2\times3^2$$

$$\text{LCM} = 2^2\times3^2 = 36$$

They next flash together after 36 seconds.

**Example 5.** A shop has 48 blue pens and 60 black pens. They go into boxes of one
colour, every box holds the same number, and nothing is left over. What is the greatest
number of pens a box can hold?

The box size must divide 48 and it must divide 60. It must also be as large as
possible, so it is the highest common factor.

$$48 = 2^4\times3 \qquad 60 = 2^2\times3\times5$$

$$\text{HCF} = 2^2\times3 = 12$$

A box can hold 12 pens. Then there are 4 boxes of blue pens and 5 boxes of black pens.

Notice how much the two examples look alike. Both give two numbers and ask for a
single number back. The words *together* and *next* point at the lowest common
multiple; the words *greatest* and *same number in each* point at the highest common
factor.
