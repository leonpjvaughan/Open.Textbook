# 3.3 The highest common factor and the lowest common multiple — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write 36 as a product of its prime factors.
2. Write 60 as a product of its prime factors.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $36=2^2\times3^2$.
2. $60=2^2\times3\times5$.

You will need both of these in this section. Taking the **smaller** power of each
shared prime gives one answer, and the **larger** power gives the other.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the highest common factor of 12 and 18.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write both in prime factors: $12=2^2\times3$ and $18=2\times3^2$. Which power of each
shared prime do you take?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The smaller: $2^1$ and $3^1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{HCF} = 2\times3 = 6$$

Check: 6 divides 12 (twice) and divides 18 (three times), and nothing larger does —
8 divides 12 but not 18, and 9 divides 18 but not 12.

The highest common factor cannot be bigger than 12, the smaller number, and 6 is not
bigger. That is the right size.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the lowest common multiple of 12 and 18.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Same two factorisations, but take the **larger** power of each prime.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2^2$ and $3^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{LCM} = 2^2\times3^2 = 36$$

Check: $36\div12=3$ and $36\div18=2$, so 36 is a multiple of both.

The lowest common multiple cannot be smaller than 18, the larger number, and 36 is not
smaller.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the highest common factor of 36 and 60.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From the warm-up: $36=2^2\times3^2$ and $60=2^2\times3\times5$. Both contain 2 and 3.
Does either contain 5?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

5 appears only in 60, so it cannot be in a common factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{HCF} = 2^2\times3 = 12$$

Check: $36\div12=3$ and $60\div12=5$. Both are whole numbers, so 12 divides both.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the lowest common multiple of 36 and 60.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Now you need **every** prime that appears in either number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

That is 2, 3 and 5 — with the largest power each one reaches.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{LCM} = 2^2\times3^2\times5 = 180$$

Check: $180\div36=5$ and $180\div60=3$. Both whole, so 180 is a common multiple. And
36 is not a multiple of 60, nor 60 of 36, so nothing between 60 and 180 works.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Buses leave a stop every 15 minutes and every 20 minutes. They leave together
at 9:00. When do they next leave together?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two repeating events lining up. Which of the two ideas does that need?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The lowest common multiple. $15=3\times5$ and $20=2^2\times5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{LCM} = 2^2\times3\times5 = 60$$

So they next leave together 60 minutes later, at **10:00**.

Notice the answer is bigger than both 15 and 20, which is what should happen for a
lowest common multiple.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Ribbon comes in 24 cm and 90 cm lengths. It is cut into equal pieces with none
wasted, and each piece is as long as possible. How long is each piece?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The piece length must divide both 24 and 90, and be as large as possible.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

That is the highest common factor. $24=2^3\times3$ and $90=2\times3^2\times5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{HCF} = 2\times3 = 6$$

Each piece is 6 cm. Then the 24 cm ribbon gives 4 pieces and the 90 cm gives 15.

The answer is smaller than both numbers, which is what should happen for a highest
common factor.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A question gives two numbers and asks for their highest common factor. The
answer you calculate is bigger than both numbers. What has gone wrong?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What is the biggest a common factor of two numbers can possibly be?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

It cannot be bigger than the smaller number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

You have found the lowest common multiple instead. That is a multiple, so it is at
least as big as the larger number.

Go back to the table: highest common factor takes the **smaller** power of each shared
prime, and the lowest common multiple takes the **larger**. If you took the larger
powers and the answer is too big, that is why.

This check is worth doing every time, because it costs nothing and catches the mistake
on the spot.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Two timers beep every 8 seconds and every 14 seconds. A third beeps every 20
seconds. All three beep together. How long until all three beep together again?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Three numbers this time, but the idea is the same. Work out the lowest common multiple
of two of them first, then of that answer and the third.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$8=2^3$, $14=2\times7$, $20=2^2\times5$. You need the largest power of every prime
that appears anywhere.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{LCM} = 2^3\times5\times7 = 280$$

So after 280 seconds.

Doing it in two steps gives the same answer: $8$ and $14$ have a lowest common multiple
of $56$, and the lowest common multiple of $56$ and $20$ is $280$. That is a good way
to handle three numbers without losing track.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**9.** Work out $(-3)\times(-8)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiplying two negative numbers: is the answer positive or negative?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two negatives make a positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$24$.

$3\times8=24$, and because both numbers are negative the answer is positive.

It is the same answer as $3\times8$, which is why 24 has the factors it has: $-3$ and
$-8$ also multiply to 24. Factors of a number are usually taken to be positive unless
the question says otherwise.

</details>
</details>
</details>
