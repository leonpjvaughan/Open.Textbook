# 3.2 Prime numbers and prime factorisation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down all the factors of 30.
2. Is 45 divisible by 3? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15$ and $30$. Working in pairs from 1 keeps you from missing
   one.
2. Yes. The digit sum is $4+5=9$, and 9 is a multiple of 3. So $45\div3=15$ exactly.

Both answers are used in this section: the first tells you which numbers are
**composite**, and the second is the kind of test you use to find a prime factor.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Which of these are prime numbers? $15, 17, 21, 27, 31$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Try dividing each one by 2, 3, 5 and 7 — the primes up to $\sqrt{31}$, which is about
5.6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

15 and 27 divide by 3. 21 divides by 3 and by 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

17 and 31 are prime.

- $15=3\times5$ — composite
- $17$ — prime
- $21=3\times7$ — composite
- $27=3\times3\times3$ — composite
- $31$ — prime

To check 31 you need only try 2, 3 and 5. Each leaves a remainder, and $7^2=49$ is
bigger than 31, so there is nothing further to try.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Write 60 as a product of its prime factors, in index form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide by 2 while the number stays even, then move to the next prime.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$60\div2=30$, $30\div2=15$. Then 15 is odd, so divide by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$60 = 2\times2\times3\times5 = 2^2\times3\times5$$

Check by multiplying back: $4\times3\times5=60$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Write 84 as a product of its prime factors.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

84 is even, so start with 2. Keep going while it stays even.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$84\div2=42$, $42\div2=21$. Now 21 is odd, and its digit sum is 3, so divide by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$84 = 2\times2\times3\times7 = 2^2\times3\times7$$

Check: $4\times3\times7=84$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Write 5775 as a product of its prime factors. It ends in 5, and its digit sum is
24.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ending in 5 tells you 5 divides it; the digit sum being 24 tells you 3 divides it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$5775\div3=1925$. Then divide by 5: $1925\div5=385$, and $385\div5=77$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5775 = 3\times5\times5\times7\times11 = 3\times5^2\times7\times11$$

Check: $3\times25=75$, and $75\times7=525$, and $525\times11=5775$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Write 1000 as a product of its prime factors, in index form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

1000 is $10\times10\times10$. What is each 10 made of?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Each 10 is $2\times5$. So 1000 is three 2s and three 5s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1000 = 2^3\times5^3$$

Check: $8\times125=1000$. Correct.

This is a useful one to recognise — 1000, 100 and 10 all split into equal numbers of
2s and 5s, which is exactly why the divisibility rules for 10, 100 and 1000 work.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** $72 = 2^3\times3^2$. Use the factorisation to find how many factors 72 has.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A factor uses some number of 2s and some number of 3s. How many choices for each?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

0, 1, 2 or 3 copies of 2 — that is 4 choices. And 0, 1 or 2 copies of 3 — 3 choices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3+1)\times(2+1) = 4\times3 = 12$$

So 72 has 12 factors. You can check the count by listing them: $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9,
12, 18, 24, 36$ and $72$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why 1 is not a prime number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the factors of 1. How many are there?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

1 has exactly one factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A prime number has exactly **two** factors: 1 and itself. The number 1 has only one
factor, because "1 and itself" is the same number counted once.

So 1 fails the definition. This also matters for factorisation: if 1 were allowed as a
prime, you could write $12 = 1\times1\times2\times2\times3$ using as many 1s as you
liked, and the factorisation would no longer be unique.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A number's prime factorisation is $2\times3^3\times5^2$. Without working out the
number, say how many factors it has.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the same rule: add one to each index and multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(1+1)\times(3+1)\times(2+1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2\times4\times3 = 24$$

So it has 24 factors. You did not need to know that the number is 1350 — the
factorisation alone answers the question.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Write down all the factors of 40.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work in pairs from 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$40=1\times40=2\times20=4\times10=5\times8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20$ and $40$.

Now check it with this section's method. $40=2^3\times5$, so the number of factors is
$(3+1)\times(1+1)=8$. There are eight in the list above, which agrees.

That is a useful check: counting with the formula tells you how many factors to look
for, so you know when your list is complete.

</details>
</details>
</details>
