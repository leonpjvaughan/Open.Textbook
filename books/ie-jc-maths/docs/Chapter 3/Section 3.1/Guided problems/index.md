# 3.1 Factors, multiples and divisibility — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $7\times8$.
2. Work out $5^2$ and $2^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $56$. If you used $7\times7=49$ and added one more 7, that also works.
2. $5^2=25$, because $5\times5=25$. And $2^3=8$, because $2\times2\times2=8$. The
   index tells you how many factors, not what to multiply the base by.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write down all the factors of 18.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start at 1 and work up in pairs. Each pair multiplies to 18.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$18=1\times18=2\times9=3\times6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The factors are $1, 2, 3, 6, 9$ and $18$.

You can stop once you reach the square root. $\sqrt{18}$ is about 4.2, so you only
needed to try 1, 2, 3 and 4. The 4 does not divide 18, and 5 is past the square root,
so the list is complete.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Write down the first five multiples of 9.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A multiple of 9 is 9 times a whole number. Start at 9 times 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$9\times1$, $9\times2$, $9\times3$, $9\times4$, $9\times5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9, 18, 27, 36, 45$$

Notice that the digit sums are $9, 9, 9, 9, 9$ — that is the divisibility rule for 9
appearing in the answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Is 372 divisible by 3? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the digits. Do not look at the last digit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3+7+2=12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Yes. The digit sum is $3+7+2=12$, and 12 is a multiple of 3. So 372 is divisible by 3.

You can check: $372\div3=124$ exactly.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Is 372 divisible by 6? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For 6 you need **two** things to be true. What are they?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A number divides by 6 when it divides by 2 **and** by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Yes. It ends in 2, so it is even and divides by 2. From question 3, its digit sum is
12, so it divides by 3. Dividing by both 2 and 3 means it divides by 6.

Check: $372\div6=62$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Write down all the factors of 64.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

64 is a square number, so one of its factor pairs is a number paired with itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$64=1\times64=2\times32=4\times16=8\times8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The factors are $1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32$ and $64$.

There are **seven** — an odd number, because $8\times8=64$ pairs 8 with itself. Every
square number has an odd number of factors, and every other number has an even number.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A number is divisible by 4 when its last two digits form a multiple of 4. Use
this to decide whether 72516 is divisible by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look only at the last two digits: 16.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$16\div4=4$ exactly.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Yes. The last two digits are 16, and $16=4\times4$, so 72516 is divisible by 4.

Check: $72516\div4=18129$.

The rule works because 100 is a multiple of 4, so the hundreds and beyond never affect
the remainder.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Write down the square numbers between 50 and 100, and say how many there are.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which whole numbers squared land between 50 and 100?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$7^2=49$ is too small. Try $8^2$, $9^2$ and $10^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$8^2=64$, $9^2=81$, and $10^2=100$ is not *between* 50 and 100 because it is equal to
the upper end.

So there are two: $64$ and $81$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A teacher has 30 stickers and wants to give every pupil the same number, with
none left over. Which numbers of pupils is this possible for?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The number of pupils must divide 30 exactly. So you need the factors of 30.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$30=1\times30=2\times15=3\times10=5\times6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The pupils could be $1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15$ or $30$ in number — one for each factor
of 30.

Each of those gives the same number of stickers per pupil: 30, 15, 10, 6, 5, 3, 2 or
1 respectively.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Round 3174 to the nearest hundred.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the two hundreds it sits between: 3100 and 3200. Which is it closer to?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

74 is nearer to 100 than to 0.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$3200$. The tens digit is 7, which is 5 or more, so you round up from 3100 to 3200.

This is the kind of check worth doing before you trust an answer: $3174\div4$ is about
$3200\div4$, which is 800.

</details>
</details>
</details>
