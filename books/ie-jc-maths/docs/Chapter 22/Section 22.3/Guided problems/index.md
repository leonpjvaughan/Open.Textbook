# 22.3 Tallying and frequency tables — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $4+7+9+5$.
2. Work out $3\times6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4+7+9+5=25$.
2. $3\times6=18$.

Adding and multiplying are the two jobs in a frequency table. You add the
frequencies to find the total, and you multiply when you work out a mean later.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A tally shows 3 marks for 5, 5 marks for 6 and 2 marks for 7. How many
answers were collected?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the three counts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3+5+2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3+5+2=10$$

Ten answers were collected. The frequencies always add to the sample size.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Here are the number of pets in 10 homes.

$$0, 1, 2, 1, 1, 3, 0, 1, 2, 1$$

Build a frequency table for the values 0, 1, 2 and 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count how many times each value appears.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are two 0s, five 1s, two 2s and one 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

| Number of pets | Frequency |
|---|---|
| 0 | 2 |
| 1 | 5 |
| 2 | 2 |
| 3 | 1 |
| **Total** | **10** |

The frequencies add to 10, which is the number of homes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A die is rolled 20 times. The faces 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6 come up with
frequencies 4, 3, 2, 5, 3 and 3. Check that the frequencies add to 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add all six frequencies.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4+3+2+5+3+3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4+3+2+5+3+3=20$$

The total is 20, which matches the number of rolls. The table is correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Use the die table above. How many rolls showed a 4 or a 5?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the frequencies of 4 and 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5+3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5+3=8$$

Eight rolls showed a 4 or a 5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** The grouped table below shows the minutes 30 students spent on homework.

| Minutes | Frequency |
|---|---|
| 0 to 19 | 4 |
| 20 to 39 | 9 |
| 40 to 59 | 8 |
| 60 to 79 | 6 |
| 80 to 99 | 3 |

How many students spent at least 40 minutes on homework?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At least 40 means the classes from 40 to 59 upwards.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the frequencies of 40 to 59, 60 to 79 and 80 to 99.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8+6+3=17$$

Seventeen students spent at least 40 minutes on homework.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The chapter problem. The 20 sleep answers are

$$9, 10, 9, 10, 8, 8, 8, 9, 7, 8, 8, 7, 6, 7, 6, 7, 5, 6, 5, 5$$

Build the frequency table for the values 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the 5s, then the 6s, and so on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are three 5s, three 6s, four 7s and five 8s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

| Sleep (hours) | Frequency |
|---|---|
| 5 | 3 |
| 6 | 3 |
| 7 | 4 |
| 8 | 5 |
| 9 | 3 |
| 10 | 2 |
| **Total** | **20** |

The frequencies add to 20, which is the number of students.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Use the sleep frequency table. How many students slept fewer than 7 hours?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Fewer than 7 means 5 or 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3+3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3+3=6$$

Six students slept fewer than 7 hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Use the sleep frequency table. How many students slept at least 8 hours?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At least 8 means 8, 9 or 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5+3+2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5+3+2=10$$

Ten students slept at least 8 hours. That is half of the sample.

</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** A two-way table shows 14 students who take the bus and 22 who do not. How
many students were surveyed?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the two groups.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$14+22$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$14+22=36$$

Thirty-six students were surveyed. The two groups together are the whole sample,
just as the frequencies in a frequency table add to the total.

</details>
</details>
