# 22.5 Measures of centre: mean, median and mode — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $6+8+7+9$.
2. Work out $30\div4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $6+8+7+9=30$.
2. $30\div4=7.5$.

The mean is a total divided by a count. The two steps are to add the values, then
divide by how many there are.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the mean of $5, 6, 7, 8, 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the values, then divide by 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5+6+7+8+9=35$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{35}{5}=7$$

The mean is 7.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the median of $8, 3, 6, 5, 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put the values in order first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

In order they are $3, 5, 6, 8, 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

There are 5 values, so the middle one is the third. The median is 6.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the median of $4, 9, 2, 7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 4 values, so take the mean of the middle two.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

In order they are $2, 4, 7, 9$. The middle two are 4 and 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{4+7}{2}=5.5$$

The median is 5.5. It is halfway between the two middle values.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the mode of $3, 5, 5, 6, 8, 8, 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which value appears most often?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The value 5 appears twice and the value 8 appears three times.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The mode is 8, because it appears three times, more than any other value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the mean of $2, 3, 3, 4, 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the values, then divide by 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2+3+3+4+8=20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{20}{5}=4$$

The mean is 4. The value 8 is larger than the rest and pulls the mean up, which is
why the mean is larger than the median.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A shop sells 4, 6, 5 and 9 ice creams on four days. Find the mean number
sold each day.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the four numbers, then divide by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4+6+5+9=24$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{24}{4}=6$$

The mean is 6 ice creams each day.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. The 20 sleep answers add to 148. Work out the mean
sleep.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the total by the number of students.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{148}{20}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{148}{20}=7.4$$

The mean sleep is 7.4 hours. That is below the school target of 8 hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. The ordered sleep values are

$$5, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10$$

Work out the median.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 20 values, so the middle two are the 10th and the 11th.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The 10th value is 7 and the 11th value is 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{7+8}{2}=7.5$$

The median sleep is 7.5 hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** A student scores 6, 7 and 8 in three tests. What score is needed in the
fourth test to make the mean 8?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A mean of 8 over 4 tests needs a total of $4\times8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The total must be 32, and the first three scores add to 21.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4\times8=32 \qquad 6+7+8=21 \qquad 32-21=11$$

The student needs 11 in the fourth test.

</details>
</details>
