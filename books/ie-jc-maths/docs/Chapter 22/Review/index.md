# Chapter 22: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**Is the school getting enough sleep?** The school nurse says that students should
sleep for at least 8 hours on a school night. Your year group surveyed 20 students
from a school of about 600.

**Step 1. The population and the sample.** The population is every student in the
school. The sample is the 20 students who were asked. A sample is used because
asking all 600 students would take too long.

**Step 2. A fair choice of sample.** Number every student on the roll from 1 to
600. Use a random number generator to pick 20 different numbers. Every student
then has the same chance of being chosen, so the sample is not biased.

**Step 3. The frequency table.** The 20 sleep answers are

$$9, 10, 9, 10, 8, 8, 8, 9, 7, 8, 8, 7, 6, 7, 6, 7, 5, 6, 5, 5$$

| Sleep (hours) | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 2 |

The frequencies add to 20, which matches the sample size.

**Step 4. The bar chart.** The tallest bar is at 8 hours, with height 5. The
bar chart is the right graph, because the sleep times were recorded in whole
hours and each value is separate.

**Step 5. The measures of centre.** The total is 148.

$$\text{mean}=\frac{148}{20}=7.4 \text{ hours}$$

The ordered values are

$$5, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10$$

The middle two are 7 and 8, so

$$\text{median}=\frac{7+8}{2}=7.5 \text{ hours}$$

The mode is 8 hours, because it appears 5 times.

**Step 6. The measures of spread.** The range is

$$10-5=5 \text{ hours}$$

Each half of the data has 10 values. The lower half has median 6, so $Q_1=6$. The
upper half has median 8.5, so $Q_3=8.5$. The interquartile range is

$$8.5-6=2.5 \text{ hours}$$

The middle half of the students sleep between 6 and 8.5 hours.

**Step 7. The link with screen time.** The scatter graph of screen time against
sleep falls from left to right, so the correlation is negative and fairly strong.
Students with more screen time tended to sleep less.

**Step 8. What the survey supports.** In this sample, the mean sleep is 7.4 hours,
which is below the 8-hour target. Ten of the 20 students slept at least 8 hours.
The survey gives an estimate for the school, but 20 is a small sample, so the
estimate is not exact.

**Step 9. What the survey does not support.** The survey does not prove that
screens cause less sleep. It shows a link. A third factor could affect both screen
time and sleep.

**Step 10. The misleading graph.** A poster compares 7.4 hours with the target of
8 hours, and its axis starts at 7.0. The real difference is 0.6 hours, but the
narrow axis makes it look large. On an axis from 0 to 8, both bars are tall and
close together, which is the honest picture.

**So, is the school getting enough sleep?** The sample suggests that the students
sleep less than 8 hours on average, and that screen time is linked to less sleep.
That is evidence, not proof. A larger, fairer sample would make the estimate
stronger, and it still would not prove cause.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and
say why in a few words. The idea might be a population, a sample, a bias, a
frequency table, a graph, a mean, a median, a mode, a range, a quartile, an
interquartile range, a correlation, a claim, or a misleading axis.

1. A café asks 30 customers what they think of the new menu. Name the population
   and the sample.
2. A survey asks only students in the library about reading. Explain the bias.
3. A tally shows 4, 6 and 5 marks for three values. How many answers were
   collected?
4. A bar chart has heights 3, 5, 2 and 6. Which bar is tallest?
5. Find the mean of $4, 6, 7, 8, 10$.
6. Find the median of $3, 9, 5, 7$.
7. Find the mode of $2, 3, 3, 5, 7$.
8. Find the range of $12, 18, 9, 15, 20$.
9. Find the interquartile range of $2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16$.
10. A scatter graph shows that students who read more tend to spell better.
    Describe the correlation.
11. A survey of 40 students finds that 24 take the bus. Estimate how many of 500
    students take the bus.
12. A poster's axis starts at 90 instead of 0. Explain why this can mislead.
13. A pictogram symbol doubles in value and doubles in height and width. How many
    times larger does it look?
14. Two classes have the same mean mark. Class A has an IQR of 5 and Class B has
    an IQR of 12. Which class is more consistent?
15. A headline says a new app makes students work harder. The survey asked 12
    students. Give one reason to question the claim.
16. A school has 300 students and takes a systematic sample of 30. How many
    students are between each chosen name?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and concise solutions</summary>

1. The population is every customer of the café. The sample is the 30 customers
   who were asked.
2. The sample is biased towards students who read, because students in the library
   are more likely to enjoy reading than the average student.
3. $4+6+5=15$ answers.
4. The tallest bar has height 6, so the bar with height 6.
5. $4+6+7+8+10=35$, so the mean is $35\div5=7$.
6. In order the values are $3, 5, 7, 9$. The median is $(5+7)\div2=6$.
7. The mode is 3, because it appears twice and every other value appears once.
8. $20-9=11$.
9. The lower half is $2, 4, 6, 8$ with $Q_1=5$, and the upper half is
   $10, 12, 14, 16$ with $Q_3=13$. The IQR is $13-5=8$.
10. The correlation is positive, because as reading increases, spelling tends to
    increase.
11. $\frac{24}{40}\times500=300$ students.
12. Starting the axis at 90 makes small differences look large, because the bars
    are drawn from a high base.
13. It looks $2\times2=4$ times larger.
14. Class A is more consistent, because its interquartile range is smaller.
15. The sample is very small, so it cannot describe all students. The claim is too
    strong for the evidence.
16. $300\div30=10$, so take every 10th name.

</details>

## What you can now do

- say what a population and a sample are, and why a sample is used;
- choose a sample in a way that avoids bias;
- build a frequency table and check that the frequencies add to the total;
- draw and read a bar chart, a line plot and a histogram;
- work out the mean, the median and the mode;
- work out the range, the quartiles and the interquartile range;
- describe a scatter graph and its correlation, and explain why a link is not a
  cause;
- question a claim by asking who was asked, how many, what was asked, and what
  the number means;
- spot a misleading graph and redraw it honestly.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you predicted whether the mean sleep would be above or
below 8 hours, and whether more screen time goes with less sleep. Look back at
that prediction now.

The mean sleep is 7.4 hours, which is below 8, and the survey did find a negative
correlation between screen time and sleep. If you predicted both, you read the
table well. If you expected the mean to be above 8, the low sleepers at 5 and 6
hours pulled it down.

You also wrote about a graph you have seen used to persuade someone. Take that
graph now and ask the five questions from Section 22.9. Does the vertical axis
start at zero? Is the scale even? Are the pictures the right size? What time range
is shown? Are the groups the same size?

Then write one sentence saying what the graph told you, and one sentence saying
what it left out. That is the whole of this chapter in one small piece of work:
one graph, one question, and an honest answer about what the data can support.
