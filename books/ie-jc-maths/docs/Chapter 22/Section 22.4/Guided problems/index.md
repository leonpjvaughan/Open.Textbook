# 22.4 Bar charts, line plots and histograms — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A number line runs from 0 to 10. What number is halfway?
2. Work out $20\div4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $5$, because 5 is the same distance from 0 and from 10.
2. $20\div4=5$.

Reading a graph starts with reading its scale. Find what one step along the axis
stands for before you read any bar.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A bar chart shows the number of goals scored by a team in five matches. The
bar heights are 2, 3, 1, 4 and 0. How many goals were scored in the match with the
tallest bar?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The tallest bar has the largest height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The heights are 2, 3, 1, 4 and 0, so the largest is 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The tallest bar has height 4, so 4 goals were scored in that match. The bar height
is the frequency.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Use the same bar chart with heights 2, 3, 1, 4 and 0. How many goals were
scored in total over the five matches?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the five bar heights.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2+3+1+4+0$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2+3+1+4+0=10$$

Ten goals were scored in total. The total is the sum of the bar heights.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A histogram of homework minutes has bar heights 4, 9, 8, 6 and 3 for the
classes 0 to 19, 20 to 39, 40 to 59, 60 to 79 and 80 to 99. Which class has the
tallest bar?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the largest height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The heights are 4, 9, 8, 6 and 3, so the largest is 9.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The tallest bar has height 9, and it belongs to the class 20 to 39 minutes. That
class is the most common.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Use the same histogram. How many students spent 40 minutes or more on
homework?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

40 minutes or more means the classes 40 to 59, 60 to 79 and 80 to 99.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8+6+3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8+6+3=17$$

Seventeen students spent 40 minutes or more on homework.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A line plot has crosses above the values 1, 2, 3 and 4. The numbers of
crosses are 2, 5, 3 and 1. How many pieces of data are there?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the number of crosses.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2+5+3+1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2+5+3+1=11$$

There are 11 pieces of data. Every cross is one answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Which graph suits each set of data: a bar chart or a histogram? Give a
reason in each case.

- (a) The favourite colours of a class.
- (b) The heights of 30 students, grouped into classes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Are the values separate, or do they sit on a scale?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Colours are separate categories. Heights are on a continuous scale.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

(a) A bar chart, because colours are separate categories and the bars need gaps.
(b) A histogram, because heights are continuous and the classes join up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. The sleep bar chart has heights 3, 3, 4, 5, 3 and 2 for
the values 5 to 10. How many students slept 7 hours or more?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

7 hours or more means 7, 8, 9 and 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4+5+3+2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4+5+3+2=14$$

Fourteen students slept 7 hours or more. That is most of the sample.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. The sleep data is grouped by whole hours. Explain why
a bar chart is the right graph for it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Are the values 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10 separate, or do they form a scale with gaps in
between?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The data is recorded in whole hours, so each value is separate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The sleep times were recorded as whole hours, so the values 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10
are separate. A bar chart keeps a gap between them. A histogram would join the
bars, which is right only for continuous data.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** A pictogram uses one symbol for 4 books. A row has 3 symbols. How many
books does the row show?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each symbol stands for 4 books.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3\times4=12$$

The row shows 12 books. A pictogram is another way to show a frequency, just as a
bar chart and a line plot are.

</details>
</details>
