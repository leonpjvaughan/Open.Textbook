# 22.6 Measures of spread: range and interquartile range — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Put $5, 2, 9, 4$ in order from smallest to largest.
2. What is the middle value of $3, 5, 7, 8, 10$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2, 4, 5, 9$.
2. $7$, because it is the third of the five values.

Ordering the data is the first step in every spread calculation. A quartile is
a median of half the ordered list.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the range of $4, 9, 2, 7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the smallest from the largest.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The largest is 9 and the smallest is 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9-2=7$$

The range is 7.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the range of $12, 15, 11, 20, 13$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the largest and the smallest value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The largest is 20 and the smallest is 11.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$20-11=9$$

The range is 9.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the lower quartile of $2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Split the ordered list into two halves of four.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The lower half is $2, 4, 6, 8$, and its median is the mean of 4 and 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{4+6}{2}=5$$

The lower quartile is $Q_1=5$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Use the same data $2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16$. Find the upper quartile
and the interquartile range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The upper half is $10, 12, 14, 16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$Q_3$ is the mean of 12 and 14. Then subtract $Q_1=5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$Q_3=\frac{12+14}{2}=13$$

$$\text{IQR}=13-5=8$$

The interquartile range is 8.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the interquartile range of $3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 7 values, so the median is 6 and it is not in either half.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The lower half is $3, 4, 5$ and the upper half is $7, 8, 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$Q_1=4 \qquad Q_3=8 \qquad \text{IQR}=8-4=4$$

The interquartile range is 4.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** One value in a set is much larger than all the others. Which is affected
more, the range or the interquartile range? Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The range uses the largest value. Which values does the IQR use?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The IQR uses the middle half of the data.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The range is affected more. It uses the largest value, so one extreme value makes
it large. The IQR uses the middle half of the data and ignores the highest quarter,
so it hardly changes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. The ordered sleep values are

$$5, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 7, 8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10$$

Work out the range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The largest is 10 and the smallest is 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$10-5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10-5=5$$

The range is 5 hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. Use the same ordered sleep values. Work out $Q_1$,
$Q_3$ and the interquartile range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each half has 10 values. Take the median of each half.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The lower half is $5, 5, 5, 6, 6, 6, 7, 7, 7, 7$, and its median is 6. The upper
half is $8, 8, 8, 8, 8, 9, 9, 9, 10, 10$, and its median is 8.5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$Q_1=6 \qquad Q_3=8.5$$

$$\text{IQR}=8.5-6=2.5$$

The interquartile range is 2.5 hours. The middle half of the students sleep
between 6 and 8.5 hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** Find the mean of $10, 12, 14, 16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the values, then divide by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$10+12+14+16=52$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{52}{4}=13$$

The mean is 13. A measure of centre and a measure of spread together describe a
set of data.

</details>
</details>
