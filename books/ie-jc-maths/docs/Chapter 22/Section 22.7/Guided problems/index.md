# 22.7 Scatter graphs and correlation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. In the point $(3,7)$, which number is the $x$-coordinate?
2. A set of points falls from left to right. As $x$ increases, does $y$ increase
   or decrease?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The $x$-coordinate is 3. The first number is always the horizontal one.
2. $y$ decreases. Points that fall from left to right show a negative correlation.

Plotting a scatter graph starts with one point for each pair, using the first
value across and the second value up.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A scatter graph shows that taller students tend to weigh more. Is the
correlation positive, negative or none?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

As one value increases, what happens to the other?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both values increase together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The correlation is positive. As height increases, weight tends to increase, so the
points rise from left to right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A scatter graph shows that cars with more engine size tend to use more fuel
per kilometre. Is the correlation positive, negative or none?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

As engine size increases, what happens to fuel use?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both increase together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The correlation is positive. As engine size increases, fuel use tends to increase,
so the points rise from left to right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A scatter graph shows that students who spend more time practising a skill
tend to make fewer mistakes. Is the correlation positive, negative or none?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

As practice time increases, mistakes go down.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The points fall from left to right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The correlation is negative. As practice time increases, the number of mistakes
tends to decrease, so the points fall from left to right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A scatter graph has points spread widely with no pattern. Describe the
correlation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Is there a line the points follow?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There is no line and no trend.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

There is no correlation. The points show no pattern, so the two variables are not
linked in the data.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A scatter graph has points that lie very close to a straight line that
falls from left to right. Describe the correlation, including its strength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which way does the line go, and how close are the points?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The line falls, so the correlation is negative. The points are close, so it is
strong.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The correlation is negative and strong. The points fall from left to right, and
they lie close to a straight line.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Ice cream sales and the number of sunburn cases rise together in summer.
Does eating ice cream cause sunburn? Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What else happens in summer?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Hot, sunny weather causes both more ice cream sales and more sunburn.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Eating ice cream does not cause sunburn. The two are correlated because hot
weather causes both. A correlation shows a link, not a cause.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. The scatter graph of screen time against sleep has
points that fall from left to right. Describe the correlation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which way do the points go, and how close are they to a line?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The points fall, and they lie fairly close to a straight line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The correlation is negative and strong. As screen time increases, sleep time tends
to decrease. The points lie fairly close to a straight line.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. Does the survey prove that screens cause less sleep?
Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A correlation shows a link. Does it show why the link exists?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A third variable could affect both screen time and sleep.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The survey does not prove that screens cause less sleep. It shows a link between
screen time and sleep. A third factor, such as going to bed late for other
reasons, could affect both. The data supports a link, not a cause.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** A point has $x=2$ and $y=5$. Plot it on a coordinate grid by describing
its position.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Move along the $x$-axis first, then up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Start at the origin, move 2 units right, then 5 units up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

From the origin, move 2 units to the right and 5 units up. The point $(2,5)$ is
there. Every point on a scatter graph is placed the same way.

</details>
</details>
