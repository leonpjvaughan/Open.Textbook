# 1.5 Commutative, associative and distributive properties — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the four operations on integers from Sections 1.2 to 1.4.

**Objectives:** name and use the commutative, associative and distributive properties;
use them to choose a quicker calculation; know which operations do not have them.

By the end of this section, you can explain why the methods you already use are
allowed, and use the properties to calculate in a smarter order.

## The commutative property

Two numbers can be added in either order, and the total is the same. The same is true
of multiplication.

$$3 + 5 = 5 + 3 = 8 \qquad 4 \times 7 = 7 \times 4 = 28$$

This is the **commutative property**. The word means that the numbers may commute,
which is an old word for swap.

Subtraction and division do **not** commute. Swapping the numbers changes the answer.

$$7 - 3 = 4 \qquad \text{but} \qquad 3 - 7 = -4$$

$$12 \div 4 = 3 \qquad \text{but} \qquad 4 \div 12 = \frac{1}{3}$$

## The associative property

When you add three numbers, the grouping does not change the total. The same is true
of multiplication.

$$(2 + 3) + 4 = 2 + (3 + 4) = 9$$

$$(2 \times 3) \times 4 = 2 \times (3 \times 4) = 24$$

This is the **associative property**. It says that you may regroup, as long as you do
not change the order of the numbers or the operation.

Subtraction and division are not associative either.

$$(8 - 4) - 2 = 2 \qquad \text{but} \qquad 8 - (4 - 2) = 6$$

**Example 1.** Work out $4 \times 13 \times 25$ the quick way.

Multiplication is commutative and associative, so you may pair 4 with 25.

$$4 \times 25 = 100 \qquad 100 \times 13 = 1300$$

## The distributive property

Multiplication distributes over addition and over subtraction.

$$a(b + c) = ab + ac \qquad a(b - c) = ab - ac$$

This is the **distributive property**, and it is the reason a written method for
multiplication works. It also gives you a quicker route when one number is close to a
round number.

**Example 2.** Work out $6 \times 47$.

Write 47 as $40 + 7$, then distribute.

$$6 \times (40 + 7) = 6 \times 40 + 6 \times 7 = 240 + 42 = 282$$

**Example 3.** Work out $8 \times 99$.

Write 99 as $100 - 1$.

$$8 \times (100 - 1) = 800 - 8 = 792$$

## Reading the distributive property backwards

If two products share a factor, you can take that factor out. This is called
**factorising**, and it often makes a calculation shorter.

$$15 \times 4 + 15 \times 6 = 15 \times (4 + 6) = 15 \times 10 = 150$$

**Example 4.** Work out $17 + 38 + 3$.

Addition is commutative and associative, so you may reorder and regroup. Pair 17 with
3 to make a round number.

$$17 + 3 = 20 \qquad 20 + 38 = 58$$

> **Watch out.** The distributive property is about a sum or a difference inside the
> bracket. It does not apply to a product inside the bracket.
>
> $$3 \times (2 + 4) = 3 \times 2 + 3 \times 4$$
>
> $$3 \times (2 \times 4) \text{ is not } 3 \times 2 \times 3 \times 4$$
>
> **Re-check:** look at the sign inside the bracket. If it is a plus or a minus, you
> may distribute. If it is a multiplication, leave the bracket alone.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The centre costs €14 for each person and lunch costs
€6.50 for each person, for 35 people.

1. Write $35 \times (14 + 6.50)$ as a sum of two products.
2. Work out the bracket first, and then the total.
3. Work out the two products and add them. Do the two methods agree?

In Section 1.6 you will meet a square root that is not a whole number, which needs a
different way of writing an exact answer.

## You should now be able to

- state the commutative, associative and distributive properties;
- use them to reorder and regroup a calculation into a quicker one;
- say which operations are not commutative and not associative, with an example;
- take out a common factor to shorten a calculation;
- explain why the distributive property does not apply to a product inside a bracket.
