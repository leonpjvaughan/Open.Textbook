# 1.5 Commutative, associative and distributive properties — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $4 \times 25$.
2. Work out $(-8) + 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $100$.
2. $-5$. Start at $-8$ and move 3 to the right.

The product $4 \times 25 = 100$ is worth remembering. You will pair those two numbers
in this section to turn an awkward multiplication into a round one.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Use the commutative property to write $9 + 14$ another way, and work it out.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The commutative property lets you swap the two numbers in an addition.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$14 + 9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$14 + 9 = 23$$

The total is the same as $9 + 14 = 23$. Swapping the order of an addition never
changes the answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Work out $2 \times 17 \times 5$ by pairing 2 and 5 first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiplication is commutative, so you may reorder the numbers. Which pair makes a
round number?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2 \times 5 = 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2 \times 5 = 10 \qquad 10 \times 17 = 170$$

The associative property lets you regroup as $(2 \times 5) \times 17$. The round
number 10 makes the rest of the calculation quick.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Use the distributive property to work out $7 \times 43$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write 43 as $40 + 3$, then multiply each part by 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$7 \times (40 + 3) = 7 \times 40 + 7 \times 3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$280 + 21 = 301$$

The distributive property turned one awkward multiplication into two straightforward
ones.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Use the distributive property to work out $9 \times 98$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

98 is close to 100. Write it as $100 - 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$9 \times (100 - 2) = 9 \times 100 - 9 \times 2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$900 - 18 = 882$$

Multiplication distributes over subtraction as well as over addition.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Work out $25 \times 7 \times 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Reorder the numbers so that 25 pairs with 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$25 \times 4 = 100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$100 \times 7 = 700$$

Both the commutative property, which lets you reorder, and the associative property,
which lets you regroup, are used here.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $12 \times 7 + 12 \times 3$ by taking out the common factor 12.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both products contain a factor of 12. Take it outside a bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12 \times (7 + 3)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$12 \times 10 = 120$$

Working it out the long way gives the same answer: $84 + 36 = 120$. Taking out the
common factor is quicker and is the same property read backwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Is subtraction associative? Give an example to explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Try $(8 - 4) - 2$ and $8 - (4 - 2)$. Do they agree?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(8 - 4) - 2 = 4 - 2 = 2 \qquad 8 - (4 - 2) = 8 - 2 = 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

No, subtraction is not associative. The two groupings give 2 and 6, so the answer
depends on where the bracket sits. Addition and multiplication are the two operations
that are associative.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Work out $6 \times (30 - 2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Brackets first, or distribute. Either route is allowed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$30 - 2 = 28$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6 \times 28 = 168$$

By the distributive property: $180 - 12 = 168$. Both routes agree, which is a good
check on your arithmetic.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $5 + 3 \times 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 1.4. Which operation goes first?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiplication before addition: $3 \times 4 = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5 + 12 = 17$$

The properties in this section tell you what you may reorder. The order of operations
tells you what you may not. A multiplication inside an addition still goes first.

</details>
</details>
</details>
