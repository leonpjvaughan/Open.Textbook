# 1.2 Adding and subtracting integers — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $9 + 6$.
2. What number is 4 steps to the left of 3 on a number line?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $15$.
2. $-1$. Moving left is subtracting, so $3 - 4 = -1$. You pass zero on the way.

Moving left and right on the number line is the whole method in this section. Say the
movement before you write the answer.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Work out $-5 + 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start at $-5$ and move 8 to the right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

You pass $-4$, $-3$, $-2$, $-1$, $0$, then three more.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$-5 + 8 = 3$$

The answer is positive, because 8 is larger than 5. The 8 carried you past zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Work out $4 - 7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start at 4 and move 7 to the left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

You pass zero after 4 steps, with 3 steps still to go.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4 - 7 = -3$$

The answer is negative, because 7 is larger than 4. The minus sign says you ended up
to the left of zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out $-6 - 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both numbers push to the left. Do the amounts add or cancel?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Start at $-6$ and move 3 further left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$-6 - 3 = -9$$

Both movements are to the left, so the amounts add: $6 + 3 = 9$, and the answer is
negative.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $-2 + (-9)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two signs meet: $+(-)$. Which single sign replaces them?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$+(-)$ becomes $-$, so the question is $-2 - 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$-2 + (-9) = -2 - 9 = -11$$

Adding a negative number is the same as subtracting its positive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Work out $7 - (-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two signs meet: $-(-)$. Which single sign replaces them?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$-(-)$ becomes $+$, so the question is $7 + 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7 - (-4) = 7 + 4 = 11$$

Subtracting a negative number moves you to the right. The answer is larger than
either starting number, which is a useful check.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $-3 - (-8)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Replace $-(-)$ with $+$ first, then move.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-3 - (-8) = -3 + 8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$-3 + 8 = 5$$

Start at $-3$ and move 8 to the right. You finish at 5, which is above zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The temperature is $-5$ degrees and rises by 9 degrees. Work out the new
temperature.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A rise is an addition. Write it as $-5 + 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From $-5$, moving 9 to the right passes zero after 5 steps.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The new temperature is $4$ degrees.

$$-5 + 9 = 4$$

There are 4 steps left after reaching zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Work out the difference between $-8$ and $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The difference is the distance between the two numbers. Count the gap through zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From $-8$ to 0 is 8, and from 0 to 3 is 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The difference is $11$.

$$3 - (-8) = 3 + 8 = 11$$

The answer is positive, because a distance is never negative.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Is $-7$ a natural number? Is it an integer?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 1.1. Where do the natural numbers start?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The natural numbers are $1, 2, 3, \ldots$, so a negative number is not among them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$-7$ is an integer but not a natural number. The integers contain the negatives,
zero and the positives, so $-7$ belongs there.

This section moves numbers like $-7$ around the number line. You need to know which
family they are in before you can trust the sign of an answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>
