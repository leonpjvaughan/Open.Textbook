# 1.4 The order of operations and brackets — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $(-6) \times 5$.
2. Work out $20 \div 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $-30$. One negative and one positive give a negative answer.
2. $5$, because $5 \times 4 = 20$.

You need both skills in this section. The order of operations tells you **when** to
do each multiplication or division, and the sign rules tell you what sign the answer
carries.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Work out $4 + 5 \times 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which is stronger, addition or multiplication?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiplication goes first: $5 \times 2 = 10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5 \times 2 = 10 \qquad 4 + 10 = 14$$

The answer is 14. Adding first would give $9 \times 2 = 18$, which is wrong.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Work out $(4 + 5) \times 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bracket comes first this time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4 + 5 = 9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9 \times 2 = 18$$

The bracket raised the answer from 14 to 18. That is what brackets are for.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out $30 - 4 \times 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiplication before subtraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4 \times 6 = 24$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$30 - 24 = 6$$

The subtraction waits until the multiplication is finished.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $3 + 4^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The index is stronger than the addition. What is $4^2$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4^2 = 16$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3 + 16 = 19$$

Only the 4 is squared, because the index sits next to the 4.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Work out $(3 + 4)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bracket goes first, and then the whole bracket is squared.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3 + 4 = 7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7^2 = 49$$

Compare this with question 4. The bracket makes the index apply to the whole sum,
which gives a much larger answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $18 \div 3 + 2 \times 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Division and multiplication both outrank addition. Work them out first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$18 \div 3 = 6 \qquad 2 \times 4 = 8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6 + 8 = 14$$

The division and the multiplication are equally strong, so you may do them in either
order here. The addition waits.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Work out $8 + 2 \times (9 - 5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Bracket first, then the multiplication, then the addition.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$9 - 5 = 4 \qquad 2 \times 4 = 8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8 + 8 = 16$$

Notice that the 8 outside the bracket is not multiplied by the 4. Only the 2 touches
the bracket.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Work out $\frac{10 + 6}{2 + 2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The fraction bar is a bracket. Work out the top and the bottom separately.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\text{top} = 10 + 6 = 16 \qquad \text{bottom} = 2 + 2 = 4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$16 \div 4 = 4$$

If you had divided 10 by 2 first, you would have got a different answer. The bar
groups the whole top and the whole bottom.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $(-4) \times (-5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 1.3. What sign does a product of two negatives have?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Same signs give a positive answer. Work out $4 \times 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(-4) \times (-5) = 20$$

Two negatives make a positive. You will meet this inside larger calculations in this
section, so the sign rule needs to be automatic.

</details>
</details>
</details>
