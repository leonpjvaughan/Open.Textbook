# 1.4 The order of operations and brackets — Learn

**Prerequisites:** adding, subtracting, multiplying and dividing integers from
Sections 1.2 and 1.3, and powers from earlier work.

**Objectives:** apply the order of operations; use brackets to change the order; work
out a calculation that contains indices and a fraction bar.

By the end of this section, you can work out any calculation with several operations
and get the one agreed answer.

## Why the order matters

Look at $3 + 2 \times 4$. Two different answers are possible.

$$3 + 2 = 5 \qquad 5 \times 4 = 20$$

$$2 \times 4 = 8 \qquad 3 + 8 = 11$$

Both calculations use the same numbers and the same two operations. Only one answer
can be right, so everyone agrees on an order. The order is:

1. **Brackets** first, including the bar of a fraction;
2. **Indices** next, which means powers and roots;
3. **Multiplication and division**, working from left to right;
4. **Addition and subtraction**, working from left to right.

The memory word is **BIDMAS**: Brackets, Indices, Division and Multiplication,
Addition and Subtraction.

Multiplication and division are equally strong, so you do them in the order you read
them. The same is true of addition and subtraction.

## Brackets

A bracket groups the operations inside it, and the whole bracket is worked out first.

**Example 1.** Work out $(3 + 2) \times 4$.

$$3 + 2 = 5 \qquad 5 \times 4 = 20$$

Compare this with $3 + 2 \times 4 = 11$. The brackets changed the answer.

**Example 2.** Work out $2 \times (3 + 4 \times 2)$.

Inside the bracket, multiplication goes first.

$$4 \times 2 = 8 \qquad 3 + 8 = 11 \qquad 2 \times 11 = 22$$

## Indices

An index is a power, and it is worked out before any multiplication or addition.

**Example 3.** Work out $2 + 3^2$.

$$3^2 = 9 \qquad 2 + 9 = 11$$

**Example 4.** Work out $(2 + 3)^2$.

The bracket goes first, so the index applies to 5.

$$2 + 3 = 5 \qquad 5^2 = 25$$

## Multiplication and division from left to right

These two operations are equally strong, so the reading order decides.

**Example 5.** Work out $12 \div 3 + 2 \times 5$.

The division and the multiplication both come before the addition.

$$12 \div 3 = 4 \qquad 2 \times 5 = 10 \qquad 4 + 10 = 14$$

**Example 6.** Work out $12 \div 3 \times 2$.

Work from left to right: divide first, then multiply.

$$12 \div 3 = 4 \qquad 4 \times 2 = 8$$

Doing the multiplication first would give $12 \div 6 = 2$, which is wrong.

## Addition and subtraction from left to right

**Example 7.** Work out $100 - (4 + 6) \times 5$.

$$4 + 6 = 10 \qquad 10 \times 5 = 50 \qquad 100 - 50 = 50$$

## The fraction bar is a bracket

The bar of a fraction groups the whole top and the whole bottom. Work out each of them
before you divide.

**Example 8.** Work out $\frac{8 + 4}{2 + 4}$.

$$\text{top} = 8 + 4 = 12 \qquad \text{bottom} = 2 + 4 = 6 \qquad 12 \div 6 = 2$$

> **Watch out.** The operations are not done in the order you read them from left to
> right. The common error is to work out $3 + 2 \times 4$ as $5 \times 4 = 20$.
> Multiplication outranks addition, so the answer is 11.
>
> **Re-check:** underline the multiplication and division first, work those out, and
> only then add and subtract. If a bracket or a fraction bar is present, start there.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The whole trip can be written as one calculation:

$$480 + 35 \times 14 + 35 \times 6.50$$

1. Which operation happens first?
2. Work out the two multiplications.
3. Work out the total cost of the trip.

In Section 1.5 you will see why the same total can be reached by grouping the two
per-person costs first.

## You should now be able to

- state the order of operations and use it on a mixed calculation;
- use brackets to change the order, and explain the difference they make;
- work out an index before the operations around it;
- treat a fraction bar as a bracket around the top and the bottom;
- work from left to right when two operations are equally strong.
