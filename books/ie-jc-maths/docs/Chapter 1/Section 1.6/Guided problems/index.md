# 1.6 Surds and irrational numbers — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $9^2$ and $\sqrt{81}$.
2. Work out $2^3$ and $(-2)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $9^2 = 81$ and $\sqrt{81} = 9$. Squaring and taking the square root undo each
   other.
2. $2^3 = 8$, and $(-2)^2 = 4$. An even index makes the negative answer positive.

You need square numbers by heart for this section, because you look for the largest
one inside a surd.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Simplify $\sqrt{20}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the largest square number that divides 20. Try 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$20 = 4 \times 5 \qquad \sqrt{20} = \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{5}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{20} = 2\sqrt{5}$$

The largest square factor is 4, so the answer is finished. There is no square number
larger than 1 that divides 5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Simplify $\sqrt{45}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

45 is $9 \times 5$. Is 9 a square number?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$45 = 9 \times 5 \qquad \sqrt{45} = \sqrt{9} \times \sqrt{5}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{45} = 3\sqrt{5}$$

The largest square factor of 45 is 9. Taking out 9 leaves 5, which has no square
factor.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Simplify $\sqrt{32}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

32 is $16 \times 2$, and 16 is a square number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{32} = \sqrt{16} \times \sqrt{2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}$$

If you took out 4 instead, you would get $2\sqrt{8}$, and 8 still contains a square
factor. Keep going until the number under the root has no square factor left.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $\sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The product of two square roots is the square root of the product.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{5} = \sqrt{25}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{25} = 5$$

A square root multiplied by itself gives the number inside the root. That is why it is
called a square root.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Work out $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the numbers inside the roots first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{8} = \sqrt{16}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{16} = 4$$

You can also simplify first: $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$, and
$2\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} = 2 \times 2 = 4$. Both routes agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $3\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

These are like surds. Add the numbers in front, and keep the surd.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3 + 4 = 7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3\sqrt{2} + 4\sqrt{2} = 7\sqrt{2}$$

It works exactly like $3x + 4x = 7x$. The root does not change.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Work out $\sqrt{12} + \sqrt{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The surds are not like yet. Simplify $\sqrt{12}$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{3} = 2\sqrt{3}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2\sqrt{3} + \sqrt{3} = 3\sqrt{3}$$

Now the surds are like, so they add. Always simplify before you decide whether two
surds can be added.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Write $\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}}$ with a whole number on the bottom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the top and the bottom by $\sqrt{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}} \times \frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{5}}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{2\sqrt{5}}{5}$$

The bottom becomes 5, because $\sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{5} = 5$. The value of the number
is unchanged, because you multiplied by 1 in the form $\frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $35 \times (14 + 6.5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 1.5. Either add inside the bracket first, or distribute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$14 + 6.5 = 20.5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$35 \times 20.5 = 717.5$$

By the distributive property the same total comes from $490 + 227.5 = 717.5$. This is
the cost of the centre and lunch for the whole trip, which you will use in the Review.

</details>
</details>
</details>
