# 13.3 Formulae with brackets and fractions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Expand $3(x+4)$.
2. Work out $\frac{10+6}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3x+12$. Multiply each term inside the bracket by 3.
2. $8$. The fraction bar groups the top, so add first: $10+6=16$, and then $16\div2=8$.

Both skills appear in this section. A bracket is worked out first, and the bar of a
fraction is a bracket around the whole top and the whole bottom.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find $P$ when $l=4.5$ m and $w=2.5$ m, using $P=2(l+w)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bracket comes first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4.5+2.5 = 7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P = 2\times7 = 14$$

The perimeter is 14 m. The decimals add to a whole number here, which is a useful
check that you added before doubling.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find $m$ when $a=6$ and $b=14$, using $m=\dfrac{a+b}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bar groups the whole top, so add before you divide.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6+14 = 20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$m = \frac{20}{2} = 10$$

The mean of 6 and 14 is 10, which sits halfway between them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Make $x$ the subject of $y=2(x+5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bracket is multiplied by 2. Undo that first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide both sides by 2.

$$\frac{y}{2} = x+5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = \frac{y}{2}-5$$

The bracket is now on its own, so subtract 5 from both sides.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Make $w$ the subject of $A=3(l+w)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide both sides by 3 first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{A}{3} = l+w$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$w = \frac{A}{3}-l$$

The whole of $A$ is divided by 3, because the whole of $3(l+w)$ was divided by 3.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Make $b$ the subject of $m=\dfrac{a+b}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply both sides by 2 to clear the fraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2m = a+b$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$b = 2m-a$$

Multiplying by 2 undoes dividing by 2. Then subtract $a$ from both sides.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Make $v$ the subject of $t=\dfrac{v-u}{a}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply both sides by $a$ to clear the fraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$at = v-u$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = u+at$$

Add $u$ to both sides. This is the formula from Section 13.2 with $v$ as the subject.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Make $h$ the subject of $V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply both sides by 3 to clear the fraction first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3V = \pi r^2h$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h = \frac{3V}{\pi r^2}$$

Divide both sides by $\pi r^2$. The whole of $\pi r^2$ is the divisor, so write it under
the whole of $3V$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Find $A$ when $h=0.5$ m, $l=2$ m and $w=1.5$ m, using $A=2h(l+w)+lw$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the bracket $l+w$ first, and then work out each product.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$l+w = 3.5 \qquad 2\times0.5 = 1 \qquad lw = 3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 1\times3.5+3 = 6.5$$

The area is $6.5\ \text{m}^2$. This is the formula for the board on the planter box
from the chapter problem.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Solve $2(x+3)=16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 12.2. You can divide both sides by 2 first, or expand the bracket
first. Both routes work.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide both sides by 2: $x+3=8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 5$$

Changing the subject of $y=2(x+3)$ uses exactly these steps, with $y$ in place of 16.

</details>
</details>
</details>
