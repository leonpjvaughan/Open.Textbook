# 13.3 Formulae with brackets and fractions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** changing the subject from Section 13.2, multiplying out a bracket
from Section 11.2, and equations with brackets and fractions from Section 12.2.

**Objectives:** substitute into a formula with a bracket or a fraction; change the
subject when the letter is inside a bracket; clear a fraction by multiplying both
sides; change the subject of a formula with a fraction and an index.

By the end of this section, you can handle a formula where the letter you want is
inside a bracket or under a fraction bar.

## Substituting into a formula with brackets

A bracket groups operations, and the whole bracket is worked out first. This does not
change when you substitute numbers into a formula.

**Example 1.** Find $P$ when $l=6.5$ m and $w=3.5$ m, using $P=2(l+w)$.

$$P = 2(6.5+3.5) = 2\times10 = 20$$

The perimeter is 20 m.

**Example 2.** Find the area of the four sides of a box with $h=4$ cm, $l=10$ cm and
$w=3$ cm, using $A=2h(l+w)$.

$$A = 2\times4\times(10+3) = 8\times13 = 104$$

The area is $104\ \text{cm}^2$.

## Substituting into a formula with a fraction

The bar of a fraction is a bracket around the whole top and the whole bottom. Work out
each of them before you divide.

**Example 3.** Find $m$ when $a=7$ and $b=13$, using $m=\dfrac{a+b}{2}$.

$$m = \frac{7+13}{2} = \frac{20}{2} = 10$$

**Example 4.** Find $F$ when $C=35$, using $F=\dfrac{9}{5}C+32$.

$$F = \frac{9}{5}\times35+32 = 63+32 = 95$$

So $35^\circ$C is $95^\circ$F.

## Changing the subject when the letter is inside a bracket

Undo the operations outside the bracket first. That leaves the bracket on its own, and
then you can undo the bracket.

**Example 5.** Make $x$ the subject of $y=3(x+2)$.

The bracket is multiplied by 3, so divide both sides by 3.

$$\frac{y}{3} = x+2$$

Now subtract 2 from both sides.

$$x = \frac{y}{3}-2$$

**Example 6.** Make $w$ the subject of $A=2h(l+w)$.

The bracket is multiplied by $2h$, so divide both sides by $2h$.

$$\frac{A}{2h} = l+w$$

Now subtract $l$ from both sides.

$$w = \frac{A}{2h}-l$$

## Changing the subject when the formula has a fraction

Multiply both sides by the denominator to clear the fraction. The whole of each side
is multiplied, not only part of it.

**Example 7.** Make $b$ the subject of $m=\dfrac{a+b}{2}$.

Multiply both sides by 2.

$$2m = a+b$$

Now subtract $a$ from both sides.

$$b = 2m-a$$

**Example 8.** Make $v$ the subject of $t=\dfrac{v-u}{a}$.

Multiply both sides by $a$.

$$at = v-u$$

Now add $u$ to both sides.

$$v = u+at$$

**Example 9.** Make $C$ the subject of $F=\dfrac{9}{5}C+32$.

Subtract 32 from both sides first.

$$F-32 = \frac{9}{5}C$$

Multiply both sides by $\dfrac{5}{9}$, which undoes multiplying by $\dfrac{9}{5}$.

$$C = \frac{5(F-32)}{9}$$

**Example 10.** Make $h$ the subject of $V=\dfrac{1}{3}\pi r^2h$.

Multiply both sides by 3 to clear the fraction.

$$3V = \pi r^2h$$

Now divide both sides by $\pi r^2$.

$$h = \frac{3V}{\pi r^2}$$

> **Watch out.** Clearing a fraction means multiplying the whole of the other side.
> From $m=\dfrac{a+b}{2}$, the common error is to divide by 2 instead of multiplying,
> and write $b=\frac{m}{2}-a$. The correct step multiplies both sides by 2, giving
> $2m=a+b$ and then $b=2m-a$.
>
> **Re-check:** use numbers. With $m=10$ and $a=7$, the correct form gives
> $b=2(10)-7=13$, and $\frac{7+13}{2}=10$, which is the value of $m$ you started with.
> The wrong form gives $b=\frac{10}{2}-7=-2$, and $\frac{7+(-2)}{2}=2.5$, which is not 10.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The planter box has no lid, and the area of board for
its four sides and its base is $A=2h(l+w)+lw$.

1. Substitute $l=1.5$, $w=1$ and $h=0.4$ into the formula.
2. Work out each bracket first.
3. How much board does the box need?

The Review brings the width and the area of board together for the finished box.

## You should now be able to

- substitute into a formula with a bracket or a fraction;
- change the subject when the letter is inside a bracket;
- clear a fraction by multiplying both sides by the denominator;
- change the subject of a formula with a fraction, a bracket and an index.
