# 13.2 Changing the subject of a formula — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Solve $x+7=12$.
2. Solve $4x=20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x=5$. Subtract 7 from both sides.
2. $x=5$. Divide both sides by 4.

Changing the subject is the same skill. The other letters behave like the numbers
here, and you undo the operations one at a time.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Make $x$ the subject of $y=x+9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 9 is added to $x$. What undoes adding?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 9 from both sides.

$$y-9 = x+9-9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = y-9$$

The subject is now $x$, and it stands on its own.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Make $a$ the subject of $b=a-4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 4 is subtracted from $a$. What undoes subtracting?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 4 to both sides.

$$b+4 = a-4+4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = b+4$$

Adding undoes subtracting, so the sign of the 4 flips when it moves across.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Make $w$ the subject of $A=5w$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $w$ is multiplied by 5. What undoes multiplying?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide both sides by 5.

$$\frac{A}{5} = \frac{5w}{5}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$w = \frac{A}{5}$$

The whole of $A$ is divided by 5, because the whole of $5w$ was divided by 5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Make $t$ the subject of $d=st$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $t$ is multiplied by $s$. Divide both sides by $s$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{d}{s} = \frac{st}{s}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = \frac{d}{s}$$

This is the distance formula turned around. If you know the distance and the speed,
this gives the time.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Make $x$ the subject of $y=3x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 3 multiplies $x$. What undoes multiplying?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide both sides by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = \frac{y}{3}$$

Compare this with question 3. A number in front of the letter is always divided out.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Make $w$ the subject of $P=2l+2w$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two operations are applied to $w$: multiply by 2, then add $2l$. Undo them in reverse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $2l$ from both sides first.

$$P-2l = 2w$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Now divide both sides by 2.

$$w = \frac{P-2l}{2}$$

The whole of $P-2l$ is divided by 2. Dividing only the $2l$ would give a different
value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Make $a$ the subject of $v=u+at$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $a$ is multiplied by $t$, and then $u$ is added. Undo the addition first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $u$ from both sides.

$$v-u = at$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Now divide both sides by $t$.

$$a = \frac{v-u}{t}$$

This is the acceleration formula from Section 13.1, with $a$ as the subject.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Make $r$ the subject of $C=2\pi r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $r$ is multiplied by $2\pi$. Divide both sides by $2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{C}{2\pi} = \frac{2\pi r}{2\pi}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$r = \frac{C}{2\pi}$$

This is the circumference formula turned around, so you can find a radius from a
circumference.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Solve $3x-4=11$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 12.1. Undo the subtraction first, and then the multiplication.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 4 to both sides: $3x=15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 5$$

This is the same two-step process you used in question 6, with numbers in place of
some of the letters. Solving an equation and changing the subject are one skill.

</details>
</details>
</details>
