# 13.2 Changing the subject of a formula — Learn

**Prerequisites:** using formulae from Section 13.1, and solving linear equations
from Section 12.1.

**Objectives:** name the subject of a formula; change the subject by doing the inverse
operation to both sides; change the subject when the letter is multiplied or divided;
change the subject of a formula with a square.

By the end of this section, you can rearrange a formula so that a different letter is
the subject.

## The subject

The **subject** of a formula is the letter that stands on its own on one side. In
$v=u+at$, the subject is $v$. Here $v$ is the final velocity, $u$ is the initial
velocity, $a$ is the acceleration and $t$ is the time.

A formula is often written with the subject that was convenient when it was first
written. The speed formula $s=\frac{d}{t}$ has $s$ as its subject. A driver who knows
the speed and the time wants the distance instead, so the subject has to change.
Here $d$ is the distance.

## Inverse operations

To change the subject, you undo the operations that are applied to the letter you
want. Each operation is undone by its **inverse**:

| Operation | Inverse |
|---|---|
| add | subtract |
| subtract | add |
| multiply | divide |
| divide | multiply |
| square | square root |

Whatever you do to one side, you do to the other side. The two sides stay equal, so
the formula stays true.

Undo the operations in the reverse of the order they were applied. The last operation
applied is the first one you undo.

**Example 1.** Make $x$ the subject of $y=x+5$.

The 5 is added to $x$, so subtract 5 from both sides.

$$y-5 = x+5-5$$

$$x = y-5$$

**Example 2.** Make $w$ the subject of $A=lw$.

The $w$ is multiplied by $l$, so divide both sides by $l$.

$$\frac{A}{l} = \frac{lw}{l} \qquad w = \frac{A}{l}$$

**Example 3.** Make $t$ the subject of $s=\frac{d}{t}$.

The $t$ is in the bottom of the fraction. Multiply both sides by $t$.

$$st = d$$

Now divide both sides by $s$.

$$t = \frac{d}{s}$$

**Example 4.** Make $w$ the subject of $P=2l+2w$.

Two operations are applied to $w$: it is multiplied by 2, and then $2l$ is added.
Undo the addition first, and then the multiplication.

$$P-2l = 2w \qquad \frac{P-2l}{2} = w$$

So $w=\frac{P-2l}{2}$.

**Example 5.** Make $a$ the subject of $v=u+at$.

Subtract $u$ from both sides, and then divide both sides by $t$.

$$v-u = at \qquad a = \frac{v-u}{t}$$

**Example 6.** Make $x$ the subject of $y=mx+c$.

Subtract $c$ from both sides, and then divide both sides by $m$.

$$x = \frac{y-c}{m}$$

**Example 7.** Make $r$ the subject of $C=2\pi r$.

The $r$ is multiplied by $2\pi$, so divide both sides by $2\pi$.

$$r = \frac{C}{2\pi}$$

**Example 8.** Make $w$ the subject of $V=lwh$.

Both $l$ and $h$ multiply $w$, so divide both sides by $lh$.

$$w = \frac{V}{lh}$$

This is the rearrangement you need for the planter box.

## Changing the subject of a formula with a square

If the letter you want is squared, take the square root at the end. A square root has
two signs, so choose the one the quantity allows.

**Example 9.** Make $r$ the subject of $A=\pi r^2$. A radius is a length, so it is
positive.

Divide both sides by $\pi$.

$$r^2 = \frac{A}{\pi}$$

Take the square root of both sides.

$$r = \sqrt{\frac{A}{\pi}}$$

The negative root is rejected, because a radius cannot be negative.

> **Watch out.** Every part of a side must be changed, not only the part you are
> looking at. From $P=2l+2w$, the common error is to write $w=P-\frac{2l}{2}$,
> dividing only the $2l$. The whole of $P-2l$ is divided by 2, so $w=\frac{P-2l}{2}$.
>
> **Re-check:** choose numbers and test both forms. With $P=20$ and $l=3$, the correct
> form gives $w=\frac{20-6}{2}=7$. The wrong form gives $w=20-3=17$, and
> $2(3)+2(17)=40$, which is not 20.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The planter box must hold 0.6 m³, and it is 1.5 m long
and 0.4 m tall.

1. Start with $V=lwh$.
2. Make $w$ the subject.
3. Substitute $V=0.6$, $l=1.5$ and $h=0.4$ to find the width.

In Section 13.3 you will find the area of board the box needs.

## You should now be able to

- name the subject of a formula;
- change the subject by doing the inverse operation to both sides;
- undo the operations in the reverse of the order they were applied;
- change the subject when the letter is multiplied, divided or squared.
