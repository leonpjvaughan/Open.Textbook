# 13.1 Using formulae — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $3+4\times2$.
2. Work out $(-3)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $11$. Multiplication goes first: $4\times2=8$, and then $3+8=11$.
2. $9$, because $(-3)\times(-3)=9$. The square of a negative number is positive.

Both skills appear in every formula in this section. The order of operations decides
what to work out first, and the sign rule decides the sign of the answer.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the area of a rectangle with $l=9$ cm and $w=4$ cm, using $A=lw$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Replace $l$ with 9 and $w$ with 4. The formula says multiply them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A = 9\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 36$$

The area is $36\ \text{cm}^2$. The units of an area are squared units, because you
multiplied a length by a length.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the perimeter of a square with side 7 m, using $P=4s$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The letter $s$ stands for the side. The formula multiplies the side by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$P = 4\times7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P = 28$$

The perimeter is 28 m. A square has four equal sides, which is where the 4 comes from.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the value of $y=5x-2$ when $x=6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply before you subtract.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5\times6 = 30$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = 30-2 = 28$$

The multiplication is stronger than the subtraction, so it happens first.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the value of $y=5x-2$ when $x=-3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put the $-3$ in brackets when you substitute it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5\times(-3) = -15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = -15-2 = -17$$

Compare this with question 3. The same formula gives a positive answer for $x=6$ and
a negative answer for $x=-3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the volume of a cuboid with $l=2$ m, $w=3$ m and $h=0.5$ m, using $V=lwh$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

All three letters are multiplied together. Work left to right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times3 = 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = 6\times0.5 = 3$$

The volume is $3\ \text{m}^3$. A volume has cubed units, because three lengths were
multiplied.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Find the perimeter of a rectangle with $l=12$ cm and $w=8$ cm, using
$P=2(l+w)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bracket comes first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12+8 = 20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P = 2\times20 = 40$$

The perimeter is 40 cm. Doubling the bracket adds the length and the width, and then
counts both of each.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Find the area of a circle with radius 4 cm, using $A=\pi r^2$ and
$\pi\approx3.14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the index before the multiplication.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4^2 = 16$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 3.14\times16 = 50.24$$

The area is $50.24\ \text{cm}^2$. Only the 4 is squared, because the index sits next
to the $r$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A taxi charges €4 to start and €2 for each kilometre. The fare is $C=2k+4$.
Find the fare for a journey of 9 km.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $k=9$. Multiply before you add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times9 = 18$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$C = 18+4 = 22$$

The fare is €22. The €4 is charged once, and the €2 is charged for each kilometre.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Substitute $a=4$ into the expression $3a^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 10.2. Work out the power before the multiplication.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a^2 = 4^2 = 16$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3a^2 = 3\times16 = 48$$

The index applies to the $a$ alone, so $3a^2$ means $3\times(a^2)$, not $(3a)^2$.
Compare: $(3\times4)^2=144$, which is a different value.

</details>
</details>
</details>
