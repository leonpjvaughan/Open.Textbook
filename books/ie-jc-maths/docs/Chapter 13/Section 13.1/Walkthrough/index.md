# 13.1 Using formulae — Learn

**Prerequisites:** substituting values into expressions from Section 10.2, and the
order of operations from Section 1.4.

**Objectives:** read a formula and say what each letter means; substitute numbers
into a formula; work out the value with the order of operations; substitute a
negative number or a decimal.

By the end of this section, you can put numbers into a formula and work out the value
correctly.

## What a formula is

A **formula** is a rule that connects quantities. It is written with letters, and each
letter stands for a quantity you can measure or count.

$$A = lw$$

This is the area of a rectangle. The letter $A$ stands for the area, $l$ for the
length and $w$ for the width. The formula says: multiply the length by the width.

The letter that stands on its own on one side is the **subject** of the formula. In
$A=lw$, the subject is $A$. A formula is a kind of equation, and it is true for every
rectangle you can draw.

## Substituting into a formula

To **substitute** means to replace a letter with a number. You use a formula by
substituting the numbers you know, and then working out the value.

**Example 1.** Find the area of a rectangle with $l=7$ cm and $w=3$ cm.

$$A = lw = 7\times3 = 21$$

The area is $21\ \text{cm}^2$. Write the units with your answer.

**Example 2.** Find the perimeter of a rectangle with $l=8$ m and $w=5$ m, using
$P=2(l+w)$.

$$P = 2(l+w) = 2(8+5) = 2\times13 = 26$$

The perimeter is 26 m. The bracket is worked out first.

**Example 3.** A car travels 150 km in 3 hours. Find its average speed, using
$s=\dfrac{d}{t}$.

$$s = \frac{d}{t} = \frac{150}{3} = 50$$

The average speed is 50 km/h.

## Formulae with an index

An index tells you how many copies of a number to multiply. Work out the index before
the operations around it.

**Example 4.** Find the area of a circle with radius 5 cm, using $A=\pi r^2$ and
$\pi\approx3.14$.

$$A = \pi r^2 = 3.14\times5^2 = 3.14\times25 = 78.5$$

The area is $78.5\ \text{cm}^2$. The index applies only to the $r$.

## Substituting a negative number

Put a negative number in brackets when you substitute it. The brackets keep the sign
attached to the number.

**Example 5.** Find $y$ when $x=-2$, using $y=3x-5$.

$$y = 3\times(-2)-5 = -6-5 = -11$$

**Example 6.** Find $y$ when $x=-4$, using $y=x^2+2x-3$.

$$y = (-4)^2+2\times(-4)-3 = 16-8-3 = 5$$

The square of a negative number is positive, because $(-4)\times(-4)=16$.

## Formulae from words

Sometimes a question gives the rule in words, and you write the formula yourself.

**Example 7.** A club charges €12 for each ticket and €5 for booking. Write a formula
for the cost $C$ of $n$ tickets, and find the cost of 8 tickets.

$$C = 12n+5$$

$$C = 12\times8+5 = 96+5 = 101$$

Eight tickets cost €101.

> **Watch out.** Substituting does not change the order of operations. In
> $A=2h(l+w)$ with $h=4$, $l=10$ and $w=3$, the common error is to work out
> $2\times4\times10+3=83$, by dropping the bracket. The bracket is still there:
> $l+w=13$, and then $2\times4\times13=104$.
>
> **Re-check:** replace each letter with its number in brackets, then follow the order
> of operations. A bracket in the formula is a bracket in the calculation.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The planter box must hold 0.6 m³ of soil. In the first
design it is 1.5 m long, 1 m wide and 0.4 m tall.

1. Which formula gives the volume of soil the box holds?
2. Substitute $l=1.5$, $w=1$ and $h=0.4$ into it.
3. Does the first design hold the soil the club needs?

In Section 13.2 you will find the width directly, without guessing.

## You should now be able to

- say what each letter in a formula stands for, and name the subject;
- substitute numbers into a formula, including negative numbers and decimals;
- work out the value with the order of operations and brackets;
- write a formula from a rule given in words.
