# 4.1 Rounding and significant figures — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $4.6\times10$ and $4.6\div10$.
2. Write down the value of the digit 7 in 2734.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4.6\times10=46$ and $4.6\div10=0.46$. Multiplying by 10 moves every digit one
   place left, and dividing by 10 moves every digit one place right.
2. The 7 is in the hundreds place, so it is worth 700. Place value is the whole
   idea behind rounding: you keep one place and drop the rest.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Round 4738 to the nearest 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the two hundreds it sits between: 4700 and 4800. Look at the tens digit to
choose.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The tens digit is 3. Is that 5 or more?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$4700$. The tens digit is 3, which is less than 5, so the hundreds digit stays at
7.

Check the answer is close: 4738 and 4700 differ by 38, which is small next to the
step of 100.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Round 2.648 to 2 decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Keep two digits after the decimal point: the 6 and the 4. Look at the digit on
their right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The third decimal digit is 8, so the second decimal digit goes up by one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2.65$. The 8 is 5 or more, so the 4 becomes 5.

Check: $2.65-2.648=0.002$, so the rounded number is very close to the original.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Round 3.996 to 2 decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The third decimal digit is 6, so the second one goes up. What happens when a 9
goes up?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The 9 becomes 0 and the carry passes to the digit on its left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$4.00$. The hundredths digit 9 goes up to 10, so it becomes 0 and the carry turns
3.99 into 4.00.

Write both zeros. They show the answer is correct to 2 decimal places.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Round 0.005607 to 2 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start at the first digit that is not zero. Which digit is that?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first significant figure is 5. The zeros in front of it are place holders.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$0.0056$. The first two significant figures are 5 and 6. The digit on the right is
0, which is less than 5, so the 6 stays.

The three zeros in front of the 5 do not count. Only the 5 and the 6 are
significant.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Round 3.14159 to 3 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first digit here is not zero, so the count starts at the 3. Take three digits.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first three significant figures are 3, 1 and 4. Look at the 1 on the right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$3.14$. The next digit is 1, which is less than 5, so the 4 stays.

This number is $\pi$ to five decimal places. Rounding it to 3 significant figures
gives 3.14, which is the value you use in calculations.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Round 27400 to 2 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first two significant figures are 2 and 7. The digit on the right decides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The third digit is 4, which is less than 5, so the 7 stays.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$27000$. You must keep the two zeros, because they hold the size of the number.

If you dropped them, the answer would be 27, which is a thousand times too small.
The rounded number 27000 has two significant figures, even though it has five
digits.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** How many significant figures does 20.50 have? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count from the left, starting at the first digit that is not zero. Do the zeros
inside the number and at the end count?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The count starts at the 2, and every digit after it counts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Four. The digits are 2, 0, 5 and 0.

The zero between the 2 and the 5 counts, because it sits between two other digits.
The final zero counts too, because it comes after the decimal point. Trailing zeros
only count when there is a decimal point to show they were written on purpose.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A phone is priced at €249.99. Round the price to the nearest euro.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

To the nearest euro means the nearest whole number. Look at the first decimal
digit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first decimal digit is 9, so the price rounds up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

€250. The 9 is 5 or more, so 249.99 rounds up to 250.

The shop may still charge €249.99. Rounding the price makes it quicker to compare
with other phones, but the exact price is the one on the label.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Write 0.75 as a fraction in its simplest form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The digits after the decimal point tell you the denominator. Two decimal places
means hundredths.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.75=\frac{75}{100}$. Now divide the top and the bottom by the same number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{75}{100}=\frac{3}{4}$$

Divide the top and the bottom by 25. The fraction $\frac{3}{4}$ is in its simplest
form, because 3 and 4 share no factor other than 1.

This connects to rounding in a useful way. As a decimal, $\frac{3}{4}=0.75$ exactly.
Written as a decimal to 1 decimal place, it is 0.8, and that rounded value is no
longer exactly three quarters.

</details>
</details>
</details>
