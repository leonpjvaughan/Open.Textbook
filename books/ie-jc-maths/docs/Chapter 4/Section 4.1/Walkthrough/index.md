# 4.1 Rounding and significant figures — Learn

**Prerequisites:** place value, and rounding a number to the nearest 10, 100 or
1000.

**Objectives:** round a number to a place value, to a number of decimal places and
to a number of significant figures; count the significant figures in a number; say
why leading zeros do not count.

By the end of this section, you can round any number to the accuracy a question
asks for.

## Rounding to a place value

A **rounded** number is a simpler number that is close to the original. You choose
the place you want to keep, then look at the digit on its right.

- If that digit is 5 or more, the kept digit goes up by one.
- If it is 4 or less, the kept digit stays the same.

**Example 1.** Round 4738 to the nearest 100.

The number sits between 4700 and 4800. The hundreds digit is 7. The digit on its
right is the tens digit, which is 3. Three is less than 5, so the 7 stays.

$$4738 \to 4700$$

**Example 2.** Round 25481 to the nearest 1000.

The number sits between 25000 and 26000. The thousands digit is 5, and the digit on
its right is 4. Four is less than 5, so the 5 stays.

$$25481 \to 25000$$

## Rounding to decimal places

A **decimal place** is a position after the decimal point. One decimal place means
one digit after the point, two decimal places means two digits, and so on.

**Example 3.** Round 2.648 to 2 decimal places.

The first two decimal digits are 6 and 4. The digit on the right of the 4 is 8.
Eight is 5 or more, so the 4 goes up to 5.

$$2.648 \to 2.65$$

**Example 4.** Round 3.996 to 2 decimal places.

The digit on the right of the 9 is 6, so the 9 goes up. A 9 cannot go up to 10, so
it becomes 0 and the carry passes to the next digit on the left.

$$3.996 \to 4.00$$

You must write the two zeros. They show that the answer is given to 2 decimal
places, not to the nearest whole number.

## Significant figures

**Significant figures** count the digits that carry information about the size of
a number. The first significant figure is the first digit that is not zero,
counting from the left.

The zeros at the start of a small number are place holders. They fix the position
of the decimal point, and they do not count. So 0.0042 has two significant figures:
the 4 and the 2.

Once the count has started, every digit counts, including zeros. A zero between two
other digits counts. A zero after the decimal point counts. So 20.50 has four
significant figures.

**Example 5.** Round 3.14159 to 3 significant figures.

The first three significant figures are 3, 1 and 4. The digit on the right is 1,
which is less than 5.

$$3.14159 \to 3.14$$

**Example 6.** Round 0.005607 to 2 significant figures.

The first significant figure is 5, because the zeros in front of it are place
holders. The first two significant figures are 5 and 6. The digit on the right is
0, so the 6 stays.

$$0.005607 \to 0.0056$$

**Example 7.** Round 0.003049 to 2 significant figures.

The first two significant figures are 3 and 0. The digit on the right is 4, which
is less than 5, so the 0 stays. That zero is a significant figure, not a place
holder, so you write it.

$$0.003049 \to 0.0030$$

**Example 8.** Round 27400 to 2 significant figures.

The first two significant figures are 2 and 7. The digit on the right is 4, which
is less than 5, so the 7 stays.

$$27400 \to 27000$$

The two zeros hold the size of the number. Without them the number would be 27,
which is a thousand times smaller.

> **Watch out.** Leading zeros are not significant figures. In 0.0042 the first
> significant figure is 4, not 0. Counting from the left, the two zeros before the
> 4 are place holders only. The same trap appears at the other end: 27000 could
> mean two significant figures or five, and the digits alone cannot tell you. A
> scientist writes $2.7\times10^{4}$ to say two. You will meet that way of writing
> numbers in Section 4.3.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The tap drips 15 times in one minute, and each drip
is 0.25 ml.

1. Work out the volume of water the tap loses in one minute.
2. Round your answer to 1 significant figure, and also to the nearest whole ml.
3. The tap drips for a whole year. Do you expect the yearly total in ml to be
   nearer 20000, 2000000 or 200000000? Write down your choice, and one sentence
   saying why. You will estimate it in Section 4.2.

## You should now be able to

- round to the nearest 10, 100 or 1000, and to a whole number;
- round to a given number of decimal places, and write the zeros when a carry
  makes them necessary;
- find the significant figures of a number, starting at the first digit that is
  not zero;
- round to a given number of significant figures, and explain why leading zeros do
  not count.
