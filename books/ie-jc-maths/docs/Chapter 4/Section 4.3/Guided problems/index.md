# 4.3 Standard form — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $10^{4}$ and $10^{3}$.
2. Work out $2.5\times1000$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $10^{4}=10000$ and $10^{3}=1000$. The index counts the zeros when the base is
   10.
2. $2.5\times1000=2500$. Multiplying by 1000 moves every digit three places left.

Both answers are the machinery behind standard form. The index chooses the power
of 10, and moving the decimal point is what changes the coefficient.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Write 47000 in standard form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Move the decimal point left until one digit remains in front of it. Count the
places you moved.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$47000 \to 4.7000$. You moved the point four places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$47000=4.7\times10^{4}$$

Check the coefficient is between 1 and 10. It is 4.7, which is in range, so the
answer is in standard form.

Check the value by moving back: $4.7\times10^{4}=4.7\times10000=47000$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Write 0.00052 in standard form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Move the decimal point right until one digit remains in front of it. The index
will be negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.00052 \to 00005.2$. You moved the point four places to the right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.00052=5.2\times10^{-4}$$

The index is $-4$, because the number is smaller than 1 and the point moved to the
right.

Check: $5.2\times10^{-4}=5.2\div10000=0.00052$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Write $5.3\times10^{-3}$ as an ordinary number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A negative index moves the decimal point to the left. How far?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The index is $-3$, so move the point three places left from 5.3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5.3\times10^{-3}=0.0053$$

The answer is a small positive number. The negative index does not make the answer
negative.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Write $7.02\times10^{5}$ as an ordinary number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A positive index moves the decimal point to the right. The coefficient already has
two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Move the point five places right. Two places take you to 702, and three more places
add the zeros.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7.02\times10^{5}=702000$$

Check the size: $10^{5}$ is 100000, and $7.02\times100000$ is a little over 700000.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Which is larger, $4.1\times10^{4}$ or $9.7\times10^{3}$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the indices first. The larger index means the larger power of 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The indices are 4 and 3. Which is larger?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$4.1\times10^{4}$ is larger. The index 4 is bigger than the index 3, so the first
number is multiplied by the larger power of 10.

As ordinary numbers they are 41000 and 9700. A larger index beats a larger
coefficient, because $10^{4}$ is ten times $10^{3}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $(3\times10^{4})\times(2\times10^{3})$, and give your answer in
standard form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the coefficients, and add the indices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3\times2=6$, and $10^{4}\times10^{3}=10^{4+3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3\times10^{4})\times(2\times10^{3})=6\times10^{7}$$

The coefficient is 6, which is between 1 and 10, so the answer is already in
standard form.

Check the size: $3\times10^{4}$ is 30000 and $2\times10^{3}$ is 2000, and
$30000\times2000=60000000$, which is $6\times10^{7}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Work out $(8\times10^{6})\div(2\times10^{2})$, and give your answer in
standard form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the coefficients, and subtract the indices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$8\div2=4$, and $10^{6}\div10^{2}=10^{6-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(8\times10^{6})\div(2\times10^{2})=4\times10^{4}$$

The coefficient is 4, which is between 1 and 10, so the answer is in standard form.

Check: $8\times10^{6}=8000000$ and $2\times10^{2}=200$, and
$8000000\div200=40000=4\times10^{4}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Is $0.5\times10^{3}$ in standard form? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the coefficient. What are the rules for it?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The coefficient must be at least 1 and less than 10. Here it is 0.5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

No. The coefficient 0.5 is less than 1, so the number is not in standard form.

Write it correctly by moving the decimal point one place right and reducing the
index by 1:

$$0.5\times10^{3}=5\times10^{2}$$

Both are equal to 500, but only $5\times10^{2}$ is in standard form.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $10^{5}\div10^{2}$, and write down the index law you used.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

When you divide powers with the same base, what happens to the indices?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract the index on the bottom from the index on the top: $5-2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10^{5}\div10^{2}=10^{5-2}=10^{3}=1000$$

The law is: when you divide powers with the same base, you subtract the indices.

This is exactly the law you use for standard form. In $8\times10^{6}$ divided by
$2\times10^{2}$, the powers of 10 follow this rule and the coefficients are divided
separately.

</details>
</details>
</details>
