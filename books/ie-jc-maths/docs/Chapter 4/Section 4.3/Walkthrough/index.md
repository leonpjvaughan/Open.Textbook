# 4.3 Standard form — Learn

**Prerequisites:** place value, powers of 10, and the index laws from Chapter 2.

**Objectives:** write a number in standard form; change standard form back to an
ordinary number; compare two numbers written in standard form; multiply and divide
numbers in standard form.

By the end of this section, you can write very large and very small numbers in a
form that is short, exact and straightforward to compare.

## What standard form is

**Standard form** writes a number as a value between 1 and 10, multiplied by a
power of 10.

$$a\times10^{n}, \qquad 1\le a<10$$

The number $a$ is called the **coefficient**. It must be at least 1 and less than
10. The index $n$ is a whole number. It is positive for large numbers, zero for
numbers from 1 up to 10, and negative for small numbers.

A number written in standard form shows its significant figures directly. The
coefficient holds them, and the power of 10 only fixes the size.

## Writing a large number in standard form

Move the decimal point left until one digit remains in front of it. The number of
places you moved is the index.

**Example 1.** Write 47000 in standard form.

Move the point four places left: $47000 \to 4.7000$. The digits that were holding
the zeros are no longer needed, so the coefficient is 4.7.

$$47000=4.7\times10^{4}$$

**Example 2.** Write 3600000 in standard form.

Move the point six places left: $3600000 \to 3.600000$. The coefficient is 3.6, and
the index is 6.

$$3600000=3.6\times10^{6}$$

## Writing a small number in standard form

Move the decimal point right until one digit remains in front of it. Count the
places you moved, and write the index as a negative number.

**Example 3.** Write 0.00052 in standard form.

Move the point four places right: $0.00052 \to 00005.2$. The coefficient is 5.2, and
the index is $-4$.

$$0.00052=5.2\times10^{-4}$$

**Example 4.** Write 0.000306 in standard form.

Move the point four places right: $0.000306 \to 00003.06$. The coefficient is 3.06,
and the index is $-4$.

$$0.000306=3.06\times10^{-4}$$

## Changing standard form back

The index tells you how far, and which way, to move the decimal point. A positive
index moves the point right. A negative index moves the point left.

**Example 5.** Write $5.3\times10^{-3}$ as an ordinary number.

The index is $-3$, so move the point three places left.

$$5.3\times10^{-3}=0.0053$$

**Example 6.** Write $7.02\times10^{5}$ as an ordinary number.

The index is 5, so move the point five places right. Move the point two places to
reach 702, then add three zeros.

$$7.02\times10^{5}=702000$$

## Comparing and calculating

To compare two numbers in standard form, look at the indices first. The number
with the larger index is the larger number, because it is multiplied by the larger
power of 10.

**Example 7.** Which is larger, $4.1\times10^{4}$ or $9.7\times10^{3}$?

The indices are 4 and 3. Four is larger than three, so $4.1\times10^{4}$ is larger.
As ordinary numbers they are 41000 and 9700.

To multiply, multiply the coefficients and add the indices. To divide, divide the
coefficients and subtract the indices.

**Example 8.** Work out $(3\times10^{4})\times(2\times10^{3})$.

$$3\times2\times10^{4+3}=6\times10^{7}$$

**Example 9.** Work out $(8\times10^{6})\div(2\times10^{2})$.

$$\frac{8}{2}\times10^{6-2}=4\times10^{4}$$

An answer is in standard form only when the coefficient is between 1 and 10. If the
coefficient comes out as 12, say, adjust it: $12\times10^{3}=1.2\times10^{4}$.

> **Watch out.** Two traps appear again and again.
>
> - The coefficient must be at least 1 and less than 10. So $0.5\times10^{3}$ and
>   $12\times10^{5}$ are not standard form. Write them as $5\times10^{2}$ and
>   $1.2\times10^{6}$.
> - A negative index does not make the number negative. $4\times10^{-4}$ is the
>   small positive number 0.0004. It is not $-4$.

## Progress check

Back to the dripping tap. The exact volume of water lost in a year is 1,971,000 ml.

1. Write 1,971,000 in standard form.
2. There are 1000 ml in a litre. Write 1971 litres in standard form.
3. Round 1971 litres to 2 significant figures, and write the result in standard
   form. That is the answer to the chapter problem, in the form a scientist would
   write it.

## You should now be able to

- write a large or a small number as a value between 1 and 10 times a power of 10;
- change a number from standard form back to an ordinary number;
- compare two numbers in standard form by their indices;
- multiply and divide numbers in standard form, and adjust the coefficient when it
  is not between 1 and 10.
