# 4.2 Estimating and checking a result — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Round 318 to 1 significant figure.
2. Work out $40\times30$ and $600\div20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $300$. The first significant figure is 3, and the digit on its right is 1,
   which is less than 5, so the 3 stays. The two zeros hold the size of the number.
2. $40\times30=1200$ and $600\div20=30$.

Both of these are the moves you make in an estimate: round each number to one
significant figure, then multiply or divide.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Estimate $4.9\times21.3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Round each number to one significant figure first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4.9$ rounds to 5, and $21.3$ rounds to 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5\times20=100$$

The exact answer is 104.37, so the estimate is close. Both numbers were rounded
down, so you would expect the estimate to be a little small, and it is.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Estimate $813\div18.6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Round the two numbers to one significant figure, then divide.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$813$ rounds to 800, and $18.6$ rounds to 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$800\div20=40$$

The exact answer is about 43.7. The dividend was rounded down and the divisor was
rounded up, so the estimate came out a little small.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Estimate $0.42\times5.9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first significant figure of 0.42 is 4. The zeros in front of it do not count.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.42$ rounds to 0.4, and $5.9$ rounds to 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.4\times6=2.4$$

The exact answer is 2.478. The estimate is a little small, because 0.42 was
rounded down.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Estimate $612\div29$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Round 612 and 29 to one significant figure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$612$ rounds to 600, and $29$ rounds to 30.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$600\div30=20$$

The exact answer is about 21.1. The estimate is close, so an answer near 20 is
plausible.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A calculator shows $4.9\times21.3=1043.7$. Use an estimate to decide whether
this is plausible.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Do not trust the calculator. Work out the estimate first and compare the sizes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4.9$ rounds to 5 and $21.3$ rounds to 20, so the estimate is $5\times20=100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

It is not plausible. The estimate is 100, so the answer must be near 100, not near
1000.

The digits 10437 are the ones you expect, so the digits are right and the decimal
point is wrong. The correct answer is 104.37.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Estimate $7.8\times0.51$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Round 7.8 to 8 and 0.51 to 0.5. Multiplying by 0.5 is the same as halving.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$8\times0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8\times0.5=4$$

The exact answer is 3.978. The estimate of 4 is very close, because one number
was rounded up and the other was rounded down, so the two errors nearly cancel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A pupil estimates $3.7\times4.2$ as $4\times4=16$. Is the estimate too big or
too small? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at what happened to each number. Did it go up or down?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

3.7 went up to 4, and 4.2 went up to 4. Both were rounded up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The estimate is too big. Both numbers were rounded up, so both errors push the
estimate above the true value.

The exact answer is 15.54, which is less than 16. That agrees with the reasoning.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Estimate $19.95+4.05+3.10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For a sum, round each number to the nearest whole number or to one significant
figure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$19.95$ is close to 20, $4.05$ is close to 4, and $3.10$ is close to 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$20+4+3=27$$

The exact total is 27.10, so the estimate is very close.

Rounding to one significant figure would give $20+4+3$ as well, because 4.05 and
3.10 both keep one figure at that size.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A class trip costs €480 for a bus and €14 for each of 35 people. Estimate
the total cost.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Round each number to one significant figure before you multiply. Keep the two
parts of the cost separate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The bus is about €500. For the people, $14\times35$ rounds to $10\times40$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$500+10\times40=500+400=900$$

The estimate is about €900.

The exact cost is $480+14\times35=480+490=970$. The estimate is a little low,
because 14 was rounded down to 10. Even so, it tells you the total is near €900
rather than near €90 or €9000.

</details>
</details>
</details>
