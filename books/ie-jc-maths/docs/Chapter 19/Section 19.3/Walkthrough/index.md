# 19.3 Central symmetry — Learn

**Prerequisites:** reading coordinates from Chapter 17, and reflection from
Section 19.2.

**Objectives:** explain what central symmetry means; find the image of a point or
a shape under a half-turn about a centre; find the centre from a point and its
image.

By the end of this section you can turn a shape through 180° about any point and
write down where every point lands.

## Turning through 180°

**Central symmetry** is a turn through 180° about a point. The turn is called a
**half-turn**, and the point is the **centre of symmetry**.

A half-turn is exactly half of a full turn. The shape ends up facing the opposite
way, but it is not flipped over. A shape with central symmetry looks the same
after the half-turn.

The letter S has central symmetry. So does the letter N. Turn either one through
180° about its centre and it looks the same.

## The rule

For a half-turn about the origin, both coordinates change sign.

$$(x,y)\to(-x,-y)$$

For a half-turn about the point $(a,b)$, the rule is

$$(x,y)\to(2a-x,\ 2b-y)$$

The centre is the **midpoint** of a point and its image. That is why the rule
doubles the centre: the centre sits halfway between the two points.

**Example 1.** Find the image of $P(3,2)$ under a half-turn about the origin.

Both coordinates change sign.

$$P(3,2)\to(-3,-2)$$

The image is $(-3,-2)$. The origin is the midpoint of $P$ and its image.

**Example 2.** Find the image of $A(4,0)$ under a half-turn about $(2,2)$.

Use the rule with $a=2$ and $b=2$.

$$A(4,0)\to(2\times2-4,\ 2\times2-0)=(0,4)$$

The image is $(0,4)$. The centre $(2,2)$ is the midpoint of $(4,0)$ and $(0,4)$.

**Example 3.** A half-turn maps $P(1,3)$ to $P''(5,7)$. Find the centre.

The centre is the midpoint of the point and its image.

$$\left(\frac{1+5}{2},\ \frac{3+7}{2}\right)=(3,5)$$

The centre is $(3,5)$.

## Central symmetry of a shape

A shape has central symmetry when a half-turn about some point maps the shape
onto itself. That point is the centre of symmetry.

A parallelogram has central symmetry. The centre is where the diagonals cross. A
half-turn about that point maps each corner to the opposite corner.

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** Central symmetry is not a reflection. A half-turn keeps the
> orientation of a shape, and a reflection reverses it.
>
> The common error is to change the sign of only one coordinate, which is a
> reflection. A half-turn about the origin changes the sign of both coordinates:
> $(3,2)\to(-3,-2)$, not $(-3,2)$.
>
> **Re-check:** the centre must be the midpoint of the point and its image. Check
> that the two distances from the centre are equal.

A second trap is the centre. The half-turn is about a named point, not always the
origin. For a half-turn about $(2,2)$, the image of $(4,0)$ is $(0,4)$, not
$(-4,0)$.

## You should now be able to

- explain what central symmetry and a half-turn mean;
- find the image under a half-turn about the origin by changing both signs;
- find the image under a half-turn about $(a,b)$ using $2a-x$ and $2b-y$;
- find the centre of a half-turn as the midpoint of a point and its image;
- check that the centre is the midpoint of a point and its image;
- remember that a half-turn keeps the orientation of a shape.
