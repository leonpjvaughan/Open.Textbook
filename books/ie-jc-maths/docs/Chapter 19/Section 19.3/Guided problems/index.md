# 19.3 Central symmetry — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Reflect the point $(3,1)$ in the $y$-axis.
2. Reflect the point $(2,4)$ in the line $y=x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(-3,1)$. The $y$-axis is the mirror line, so the $x$-coordinate changes sign.
2. $(4,2)$. The line $y=x$ swaps the two coordinates.

A half-turn also changes the signs of coordinates, but it changes both of them
and it turns the shape rather than flipping it.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the image of $(2,3)$ under a half-turn about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A half-turn about the origin changes the sign of both coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(-2,-3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,3)\to(-2,-3)$$

The origin is the midpoint of the point and its image, so the image is the same
distance from the origin on the other side.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the image of $(-1,4)$ under a half-turn about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the sign of each coordinate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(1,-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(-1,4)\to(1,-4)$$

The point is in the second quadrant and the image is in the fourth. The half-turn
moves it to the opposite side of the origin.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the image of $(3,2)$ under a half-turn about the point $(1,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the rule $(2a-x,\ 2b-y)$ with $a=1$ and $b=1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(2\times1-3,\ 2\times1-2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,2)\to(2\times1-3,\ 2\times1-2)=(-1,0)$$

The image is $(-1,0)$. The centre $(1,1)$ is the midpoint of $(3,2)$ and
$(-1,0)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the image of $(2,3)$ under a half-turn about the point $(-1,2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Here $a=-1$ and $b=2$. Watch the negative sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(2\times(-1)-2,\ 2\times2-3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,3)\to(2\times(-1)-2,\ 2\times2-3)=(-4,1)$$

The image is $(-4,1)$. The centre $(-1,2)$ is the midpoint of $(2,3)$ and
$(-4,1)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A half-turn maps the point $(1,3)$ to $(5,7)$. Find the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The centre is the midpoint of the point and its image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Average the coordinates: $((1+5)\div2,\ (3+7)\div2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\left(\frac{1+5}{2},\ \frac{3+7}{2}\right)=(3,5)$$

The centre is $(3,5)$. It is the same distance from $(1,3)$ as from $(5,7)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A triangle has vertices $(1,1)$, $(3,1)$ and $(1,2)$. Find the image
vertices under a half-turn about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each vertex changes the sign of both coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(1,1)\to(-1,-1)$ and $(3,1)\to(-3,-1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,1)\to(-1,-1)\qquad(3,1)\to(-3,-1)\qquad(1,2)\to(-1,-2)$$

The image vertices are $(-1,-1)$, $(-3,-1)$ and $(-1,-2)$. The image is congruent
to the object, and it faces the opposite way.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. The crest has centre $M(2,2)$ and a corner $A(4,0)$.
Find the image of $A$ under a half-turn about $M$, and name the corner it lands
on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the rule $(2a-x,\ 2b-y)$ with the centre $M(2,2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(2\times2-4,\ 2\times2-0)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A(4,0)\to(2\times2-4,\ 2\times2-0)=(0,4)$$

The image is $(0,4)$, which is the corner $C$. The half-turn about $M$ maps each
corner to the opposite corner, so it maps the whole crest onto itself.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A shape has central symmetry about the origin. The point $(4,-2)$ lies on
the shape. Find the matching point on the shape.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The matching point is the image under a half-turn about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Change the sign of each coordinate of $(4,-2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(4,-2)\to(-4,2)$$

The matching point is $(-4,2)$. A half-turn about the origin sends it back to
$(4,-2)$, so the two points come in a pair.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Reflect the point $(5,1)$ in the line $x=2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The point is 3 units right of the line, so the image is 3 units left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(2\times2-5,\ 1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(5,1)\to(2\times2-5,\ 1)=(1,1)$$

The image is $(1,1)$. A reflection reverses the orientation of a shape, but a
half-turn keeps it. Both moves appear in this chapter.

</details>
</details>
</details>
