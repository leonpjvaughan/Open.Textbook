# Chapter 19: Transformations and symmetry

A transformation moves a shape. One transformation slides a shape along. Another
turns it about a point. Another flips it over a line. The shape keeps its size
and its angles in all of them.

Transformations are how patterns are built. A tiled floor, a wallpaper, a flag
and a logo all repeat a shape by sliding, turning or flipping it. This chapter
shows you how to do each move on a coordinate grid, and how to describe it
exactly.

## The chapter problem

**The club crest.** A club is designing a square crest on a coordinate grid. The
corners are $O(0,0)$, $A(4,0)$, $B(4,4)$ and $C(0,4)$. The centre is $M(2,2)$.

The designer joins the centre $M$ to each corner. These two diagonals split the
square into four triangles: the bottom triangle $MOA$, the right triangle $MAB$,
the top triangle $MBC$ and the left triangle $MCO$. The designer shades the left
and right triangles, and leaves the top and bottom triangles plain.

Before you calculate anything, look at the crest. Do you think a half-turn about
the centre leaves it looking the same? Write down your guess. How many lines of
symmetry do you think the crest has?

By the end of the chapter you can write down the corners of a copy moved by a
translation, reflect and turn the shaded triangles, and count the lines of
symmetry of the finished crest.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- translate a point or a shape by a vector, and find the vector of a translation;
- reflect a point or a shape in the $x$-axis, the $y$-axis, the line $y=x$, or a
  line such as $x=2$ or $y=1$;
- find the image of a point or a shape under central symmetry about a point;
- rotate a point or a shape through 90° or 180° about a centre;
- draw the axes of symmetry of a shape and count them;
<!-- tier:h -->
- combine two transformations and say what single transformation they give.

## Where it is used

Designers use transformations every day. A wallpaper pattern repeats one shape by
translations, reflections and rotations. A company logo is often one shape with
its images around a centre. Artists, architects and engineers use the same moves
to place windows, tiles and panels.

Computer graphics uses transformations to move every object on a screen. When you
drag a map, the picture is translated. When a game character turns, a rotation is
applied. Animators use reflections to make a character look the same on both
sides.
