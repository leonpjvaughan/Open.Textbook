# 19.6 Combining transformations — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. How many axes of symmetry does a square have?
2. Rotate the point $(3,1)$ through 90° anticlockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4$. Two axes join the midpoints of opposite sides, and two run along the
   diagonals.
2. $(-1,3)$. A quarter-turn anticlockwise sends $(x,y)$ to $(-y,x)$.

Combining transformations uses every rule from this chapter, so keep the
reflection and rotation rules close at hand.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Reflect the point $(1,1)$ in the line $x=0$, then reflect the image in the
line $x=3$. Find the final image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two mirror lines are parallel, so the result is a translation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first reflection sends $(1,1)$ to $(-1,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,1)\to(-1,1)\to(2\times3-(-1),\ 1)=(7,1)$$

The final image is $(7,1)$. The lines are 3 apart, so the pair of reflections is a
translation of 6 right. Starting from $x=1$, that gives $x=7$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Reflect the point $(2,4)$ in the line $y=0$, then reflect the image in the
line $y=2$. Find the final image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two mirror lines are parallel and horizontal, so the result is a vertical
translation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first reflection sends $(2,4)$ to $(2,-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,4)\to(2,-4)\to(2,\ 2\times2-(-4))=(2,8)$$

The final image is $(2,8)$. The lines are 2 apart, so the translation is 4 up.
Starting from $y=4$, that gives $y=8$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Reflect the point $(4,1)$ in the line $x=2$, then reflect the image in the
line $y=2$. Find the final image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lines meet at $(2,2)$ at a right angle, so the pair of reflections is a
half-turn.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first reflection sends $(4,1)$ to $(0,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(4,1)\to(0,1)\to(0,\ 2\times2-1)=(0,3)$$

The final image is $(0,3)$. The two lines meet at $(2,2)$, so the pair is a
half-turn about $(2,2)$: $(2\times2-4,\ 2\times2-1)=(0,3)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Rotate the point $(2,1)$ through 90° anticlockwise about the origin, then
reflect the image in the $x$-axis. Find the final image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Do the rotation first, and write down the intermediate point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The quarter-turn sends $(2,1)$ to $(-1,2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,1)\to(-1,2)\to(-1,-2)$$

The final image is $(-1,-2)$. The reflection in the $x$-axis changes the sign of
the $y$-coordinate only.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Translate the point $(1,2)$ by the vector $\begin{pmatrix}2\\1\end{pmatrix}$
and then by the vector $\begin{pmatrix}-3\\4\end{pmatrix}$. Find the final image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two translations are the same as one translation whose vector is the sum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the vectors: $2-3=-1$ across and $1+4=5$ up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,2)+(2,1)+(-3,4)=(0,7)$$

The final image is $(0,7)$. The combined vector is
$\begin{pmatrix}-1\\5\end{pmatrix}$, so the point moves 1 left and 5 up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The chapter problem. Reflect the crest corner $A(4,0)$ in the line $x=2$,
then reflect the image in the line $y=2$. Find the final image and name the
corner.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lines meet at the centre $M(2,2)$, so the pair is a half-turn about $M$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first reflection sends $(4,0)$ to $(0,0)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(4,0)\to(0,0)\to(0,4)$$

The final image is $(0,4)$, which is the corner $C$. The pair of reflections is a
half-turn about $M(2,2)$, and that half-turn sends $A$ to $C$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Reflect the point $(3,1)$ in the line $x=2$, then rotate the image through
90° anticlockwise about the origin. Find the final image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The reflection comes first. Find the intermediate point before you rotate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The reflection sends $(3,1)$ to $(1,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,1)\to(1,1)\to(-1,1)$$

The final image is $(-1,1)$. The quarter-turn anticlockwise sends $(1,1)$ to
$(-1,1)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Start with the point $(3,1)$. Rotate it through 90° anticlockwise about the
origin, then reflect the image in the line $x=2$. Find the final image and
compare it with question 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The order is swapped from question 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The quarter-turn sends $(3,1)$ to $(-1,3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,1)\to(-1,3)\to(2\times2-(-1),\ 3)=(5,3)$$

The final image is $(5,3)$. Question 7 gave $(-1,1)$, so the two orders give
different answers. The order of the two transformations matters.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Rotate the point $(2,3)$ through 180° about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A half-turn changes the sign of both coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(-2,-3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,3)\to(-2,-3)$$

The image is $(-2,-3)$. A half-turn about the origin is central symmetry about the
origin.

</details>
</details>
</details>
