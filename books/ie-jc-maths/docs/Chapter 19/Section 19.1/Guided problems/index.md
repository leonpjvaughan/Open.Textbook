# 19.1 Translation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the coordinates of the point that is 3 units right and 2 units up
   from the origin.
2. The point $(-4,5)$ lies in which quadrant?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(3,2)$. The first coordinate counts across and the second counts up.
2. The second quadrant. A negative $x$-coordinate is to the left, and a positive
   $y$-coordinate is up.

Both skills return in this section. A translation adds a vector to the
coordinates, so you need to read and write coordinates with care.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Translate the point $P(1,4)$ by the vector $\begin{pmatrix}3\\2\end{pmatrix}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the top number to the $x$-coordinate and the bottom number to the
$y$-coordinate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(1+3,\ 4+2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(1,4)\to(1+3,\ 4+2)=(4,6)$$

The image is $(4,6)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Translate the point $Q(-2,1)$ by the vector
$\begin{pmatrix}4\\-3\end{pmatrix}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The second number is negative, so the point moves down.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(-2+4,\ 1-3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$Q(-2,1)\to(-2+4,\ 1-3)=(2,-2)$$

The image is $(2,-2)$. The move is 4 right and 3 down.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Translate the point $R(0,0)$ by the vector
$\begin{pmatrix}-2\\5\end{pmatrix}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The point $R$ is the origin. The first number is negative, so the move is to the
left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(0-2,\ 0+5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R(0,0)\to(0-2,\ 0+5)=(-2,5)$$

The image is $(-2,5)$. The move is 2 left and 5 up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A translation maps $A(2,1)$ to $B(6,4)$. Find the vector of the
translation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the start from the finish.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Across: $6-2=4$. Up: $4-1=3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{across} = 6-2 = 4 \qquad \text{up} = 4-1 = 3$$

The vector is $\begin{pmatrix}4\\3\end{pmatrix}$. The translation is 4 right and
3 up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A triangle has vertices $A(1,1)$, $B(2,1)$ and $C(1,3)$. Translate the
triangle by the vector $\begin{pmatrix}3\\2\end{pmatrix}$ and write down the
image vertices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Every vertex moves by the same vector. Work through the three vertices one at a
time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A(1,1)\to(4,3)$ and $B(2,1)\to(5,3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A(1,1)\to(4,3)\qquad B(2,1)\to(5,3)\qquad C(1,3)\to(4,5)$$

The image vertices are $(4,3)$, $(5,3)$ and $(4,5)$. Every vertex moved 3 right
and 2 up, so the image is congruent to the object.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A translation maps $P(5,4)$ to $P''(2,7)$. Find the vector of the
translation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the start from the finish, and watch the signs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Across: $2-5=-3$. Up: $7-4=3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{across} = 2-5 = -3 \qquad \text{up} = 7-4 = 3$$

The vector is $\begin{pmatrix}-3\\3\end{pmatrix}$. The translation is 3 left and
3 up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. The crest is the square with corners $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$,
$(4,4)$ and $(0,4)$. A copy is translated by the vector
$\begin{pmatrix}6\\0\end{pmatrix}$. Find the four corners of the copy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The vector is 6 right and 0 up, so only the $x$-coordinates change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(0,0)\to(6,0)$ and $(4,0)\to(10,0)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(0,0)\to(6,0)\qquad(4,0)\to(10,0)\qquad(4,4)\to(10,4)\qquad(0,4)\to(6,4)$$

The copy has corners $(6,0)$, $(10,0)$, $(10,4)$ and $(6,4)$. It sits beside the
original and has the same size and shape.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A translation moves a shape from $(2,3)$ to $(7,3)$. Describe the
translation in words, and give its vector.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at what changed between the two points and what did not.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The $x$-coordinate changed from 2 to 7. The $y$-coordinate stayed at 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{across} = 7-2 = 5 \qquad \text{up} = 3-3 = 0$$

The translation is 5 right, and its vector is
$\begin{pmatrix}5\\0\end{pmatrix}$. A shape moved by this vector keeps every
$y$-coordinate the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Find the midpoint of the segment joining $(1,2)$ and $(5,8)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The midpoint is the average of the two ends.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Average the $x$-coordinates and average the $y$-coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\left(\frac{1+5}{2},\ \frac{2+8}{2}\right) = (3,5)$$

The midpoint is $(3,5)$. A midpoint is a kind of halfway point, and the same idea
of averaging coordinates returns in Section 19.3.

</details>
</details>
</details>
