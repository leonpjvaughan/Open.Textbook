# 19.5 Axes of symmetry — Learn

**Prerequisites:** reflection from Section 19.2, and rotation from Section 19.4.

**Objectives:** find the axes of symmetry of a shape; count them; find the order
of rotational symmetry of a shape.

By the end of this section you can look at a shape and say how many ways it can
be folded onto itself.

## What an axis of symmetry is

An **axis of symmetry** is a line that divides a shape into two halves that are
mirror images. If you fold the shape along the axis, the two halves fit exactly.

A shape with at least one axis of symmetry has **axial symmetry**. Some shapes
have several axes. Some have none.

To test a line, imagine reflecting the shape in that line. If the image lands
exactly on the shape, the line is an axis of symmetry.

## Counting the axes of common shapes

| Shape | Axes of symmetry |
|---|---|
| Square | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 |
| Rhombus | 2 |
| Kite | 1 |
| Parallelogram | 0 |
| Equilateral triangle | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 |
| Scalene triangle | 0 |
| Regular pentagon | 5 |
| Regular hexagon | 6 |
| Circle | infinitely many |

A regular polygon with $n$ sides has $n$ axes of symmetry. Each axis passes
through a vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side, or through the midpoints
of two opposite sides.

**Example 1.** An isosceles triangle has vertices $(0,0)$, $(4,0)$ and $(2,3)$.
Find its axis of symmetry.

The base runs from $(0,0)$ to $(4,0)$, and its midpoint is $(2,0)$.

$$\text{midpoint } x = \frac{0+4}{2} = 2$$

The apex $(2,3)$ sits directly above the midpoint, so the axis is the vertical
line $x=2$.

**Example 2.** How many axes of symmetry does a rectangle have?

A rectangle has 2 axes. Each axis joins the midpoints of a pair of opposite
sides. The diagonals are not axes.

## Rotational symmetry

A shape has **rotational symmetry** when a turn about its centre maps the shape
onto itself. The **order** of rotational symmetry is the number of times the shape
fits onto itself in a full turn.

A square has order 4: it fits onto itself at 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°. A rectangle
has order 2: it fits at 0° and 180°. An equilateral triangle has order 3.

Every shape has at least order 1, because a full turn always maps it onto itself.

**Example 3.** Find the order of rotational symmetry of a parallelogram.

A parallelogram fits onto itself at 0° and 180°, but not at 90°. Its order is 2.

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** The diagonals of a rectangle are not axes of symmetry. The
> common error is to count them, which gives 4 instead of 2.
>
> Fold a rectangle along a diagonal. The two halves do not match, because one
> corner folds over onto a different corner.
>
> **Re-check:** an axis of symmetry must map every corner onto a corner. A
> reflection in a diagonal of a rectangle sends a long side onto a short side, so
> it is not an axis.

A second trap is the parallelogram. It has order 2 rotational symmetry but no
axis of symmetry at all. A shape can turn onto itself without folding onto
itself.

## You should now be able to

- explain what an axis of symmetry is;
- test a line by reflecting the shape in it;
- count the axes of a square, a rectangle, a kite, a triangle and a regular
  polygon;
- find the order of rotational symmetry of a shape;
- avoid counting the diagonals of a rectangle;
- remember that a parallelogram turns onto itself but has no axis.
