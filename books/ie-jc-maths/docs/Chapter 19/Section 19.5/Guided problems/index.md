# 19.5 Axes of symmetry — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Rotate the point $(2,3)$ through 180° about the origin.
2. Rotate the point $(4,0)$ through 90° anticlockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(-2,-3)$. A half-turn changes the sign of both coordinates.
2. $(0,4)$. A quarter-turn anticlockwise sends $(x,y)$ to $(-y,x)$.

An axis of symmetry is a line you can reflect a shape in. The reflection rules
from Section 19.2 are the tool for testing one.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** How many axes of symmetry does a square have?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think of the folds that put the square onto itself: through the middles of the
sides, and along the diagonals.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two axes join the midpoints of opposite sides, and two run along the diagonals.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A square has 4 axes of symmetry. Two join the midpoints of opposite sides, and
two run along the diagonals. A reflection in any of the four leaves the square
looking the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** How many axes of symmetry does an equilateral triangle have?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each axis passes through a vertex.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Each axis goes through one vertex and the midpoint of the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An equilateral triangle has 3 axes of symmetry. Each axis passes through a vertex
and the midpoint of the opposite side. The three sides are equal, so there are
three such lines.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** An isosceles triangle has vertices $(0,0)$, $(6,0)$ and $(3,4)$. Find the
equation of its axis of symmetry.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The axis is the perpendicular bisector of the base.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The midpoint of the base $(0,0)$ to $(6,0)$ has $x=(0+6)\div2=3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The midpoint of the base has $x=3$, and the apex $(3,4)$ sits directly above it.
The axis is the vertical line $x=3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A shape has an axis of symmetry along the line $x=2$. The point $(0,5)$ is
on the shape. Find its mirror image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The point is 2 units left of the axis, so the image is 2 units right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(2\times2-0,\ 5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(0,5)\to(2\times2-0,\ 5)=(4,5)$$

The mirror image is $(4,5)$. The $y$-coordinate does not change, because the axis
is vertical.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A shape has an axis of symmetry along the line $y=1$. The point $(3,4)$ is
on the shape. Find its mirror image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The point is 3 units above the axis, so the image is 3 units below.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(3,\ 2\times1-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,4)\to(3,\ 2\times1-4)=(3,-2)$$

The mirror image is $(3,-2)$. The $x$-coordinate does not change, because the
axis is horizontal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Does a parallelogram have an axis of symmetry? Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Try to find a line that folds the parallelogram onto itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

No line divides a general parallelogram into two mirror-image halves.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

No. A parallelogram has no axis of symmetry. A reflection always reverses the
orientation, and no line does that while mapping a general parallelogram onto
itself. A rectangle, a rhombus and a square do have axes, because they are
special parallelograms.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. The crest is shaded on the left and right triangles
and plain on the top and bottom triangles. How many axes of symmetry does the
crest have? Name them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Test the vertical line through the centre, the horizontal line, and a diagonal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The vertical line $x=2$ swaps the left and right shaded triangles. The horizontal
line $y=2$ maps each shaded triangle to itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The crest has 2 axes of symmetry: the vertical line $x=2$ and the horizontal line
$y=2$. A diagonal is not an axis, because a reflection in a diagonal sends a
shaded triangle onto a plain one.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Find the order of rotational symmetry of a rectangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count how many times the rectangle fits onto itself in a full turn.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

It fits at 0° and at 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The order is 2. A rectangle fits onto itself at 0° and 180°, so it looks the same
twice in a full turn. It does not fit at 90°, because a rectangle is longer than
it is wide.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Rotate the point $(1,2)$ through 90° clockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the rule $(x,y)\to(y,-x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(2,-1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,2)\to(2,-1)$$

The image is $(2,-1)$. A clockwise quarter-turn sends the point down and to the
right.

</details>
</details>
</details>
