# Chapter 19: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The club crest.** The crest is the square with corners $O(0,0)$, $A(4,0)$,
$B(4,4)$ and $C(0,4)$, with centre $M(2,2)$. Joining $M$ to each corner makes four
triangles: bottom $MOA$, right $MAB$, top $MBC$ and left $MCO$. The left and right
triangles are shaded.

**Step 1. A translated copy.** A copy of the crest is translated by the vector
$\begin{pmatrix}6\\0\end{pmatrix}$. Add 6 to each $x$-coordinate.

$$O(0,0)\to(6,0)\qquad A(4,0)\to(10,0)\qquad B(4,4)\to(10,4)\qquad C(0,4)\to(6,4)$$

**The copy has corners $(6,0)$, $(10,0)$, $(10,4)$ and $(6,4)$.**

**Step 2. A reflection.** Reflect the right shaded triangle, with vertices
$M(2,2)$, $A(4,0)$ and $B(4,4)$, in the line $x=2$.

$$M(2,2)\to(2,2)\qquad A(4,0)\to(0,0)\qquad B(4,4)\to(0,4)$$

**The image is the left shaded triangle $MCO$.** So the line $x=2$ maps the crest
onto itself. It is an axis of symmetry.

**Step 3. Central symmetry.** A half-turn about $M(2,2)$ sends a point $(x,y)$ to
$(4-x,\ 4-y)$.

$$A(4,0)\to(0,4)=C\qquad B(4,4)\to(0,0)=O$$

**The half-turn swaps $A$ with $C$ and $B$ with $O$.** It also swaps the two
shaded triangles, so the crest maps onto itself. The crest has central symmetry
about $M$.

**Step 4. A quarter-turn.** A quarter-turn anticlockwise about $M$ sends $A$ to
$B$ and $B$ to $C$.

$$A(4,0)\to B(4,4)\qquad B(4,4)\to C(0,4)$$

**The right shaded triangle $MAB$ maps to the top plain triangle $MBC$.** A
quarter-turn swaps shaded and plain, so the crest does not map onto itself under
a quarter-turn.

**Step 5. The axes of symmetry.** The crest has **2 axes of symmetry**: the
vertical line $x=2$ and the horizontal line $y=2$. A diagonal is not an axis,
because a reflection in a diagonal sends a shaded triangle onto a plain one.

**Step 6. Rotational symmetry.** A half-turn about $M$ leaves the crest looking
the same, and a full turn does too. **The crest has rotational symmetry of order
2.** A quarter-turn does not, because it swaps the shading.

**Step 7. Two reflections (Higher only).** Reflect $A(4,0)$ in $x=2$, then in
$y=2$.

$$A(4,0)\to(0,0)\to(0,4)=C$$

This is the same result as the half-turn about $M$ in Step 3. Two reflections in
perpendicular lines through $M$ combine to give a half-turn about $M$.

Every section of this chapter met this one crest: the translation that copies it,
the reflection that makes an axis, the half-turn that gives central symmetry, the
quarter-turn that swaps the shading, and the count of the axes.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and
say why in a few words. The idea might be a translation, a reflection, central
symmetry, a rotation, an axis of symmetry, a distance, a midpoint, a slope,
Pythagoras' theorem, a ratio, a percentage, an equation, an area, or standard
form.

1. Translate the point $(2,5)$ by the vector $\begin{pmatrix}-3\\4\end{pmatrix}$.
2. Reflect the point $(-2,3)$ in the $y$-axis.
3. Find the image of $(4,-1)$ under a half-turn about the origin.
4. Rotate the point $(3,0)$ through 90° clockwise about the origin.
5. How many axes of symmetry does a regular pentagon have?
6. Find the distance between $(1,1)$ and $(4,5)$.
7. Find the midpoint of the segment joining $(2,3)$ and $(8,7)$.
8. A right-angled triangle has legs of 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
9. A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 6 m and an adjacent side of
   8 m. Find $\tan\theta$.
10. Work out 15% of 240.
11. Solve $5x-3=17$.
12. Write $0.00036$ in standard form.
13. Divide €70 in the ratio $2:5$.
14. Find the area of a circle of radius 6 cm, in terms of $\pi$.
15. A line passes through $(1,2)$ and $(4,8)$. Find its slope.
16. The point $(3,4)$ is reflected in the line $x=1$. Find the image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and concise solutions</summary>

1. $(-1,9)$. Add the vector to the coordinates: $2-3=-1$ and $5+4=9$.
2. $(2,3)$. A reflection in the $y$-axis changes the sign of the $x$-coordinate.
3. $(-4,1)$. A half-turn about the origin changes the sign of both coordinates.
4. $(0,-3)$. A quarter-turn clockwise sends $(x,y)$ to $(y,-x)$.
5. $5$. A regular pentagon has one axis through each vertex and the midpoint of
   the opposite side.
6. $5$. The steps are 3 and 4, and $3^2+4^2=9+16=25$, so $\sqrt{25}=5$.
7. $(5,5)$. Average the coordinates: $(2+8)\div2=5$ and $(3+7)\div2=5$.
8. $15$ cm. $9^2+12^2=81+144=225$, and $\sqrt{225}=15$.
9. $0.75$. Tangent is opposite over adjacent: $6\div8=0.75$.
10. $36$. Ten per cent of 240 is 24, so 5% is 12. Add them: $24+12=36$.
11. $x=4$. Add 3 to both sides to get $5x=20$, then divide by 5.
12. $3.6\times10^{-4}$. Move the decimal point 4 places right, so the power is
    $-4$.
13. €20 and €50. There are $2+5=7$ parts, and $70\div7=10$. Then $2\times10=20$
    and $5\times10=50$.
14. $36\pi$ cm². Use $A=\pi r^2$: $\pi\times6^2=36\pi$.
15. $2$. The rise is $8-2=6$ and the run is $4-1=3$, so $6\div3=2$.
16. $(-1,4)$. The point is 2 units to the right of the line $x=1$, so the image
    is 2 units to the left. The $y$-coordinate does not change.

## What you can now do

- translate a point or a shape by a vector, and find the vector of a translation;
- reflect a point or a shape in the $x$-axis, the $y$-axis, the line $y=x$, or a
  line such as $x=2$ or $y=1$;
- find the image of a point or a shape under central symmetry about a point;
- rotate a point or a shape through 90° or 180° about a centre;
- draw the axes of symmetry of a shape and count them;
- find the order of rotational symmetry of a shape;
- combine two transformations and say what single transformation they give
  (Higher only).

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you guessed whether a half-turn about the centre
leaves the crest looking the same, and how many lines of symmetry it has. Look
back at your guess now.

A half-turn about $M$ does leave the crest looking the same, and the crest has
2 axes of symmetry. If you guessed both, you read the picture well. If you
guessed that a quarter-turn would work too, the shading is why it does not: the
shaded triangles swap with the plain ones.

You also wrote down one shape from a pattern you can see, and how it repeats.
Take that shape now. Say which single transformation turns one copy into the
next: a translation, a reflection, or a rotation. Then find the exact vector,
mirror line or angle if you can.

That is the whole of this chapter in one small piece of work: one shape, one
move, and a description exact enough for someone else to repeat it.
