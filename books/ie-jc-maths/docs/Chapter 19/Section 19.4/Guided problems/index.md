# 19.4 Rotation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the image of $(3,1)$ under a half-turn about the origin.
2. Find the image of $(2,5)$ under a half-turn about the point $(1,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(-3,-1)$. Both coordinates change sign.
2. $(0,-3)$. Use $(2a-x,\ 2b-y)$ with $a=1$ and $b=1$: $2-2=0$ and $2-5=-3$.

A half-turn is a rotation through 180°. This section also turns shapes through
90°, and the direction of the turn matters.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Rotate the point $(2,1)$ through 90° anticlockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the rule $(x,y)\to(-y,x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The new first coordinate is $-1$ and the new second coordinate is 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,1)\to(-1,2)$$

The image is $(-1,2)$. The point starts on the right and turns anticlockwise,
which takes it up and to the left.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Rotate the point $(4,2)$ through 90° clockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the rule $(x,y)\to(y,-x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The new first coordinate is 2 and the new second coordinate is $-4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(4,2)\to(2,-4)$$

The image is $(2,-4)$. A clockwise quarter-turn sends the point down and to the
right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Rotate the point $(3,-1)$ through 180° about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A half-turn changes the sign of both coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(-3,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,-1)\to(-3,1)$$

The image is $(-3,1)$. A half-turn about the origin is the same as central
symmetry about the origin.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Rotate the point $(-2,3)$ through 90° anticlockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $(x,y)\to(-y,x)$ and watch the negative sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The new first coordinate is $-3$ and the new second coordinate is $-2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(-2,3)\to(-3,-2)$$

The image is $(-3,-2)$. The point starts in the second quadrant and turns into
the third.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Rotate the point $(3,1)$ through 90° anticlockwise about the centre
$(1,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the centre first, rotate, then add the centre back.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From the centre, the point is $(3-1,\ 1-1)=(2,0)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

From the centre, the point is $(2,0)$. A quarter-turn anticlockwise sends
$(2,0)$ to $(0,2)$. Add the centre back: $(0+1,\ 2+1)=(1,3)$.

The image is $(1,3)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Rotate the point $A(4,0)$ through 90° clockwise about the centre $(2,2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From the centre, $A$ is $(2,-2)$. A clockwise quarter-turn sends $(x,y)$ to
$(y,-x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(2,-2)\to(-2,-2)$, and then add the centre back.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

From the centre, $A$ is $(2,-2)$. A clockwise quarter-turn gives $(-2,-2)$. Add
the centre $(2,2)$: $(-2+2,\ -2+2)=(0,0)$.

The image is $(0,0)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter problem. Rotate the crest corner $A(4,0)$ through 90°
anticlockwise about the centre $M(2,2)$, and name the corner it lands on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From $M$, the point $A$ is $(2,-2)$. A quarter-turn anticlockwise sends
$(x,y)$ to $(-y,x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(2,-2)\to(2,2)$, and then add $M(2,2)$ back.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

From the centre, $A$ is $(2,-2)$. A quarter-turn anticlockwise sends it to
$(2,2)$. Add the centre back: $(2+2,\ 2+2)=(4,4)$.

The image is $(4,4)$, which is the corner $B$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A rotation about the origin maps the point $A(4,0)$ to $B(0,4)$. Describe
the rotation fully.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Name the centre, the angle and the direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The origin stays fixed, so it is the centre. The point moved from the positive
$x$-axis to the positive $y$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The rotation is 90° anticlockwise about the origin. The origin is the only point
that stays fixed, so it is the centre. The point moved from the positive
$x$-axis onto the positive $y$-axis, and only an anticlockwise quarter-turn does
that.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Find the image of $(1,4)$ under a half-turn about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both coordinates change sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The image is $(-1,-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,4)\to(-1,-4)$$

The image is $(-1,-4)$. A half-turn about the origin is central symmetry about
the origin, and it is also a rotation through 180°.

</details>
</details>
</details>
