# 20.2 The constructions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $90\div2$ and $180\div2$.
2. Work out $2+3$ and compare it with 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $45$ and $90$. Halving an angle is what the bisector does, so this subtraction
   comes back in every bisection.
2. $2+3=5$, which is less than 8. Two sides of a triangle are together greater than
   the third, so no triangle has sides 2, 3 and 8.

The first skill is the bisector; the second decides whether a construction is
possible at all.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** An angle measures 74°. A student constructs its bisector. Work out the size
of each of the two new angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A bisector divides the angle into two equal angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide 74 by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$74\div2=37°$. Each of the two new angles is 37°, because the bisector makes them
equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** $[AB]$ is 10 cm long. The perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$ meets it at $M$.
How far is $M$ from $A$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The perpendicular bisector meets the segment at its midpoint.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The midpoint is halfway along.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$10\div2=5$ cm. $M$ is the midpoint, so it is 5 cm from $A$ and 5 cm from $B$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Describe, in order, the steps that bisect a given angle with compass and
straight edge.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First mark both arms, then find a point inside the angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two arcs inside the angle must use the same compass width.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

With the vertex as centre, draw an arc that cuts both arms. With each cut point as
centre, and the same compass width, draw two arcs that cross inside the angle. Join
the vertex to the crossing point. That ray is the bisector.

The equal compass widths are what make the crossing point the same distance from
both arms.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Describe, in order, the steps that construct the perpendicular bisector of a
segment $[AB]$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need two points, one on each side of the segment.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Open the compass to more than half of $[AB]$ and keep it there.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

With $A$ as centre and a radius more than half of $[AB]$, draw an arc above and an
arc below the segment. With $B$ as centre and the same radius, draw arcs that cross
the first two. Join the two crossing points. That line is the perpendicular
bisector.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Which construction draws a line through a given point $P$ parallel to a
given line $l$? Name the construction and give one step.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The construction uses equal corresponding angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Draw a transversal through $P$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

It is **Construction 5**. Draw any line through $P$ that cuts $l$. Copy the angle
that line makes with $l$ at $P$, so the corresponding angles are equal. Equal
corresponding angles mean the lines are parallel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student constructs a triangle with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm. Check that
the construction is possible by adding the two shorter sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two sides of a triangle are together greater than the third.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 5 and 6, and compare the total with 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$5+6=11$, which is greater than 7. The triangle can be constructed. If the total
had been less than 7, the two arcs would not have crossed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why a triangle with sides 2 cm, 3 cm and 6 cm cannot be constructed.
Work out $2+3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two shorter sides have to reach across the longest side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2+3=5$, and the longest side is 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2+3=5$, which is less than 6. The two short sides cannot reach across the longest
side, so the arcs in the construction would never meet. No such triangle exists.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A right-angled triangle is constructed with a hypotenuse of 10 cm and one
other side of 6 cm. Work out the third side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Pythagoras' theorem: the hypotenuse squared minus the known side squared.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10^2-6^2=100-36$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$10^2-6^2=100-36=64$, and $\sqrt{64}=8$. The third side is 8 cm. This is
Construction 13: the hypotenuse and one side fix the triangle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** Describe how to construct an angle of 60° without a protractor or set square.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An equilateral triangle has three angles of 60°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Build an equilateral triangle on a ray.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw a ray from $A$. With centre $A$, draw an arc that cuts the ray at $B$. With
centre $B$ and the same radius, draw an arc that cuts the first arc at $C$. Join
$A$ to $C$. The triangle $ABC$ is equilateral, so angle $BAC$ is 60°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Write down the first five multiples of 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply 6 by 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6\times1=6$, $6\times2=12$, and so on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$6, 12, 18, 24, 30$.

Multiples return when a construction divides a segment into equal parts, because
the parts are equal steps along the segment.

</details>
</details>
</details>
