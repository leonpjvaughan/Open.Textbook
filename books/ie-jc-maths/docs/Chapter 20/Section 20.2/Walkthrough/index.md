# 20.2 The constructions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** using a straight edge, a compass and a ruler, and measuring a
segment in centimetres.

**Objectives:** name the instruments and what each is allowed to do; carry out the
constructions in the course; describe a construction in order; say why a
construction gives the result it claims.

By the end of this section you can construct the figures the course names, and you
can explain each construction in words.

## The instruments

A construction is a drawing made with the instruments the course allows, in a
fixed order of steps. The instruments are:

- a **straight edge**, which draws a straight line through two marked points;
- a **compass**, which draws a circle with a given centre through a given point,
  and copies a length from one place to another;
- a **ruler**, which measures a length and marks a point at a given distance;
- a **protractor**, which measures an angle and marks a point at a given angle;
- **set squares**, which draw right angles and angles of 30°, 60° and 45°.

A construction is not a measurement. When a construction says "bisect this angle",
the bisector must come out exact, from the compass alone. A protractor would give an
answer that is only as good as your eye.

## The constructions in the course

The course names fifteen constructions. They are the ones you may be asked to
perform.

| Number | Construction |
|---|---|
| 1 | Bisector of a given angle |
| 2 | Perpendicular bisector of a segment |
| 3 | Line perpendicular to a given line, through a point not on it |
| 4 | Line perpendicular to a given line, through a point on it |
| 5 | Line parallel to a given line, through a given point |
| 6 | Division of a segment into 2 or 3 equal parts |
| 7 | Division of a segment into any number of equal parts |
| 8 | Line segment of a given length on a given ray |
| 9 | Angle of a given number of degrees with a given ray as one arm |
| 10 | Triangle, given the lengths of three sides |
| 11 | Triangle, given two sides and the included angle |
| 12 | Triangle, given two angles and the included side |

| 14 | Right-angled triangle, given one side and one acute angle |
| 15 | Rectangle, given the side lengths |

Constructions 3 and 7 are for Higher level. The rest are for everyone.

## Bisecting an angle

To bisect an angle means to divide it into two equal angles.

**Example 1.** Bisect the angle $BAC$.

1. With centre $A$, draw an arc that cuts both arms of the angle. Call the cut
   points $P$ and $Q$.
2. With centre $P$, and a compass width more than half of $PQ$, draw an arc inside
   the angle.
3. With centre $Q$, and the **same** compass width, draw an arc that crosses the
   one from step 2. Call the crossing point $R$.
4. Draw the ray from $A$ through $R$. This ray is the bisector.

The two arcs in steps 2 and 3 must use the same compass width. That is what makes
$R$ the same distance from $P$ as from $Q$, and that is why the bisector comes out
exact.

If the angle is 74°, the bisector makes two angles of $74\div2=37°$.

## The perpendicular bisector

The **perpendicular bisector** of a segment is the line through its midpoint at a
right angle to it. Every point on it is the same distance from the two ends of the
segment.

**Example 2.** Construct the perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$.

1. Open the compass to more than half of $[AB]$.
2. With centre $A$, draw an arc above the segment and an arc below it.
3. With centre $B$, and the same compass width, draw arcs that cross the first two.
4. Draw the line through the two crossing points. That line is the perpendicular
   bisector.

If $[AB]$ is 10 cm long, the bisector meets it at 5 cm from $A$ and 5 cm from $B$.

## A parallel line, and a triangle

**Example 3.** Construct the line through a point $P$ parallel to a given line $l$
(Construction 5).

1. Draw any line through $P$ that cuts $l$. Call the crossing point $Q$.
2. Copy the angle that this line makes with $l$ at $Q$, but put the copy at $P$.
3. The line you draw at $P$ makes equal corresponding angles with the transversal,
   so it is parallel to $l$.

**Example 4.** Construct a triangle with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm (Construction
10).

1. Draw the longest side, 7 cm, as the base.
2. With centre at one end and radius 5 cm, draw an arc.
3. With centre at the other end and radius 6 cm, draw an arc that crosses the first.
4. Join the crossing point to both ends.

The three sides are 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm, so the triangle is fixed. A triangle can
be constructed only when the two shorter sides together are longer than the
longest. For 2 cm, 3 cm and 6 cm, $2+3=5$ is less than 6, so no triangle exists.

## Investigate the perpendicular bisector

**Predict.** Before you move the control, predict whether the points P and Q lie the
same distance from A as they do from B.

**Test.** Step through the construction with the slider and the two buttons. Watch the
compass width stay the same, and watch where the last line meets $[AB]$.

**Explain.** Write two or three sentences. Say why the two arcs must use the same
compass width. Use the words "distance", "midpoint" and "right angle".

> **Watch out.** In a construction, do not measure what the compass can copy. When
> you bisect an angle or a segment, the two arcs must use the same compass width,
> and you must not move the compass between them. Changing the width is the most
> common reason a construction comes out slightly wrong.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The proof that the angles of a triangle add to 180°
begins by drawing a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side.

Which construction draws that line? Name its number, and write the first two steps
in order. In Section 20.3 you will use the line to move two of the angles.

## You should now be able to

- name the five instruments and say what each is allowed to do;
- bisect an angle, and construct the perpendicular bisector of a segment;
- construct a line parallel to a given line through a given point;
- construct a triangle from three sides, two sides and the included angle, or two
  angles and the included side;
- describe a construction in order, and say why each step is needed;
- decide from the side lengths whether a triangle can be constructed.
