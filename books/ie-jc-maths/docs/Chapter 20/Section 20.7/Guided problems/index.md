# 20.7 Similar triangles and proportionality — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $12\div4$ and $8\times3$.
2. Work out $5\times3^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3$ and $24$. Dividing gives a scale factor; multiplying by it gives a
   matching side.
2. $3^2=9$, and $5\times9=45$. The square is the reason an area scales by the
   square of the scale factor.

Both skills return here. Every similar-triangle question is either a scale factor
or a missing side found with one.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Two triangles are similar. A side of 5 cm in the small triangle matches a
side of 15 cm in the large one. Work out the scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the large side by the matching small side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $15\div5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$15\div5=3$. The scale factor is 3, so every side of the small triangle is
multiplied by 3.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two triangles are similar. A side of 4 cm in the small triangle matches a
side of 12 cm in the large one. Another side of the small triangle is 5 cm. Find
the matching side in the large triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the scale factor first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$12\div4=3$, so multiply 5 by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The scale factor is $12\div4=3$, so the matching side is $5\times3=15$ cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Two similar triangles have $\dfrac{3}{5}=\dfrac{9}{x}$. Find $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Cross-multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3x=5\times9=45$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$3x=45$, so $x=45\div3=15$. In similar triangles the matching sides are in the
same ratio.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two similar triangles have matching sides in the ratio $3:4$. A side of the
small triangle is 9 cm. Find the matching side of the large one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The scale factor is $\dfrac{4}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $9\times\dfrac{4}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$9\times\dfrac{4}{3}=12$ cm. The matching side of the large triangle is 12 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A vertical pole 2 m tall casts a shadow 3 m long. At the same time a tree
casts a shadow 12 m long. The triangles are similar. Find the height of the tree.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the ratio $\dfrac{\text{height}}{\text{shadow}}$ for each.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{h}{12}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$\dfrac{2}{3}=\dfrac{h}{12}$, so $h=12\times2\div3=8$ m. The tree is 8 m tall.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Two triangles are similar with scale factor 4. The area of the small
triangle is 6 cm². Work out the area of the large one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Areas scale by the square of the scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $6\times4^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$4^2=16$, and $6\times16=96$ cm². The area of the large triangle is 96 cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why two triangles are similar if two of their angles are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the triangle angle sum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the third angle of each triangle by subtracting from 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angles of a triangle add to 180°. If two angles of one triangle equal two
angles of another, subtracting each pair from 180° gives equal third angles. All
three angles match, so the triangles are similar. This is why two angles are
enough.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** In triangle $ABC$, a line parallel to $BC$ cuts $AB$ at $D$ and $AC$ at
$E$. If $|AD|=5$ cm, $|DB|=7$ cm and $|AE|=10$ cm, find $|EC|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A parallel line cuts the two sides in the same ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{5}{7}=\dfrac{10}{|EC|}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$\dfrac{5}{7}=\dfrac{10}{|EC|}$, so $5\times|EC|=70$ and $|EC|=14$ cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** Prove that if two triangles are similar, their sides are proportional in
order.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Place the smaller triangle on the larger one so that a corner and a side match.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The matching angles are equal, so the third side of the small triangle is parallel
to the matching side of the large one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Place the smaller triangle on the larger one so that one corner and one side
match. The matching angles are equal, so the third side of the small triangle is
parallel to the matching side of the large one. A line parallel to one side cuts
the other two sides in the same ratio, so every pair of matching sides is in the
same ratio. The sides are proportional in order.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** In a parallelogram, one angle is 80°. Work out the angle next to it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Adjacent angles of a parallelogram add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 80 from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-80=100°$. Adjacent angles add to 180°.

The parallelogram result from Section 20.6 is a good reminder that equal angles
and equal sides are different claims, and each needs its own reason.

</details>
</details>
</details>
