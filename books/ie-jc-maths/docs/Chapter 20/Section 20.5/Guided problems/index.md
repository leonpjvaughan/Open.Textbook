# 20.5 The isosceles triangle and its converse — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Which congruence test needs two sides and the angle between them?
2. Work out $180-2\times58$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. **SAS**, two sides and the included angle. It is the test used to prove the
   isosceles theorem.
2. $2\times58=116$, and $180-116=64$.

Both skills return here. The theorem is proved with SAS, and the apex angle is
found by subtracting the two base angles from 180.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 50°. Work out each base angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two base angles are equal, and all three angles add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 50 from 180, then halve.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-50=130$, and $130\div2=65°$. Each base angle is 65°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** An isosceles triangle has base angles of 72°. Work out the apex angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two base angles are equal, so double 72 and subtract from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2\times72=144$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2\times72=144$, and $180-144=36°$. The apex angle is 36°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** An equilateral triangle has three equal sides. Work out each angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An equilateral triangle is isosceles in three ways, so all three angles are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide 180 by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180\div3=60°$. All three angles are 60°, because they are equal and they add to
180°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A right-angled triangle is also isosceles. Work out each acute angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The right angle is 90°, and the two acute angles are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 90 from 180, then halve.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The two equal acute angles add to $180-90=90°$. Each is $90\div2=45°$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** An isosceles triangle has equal sides of 12 cm and a base of 7 cm. Work out
its perimeter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two sides are 12 cm, and the base is 7 cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $12+12+7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$12+12+7=31$ cm. The two equal sides are both 12 cm, because the triangle is
isosceles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Prove that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare triangle $ABC$ with triangle $ACB$, and use SAS.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$|AB|=|AC|$, $|AC|=|AB|$, and the angle at $A$ is common.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $ABC$ have $|AB|=|AC|$. Then triangle $ABC$ is congruent to triangle $ACB$ by
SAS: $|AB|=|AC|$, $|AC|=|AB|$, and the angle at $A$ is the same angle in both.
Matching angles are equal, so the angle at $B$ equals the angle at $C$. The base
angles are equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Prove the converse: if two angles of a triangle are equal, the triangle is
isosceles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare triangle $ABC$ with triangle $ACB$, and use ASA.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle at $B$ equals the angle at $C$, the angle at $C$ equals the angle at
$B$, and the side $BC$ is common.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the angle at $B$ equal the angle at $C$. Then triangle $ABC$ is congruent to
triangle $ACB$ by ASA: the two angles at $B$ and $C$ match, and the side $BC$ is
common. Matching sides are equal, so $|AB|=|AC|$. The triangle is isosceles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A triangle has two equal angles of 64°. Work out the third angle, and say
whether the triangle is isosceles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The third angle is $180-2\times64$. Then use the converse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two angles are equal, so by the converse the sides opposite them are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-2\times64=180-128=52°$. Two angles are equal, so the sides opposite them are
equal. The triangle is isosceles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 96°. Work out each base angle
and say why they are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 96 from 180, then halve.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$180-96=84$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-96=84$, and $84\div2=42°$. The two base angles are equal because they are
opposite the two equal sides, by the isosceles theorem.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** A triangle has angles of 47° and 63°. Work out the third angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angles of a triangle add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$47+63=110$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$47+63=110$, and $180-110=70°$. The third angle is 70°.

This is the triangle angle sum from Section 20.3, and it is the reason the base
angles of an isosceles triangle can be found at all.

</details>
</details>
