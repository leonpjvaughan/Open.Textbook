# 20.5 The isosceles triangle and its converse — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the congruence tests from Section 20.4, and the triangle angle
sum from Section 20.3.

**Objectives:** prove that the base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal;
prove the converse; use both results to find angles and sides.

By the end of this section you can recognise an isosceles triangle from its sides
or from its angles, and you can prove both directions of the result.

## The parts of an isosceles triangle

An **isosceles triangle** has two sides of equal length. The two equal sides are
the **legs**, and the third side is the **base**. The angle between the two legs
is the **apex angle**. The other two angles are the **base angles**.

The two base angles sit opposite the two equal sides. That is the clue to the
whole section.

## The theorem, and its proof

**Theorem.** In an isosceles triangle, the angles opposite the equal sides are
equal.

**Proof.** Let $ABC$ have $|AB|=|AC|$. Compare triangle $ABC$ with triangle
$ACB$.

- $|AB|=|AC|$, from the given information.
- $|AC|=|AB|$, which is the same fact the other way round.
- The angle at $A$ is the same angle in both triangles, so it is equal to itself.

Two sides and the included angle match, so the two triangles are congruent by
SAS. Matching angles in congruent triangles are equal, so the angle at $B$ equals
the angle at $C$. The base angles are equal.

**Example 1.** An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 40°. Work out each base
angle.

The two base angles are equal. They and the apex angle add to 180°, so the two
base angles together are $180-40=140°$. Each is $140\div2=70°$.

**Example 2.** An isosceles triangle has base angles of 65°. Work out the apex
angle.

$$2\times65=130 \qquad 180-130=50$$

The apex angle is **50°**.

## The converse

The converse of the theorem is also true, and it is proved separately.

**Theorem (converse).** If two angles of a triangle are equal, the triangle is
isosceles.

**Proof.** Let $ABC$ have the angle at $B$ equal to the angle at $C$. Compare
triangle $ABC$ with triangle $ACB$.

- The angle at $B$ equals the angle at $C$, from the given information.
- The angle at $C$ equals the angle at $B$, the same fact the other way round.
- The side $BC$ is common to both triangles.

Two angles and the included side match, so the triangles are congruent by ASA.
Matching sides are equal, so $|AB|=|AC|$. The triangle is isosceles.

The two results are different statements, and each needs its own proof. A true
theorem does not always have a true converse, as Section 20.1 showed.

## The equilateral triangle

An **equilateral triangle** has three equal sides. It is isosceles in three
different ways, so all three of its angles are equal. The three angles add to
180°, so each is $180\div3=60°$.

That gives a useful construction fact: an equilateral triangle is how you make an
angle of 60° with compass and straight edge alone.

## A right-angled isosceles triangle

Sometimes a triangle is both right-angled and isosceles. The right angle is 90°,
so the two equal acute angles add to $180-90=90°$. Each is $90\div2=45°$.

**Example 3.** A right-angled triangle is also isosceles. Work out each acute
angle.

$$180-90=90 \qquad 90\div2=45$$

Each acute angle is **45°**.

**Example 4.** An isosceles triangle has equal sides of 8 cm and a base of 5 cm.
Work out its perimeter.

$$8+8+5=21$$

The perimeter is **21 cm**.

> **Watch out.** The equal angles are the ones **opposite** the equal sides, and
> the equal sides are opposite the equal angles. Read the question to see whether
> you are given the apex angle or a base angle, and label the triangle before you
> calculate. Mixing up the apex angle with a base angle is the most common error
> here.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The proof that the angles of a triangle add to 180°
is now available, and in this section you used it inside a proof of your own.

An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of $x$ and two equal base angles. Write
down an expression for one base angle in terms of $x$. Then say what this
expression tells you when $x=60$. You will meet this triangle again in the
Review.

## You should now be able to

- name the legs, the base, the apex angle and the base angles of an isosceles
  triangle;
- prove that the base angles are equal;
- prove the converse, that equal angles make the triangle isosceles;
- use the two results to find a missing angle;
- find the angles of an equilateral triangle and of a right-angled isosceles
  triangle;
- find the perimeter of an isosceles triangle.
