# 20.6 Parallelograms and their corollaries — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $180-2\times40$.
2. The angles of a triangle add to 180°. What do the angles of two triangles add
   to?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2\times40=80$, and $180-80=100$. An isosceles triangle with an apex angle of
   40° has two base angles of 50° each.
2. $2\times180=360$. Splitting a quadrilateral into two triangles is how the
   quadrilateral angle sum is proved.

Both facts return here. A parallelogram is split into two triangles, and its
adjacent angles are found by subtracting from 180.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** In a parallelogram, one angle is 65°. Write down the opposite angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the angle opposite the 65° angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The opposite angle is **65°**. In a parallelogram, opposite angles are equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** In a parallelogram, one angle is 65°. Work out the angle next to it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Adjacent angles of a parallelogram add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 65 from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-65=115°$. Adjacent angles add to 180°, because the opposite sides are
parallel and the angles are interior on the same side of a transversal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** In a parallelogram, one side is 13 cm. Write down the length of the
opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The opposite side has the same length as the given side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

**13 cm.** In a parallelogram, opposite sides are equal in length.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A parallelogram has sides of 12 cm and 8 cm. Work out its perimeter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are two sides of 12 cm and two of 8 cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $2\times(12+8)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2\times(12+8)=2\times20=40$ cm. The two pairs of opposite sides are equal, so the
perimeter is $12+8+12+8$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A parallelogram has one angle of 118°. Work out its four angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One angle is 118°, the opposite angle is 118°, and the other two add to 180° with
it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$180-118=62$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The four angles are 118°, 62°, 118° and 62°. Opposite angles are equal, and
adjacent angles add to 180°. As a check, $2\times118+2\times62=236+124=360°$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Prove that the opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw one diagonal and find two congruent triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the alternate angles made by the parallel sides, and the common diagonal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram, and draw the diagonal $BD$. Because $AB$ is
parallel to $DC$, the alternate angles $\angle ABD$ and $\angle BDC$ are equal.
Because $AD$ is parallel to $BC$, the alternate angles $\angle ADB$ and
$\angle DBC$ are equal. The diagonal $BD$ is common, so triangles $DAB$ and $BCD$
are congruent by ASA. Matching sides give $|AB|=|CD|$ and $|AD|=|CB|$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Prove that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect one another.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Let the diagonals meet at $E$, and find two congruent triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the alternate angles at $E$, and the equal opposite sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the diagonals $AC$ and $BD$ meet at $E$. Because $AD$ is parallel to $BC$, the
alternate angles give $\angle EAD=\angle ECB$ and $\angle EDA=\angle EBC$.
Opposite sides are equal, so $|AD|=|BC|$. Triangles $ADE$ and $CBE$ are congruent
by ASA. Matching sides give $|AE|=|EC|$ and $|DE|=|EB|$, so the diagonals bisect
one another.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A diagonal divides a parallelogram of area 72 cm² into two triangles. Work
out the area of one triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diagonal makes two congruent triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Halve 72.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$72\div2=36$ cm². Congruent triangles have equal areas.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** A rhombus has four equal sides of 9 cm. Work out its perimeter, and say
whether it is a parallelogram.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both pairs of opposite sides are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the converse of the theorem on opposite sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$4\times9=36$ cm. A rhombus has both pairs of opposite sides equal, so by the
converse of Theorem 9 it is a parallelogram. Its opposite sides are equal and
parallel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** An isosceles triangle has base angles of 55°. Work out the apex angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Double 55 and subtract from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2\times55=110$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2\times55=110$, and $180-110=70°$. The apex angle is 70°.

The isosceles theorem from Section 20.5 is used again inside the parallelogram
proofs, because the diagonal makes isosceles triangles in a rhombus.

</details>
</details>
</details>
