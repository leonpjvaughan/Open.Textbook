# Chapter 20: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**Why do the angles of a triangle add to 180°?**

Take any triangle $ABC$. Draw the line through $A$ parallel to $BC$.

The side $AB$ is a transversal of the two parallel lines. The angle at $B$ and the
angle between $AB$ and the new line are alternate angles, so they are equal. Call
that angle $x$.

The side $AC$ is also a transversal. The angle at $C$ and the angle between $AC$
and the new line are alternate angles, so they are equal. Call that angle $y$.

The three angles at $A$ now sit on the new straight line: the angle $x$, the angle
of the triangle at $A$, and the angle $y$. Angles on a straight line add to 180°,
so

$$x+|\angle A|+y=180°$$

Replace $x$ by the angle at $B$ and $y$ by the angle at $C$:

$$|\angle B|+|\angle A|+|\angle C|=180°$$

**The three angles of the triangle add to 180°.**

Nothing in the argument used the shape of the triangle, so it holds for every
triangle. That is what makes it a proof rather than a check.

Every step of this proof stands on something already accepted:

1. The line through $A$ parallel to $BC$ exists and is unique — Axiom 5.
2. Equal alternate angles — the parallel-line fact, from the same axiom.
3. Angles on a straight line add to 180° — Axiom 3.
4. The conclusion — the triangle angle sum, now proved.

This is the tower of geometry. Each level is built only from the levels below it.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and
say why in a few words. The idea might be vertically opposite angles, angles on a
straight line, alternate angles, the triangle angle sum, the exterior angle, a
congruence test, the isosceles theorem, a parallelogram result, a similarity
ratio, Pythagoras or its converse, a circle theorem, or a counterexample.

1. Two straight lines cross. One angle is 124°. Work out the other three angles.
2. A triangle has angles of 39° and 58°. Work out the third angle.
3. A transversal cuts two parallel lines. One co-interior angle is 108°. Work out
   the other co-interior angle.
4. In a triangle, the two interior opposite angles are 37° and 56°. Work out the
   exterior angle at the third corner.
5. Two triangles each have two sides of 7 cm and 10 cm, and the angle between
   those sides is 48° in each. Which congruence test applies?
6. An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 84°. Work out each base angle.
7. A parallelogram has one angle of 105°. Work out its four angles.
8. Two triangles are similar. A side of 6 cm in the small triangle matches a side
   of 18 cm in the large one. Another side of the small triangle is 7 cm. Find the
   matching side in the large one.
9. A right-angled triangle has legs of 9 cm and 40 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
10. A triangle has sides of 10 cm, 24 cm and 26 cm. Decide whether it is
    right-angled.
11. Prove that the angles of a quadrilateral add to 360°.
12. A student claims: "if a number is divisible by 6, then it is divisible by 9."
    Test the number 12 and decide whether the claim is true.

## What you can now do

- say what an axiom, a definition, a theorem, a proof and a corollary are, and
  tell them apart;
- write the converse of a statement, and test it with a counterexample;
- carry out the constructions of the course in order, and explain each step;
- find missing angles from the parallel-line facts, the triangle angle sum and the
  exterior angle;
- decide when two triangles are congruent, and name the test;
- use and prove the isosceles theorem and its converse;
- use and prove the parallelogram results, including the diagonals;
- use similar triangles and a scale factor to find a missing side;
- state and prove Pythagoras' theorem, and use its converse;
- use the circle theorems, including the angle in a semicircle;
- build a proof step by step, and judge whether a proof is sound.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you wrote down one geometrical fact you use often
without being able to say why it is true.

Take your sentence out. Write the reason underneath it now. If the reason needs a
theorem, name the theorem. If the reason needs a proof, write the proof.

If your fact was the triangle angle sum, you have the proof above. If it was
something else, check whether this chapter proved it. If it did not, write down
which section would, and what is still missing. Knowing the shape of the gap is
part of understanding the proof.
