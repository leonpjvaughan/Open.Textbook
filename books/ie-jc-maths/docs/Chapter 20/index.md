# Chapter 20: Synthetic geometry, constructions and proof

Synthetic geometry means geometry built by reasoning, not by measuring. You start
from a small set of statements everyone accepts, and you work upwards one honest
step at a time. Nothing enters the tower without a reason.

This chapter is where the geometry you have used for years is proved. You meet
the axioms and the theorems, the constructions that go with them, and the
arguments that show why each one is true.

## The chapter problem

**Why do the angles of a triangle add to 180°?**

You have used this fact since primary school. You have added two angles together
and subtracted the total from 180, without stopping to ask where the 180 comes
from. This chapter proves it.

The proof draws one line through a corner, parallel to the opposite side. It uses
a result about parallel lines and a fact about a straight line. Nothing else. By
the end of the chapter you can write that proof out yourself, and you can say why
each step is allowed.

**First contact.** Draw a triangle on a page. Tear off the three corners and place
the three angles side by side along a straight edge. Look at what they make, and
write down what you see. Do not calculate anything yet.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- say what an axiom, a definition, a theorem, a proof and a corollary are, and
  tell them apart;
- write the converse of a statement, and test it with a counterexample;
- name the constructions of the course, carry them out in order, and explain each
  step;
- use and prove the angle facts for parallel lines and triangles;
- decide when two triangles are congruent, and say which test you used;
- use and prove the isosceles theorem and its converse;
- use and prove the results about parallelograms and their diagonals;
- use similar triangles, and find a missing side from a ratio;
- state and prove the theorem of Pythagoras, and use its converse;
<!-- tier:h -->
- use and prove the circle theorems, including the angle in a semicircle;
<!-- /tier:h -->
- build a proof step by step, and judge whether someone else's proof is sound.

## Where it is used

Construction, design, and the reasoning behind every geometrical fact you use.

An engineer checks that a frame is square by measuring the diagonals, because
equal diagonals in a quadrilateral force a rectangle. A carpenter marks a right
angle with a 3-4-5 triangle, which works because of the converse of Pythagoras. A
surveyor reasons about parallel lines and equal angles to draw a map. A designer
uses the constructions to divide a space into equal parts without measuring every
length.

The same reasoning shows up far from geometry. Whenever you test a claim by
looking for one example that breaks it, you are using a counterexample. Whenever
you say why something is true rather than only what is true, you are writing a
proof.

## Your turn to connect it

Which geometrical fact do you use most often without being able to say why it is
true?

Write it down now, in one sentence. It might be the triangle angle sum, or the
fact that the angles on a straight line add to 180°, or the result that a
rectangle has equal diagonals. Keep your sentence. At the end of the chapter you
will come back to it and write the reason.
