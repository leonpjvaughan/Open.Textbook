# 20.4 Congruent triangles — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $180-65-48$.
2. A transversal cuts two parallel lines. One alternate angle is 71°. Write down
   the other alternate angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $65+48=113$, and $180-113=67$. The angles of a triangle add to 180°.
2. $71°$. Alternate angles are equal when the lines are parallel.

Both facts come back here. A congruence proof finds a third angle by subtracting
from 180, and it finds equal angles from a parallel line.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Triangles $ABC$ and $PQR$ are congruent, with $A$ matching $P$, $B$
matching $Q$ and $C$ matching $R$. If $|BC|=11$ cm, write down $|QR|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The order of the letters gives the matching parts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$QR$ matches $BC$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$|QR|=11$ cm. In congruent triangles, matching sides are equal, and $QR$ matches
$BC$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two triangles are congruent. In the first, the angles are 35°, 65° and 80°.
In the second, two angles are 35° and 65°. Work out the third angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Matching angles are equal, and the angles of a triangle add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 35 and 65, then subtract from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$35+65=100$, and $180-100=80°$. The third angle is 80°, matching the 80° angle of
the first triangle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Two triangles each have sides of 7 cm, 9 cm and 12 cm. Which congruence
test applies, and why?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at how many pairs of sides are given.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

All three pairs of sides are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

**SSS.** All three pairs of matching sides are equal, so Axiom 4 makes the
triangles congruent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two triangles each have two sides of 5 cm and 11 cm, and the angle between
those sides is 62° in each. Which congruence test applies, and why?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Is the given angle between the two given sides?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Yes. So the angle is the included angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

**SAS.** Two sides and the angle between them are given, so the angle is included.
Axiom 4 makes the triangles congruent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two triangles each have two angles of 45° and 85°, and the side between
them is 10 cm in each. Which congruence test applies, and why?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Is the given side between the two given angles?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Yes. So the side is the included side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

**ASA.** Two angles and the side between them are given, so the side is included.
Axiom 4 makes the triangles congruent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Two right-angled triangles each have a hypotenuse of 15 cm and one other
side of 9 cm. Which test applies, and what is the third side in each?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Pythagoras' theorem to find the third side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$15^2-9^2=225-81=144$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

**RHS.** Both triangles have a right angle, the hypotenuses are equal, and one
other side is equal. Pythagoras gives the third side:
$\sqrt{225-81}=\sqrt{144}=12$ cm in each. The triangles are congruent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Two triangles each have sides 3 cm and 7 cm, and an angle of 40° that is
not between those sides. Explain why this does not prove them congruent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Is the 40° angle the included angle?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

No. So the data do not fix the shape.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The 40° angle is not between the two given sides, so the data are not SAS. The
third side can swing to two positions and still make the given angle, so two
different triangles fit the data. They are not congruent, and the test fails.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A triangle has sides of 6 cm, 8 cm and 11 cm. A second triangle is
congruent to it. Work out the perimeter of the second triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Congruent triangles have equal matching sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the three sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$6+8+11=25$ cm. The second triangle has the same three side lengths, so it has the
same perimeter.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** Two congruent triangles each have base 12 cm and height 7 cm. Work out the
area of one of them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area of a triangle is half the base times the height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $12\times7$, then halve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$\dfrac{12\times7}{2}=42$ cm². Congruent triangles have equal areas.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** A triangle has sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm. Check that the triangle can be
constructed by adding the two shorter sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two sides of a triangle are together greater than the third.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 5 and 6, and compare the total with 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$5+6=11$, which is greater than 7. The triangle can be constructed. If the total
had been less than 7, the arcs would not have met.

</details>
</details>
</details>
