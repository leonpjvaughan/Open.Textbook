# 20.4 Congruent triangles — Learn

**Prerequisites:** naming the sides and angles of a triangle, and the tests from
Section 20.1.

**Objectives:** say when two triangles are congruent; use SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS;
explain why SSA is not a test; match the sides and angles of congruent triangles.

By the end of this section you can decide which test applies to a pair of
triangles, and you can write down every matching side and angle.

## Congruent means the same in every way

Two triangles are **congruent** when one fits exactly on the other. Every side of
one matches a side of the other, and every angle matches too. The two triangles
have the same shape and the same size.

When you write that two triangles are congruent, the order of the letters tells
the reader which parts match. If triangle $ABC$ is congruent to triangle $DEF$,
then:

- $A$ matches $D$, $B$ matches $E$ and $C$ matches $F$;
- $|AB|=|DE|$, $|BC|=|EF|$ and $|AC|=|DF|$;
- the angle at $A$ equals the angle at $D$, and so on.

So you do not need to list every matching part. The order does that work.

**Example 1.** Triangles $ABC$ and $DEF$ are congruent, with $A$ matching $D$,
$B$ matching $E$ and $C$ matching $F$. If $|AB|=7$ cm, write down $|DE|$.

$|DE|=7$ cm, because $DE$ matches $AB$ and matching sides are equal.

## The four tests

You do not have to check all six parts to know two triangles are congruent. The
course gives four tests, and any one of them is enough.

| Test | What you need |
|---|---|
| **SSS** | the three pairs of matching sides are equal |
| **SAS** | two pairs of matching sides are equal, and the angle **between** them is equal |
| **ASA** | two pairs of matching angles are equal, and the side **between** them is equal |
| **RHS** | both triangles have a right angle, the hypotenuses are equal, and one other pair of sides is equal |

SSS, SAS and ASA are Axiom 4 of the course. You use them without proving them.

RHS is different. It is a theorem, and it follows from the other tests.

In a right-angled triangle, Pythagoras' theorem gives the third side from the
hypotenuse and one other side.

:::prove pythagoras

So if two right-angled triangles have equal hypotenuses and one equal side, their
third sides are equal as well. Both triangles then have three pairs of equal
sides, and SSS makes them congruent. That is why RHS is a valid test.

**Example 2.** Two triangles each have sides of 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. Which test
shows they are congruent?

**SSS.** All three pairs of matching sides are equal.

**Example 3.** Two triangles each have two sides of 4 cm and 9 cm, and the angle
between those sides is 55° in each. Which test applies?

**SAS.** The 55° angle lies between the two given sides, so it is the included
angle.

**Example 4.** Two triangles each have two angles of 50° and 70°, and the side
between them is 8 cm in each. Which test applies?

**ASA.** The 8 cm side lies between the two given angles, so it is the included
side.

## Why SSA is not a test

Suppose you know two sides and an angle, but the angle is **not** between the two
sides. This is sometimes written SSA. It does not prove congruence.

Two triangles each with sides 4 cm and 6 cm, and an angle of 30° that is not
between them, can be drawn in two different shapes. One of the two sides can
swing to two positions and still make the given angle. The two shapes are not
congruent, so the data do not fix the triangle.

The lesson is short: **the angle must be included**. That is what makes SAS a
test and SSA a trap.

> **Watch out.** SAS needs the angle between the two sides, not any angle. If the
> angle is somewhere else in the triangle, you have SSA, and SSA does not work.
> Check where the angle sits before you name the test. RHS is the one other case
> that works, and it needs a right angle with the hypotenuse.

## Matching parts, area and perimeter

Congruent triangles have equal matching sides, so they have the same perimeter.
They also have the same area, because one fits exactly on the other.

**Example 5.** A triangle has sides of 5 cm, 7 cm and 9 cm. A second triangle is
congruent to it. Work out the perimeter of the second triangle.

$$5+7+9=21$$

The perimeter is 21 cm, the same as the first triangle.

**Example 6.** Two congruent triangles each have base 8 cm and height 5 cm. Work
out the area of one of them.

$$\frac{8\times5}{2}=20$$

The area is 20 cm².

## You should now be able to

- say what congruent means, and read matching parts from the order of the letters;
- use SSS, SAS, ASA and RHS to decide that two triangles are congruent;
- explain why SSA is not a congruence test;
- find the perimeter and the area of a triangle congruent to a given one.
