# 20.9 Circles and their theorems — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. An isosceles triangle has two equal base angles of 35°. Work out the apex
   angle.
2. Work out $2\times38$ and $180\div2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2\times35=70$, and $180-70=110°$. The equal base angles are opposite the two
   equal radii in a circle.
2. $76$ and $90$. Doubling gives an angle at the centre, and halving 180 gives the
   angle in a semicircle.

Both facts return here. The angle-at-the-centre theorem is an isosceles-triangle
proof, and the angle in a semicircle is half of 180°.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** The angle at the circumference standing on an arc is 42°. Work out the
angle at the centre standing on the same arc.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $2\times42$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2\times42=84°$. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference
when both stand on the same arc.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** The angle at the centre standing on an arc is 146°. Work out the angle at
the circumference standing on the same arc.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Halve the angle at the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $146\div2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$146\div2=73°$. The circumference angle is half the centre angle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** $BC$ is a diameter of a circle and $A$ is another point on the circle. Work
out the angle $BAC$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle at the centre standing on the diameter is 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180\div2=90°$. Each angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A cyclic quadrilateral has an angle of 104°. Work out the opposite angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 104 from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-104=76°$. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are supplementary.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two angles at the circumference stand on the same arc, on the same side of
the chord. One is 63°. Write down the other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Angles standing on the same arc are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The other angle has the same size.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

**63°.** All angles at points of the circle standing on the same arc are equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Prove that the angle at the centre standing on an arc is twice the angle at
the circumference standing on the same arc.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Join the point at the circumference to the centre and continue the line to the far
side of the circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two isosceles triangles appear, and the exterior angle theorem gives twice each
base angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Join $B$ to the centre $O$, and continue the line to $D$. Since $|OA|=|OB|$, both
radii, triangle $OAB$ is isosceles, so $|\angle OAB|=|\angle ABO|$. The exterior
angle theorem gives
$|\angle AOD|=|\angle OAB|+|\angle ABO|=2|\angle ABO|$. In the same way
$|\angle COD|=2|\angle CBO|$. Adding,
$|\angle AOC|=2|\angle ABO|+2|\angle CBO|=2|\angle ABC|$. The angle at the centre
is twice the angle at the circumference.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Prove that each angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the angle-at-the-centre theorem with a diameter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle at the centre standing on the diameter is 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $BC$ be a diameter and $A$ another point of the circle. The angle at the centre
standing on the arc $BC$ is the straight angle $BOC$, which is 180°. The angle at
the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, so
$|\angle BAC|=180°\div2=90°$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Prove that in a cyclic quadrilateral the opposite angles add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the two angles at the centre standing on the two arcs cut off by a pair of
opposite sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Those two centre angles add to 360°, and each quadrilateral angle is half of one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Take a pair of opposite angles of the cyclic quadrilateral. The two angles at the
centre standing on the arcs they cut off add to 360°, because together they make a
full turn. Each angle of the quadrilateral is half the angle at the centre
standing on the same arc. So the two opposite angles add to half of 360°, which is
180°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** A triangle has its three corners on a circle of radius 8 cm, and one side is
a diameter. Work out the length of that side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A diameter is twice the radius.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $2\times8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2\times8=16$ cm. The angle opposite the diameter is 90°, by the angle in a
semicircle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 cm and one leg of 12 cm.
Find the other leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the square of the leg from the square of the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$13^2-12^2=169-144=25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$169-144=25$, and $\sqrt{25}=5$ cm. The other leg is 5 cm.

Pythagoras from Section 20.8 returns whenever a circle question gives a right
angle and two lengths.

</details>
</details>
</details>
