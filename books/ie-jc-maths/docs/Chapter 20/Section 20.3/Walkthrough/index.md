# 20.3 Angles and parallel lines — Learn

**Prerequisites:** measuring an angle in degrees, adding and subtracting whole
numbers, and the vocabulary of Section 20.1.

**Objectives:** use vertically opposite angles and angles on a straight line; use
the angle facts for parallel lines; prove the triangle angle sum; use the
exterior angle of a triangle.

By the end of this section you can find a missing angle from the facts of the
course, and you can prove the result the whole chapter is built around.

## Angles at a crossing

Two straight lines cross at a point. They make four angles, and those four angles
are linked.

The two angles opposite each other at the crossing are **vertically opposite**.
They are equal.

**Example 1.** Two straight lines cross. One angle is 130°. Work out the other
three.

The angle vertically opposite the 130° angle is also 130°. The other two angles
sit on a straight line with it, so each is $180-130=50°$.

The four angles are 130°, 50°, 130° and 50°.

Why are vertically opposite angles equal? The two angles on a straight line add
to 180°. The 130° angle and the angle above it add to 180°. The other 130° angle
and that same angle above it also add to 180°. Both sums are 180°, so the two
130° angles must be equal. That is a proof, and it uses only the straight-line
fact.

## Parallel lines and a transversal

A **transversal** is a line that crosses two other lines. When the two lines are
parallel, three angle facts follow.

- **Corresponding angles** are equal. These are the angles in matching positions
  at the two crossings, in an F shape.
- **Alternate angles** are equal. These are the angles on opposite sides of the
  transversal, between the two lines, in a Z shape.
- **Co-interior angles** add to 180°. These are the two angles between the two
  lines on the same side of the transversal, in a C shape.

**Example 2.** A transversal cuts two parallel lines. An alternate angle is 68°.
Write down the corresponding angle and the co-interior angle.

The corresponding angle is **68°**, because corresponding angles are equal. The
co-interior angle is $180-68=112°$, because co-interior angles add to 180°.

These three facts are part of the course, and they come from the parallel axiom:
through a point not on a line, there is exactly one line parallel to it.

> **Watch out.** The parallel-line facts apply only when the two lines really are
> parallel. In a diagram, two lines can look parallel and not be. Use a fact about
> parallel lines only when the diagram states that the lines are parallel, or when
> you have proved it.

## The angles in a triangle add to 180°

You have used this result for years. Here is why it is true.

**Theorem.** The three interior angles of a triangle add to 180°.

:::prove angles-in-a-triangle

**Proof.** Take any triangle $ABC$. Draw the line through $A$ parallel to $BC$.

The side $AB$ is a transversal of the two parallel lines. The angle at $B$ and
the angle between $AB$ and the new line are alternate angles, so they are equal.

The side $AC$ is also a transversal. The angle at $C$ and the angle between $AC$
and the new line are alternate angles, so they are equal too.

The three angles at $A$ now sit on the new straight line. So they add to 180°.
Those three angles are the angle at $A$ of the triangle, and copies of the angles
at $B$ and at $C$. Therefore the three angles of the triangle add to 180°.

Nothing in the proof used the shape of the triangle. The same argument works for
every triangle, which is what makes it a proof and not a check.

**Example 3.** A triangle has angles of 65° and 72°. Work out the third angle.

$$65+72=137 \qquad 180-137=43$$

The third angle is **43°**.

## The exterior angle

Extend one side of a triangle. The angle between the extension and the next side
is an **exterior angle** of the triangle.

**Theorem.** An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior
opposite angles.

**Proof.** The exterior angle and the interior angle at the same corner lie on a
straight line, so together they add to 180°. The three interior angles also add to
180°. Subtract the shared interior angle from both sums, and the exterior angle
equals the sum of the other two interior angles.

**Example 4.** In a triangle, the two interior opposite angles are 48° and 63°.
Work out the exterior angle at the third corner.

$$48+63=111$$

The exterior angle is **111°**.

## A right-angled triangle

A right-angled triangle has one angle of 90°. The other two angles are acute, and
they add to $180-90=90°$.

**Example 5.** A right-angled triangle has an acute angle of 35°. Work out the
other acute angle.

$$90-35=55$$

The other acute angle is **55°**.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. You now have every piece of the proof that the
angles of a triangle add to 180°.

Write the proof out from memory. Start with the triangle $ABC$ and the line
through $A$ parallel to $BC$. Name the two pairs of alternate angles. Finish with
the straight line at $A$.

Keep your proof. Section 20.10 asks you to check it against a full model.

## You should now be able to

- use vertically opposite angles and angles on a straight line;
- find corresponding, alternate and co-interior angles at a transversal;
- prove that the angles of a triangle add to 180°;
- use the exterior angle of a triangle;
- find the acute angles of a right-angled triangle.
