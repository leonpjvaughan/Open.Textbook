# 20.3 Angles and parallel lines — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $180-47$ and $180-115$.
2. In Section 20.1 you met the five axioms. Which one says that a straight angle
   has 180°?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $133$ and $65$. You subtract from 180 whenever two angles lie on a straight
   line.
2. Axiom 3, the protractor axiom. It also says that angles can be added when they
   sit side by side.

Both facts return in this section. Every angle calculation here is either an
equal angle or a subtraction from 180.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Two straight lines cross. One of the four angles is 118°. Work out the
angle vertically opposite it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Vertically opposite angles are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the angle opposite the 118° angle at the crossing.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The vertically opposite angle is **118°**. Vertically opposite angles are equal,
because each of the two angles is supplementary to the same angle between them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two angles lie on a straight line. One of them is 63°. Work out the other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Angles on a straight line add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 63 from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-63=117°$. The two angles on a straight line add to 180°, so the missing angle
is 117°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A transversal cuts two parallel lines. One angle is 74°. Write down the
alternate angle and the co-interior angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Alternate angles are equal. Co-interior angles add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The alternate angle is 74°. For the co-interior angle, subtract from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The alternate angle is **74°**, because alternate angles are equal. The
co-interior angle is $180-74=106°$, because co-interior angles add to 180°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A triangle has angles of 58° and 79°. Work out the third angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three angles of a triangle add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the two known angles first, then subtract from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$58+79=137$, and $180-137=43°$. The third angle is 43°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** In a triangle, the two interior opposite angles are 52° and 71°. Work out
the exterior angle at the third corner.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 52 and 71.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$52+71=123°$. The exterior angle is 123°, the sum of the two interior opposite
angles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A right-angled triangle has an acute angle of 27°. Work out the other
acute angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two acute angles of a right-angled triangle add to 90°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 27 from 90.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$90-27=63°$. The third angle is 90°, so the two acute angles add to
$180-90=90°$. The other acute angle is 63°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Prove that vertically opposite angles are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the two angles on one straight line, and then the two angles on the other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Each of the two vertically opposite angles is supplementary to the same angle
between them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the two lines cross so that $A$ lies between $B$ and $C$, and also between $D$
and $E$. Then

$$|\angle BAD|+|\angle BAE|=180° \qquad |\angle CAE|+|\angle BAE|=180°$$

because each pair lies on a straight line. Both sums equal 180°, and the angle
$BAE$ is in both, so the other two angles are equal:

$$|\angle BAD|=|\angle CAE|$$

The vertically opposite angles are equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Prove that the angles in any triangle add to 180°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw a line through one corner parallel to the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the alternate angles at the two sides that meet that corner.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Take a triangle $ABC$. Draw the line through $A$ parallel to $BC$.

The side $AB$ is a transversal, so the alternate angles are equal: the angle at
$B$ equals the angle between $AB$ and the new line. The side $AC$ is also a
transversal, so the angle at $C$ equals the angle between $AC$ and the new line.

The three angles at $A$ now lie on the new straight line, so they add to 180°.
Those three angles are the angle at $A$ and copies of the angles at $B$ and $C$.
The three angles of the triangle add to 180°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** A transversal cuts two parallel lines. One interior angle on one side is
124°. Work out the interior angle on the same side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

These two angles are co-interior.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 124 from 180.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$180-124=56°$. The two interior angles on the same side of the transversal add to
180°, so the missing angle is 56°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Round 7.36 to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the second decimal digit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The digit after the first decimal is 6, which is 5 or more.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$7.4$. The hundredths digit is 6, so the tenths digit rounds up from 3 to 4.

Rounding returns when an angle is not a whole number.
