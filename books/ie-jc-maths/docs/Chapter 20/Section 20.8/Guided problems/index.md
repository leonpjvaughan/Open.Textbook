# 20.8 The theorem of Pythagoras as a theorem — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $6^2$, $8^2$ and $\sqrt{100}$.
2. Two similar triangles have scale factor 3. A side of the small triangle is
   4 cm. Find the matching side of the large one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $36$, $64$ and $10$. The proof of Pythagoras uses similar triangles and the
   squares of side lengths.
2. $4\times3=12$ cm. The proof of Pythagoras is a similar-triangle proof.

Both skills return here. The theorem is proved with similar triangles, and it is
used by squaring and square-rooting.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 3 cm and 4 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the squares of the two legs, then take the square root.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3^2+4^2=9+16=25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$9+16=25$, and $\sqrt{25}=5$. The hypotenuse is 5 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 5 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$5^2+12^2$ is a Pythagorean triple.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$25+144=169$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$25+144=169$, and $\sqrt{169}=13$. The hypotenuse is 13 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 26 cm and one leg of 24 cm.
Find the other leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the square of the leg from the square of the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$26^2-24^2=676-576=100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$676-576=100$, and $\sqrt{100}=10$. The other leg is 10 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A triangle has sides of 9 cm, 12 cm and 15 cm. Use the converse of
Pythagoras to decide whether it is right-angled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare $9^2+12^2$ with $15^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$81+144=225$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$9^2+12^2=81+144=225$, and $15^2=225$. The two values match, so by the converse
of Pythagoras the triangle is right-angled.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A triangle has sides of 6 cm, 7 cm and 8 cm. Use the converse of
Pythagoras to decide whether it is right-angled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare $6^2+7^2$ with $8^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$36+49=85$, and $64$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$6^2+7^2=36+49=85$, but $8^2=64$. The two values are different, so the triangle
is not right-angled.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A rectangle is 8 m by 15 m. Find the length of a diagonal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with legs 8 m and 15 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$8^2+15^2=64+225$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$64+225=289$, and $\sqrt{289}=17$ m. The diagonal is 17 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Prove Pythagoras' theorem using similar triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw the perpendicular from the right angle to the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two smaller triangles are each similar to the whole triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $ABC$ have a right angle at $B$, and draw $BD$ perpendicular to $AC$ at $D$.

Triangles $ABC$ and $ADB$ share the angle at $A$ and both have a right angle, so
they are similar. Matching sides give
$\dfrac{|AC|}{|AB|}=\dfrac{|AB|}{|AD|}$, so $|AB|^2=|AC|\times|AD|$.

In the same way, triangles $ABC$ and $BDC$ are similar, so
$|BC|^2=|AC|\times|DC|$.

Adding, $|AB|^2+|BC|^2=|AC|\times(|AD|+|DC|)=|AC|^2$. The square of the hypotenuse
equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Prove the converse of Pythagoras.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Construct a right-angled triangle with legs equal to two of the sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Pythagoras gives its hypotenuse, and SSS gives congruence.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Take a triangle with sides $a$, $b$ and $c$, where $a^2+b^2=c^2$. Construct a
second triangle with a right angle between sides of length $a$ and $b$. By
Pythagoras, its hypotenuse is $c$.

The two triangles have three pairs of equal sides, so they are congruent by SSS.
Matching angles give the angle opposite $c$ equal to the right angle, so it is
90°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** Show that 20, 21 and 29 form a Pythagorean triple.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out $20^2+21^2$ and compare it with $29^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$400+441=841$, and $29^2=841$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$20^2+21^2=400+441=841$, and $29^2=841$. The two values match, so 20, 21 and 29
form a Pythagorean triple.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Two triangles are similar with scale factor 2. The area of the small
triangle is 7 cm². Work out the area of the large one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Areas scale by the square of the scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $7\times2^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$7\times4=28$ cm². The area of the large triangle is 28 cm².

The proof of Pythagoras in this section is a similar-triangle argument, so the
ratio work from Section 20.7 is used directly.

</details>
</details>
</details>
