# 16.2 The circumference and area of a circle — Learn

**Prerequisites:** perimeter and area from Section 16.1, and multiplication of
decimals.

**Objectives:** name the radius, the diameter and the circumference; find the
circumference of a circle; find the area of a circle; find the radius when you know
the circumference; give an answer in terms of $\pi$ or as a decimal.

By the end of this section, you can measure the edge and the surface of a circle.

## Parts of a circle

A circle has a centre and a smooth curved edge.

- The **radius** is the distance from the centre to the edge. Its symbol is $r$.
- The **diameter** is the distance across the circle through the centre. Its symbol is
  $d$. The diameter is twice the radius, so $d=2r$.
- The **circumference** is the distance all the way around the edge. It is the
  perimeter of the circle.

Every circle is the same shape. If you divide the circumference by the diameter, you
always get the same number. That number is **$\pi$**, pronounced "pi".

$$\pi = 3.14159\ldots$$

The decimal never ends and never repeats. For most questions, use $\pi \approx 3.14$
or the $\pi$ button on a calculator.

## The circumference

The circumference is $\pi$ times the diameter.

$$C = \pi d$$

Since the diameter is twice the radius, you can also write the formula with the radius.

$$C = 2\pi r$$

**Example 1.** A circle has a radius of 5 cm. Find its circumference. Give your answer
in terms of $\pi$, and then as a decimal.

$$C = 2\pi(5) = 10\pi$$

So the circumference is $10\pi$ cm. Using $\pi \approx 3.14$,

$$C \approx 10 \times 3.14 = 31.4$$

The circumference is about 31.4 cm.

## The area

The area of a circle is $\pi$ times the radius squared.

$$A = \pi r^2$$

The radius is squared, not the diameter. Square the radius first, and then multiply by
$\pi$.

**Example 2.** A circle has a diameter of 12 m. Find its area. Give your answer in
terms of $\pi$.

First find the radius. The radius is half of the diameter.

$$r = 12 \div 2 = 6$$

Now use the area formula.

$$A = \pi (6)^2 = 36\pi$$

The area is $36\pi$ m².

## Working backwards

You can find the radius when you know the circumference.

**Example 3.** A circle has a circumference of 62.8 cm. Find its radius. Use
$\pi \approx 3.14$.

Start from $C=2\pi r$ and put in the numbers.

$$62.8 = 2 \times 3.14 \times r$$

$$62.8 = 6.28r \qquad r = 62.8 \div 6.28 = 10$$

The radius is 10 cm.

## The pond

The chapter pond has a radius of 1.5 m. Find its circumference and its area.

For the circumference,

$$C = 2\pi(1.5) = 3\pi \approx 9.42$$

The circumference is $3\pi$ m, which is about 9.42 m.

For the area,

$$A = \pi(1.5)^2 = 2.25\pi \approx 7.07$$

The area is $2.25\pi$ m², which is about 7.07 m².

## Watch out

The two formulas look similar, and the radius and diameter are often swapped.

> **Watch out.** A circle has a diameter of 10 cm. The common error is to use 10 as the
> radius in the area formula and work out $\pi\times10^2=100\pi$ cm².
>
> The radius is half of the diameter, so $r=5$ cm. The correct area is
> $\pi\times5^2=25\pi$ cm².
>
> **Re-check:** the area uses the radius. Halve the diameter first if the question
> gives you the diameter.

A second trap is rounding too early. Keep the exact value with $\pi$ until the last
step, then round once. Rounding at every step makes the final answer drift.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The circular pond has a radius of 1.5 m.

1. Find the circumference of the pond.
2. Find the area of the pond.

In Section 16.3 you will subtract this area from the plot to find the planted area.

## You should now be able to

- name the radius, the diameter and the circumference of a circle;
- use $d=2r$ to change between the radius and the diameter;
- find the circumference using $C=\pi d$ or $C=2\pi r$;
- find the area using $A=\pi r^2$, squaring the radius;
- find the radius from the circumference;
- leave an answer in terms of $\pi$, or give it as a decimal with $\pi\approx3.14$.
