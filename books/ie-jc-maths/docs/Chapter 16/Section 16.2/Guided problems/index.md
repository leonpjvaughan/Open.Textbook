# 16.2 The circumference and area of a circle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the perimeter of a rectangle 9 cm long and 4 cm wide.
2. Work out $3.14\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $26$ cm. Add the length and the width, then double: $2(9+4)=2\times13=26$.
2. $314$. Multiplying by 100 moves each digit two places to the left.

The first answer is a perimeter, which is the same idea as a circumference. The second
is the kind of multiplication you use with $\pi\approx3.14$.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A circle has a radius of 4 cm. Find its circumference in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $C=2\pi r$. The radius is 4 cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\pi\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The circumference is $8\pi$ cm.

$$C=2\pi(4)=8\pi$$

Leave the answer in terms of $\pi$ when the question asks for it. That keeps it exact.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A circle has a radius of 4 cm. Find its area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $A=\pi r^2$. Square the radius first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\pi\times4^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $16\pi$ cm².

$$A=\pi(4)^2=16\pi$$

The radius is squared, so $4^2=16$. The area unit is cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A circle has a diameter of 10 m. Find its circumference in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diameter is given, so use $C=\pi d$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\pi\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The circumference is $10\pi$ m.

$$C=\pi(10)=10\pi$$

The diameter is used directly in this formula. There is no need to halve it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A circle has a diameter of 10 m. Find its area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Halve the diameter to get the radius, then use $A=\pi r^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$r=5 \qquad \pi\times5^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $25\pi$ m².

$$r=10\div2=5 \qquad A=\pi(5)^2=25\pi$$

Halving the diameter first is the step that keeps the answer right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A circle has a radius of 6 cm. Find its circumference. Use $\pi\approx3.14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $C=2\pi r$ with 3.14 in place of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times3.14\times6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The circumference is about $37.68$ cm.

$$2\times3.14\times6=37.68$$

Round once, at the end. Rounding in the middle would make the answer drift.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A circle has a radius of 2 m. Find its area. Use $\pi\approx3.14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square the radius first, then multiply by 3.14.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2^2=4 \qquad 3.14\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is about $12.56$ m².

$$A=3.14\times2^2=3.14\times4=12.56$$

The unit is square metres, because an area is a surface.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter pond has a radius of 1.5 m. Find its circumference in terms of
$\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $C=2\pi r$ with $r=1.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\pi\times1.5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The circumference is $3\pi$ m.

$$C=2\pi(1.5)=3\pi$$

That is about 9.42 m of edging to go around the pond.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter pond has a radius of 1.5 m. Find its area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $A=\pi r^2$ with $r=1.5$. Square 1.5 first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1.5^2=2.25 \qquad \pi\times2.25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $2.25\pi$ m².

$$A=\pi(1.5)^2=2.25\pi$$

That is about 7.07 m². You will subtract this from the plot in Section 16.3.

</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A triangle has a base of 12 cm and a perpendicular height of 5 cm. Find its
area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 16.1, use half of base times perpendicular height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times12\times5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $30$ cm².

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times12\times5=6\times5=30$$

The area of a triangle is half the area of the rectangle around it.

</details>
</details>
</details>
