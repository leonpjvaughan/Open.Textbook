# 16.5 Volume of prisms, cylinders and spheres — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a triangle with base 8 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm.
2. Work out $4^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $24$ cm². Use half of base times height: $\tfrac{1}{2}\times8\times6=24$.
2. $64$. A cube means three factors of 4: $4\times4\times4=64$.

The triangle area is the cross-section of a prism. Cubing a number appears in the
volume of a sphere.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A rectangular solid is 5 cm long, 3 cm wide and 4 cm tall. Find its volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A rectangular solid is a prism. Multiply the three lengths.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5\times3\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $60$ cm³.

$$5\times3\times4=60$$

The unit is cubic centimetres, because the answer fills space.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A box is 10 cm long, 4 cm wide and 2 cm tall. Find its volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the length, the width and the height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$10\times4\times2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $80$ cm³.

$$10\times4\times2=80$$

Check the unit: three lengths are multiplied, so the unit is cubed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A triangular prism is 10 cm long. Its cross-section is a triangle with base
6 cm and height 4 cm. Find its volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the area of the triangular cross-section first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times4=12 \qquad 12\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $120$ cm³.

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times4=12 \qquad V=12\times10=120$$

A prism volume is the cross-section area times the length.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find its volume in terms
of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $V=\pi r^2h$. Square the radius first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\pi\times3^2\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $90\pi$ cm³.

$$\pi\times9\times10=90\pi$$

That is about 282.6 cm³. The exact answer with $\pi$ is cleaner to keep.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A sphere has a radius of 6 cm. Find its volume in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $V=\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$. Cube the radius first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6^3=216 \qquad \tfrac{4}{3}\times216\pi$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $288\pi$ cm³.

$$\tfrac{4}{3}\times216=288$$

The radius is cubed, so $6^3=216$. Then multiply by $\tfrac{4}{3}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A tank holds 3 m³ of water. Write this volume in litres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One cubic metre is 1000 L.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The tank holds $3000$ L.

$$3\times1000=3000$$

A cubic metre is a cube of side 1 m, and it holds 1000 litres of water.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The cylindrical part of the chapter tank has a radius of 0.5 m and a height of
1 m. Find its volume in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $V=\pi r^2h$. Square 0.5 first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.5^2=0.25 \qquad \pi\times0.25\times1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $0.25\pi$ m³.

$$V=\pi(0.5)^2(1)=0.25\pi$$

The height is 1 m, so multiplying by it does not change the value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The cylindrical part of the chapter tank has a volume of $0.25\pi$ m³. Write
this volume in litres. Use $\pi\approx3.14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the volume as a decimal first, then multiply by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.25\times3.14=0.785 \qquad 0.785\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The cylindrical part holds about $785$ L.

$$0.25\times3.14\times1000=785$$

The half-sphere on top will add more. You will find it in Section 16.7.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A rectangle is 12 cm long and 5 cm wide. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 16.1, multiply the length by the width.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12\times5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $60$ cm².

$$12\times5=60$$

A prism volume is this area multiplied by the length, so an area is always the first
step.

</details>
</details>
</details>
