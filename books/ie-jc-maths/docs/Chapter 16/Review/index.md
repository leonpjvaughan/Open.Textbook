# Chapter 16: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The school garden and its water tank.** The plot is a rectangle 10 m by 6 m. Inside
it are a right-angled triangular bed with two shorter sides of 4 m and 3 m, and a
circular pond of radius 1.5 m. The club also builds a raised bed 2 m by 1 m by 0.5 m,
and installs a tank in the shape of a cylinder with a hemisphere on top. The tank has
radius 0.5 m, and the cylindrical part is 1 m tall.

**Step 1. The perimeter of the plot.** Add the length and the width, then double.

$$P = 2(10+6) = 32$$

**The perimeter of the plot is 32 m.**

**Step 2. The area of the plot.** Multiply the length by the width.

$$A = 10 \times 6 = 60$$

**The plot has an area of 60 m².**

**Step 3. The area of the triangular bed.** The two shorter sides meet at a right
angle, so they are the base and the height.

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3 = 6$$

**The bed has an area of 6 m².**

**Step 4. The area of the pond.** Use $A=\pi r^2$ with $r=1.5$.

$$A = \pi(1.5)^2 = 2.25\pi \approx 7.07$$

**The pond has an area of $2.25\pi$ m², which is about 7.07 m².**

**Step 5. The planted area.** Subtract the bed and the pond from the plot.

$$60 - 6 - 2.25\pi = 54 - 2.25\pi \approx 54 - 7.07 = 46.93$$

**The planted area is $54-2.25\pi$ m², which is about 46.93 m².**

**Step 6. The raised bed.** Find the area of its net, which is the board needed.

$$S = 2(2\times1 + 1\times0.5 + 2\times0.5) = 2(2+0.5+1) = 7$$

**The raised bed needs 7 m² of board.** Its volume is
$2\times1\times0.5=1$ m³.

**Step 7. The volume of the tank.** Add the cylinder and the hemisphere.

$$V = \pi r^2 h + \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = 0.25\pi + \tfrac{1}{12}\pi = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi$$

$$\tfrac{1}{3}\pi \approx 1.047\ \text{m}^3$$

**The tank has a volume of $\tfrac{1}{3}\pi$ m³, which is about 1.047 m³.**

**Step 8. The volume in litres.** One cubic metre is 1000 L.

$$1.047 \times 1000 = 1047$$

**The tank holds about 1047 L.** That is more than 1000 L, so the tank beats the
prediction.

**Step 9. The surface area of the tank.** Add the base, the curved side and the
hemisphere. The circle at the join is hidden.

$$S = 0.25\pi + \pi + 0.5\pi = 1.75\pi \approx 5.5$$

**The surface area of the tank is $1.75\pi$ m², which is about 5.5 m².**

Every step used one of the seven sections: perimeter and area, the circle, the
composite area, the net, volume, surface area, and the composite solid.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and say
why in a few words. The idea might be a perimeter, an area, a circumference, a
volume, a surface area, a unit conversion, a percentage, a ratio, an equation or a
formula.

1. Convert 3 km to metres.
2. Find the perimeter of a rectangle 14 m long and 9 m wide.
3. Work out 35% of 240.
4. Find the circumference of a circle of radius 7 cm, in terms of $\pi$.
5. Solve $5x-3=27$.
6. Find the area of a triangle with base 11 cm and perpendicular height 6 cm.
7. Find the volume of a cuboid 7 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm.
8. Make $r$ the subject of $C=2\pi r$.
9. Find the area of a circle of diameter 16 m, in terms of $\pi$.
10. Divide €84 in the ratio $3:4$.
11. An L-shape is a rectangle 9 m by 5 m with a square of side 2 m cut from a corner.
    Find its area.
12. Find the volume of a cylinder of radius 5 m and height 2 m, in terms of $\pi$.
13. Find the curved surface area of a cylinder of radius 3 m and height 4 m, in terms
    of $\pi$.
14. A car travels 180 km in 2.5 hours. Find its average speed.
15. Find the surface area of a sphere of radius 5 cm, in terms of $\pi$.
16. A solid is a cuboid 6 cm by 4 cm by 2 cm, with a cube of side 2 cm on top. Find
    its volume.

<summary>Answers and concise solutions</summary>

1. $3000$ m. There are 1000 m in 1 km, so multiply by 1000.
2. $46$ m. Add the length and the width, then double: $2(14+9)=2\times23=46$.
3. $84$. Ten per cent of 240 is 24, so 30% is $3\times24=72$ and 5% is 12. Add them:
   $72+12=84$. Working directly, $0.35\times240=84$.
4. $14\pi$ cm. Use $C=2\pi r$: $2\times\pi\times7=14\pi$.
5. $x=6$. Add 3 to both sides to get $5x=30$, then divide by 5.
6. $33$ cm². Use half of base times height: $\tfrac{1}{2}\times11\times6=33$.
7. $84$ cm³. Multiply the three lengths: $7\times4\times3=84$.
8. $r=\dfrac{C}{2\pi}$. Divide both sides by $2\pi$.
9. $64\pi$ m². Halve the diameter to get $r=8$ m, then $A=\pi\times8^2=64\pi$.
10. €36 and €48. There are $3+4=7$ parts, and $84\div7=12$. Then $3\times12=36$ and
    $4\times12=48$.
11. $41$ m². The rectangle is $45$ m², and the square is $4$ m². Subtract: $45-4=41$.
12. $50\pi$ m³. Use $V=\pi r^2h$: $\pi\times25\times2=50\pi$.
13. $24\pi$ m². Use $2\pi rh$: $2\times\pi\times3\times4=24\pi$. The ends are not
    included.
14. $72$ km/h. Speed is distance divided by time: $180\div2.5=72$.
15. $100\pi$ cm². Use $S=4\pi r^2$: $4\times\pi\times25=100\pi$.
16. $56$ cm³. The cuboid is $6\times4\times2=48$ cm³, and the cube is $2^3=8$ cm³.
    Add them: $48+8=56$.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you predicted whether the tank would hold more or less
than 1000 litres. Look back at that prediction now.

The tank holds about 1047 L, which is just over 1000 L. The prediction was a good one
if you reasoned that a cylinder of radius 0.5 m and height 1 m holds about 785 L, and
that the rounded top adds roughly another 260 L.

You also wrote down one rectangular box from your own day, with the measurements for
its volume and for the area of one face. Take that box now. Find its volume, and find
the area of one face. Then say which of the two answers would be measured in cm² and
which in cm³.

That is the whole of this chapter in one small piece of work: two measurements for a
face, three for a solid, and a unit that tells you which one you have found.
