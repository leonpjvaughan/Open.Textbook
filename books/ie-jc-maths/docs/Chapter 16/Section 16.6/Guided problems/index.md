# 16.6 Surface area and curved surface area — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a circle of radius 5 cm in terms of $\pi$.
2. Work out $2\times3.14\times4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $25\pi$ cm². Square the radius first: $5^2=25$. Then multiply by $\pi$.
2. $25.12$. Multiply $2\times4=8$ first, then $8\times3.14=25.12$.

The first answer is the area of a circular end. The second is the kind of calculation
you use for a curved surface.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A rectangular solid is 4 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm tall. Find its surface
area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find one of each pair of faces, add them, then double.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(4\times3+3\times2+4\times2)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $52$ cm².

$$2(12+6+8)=2\times26=52$$

There are six faces in three pairs, so one of each pair is doubled.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A cube has sides of 5 cm. Find its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A cube has six equal square faces.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6\times5^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $150$ cm².

$$6\times25=150$$

Each face is $25$ cm², and there are six faces.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A box is 6 cm long, 5 cm wide and 2 cm tall. Find its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three pairs of faces are $6\times5$, $5\times2$ and $6\times2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(30+10+12)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $104$ cm².

$$2\times52=104$$

Add one of each pair to get $52$ cm², then double it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A triangular prism is 10 cm long. Its cross-section is a triangle with base
6 cm, height 4 cm and the other two sides each 5 cm. Find its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The surface has two triangular ends and three rectangular sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times4+(6+5+5)\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $184$ cm².

$$24+160=184$$

The ends give $24$ cm², and the three rectangular sides give $160$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find its curved surface
area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $2\pi rh$. The curved surface is the side only.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times\pi\times3\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The curved surface area is $60\pi$ cm².

$$2\pi(3)(10)=60\pi$$

The two circular ends are not included, because the question asks for the curved
surface.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find its total surface
area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the two circular ends to the curved surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$60\pi+2\times\pi\times3^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The total surface area is $78\pi$ cm².

$$60\pi+18\pi=78\pi$$

The curved side gives $60\pi$ cm², and the two ends give $18\pi$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A sphere has a radius of 6 cm. Find its surface area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $S=4\pi r^2$. Square the radius first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4\times\pi\times6^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $144\pi$ cm².

$$4\pi(36)=144\pi$$

A sphere has only a curved surface, so there are no flat ends to add.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The cylindrical part of the chapter tank has a radius of 0.5 m and a height of
1 m. Find its curved surface area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $2\pi rh$ with $r=0.5$ and $h=1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times\pi\times0.5\times1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The curved surface area is $\pi$ m².

$$2\pi(0.5)(1)=\pi$$

That is about 3.14 m². In Section 16.7 you will add the curved surface of the
half-sphere on top.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A rectangular solid is 3 cm long, 2 cm wide and 4 cm tall. Find its volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 16.5, multiply the three lengths.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times2\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $24$ cm³.

$$3\times2\times4=24$$

The unit is cubic centimetres. A surface area would be in square centimetres, so the
unit tells the two apart.

</details>
</details>
</details>
