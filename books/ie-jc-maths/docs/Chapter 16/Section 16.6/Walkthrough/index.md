# 16.6 Surface area and curved surface area — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the area of a net from Section 16.4, and the area of a circle from
Section 16.2.

**Objectives:** find the surface area of a rectangular solid; find the surface area of
a prism; find the curved surface area of a cylinder; find the total surface area of a
cylinder; find the surface area of a sphere.

By the end of this section, you can find the area of the outside of a solid.

## Surface area

The **surface area** of a solid is the total area of its outside. It is the same as
the area of its net, because the net is the outside laid flat.

The **curved surface area** is the area of the curved part only, without the flat
ends. A tin has a curved label around its side, and two flat circles at the top and
the bottom. The label covers the curved surface area.

Surface area is measured in square units: cm², m² or mm².

## A rectangular solid

A rectangular solid has six rectangular faces in three pairs.

$$S = 2(lw + wh + lh)$$

**Example 1.** A rectangular solid is 4 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm tall. Find its
surface area.

Work out one of each pair.

$$4\times3=12 \qquad 3\times2=6 \qquad 4\times2=8$$

Add them and double.

$$S = 2(12+6+8) = 2\times26 = 52$$

The surface area is 52 cm².

## A prism

For a prism, find the two ends and the rectangular sides.

**Example 2.** A triangular prism is 10 cm long. Its cross-section is a triangle with
base 6 cm, height 4 cm and the other two sides each 5 cm. Find its surface area.

The two triangular ends give $2\times\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times4=24$ cm².

The three rectangular sides are each 10 cm long. Their widths are the three sides of
the triangle.

$$(6+5+5)\times10=160$$

Add the parts.

$$S = 24 + 160 = 184$$

The surface area is 184 cm².

## A cylinder

The curved surface of a cylinder unrolls into a rectangle. Its width is the
circumference $2\pi r$, and its height is $h$.

$$\text{curved surface area} = 2\pi r h$$

The two circular ends each have area $\pi r^2$. The total surface area adds them.

$$\text{total surface area} = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$$

**Example 3.** A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find its curved
surface area and its total surface area, in terms of $\pi$.

Curved surface first.

$$2\pi(3)(10) = 60\pi$$

The curved surface area is $60\pi$ cm².

Now the two ends.

$$2 \times \pi \times 3^2 = 18\pi$$

Add them for the total.

$$60\pi + 18\pi = 78\pi$$

The total surface area is $78\pi$ cm².

## A sphere

A sphere has only a curved surface. Its surface area is four times the area of a
great circle through its centre.

$$S = 4\pi r^2$$

**Example 4.** A sphere has a radius of 6 cm. Find its surface area in terms of
$\pi$.

Square the radius first.

$$6^2 = 36$$

Then multiply by 4 and by $\pi$.

$$S = 4\pi(36) = 144\pi$$

The surface area is $144\pi$ cm², which is about 452.16 cm².

## The water tank

The chapter tank has a cylindrical part of radius 0.5 m and height 1 m. Find the
curved surface area of this part.

$$\text{curved surface area} = 2\pi r h = 2\pi(0.5)(1) = \pi$$

The curved surface area is $\pi$ m², which is about 3.14 m². This is the area that
would be painted if the ends were left unpainted.

## Watch out

Curved surface area and total surface area are different, and a question may ask for
either one.

> **Watch out.** A cylinder has a radius of 4 cm and a height of 5 cm. A question asks
> for the curved surface area. The common error is to add the two circular ends.
>
> The curved surface area is $2\pi rh=2\pi(4)(5)=40\pi$ cm².
>
> The total surface area is $40\pi+2\pi(4)^2=40\pi+32\pi=72\pi$ cm².
>
> **Re-check:** read the word "curved". If the question says curved, leave the ends
> out. If it says total, add them.

A second trap is the sphere. Its surface area uses $r^2$, and its volume uses $r^3$.
The two formulas are often confused, so check the unit. An area is in cm² and a
volume is in cm³.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The cylindrical part of the tank has a radius of 0.5 m
and a height of 1 m.

1. Find its curved surface area in terms of $\pi$.
2. Write the curved surface area as a decimal, using $\pi\approx3.14$.

In Section 16.7 you will add the surface area of the hemispherical top.

## You should now be able to

- explain the difference between surface area and curved surface area;
- find the surface area of a rectangular solid using $S=2(lw+wh+lh)$;
- find the surface area of a prism from its ends and its rectangular sides;
- find the curved surface area of a cylinder using $2\pi r h$;
- find the total surface area of a cylinder using $2\pi r^2+2\pi r h$;
- find the surface area of a sphere using $S=4\pi r^2$.
