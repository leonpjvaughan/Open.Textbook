# 16.3 Areas of composite figures — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the area of a rectangle and a triangle from Section 16.1, and the
area of a circle from Section 16.2.

**Objectives:** split a composite figure into rectangles, triangles and discs; add the
parts to find the total area; subtract a part that is cut out of a larger shape; keep
track of the dimensions you use.

By the end of this section, you can find the area of a figure built from simpler
shapes.

## A composite figure

A **composite figure** is made from two or more simpler shapes. A letter L, a house
shape and a running track are all composite figures.

There are two ways to find the area.

- **Split and add.** Cut the figure into rectangles, triangles and discs. Find each
  area, then add them.
- **Subtract.** Start with a large shape that contains the figure. Take away the parts
  that are not wanted.

Choose the method that gives you the simpler pieces.

## Split and add

**Example 1.** An L-shaped floor is 8 m wide and 5 m tall. A rectangular notch 3 m
wide and 2 m tall is missing from the top-right corner. Find the area.

Split the L into two rectangles with a vertical cut.

- The left rectangle is 5 m wide and 5 m tall: $5\times5=25$ m².
- The right rectangle is 3 m wide and 3 m tall: $3\times3=9$ m².

Add them.

$$25 + 9 = 34$$

The floor has an area of 34 m².

**Example 2.** A house shape is a rectangle 6 m wide and 4 m tall, with a triangle on
top. The triangle has a base of 6 m and a height of 3 m. Find the total area.

Rectangle first.

$$6 \times 4 = 24$$

Triangle next.

$$\tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 3 = 9$$

Add the two parts.

$$24 + 9 = 33$$

The total area is 33 m².

## A disc as a part

A disc is another name for a circle as a flat shape. A semicircle is half of a disc.

**Example 3.** A shape is a rectangle 10 cm wide and 4 cm tall, with a semicircle of
radius 2 cm on one short end. Find its area.

The rectangle has an area of $10\times4=40$ cm².

The semicircle is half of a circle of radius 2 cm.

$$\tfrac{1}{2} \times \pi \times 2^2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4\pi = 2\pi$$

Add the two parts.

$$40 + 2\pi \approx 40 + 6.28 = 46.28$$

The area is $40+2\pi$ cm², which is about 46.28 cm².

## Subtract

**Example 4.** A rectangular card is 10 cm by 6 cm. A circular hole of radius 2 cm is
cut out of it. Find the area that is left.

Start with the whole rectangle.

$$10 \times 6 = 60$$

Find the area of the hole.

$$\pi \times 2^2 = 4\pi$$

Subtract the hole.

$$60 - 4\pi \approx 60 - 12.57 = 47.43$$

The card has an area of $60-4\pi$ cm², which is about 47.43 cm².

## The planted area

The chapter plot is a rectangle 10 m by 6 m. Inside it are a triangular bed of area
6 m² and a circular pond of area $2.25\pi$ m². Find the planted area.

Start with the whole plot.

$$10 \times 6 = 60$$

Subtract the bed and the pond.

$$60 - 6 - 2.25\pi = 54 - 2.25\pi$$

Using $\pi \approx 3.14$,

$$54 - 2.25 \times 3.14 = 54 - 7.065 = 46.935$$

The planted area is $54-2.25\pi$ m², which is about 46.94 m².

## Watch out

A composite figure is a subtraction as often as it is an addition. Read the question
and decide which one it is.

> **Watch out.** A rectangle 12 cm by 5 cm has a triangular corner cut off, with base
> 4 cm and height 3 cm. The common error is to add the triangle area to the rectangle.
>
> The triangle is a piece that is **removed**. Its area is $\tfrac{1}{2}\times4\times3=6$
> cm², so the area left is $60-6=54$ cm².
>
> **Re-check:** ask whether the piece is part of the figure or a hole in it. If it is a
> hole, subtract it.

A second trap is a shared side. When two parts touch along an edge, the edge is not
part of the area. Count each region once, and never count a joining line twice.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The plot is 10 m by 6 m, the triangular bed has an area
of 6 m², and the circular pond has an area of $2.25\pi$ m².

1. Find the area of the whole plot.
2. Subtract the bed and the pond to find the planted area.
3. Write your answer in terms of $\pi$, and then as a decimal.

In Section 16.4 you will build the raised bed, which is a solid rather than a flat
shape.

## You should now be able to

- split a composite figure into rectangles, triangles and discs;
- find the total area by adding the parts;
- find the area of a figure with a part cut out by subtracting;
- handle a semicircle as half of a disc;
- avoid counting a region twice when two parts share a side.
