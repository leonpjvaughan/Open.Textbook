# 16.3 Areas of composite figures — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a rectangle 9 m long and 5 m wide.
2. Find the area of a circle of radius 3 cm in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $45$ m². Multiply the length by the width: $9\times5=45$.
2. $9\pi$ cm². Square the radius first: $3^2=9$. Then multiply by $\pi$.

Both answers are areas, so both use square units. A composite figure is built from
these two shapes, so you will need both formulas.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** An L-shaped floor is 8 m wide and 5 m tall, with a rectangular notch 3 m wide
and 2 m tall missing from a corner. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the area of the whole rectangle, then subtract the notch.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8\times5-3\times2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $34$ m².

$$40-6=34$$

The notch is a piece missing from the rectangle, so you subtract it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A house shape is a rectangle 6 m wide and 4 m tall, with a triangle on top of
base 6 m and height 3 m. Find the total area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Split the shape into the rectangle and the triangle, and add the two areas.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6\times4+\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The total area is $33$ m².

$$24+9=33$$

The rectangle is $24$ m² and the triangle is $9$ m². Both parts belong to the figure,
so you add them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A shape is a rectangle 10 cm wide and 4 cm tall, with a semicircle of radius
2 cm on one short end. Find its area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A semicircle is half of a circle. Find the area of the rectangle and half of the
circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$10\times4+\tfrac{1}{2}\times\pi\times2^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $40+2\pi$ cm².

$$40+2\pi$$

The rectangle is $40$ cm², and the semicircle is $\tfrac{1}{2}\times4\pi=2\pi$ cm².
Add the two parts.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A rectangular card is 10 cm by 6 cm. A circular hole of radius 2 cm is cut out.
Find the area that is left, in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the area of the card and the area of the hole, then subtract.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$60-4\pi$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area left is $60-4\pi$ cm².

The card is $10\times6=60$ cm². The hole is $\pi\times2^2=4\pi$ cm². The hole is
removed, so subtract it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A rectangle is 12 cm by 5 cm. A triangular corner with base 4 cm and height
3 cm is cut off. Find the area that is left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The triangle is removed, so subtract its area from the rectangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12\times5-\tfrac{1}{2}\times4\times3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area left is $54$ cm².

$$60-6=54$$

The rectangle is $60$ cm², and the corner is $6$ cm². The corner is cut off, so
subtract it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** An L-shape is made from a rectangle 7 m by 4 m joined to a rectangle 3 m by
2 m. Find the total area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find each rectangle separately, then add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$7\times4+3\times2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The total area is $34$ m².

$$28+6=34$$

The larger rectangle is $28$ m² and the smaller is $6$ m². The two rectangles do not
overlap, so add them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter plot is a rectangle 10 m by 6 m. Find the area of the whole plot.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is the first step of the chapter problem. Multiply the length by the width.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$10\times6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $60$ m².

$$10\times6=60$$

This is the area you will subtract the beds and the pond from.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter plot is 60 m². A triangular bed has an area of 6 m², and a circular
pond has an area of $2.25\pi$ m². Find the planted area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with 60 m² and subtract both areas.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$60-6-2.25\pi$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The planted area is $54-2.25\pi$ m².

$$60-6-2.25\pi=54-2.25\pi$$

That is about 46.94 m². Both the bed and the pond are removed from the plot.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A triangle has a base of 9 cm and a perpendicular height of 4 cm. Find its
area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 16.1, use half of base times perpendicular height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times9\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $18$ cm².

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times9\times4=4.5\times4=18$$

A triangle is half of the rectangle around it, so the area is half of $36$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>
