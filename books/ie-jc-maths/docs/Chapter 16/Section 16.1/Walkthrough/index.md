# 16.1 Perimeter and area of rectangles and triangles — Learn

**Prerequisites:** units of length from Chapter 15, and multiplication of whole
numbers and decimals.

**Objectives:** find the perimeter of a rectangle; find the area of a rectangle; find
the area of a triangle from its base and its perpendicular height; find a missing
length when you know the area.

By the end of this section, you can measure the edge and the surface of a rectangle
and a triangle.

## Perimeter

The **perimeter** of a shape is the distance all the way around its edge. Perimeter is
a length, so its units are metres (m), centimetres (cm) or millimetres (mm).

A rectangle has two long sides and two short sides. Add all four sides, or add the
length and the width and double the result.

$$P = 2(l + w)$$

Here $l$ is the length and $w$ is the width.

**Example 1.** A rectangle is 7 cm long and 4 cm wide. Find its perimeter.

Add the length and the width, then double.

$$P = 2(7 + 4) = 2 \times 11 = 22$$

The perimeter is 22 cm.

## Area of a rectangle

The **area** of a shape is the amount of flat surface it covers. Area is measured in
squares, so its units are square metres (m²), square centimetres (cm²) or square
millimetres (mm²).

Picture a rectangle covered by unit squares. A rectangle 4 cm long and 3 cm wide holds
4 squares along one side and 3 rows, so it holds $4\times3=12$ squares.

$$A = l \times w$$

**Example 2.** The chapter plot is a rectangle 10 m long and 6 m wide. Find its
perimeter and its area.

$$P = 2(10 + 6) = 2 \times 16 = 32$$

$$A = 10 \times 6 = 60$$

The perimeter is 32 m and the area is 60 m². The perimeter is a length, so it uses
metres. The area is a surface, so it uses square metres.

## Area of a triangle

A triangle is half of a rectangle. Cut a rectangle along a diagonal and you make two
triangles of equal area.

The **base** is any side you choose. The **perpendicular height** is the distance from
the base to the opposite corner, measured at a right angle to the base.

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$$

**Example 3.** A triangle has a base of 8 cm and a perpendicular height of 5 cm. Find
its area.

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 5 = 20$$

The area is 20 cm².

**Example 4.** The triangular bed in the chapter plot has two shorter sides of 4 m and
3 m, meeting at a right angle. Find its area.

The two shorter sides are perpendicular, so one is the base and the other is the
height.

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3 = 6$$

The bed has an area of 6 m².

## Finding a missing length

You can work backwards when you know the area.

**Example 5.** A triangle has an area of 24 m² and a base of 6 m. Find its
perpendicular height.

Start from the area formula, and put in what you know. Here $h$ is the perpendicular
height.

$$24 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times h$$

$$24 = 3h \qquad h = 8$$

The perpendicular height is 8 m.

## Watch out

Perimeter and area measure different things, and the two formulas are often mixed up.
Perimeter adds the sides. Area multiplies two sides.

> **Watch out.** A triangle has base 6 cm and a slanted side of 5 cm. The slanted side
> is not the height.
>
> The common error is to use 5 cm as the height and work out
> $\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times5=15$ cm².
>
> The perpendicular height must meet the base at a right angle. If that height is
> 4 cm, the area is $\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times4=12$ cm².
>
> **Re-check:** look for the right-angle mark between the base and the height. If
> there is no right angle, the measurement is not the height.

A second trap is the unit. A length is in m or cm, and an area is in m² or cm². Write
the unit with every answer, and make sure it is squared for an area.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The plot is a rectangle 10 m by 6 m, and the
triangular bed has two shorter sides of 4 m and 3 m.

1. Find the perimeter of the plot.
2. Find the area of the plot.
3. Find the area of the triangular bed.

In Section 16.3 you will use these two areas to find the planted area of the plot.

## You should now be able to

- find the perimeter of a rectangle using $P=2(l+w)$;
- find the area of a rectangle using $A=l\times w$;
- find the area of a triangle using $A=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\text{base}\times
  \text{perpendicular height}$;
- choose the perpendicular height, not a slanted side;
- find a missing length when you know the area;
- write the unit with every answer, and square it for an area.
