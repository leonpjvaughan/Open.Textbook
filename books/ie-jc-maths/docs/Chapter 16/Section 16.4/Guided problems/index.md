# 16.4 Nets of solids — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a triangle with base 10 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm.
2. Work out $2\times3.14\times5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $20$ cm². Use half of base times height: $\tfrac{1}{2}\times10\times4=20$.
2. $31.4$. Multiply $2\times5=10$ first, then $10\times3.14=31.4$.

The triangle area returns in the net of a prism. The second calculation is the kind
you use for a circumference, which is the width of a cylinder net.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A rectangular solid is 4 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm tall. Find the area of its
net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The net has three pairs of rectangles. Find one of each pair, then double.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(4\times3+3\times2+4\times2)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net has an area of $52$ cm².

$$2(12+6+8)=2\times26=52$$

There are two of each rectangle, so the total of one of each pair is doubled.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A cube has sides of 5 cm. Find the area of its net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A cube net has six equal squares.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6\times5^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net has an area of $150$ cm².

$$6\times25=150$$

Each square face is $25$ cm², and there are six of them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A box is 5 cm long, 4 cm wide and 3 cm tall. Find the area of its net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three pairs of faces are $5\times4$, $4\times3$ and $5\times3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(20+12+15)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net has an area of $94$ cm².

$$2\times47=94$$

Add one of each pair to get $47$ cm², then double it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A triangular prism is 10 cm long. Its cross-section is a triangle with base
6 cm, height 4 cm and the other two sides each 5 cm. Find the area of its net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The net has two triangular ends and three rectangles. The rectangles are 10 cm long,
and their widths are the three sides of the triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times4+(6+5+5)\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net has an area of $184$ cm².

$$24+160=184$$

The two triangles give $24$ cm². The three rectangles give $16\times10=160$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find the area of its net
in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The net has two circles and one rectangle. The rectangle is as wide as the
circumference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times\pi\times3^2+2\times\pi\times3\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net has an area of $78\pi$ cm².

$$18\pi+60\pi=78\pi$$

The two circular ends give $18\pi$ cm². The rectangle is $6\pi$ cm wide and 10 cm
tall, giving $60\pi$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A cylinder has a radius of 4 cm. Find the width of the rectangle in its net, in
terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The width of the rectangle is the circumference of the circular end.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\pi\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The width is $8\pi$ cm.

$$2\pi(4)=8\pi$$

The rectangle wraps right around the circle, so its width matches the circumference.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A rectangular solid is 6 m long, 2 m wide and 2 m tall. Find the area of its
net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two of the faces are squares, and the other four are equal rectangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(6\times2+2\times2+6\times2)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net has an area of $56$ m².

$$2(12+4+12)=2\times28=56$$

There are four rectangles of $12$ m² and two squares of $4$ m².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter raised bed is a rectangular solid, 2 m long, 1 m wide and 0.5 m
deep. Find the area of its net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three pairs of faces are $2\times1$, $1\times0.5$ and $2\times0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(2+0.5+1)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net has an area of $7$ m².

$$2\times3.5=7$$

That is the area of board needed to build the raised bed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A triangle has a base of 8 cm and a perpendicular height of 3 cm. Find its
area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 16.1, use half of base times perpendicular height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times8\times3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $12$ cm².

$$\tfrac{1}{2}\times8\times3=4\times3=12$$

This is the same triangle area you need for the ends of a triangular prism.

</details>
</details>
</details>
