# 16.4 Nets of solids — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the area of rectangles and triangles from Section 16.1, and the
circumference of a circle from Section 16.2.

**Objectives:** describe a net as a flat pattern that folds into a solid; name the
faces of a rectangular solid, a prism and a cylinder; draw or recognise a net; use a
net to find the area of the outside of a solid.

By the end of this section, you can unfold a solid into a flat pattern and find the
area of that pattern.

## What a net is

A **net** is a flat pattern that folds up to make a solid. If you cut a cardboard box
along some of its edges and lay it flat, you have its net.

Every flat piece of the net is a **face** of the solid. The area of the whole net is
the area of the outside of the solid. This is why a net is useful: the area of a
solid's surface becomes a set of flat areas you already know how to find.

## The net of a rectangular solid

A **rectangular solid**, or cuboid, has six rectangular faces. Opposite faces are
equal, so there are three pairs of matching rectangles.

**Example 1.** A rectangular solid is 4 cm long, 3 cm wide and 2 cm tall. Describe
its net, and find the area of the net.

The net has six rectangles in three pairs.

- Two faces are 4 cm by 3 cm: $2 \times 12 = 24$ cm².
- Two faces are 3 cm by 2 cm: $2 \times 6 = 12$ cm².
- Two faces are 4 cm by 2 cm: $2 \times 8 = 16$ cm².

Add the three pairs.

$$24 + 12 + 16 = 52$$

The net has an area of 52 cm², so the solid has a surface area of 52 cm².

A cube is a rectangular solid whose faces are all squares. Its net has six equal
squares.

## The net of a prism

A **prism** has the same cross-section all the way along. A triangular prism has a
triangle as its cross-section.

Its net has two triangular ends and three rectangles, one for each side of the
triangle.

**Example 2.** A triangular prism is 10 cm long. Its cross-section is a triangle with
base 6 cm and height 4 cm. The other two sides of the triangle are each 5 cm. Find the
area of its net.

The two triangular ends are equal.

$$2 \times \tfrac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 24$$

The three rectangles each have length 10 cm. Their widths are the three sides of the
triangle.

$$(6 + 5 + 5) \times 10 = 16 \times 10 = 160$$

Add the parts.

$$24 + 160 = 184$$

The net has an area of 184 cm².

## The net of a cylinder

A cylinder has two circular ends and one curved surface. When the curved surface is
unrolled, it becomes a rectangle.

The height of that rectangle is the height of the cylinder. Its width is the
**circumference** of the circular end, because the rectangle wraps right around the
circle.

**Example 3.** A cylinder has a radius of 3 cm and a height of 10 cm. Find the area of
its net in terms of $\pi$.

The two circular ends each have area $\pi r^2=\pi\times3^2=9\pi$ cm². Together they
give $18\pi$ cm².

The curved surface unrolls into a rectangle. Its width is the circumference
$2\pi r=6\pi$ cm, and its height is 10 cm.

$$6\pi \times 10 = 60\pi$$

Add the ends and the curved surface.

$$18\pi + 60\pi = 78\pi$$

The net has an area of $78\pi$ cm².

## The raised bed

The chapter raised bed is a rectangular solid, 2 m long, 1 m wide and 0.5 m deep. Its
net has six rectangles in three pairs.

- Two faces are 2 m by 1 m: $2 \times 2 = 4$ m².
- Two faces are 1 m by 0.5 m: $2 \times 0.5 = 1$ m².
- Two faces are 2 m by 0.5 m: $2 \times 1 = 2$ m².

Add the three pairs.

$$4 + 1 + 2 = 7$$

The net has an area of 7 m², so 7 m² of board is needed to build the bed.

## Watch out

The most common error with a cylinder net is the width of the rectangle.

> **Watch out.** A cylinder has a radius of 5 cm and a height of 12 cm. The common
> error is to make the rectangle in the net 5 cm wide and 12 cm tall.
>
> The width of the rectangle is the circumference of the end, which is
> $2\pi r=10\pi$ cm. The rectangle is $10\pi$ cm wide and 12 cm tall.
>
> **Re-check:** the rectangle must wrap right around the circular end, so its width is
> the circumference. It is never the radius or the diameter.

A second trap is a missing face. A rectangular solid has six faces, and a cylinder has
two circular ends and one curved surface. Count every face before you add the areas.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The raised bed is a rectangular solid, 2 m long, 1 m
wide and 0.5 m deep.

1. Say how many rectangles are in its net.
2. Find the area of each pair of matching faces.
3. Add them to find the area of the net.

In Section 16.6 you will find the surface area of the water tank in the same way.

## You should now be able to

- describe a net as a flat pattern that folds into a solid;
- name the faces of a rectangular solid, a prism and a cylinder;
- draw or recognise the net of a rectangular solid, a triangular prism and a
  cylinder;
- find the area of a net by adding the areas of its faces;
- use the circumference as the width of the rectangle in a cylinder net.
