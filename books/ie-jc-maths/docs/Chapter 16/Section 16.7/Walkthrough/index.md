# 16.7 Volume and surface area of composite solids — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the volume of a prism, a cylinder and a sphere from Section 16.5,
and surface area from Section 16.6.

**Objectives:** find the volume of a solid made from two simpler solids; find its
surface area; find the hidden faces where the two solids meet; use both results in a
real problem.

By the end of this section, you can find the volume and the surface area of a solid
built from two simpler solids.

## A composite solid

A **composite solid** is made by joining two or more simpler solids. A pencil, a barn
and a water tank with a rounded top are all composite solids.

The volume is the easier of the two. The two parts do not overlap, so add their
volumes.

$$V_{\text{total}} = V_1 + V_2$$

Here $V_{\text{total}}$ is the volume of the whole solid, $V_1$ and $V_2$ the
volumes of the two parts, and $S$ the surface area.

The surface area needs more care. When two solids are joined, the faces at the join
touch each other. Those faces are hidden inside the new solid, so they are not part of
the surface. Leave them out.

## A cuboid with a cube on top

**Example 1.** A solid is a cuboid 5 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm, with a cube of side 2 cm on
top. Find its volume and its surface area.

Volume first. Add the two parts.

$$V = (5\times4\times3) + 2^3 = 60 + 8 = 68$$

The volume is 68 cm³.

Surface area next. Find the surface area of each solid on its own.

$$\text{cuboid} = 2(5\times4+4\times3+5\times3) = 2(20+12+15) = 94$$

$$\text{cube} = 6\times2^2 = 24$$

At the join, a square of side 2 cm is hidden on the cuboid, and another is hidden on
the cube. Each hidden square has an area of $2\times2=4$ cm².

$$S = 94 + 24 - 2\times4 = 118 - 8 = 110$$

The surface area is 110 cm².

## A cylinder with a half-sphere on top

A half-sphere is called a **hemisphere**. Its curved surface is half of the surface of
a sphere.

**Example 2.** A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm, with a
hemisphere of radius 3 cm on top. Find its volume and its surface area in terms of
$\pi$.

Volume first. Here $V$ is the volume, $r$ is the radius and $h$ is the height of the
cylinder.

$$V = \pi r^2 h + \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = \pi(9)(10) + \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(27)$$

$$V = 90\pi + 18\pi = 108\pi$$

The volume is $108\pi$ cm³.

Surface area next. The solid has a flat circular base, a curved side and a
hemispherical top. The flat circle where the hemisphere meets the cylinder is hidden.

$$\text{base} = \pi r^2 = 9\pi$$

$$\text{curved side} = 2\pi r h = 2\pi(3)(10) = 60\pi$$

$$\text{hemisphere} = 2\pi r^2 = 2\pi(9) = 18\pi$$

Add the three parts.

$$S = 9\pi + 60\pi + 18\pi = 87\pi$$

The surface area is $87\pi$ cm².

## The water tank

The chapter tank is a cylinder with a hemisphere on top. The radius is 0.5 m, and the
cylindrical part is 1 m tall. Find the volume and the surface area of the whole tank.

Volume first. Use the cylinder volume from Section 16.5 and add the hemisphere.

$$V = \pi r^2 h + \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 = 0.25\pi + \tfrac{2}{3}\pi(0.125)$$

$$V = 0.25\pi + \tfrac{1}{12}\pi = \tfrac{3}{12}\pi + \tfrac{1}{12}\pi = \tfrac{4}{12}\pi = \tfrac{1}{3}\pi$$

The volume is $\tfrac{1}{3}\pi$ m³, which is about 1.047 m³.

Change it to litres.

$$1.047 \times 1000 = 1047$$

The tank holds about 1047 L.

Surface area next. The tank has a flat base, a curved side and a hemispherical top.

$$\text{base} = \pi(0.5)^2 = 0.25\pi$$

$$\text{curved side} = 2\pi(0.5)(1) = \pi$$

$$\text{hemisphere} = 2\pi(0.5)^2 = 0.5\pi$$

$$S = 0.25\pi + \pi + 0.5\pi = 1.75\pi$$

The surface area is $1.75\pi$ m², which is about 5.5 m².

## Watch out

The hidden face at a join is the mistake to look for.

> **Watch out.** A cube of side 3 cm sits on top of a cuboid. The common error is to
> add the two surface areas and stop there.
>
> The top of the cuboid and the bottom of the cube are both hidden. Each is
> $3\times3=9$ cm², so subtract $2\times9=18$ cm² from the total.
>
> **Re-check:** find the face where the two solids meet. It is hidden on both solids,
> so it is removed twice.

For the volume there is no such problem. The two parts fill separate spaces, so the
volumes are added with no adjustment.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The tank is a cylinder of radius 0.5 m with a
hemisphere of radius 0.5 m on top. The cylindrical part is 1 m tall.

1. Find the volume of the hemisphere.
2. Add it to the cylinder volume to find the volume of the whole tank.
3. Write the volume in litres, and say whether the tank holds more than 1000 litres.

The Review will use this answer to finish the chapter problem.

## You should now be able to

- find the volume of a composite solid by adding the volumes of its parts;
- find the surface area by adding the outer faces only;
- find the hidden faces at a join and leave them out;
- find the volume and the surface area of a cylinder with a hemisphere on top;
- change a volume in cubic metres to litres.
