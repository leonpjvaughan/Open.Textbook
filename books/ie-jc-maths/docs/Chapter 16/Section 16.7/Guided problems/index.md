# 16.7 Volume and surface area of composite solids — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the volume of a cube of side 3 cm.
2. Find the curved surface area of a cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 5 cm, in terms
   of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $27$ cm³. Cube the side: $3\times3\times3=27$.
2. $20\pi$ cm². Use $2\pi rh$: $2\times\pi\times2\times5=20\pi$.

A composite solid is built from parts like these. The cube volume and the cylinder
surface both return in this section.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A solid is a cuboid 5 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm, with a cube of side 2 cm on top. Find
its volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the volume of each part, then add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5\times4\times3+2^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $68$ cm³.

$$60+8=68$$

The two parts fill separate spaces, so there is nothing to subtract.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A solid is a cuboid 5 cm by 4 cm by 3 cm, with a cube of side 2 cm on top. Find
its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the surface area of each solid, then subtract the hidden square at the join twice.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$94+24-2\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $110$ cm².

$$94+24-8=110$$

The cuboid gives $94$ cm² and the cube gives $24$ cm². The join hides $4$ cm² on each
solid, so subtract $8$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm, with a hemisphere of
radius 3 cm on top. Find its volume in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\pi r^2h$ for the cylinder and $\tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3$ for the hemisphere.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\pi\times3^2\times10+\tfrac{2}{3}\times\pi\times3^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $108\pi$ cm³.

$$90\pi+18\pi=108\pi$$

The cylinder gives $90\pi$ cm³, and the hemisphere gives $18\pi$ cm³.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm, with a hemisphere of
radius 3 cm on top. Find its surface area in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The surface has a flat base, a curved side and a hemispherical top. The circle at the
join is hidden.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$9\pi+60\pi+18\pi$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $87\pi$ cm².

$$9\pi+60\pi+18\pi=87\pi$$

The base gives $9\pi$ cm², the curved side $60\pi$ cm² and the hemisphere $18\pi$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A solid is a cuboid 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm, with a cube of side 1 cm on top. Find
its volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the volume of each part, then add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4\times3\times2+1^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $25$ cm³.

$$24+1=25$$

The cuboid is $24$ cm³, and the small cube is $1$ cm³.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A solid is a cuboid 4 cm by 3 cm by 2 cm, with a cube of side 1 cm on top. Find
its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the hidden square at the join twice, once for each solid.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$52+6-2\times1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The surface area is $56$ cm².

$$52+6-2=56$$

The cuboid gives $52$ cm² and the cube gives $6$ cm². The join hides $1$ cm² on each
solid.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The chapter tank is a cylinder of radius 0.5 m with a hemisphere of radius 0.5 m
on top. The cylindrical part is 1 m tall. Find the volume of the whole tank in terms
of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the cylinder volume from Section 16.5 and add the hemisphere.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.25\pi+\tfrac{2}{3}\times\pi\times0.5^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $\tfrac{1}{3}\pi$ m³.

$$0.25\pi+\tfrac{1}{12}\pi=\tfrac{3}{12}\pi+\tfrac{1}{12}\pi=\tfrac{1}{3}\pi$$

The cylinder gives $0.25\pi$ m³, and the hemisphere gives $\tfrac{1}{12}\pi$ m³.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter tank has a volume of $\tfrac{1}{3}\pi$ m³. Write this volume in
litres, to the nearest litre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the volume as a decimal first, then multiply by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\tfrac{1}{3}\times3.14\approx1.047 \qquad 1.047\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The tank holds about $1047$ L.

$$1.047\times1000=1047$$

The tank holds more than 1000 L, so it beats the prediction in the chapter problem.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A sphere has a radius of 3 cm. Find its volume in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 16.5, use $V=\tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3^3=27 \qquad \tfrac{4}{3}\times27\pi$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $36\pi$ cm³.

$$\tfrac{4}{3}\times27=36$$

A hemisphere is half of this, which is the formula used in this section.

</details>
</details>
</details>
