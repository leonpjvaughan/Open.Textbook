# Chapter 16: Perimeter, area and volume

Perimeter is the distance around the edge of a shape. Area is the amount of surface
it covers. Volume is the space a solid takes up. All three measure size, but they
measure different things, so they use different units.

A rectangle 10 m long and 6 m wide has a perimeter of 32 m, an area of 60 m² and no
volume at all. A box of the same length and width, 0.5 m deep, has a volume of 3 m³.
The units tell you which of the three you have found.

This chapter builds each idea in turn. It starts with rectangles and triangles, moves
to circles, and then joins shapes together. It finishes with solids, their nets, their
volume and their surface area.

## The chapter problem

**The school garden and its water tank.** The garden club is making over a plot. The
plot is a rectangle 10 m by 6 m. Inside the plot are two more beds.

- A right-angled triangular bed has two shorter sides of 4 m and 3 m.
- A circular pond has a radius of 1.5 m.

The club also builds a raised wooden bed. It is a rectangular box, 2 m long, 1 m wide
and 0.5 m deep.

Last, the club installs a water tank. The tank is a cylinder with a half-sphere on top.
Its radius is 0.5 m, and the cylindrical part is 1 m tall.

Before you calculate anything, make a prediction and write it down. Do you think the
tank holds more than 1000 litres, or less? Write down your guess.

By the end of the chapter you can find the planted area of the plot, the volume of the
raised bed, and the volume of the tank in litres. You will return to this garden in
every section.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- find the perimeter and the area of a rectangle;

- find the circumference and the area of a circle;
- find the area of a figure made from rectangles, triangles and discs;
- draw and interpret the net of a rectangular solid, a prism and a cylinder;
- find the volume of a rectangular solid, a prism, a cylinder and a sphere;
- find the surface area and the curved surface area of these solids;
- find the volume and the surface area of a solid made from two simpler solids.

## Where it is used

Area and volume are part of ordinary work. A painter needs the area of a wall to buy
enough paint. A farmer needs the volume of a tank to know how much water it holds. A
shop needs the area of a floor to order the right number of tiles. A packaging
designer draws a net before a box is cut and folded.

The same mathematics helps you check a claim. If a shop sells a 2 litre bottle and a
500 mL bottle, the areas and volumes tell you which one gives more for your money.
