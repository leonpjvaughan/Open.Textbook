# 9.2 The general term of a linear pattern — Learn

**Prerequisites:** describing a pattern and recording it in a table, from Section 9.1.

**Objectives:** find the common difference of a linear pattern; write its general term
using the letter $n$; use the general term to find any term; find which position gives
a given value.

By the end of this section, you can write one rule that gives every term of a
straight-line pattern.

## The common difference

A **linear pattern** has the same difference between neighbouring terms. That
difference is the **common difference**.

$$7, 11, 15, 19, \ldots$$

The common difference is $4$, because $11-7=4$, $15-11=4$ and $19-15=4$.

We use the letter $n$ for the position. A rule that uses $n$ to give the term at
position $n$ is the **general term** of the pattern.

## Building the general term

The common difference is the number that multiplies $n$ in the general term. For the
pattern above, the general term starts with $4n$.

Work out the $4n$ pattern and compare it with the pattern you have.

$$4n: 4, 8, 12, 16, \ldots$$

Each term of the pattern is 3 more than the matching term of $4n$. So the general term
is $4n+3$.

Check it at two positions:

$$4(1)+3 = 7 \qquad 4(2)+3 = 11$$

Both match, so the rule fits.

## Investigate the picture behind the rule

**Predict.** Before you move the controls, predict how many dots the fourth term of a
pattern with general term $4n+3$ will have.

:::figure pattern-dots

**Test.** Leave the row at 4 dots and the extra dots at 3. Count the dots in each of the
three terms the figure draws. Then set the extra dots to 0 and the row to 2, and check
the general term the readout gives.

**Explain.** Write two or three sentences. Say which part of the picture grows with $n$
and which part stays the same, and how the two together give the general term.

**Example 1.** Find the general term of $5, 9, 13, 17, \ldots$.

The common difference is $4$, so start with $4n$.

$$4n: 4, 8, 12, 16, \ldots$$

Each term is 1 more, so the general term is $4n+1$.

Check position 2: $4(2)+1=9$. It matches.

## A decreasing pattern

The common difference can be negative. The same method still works.

**Example 2.** Find the general term of $12, 9, 6, 3, \ldots$.

The pattern goes down by 3, so the common difference is $-3$. Start with $-3n$.

$$-3n: -3, -6, -9, -12, \ldots$$

At position 1, $-3(1)=-3$. You need $15$ more to reach the first term, $12$. So the
general term is $-3n+15$.

Check position 2: $-3(2)+15=9$. It matches.

## Using the general term

Once you have the general term, you can find any term without listing the ones before
it. Put the position into the rule.

**Example 3.** A pattern has general term $6n-2$. Work out the 20th term.

$$6(20)-2 = 120-2 = 118$$

The 20th term is $118$.

You can also work backwards. Set the general term equal to the value you know, and
solve for $n$.

**Example 4.** Which term of the pattern with general term $5n-2$ is $48$?

$$
5n-2 = 48
$$

$$
5n = 50
$$

$$
n = 10
$$

The 10th term is $48$.

> **Watch out.** The first term is not always the constant in the rule. For
> $5, 9, 13, 17, \ldots$, the first term is 5 and the common difference is 4. Writing
> $4n+5$ gives $9, 13, 17, 21$, which is wrong. Match the common difference first,
> then adjust the constant.
>
> **Re-check:** put $n=1$ and $n=2$ into your rule. If either answer is wrong, the
> constant needs changing.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. Aoife saves $3, 6, 9, 12, \ldots$ euro.

1. What is the common difference of her plan?
2. Write the general term for her saving in week $n$.
3. Use the rule to find how much she saves in week 10.

## You should now be able to

- find the common difference of a linear pattern;
- use the common difference as the number that multiplies $n$;
- adjust the constant and check the rule at positions 1 and 2;
- use a general term to find a term, or to find which position gives a value.
