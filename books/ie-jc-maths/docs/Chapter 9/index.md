# Chapter 9: Patterns and sequences

A pattern is a set of things that follow a rule. A sequence is a list of numbers in a
pattern. Once you can see the rule, you can write it down and work out any term,
without listing every term before it.

## The chapter problem: the three savings plans

Three students are saving for the same school trip. Each puts money into a savings box
every week.

- **Aoife** saves €3 in week 1, €6 in week 2 and €9 in week 3. Her saving goes up by
  €3 each week.
- **Ben** saves €1 in week 1, €4 in week 2 and €9 in week 3. His amounts are the
  square numbers.
- **Cara** saves €1 in week 1, €2 in week 2 and €4 in week 3. Her saving doubles each
  week.

**First contact.** In week 1 Aoife saves the most. Before you calculate anything,
write down a prediction. Which student do you think saves the most in week 10? Which
plan do you think reaches €50 in a single week first?

By the end of the chapter you can put each plan in a table, write a rule for it, and
use the rules to compare the three. You will return to these plans in every section,
and you will solve them on the Review page.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- describe a pattern in words, and continue a repeating pattern;
- record a growing pattern in a table and find the change between terms;
- find the general term of a linear pattern and use it to work out any term;
- find the general term of a quadratic pattern using second differences;
- tell linear, quadratic and exponential growth apart;
- move between a rule, a table, a graph and a description.

## Where it is used

Patterns are how mathematics describes growth and change. A savings plan, a plant
growing, the seats in a row of a stadium and the interest on a loan all follow a
pattern. When you can write the rule, you can answer a question about any week or any
term, rather than working out one case at a time.
