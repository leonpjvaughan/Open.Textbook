# 9.4 Linear, quadratic and exponential growth — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $2^3$.
2. Work out $12\div3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $8$, because $2\times2\times2=8$.
2. $4$.

Powers build an exponential pattern, and dividing each term by the one before it is how
you find the multiplier.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Which kind of pattern is $3, 6, 9, 12, \ldots$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the first differences.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6-3=3$, $9-6=3$ and $12-9=3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first difference is always 3, so the pattern is linear. Its general term is $3n$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Which kind of pattern is $1, 4, 9, 16, \ldots$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first differences grow. Find the second difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first differences are $3, 5, 7$, and $5-3=2$ and $7-5=2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The second difference is constant, so the pattern is quadratic. Its general term is
$n^2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Which kind of pattern is $2, 6, 18, 54, \ldots$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide each term by the one before it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6\div2=3$ and $18\div6=3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The multiplier is always 3, so the pattern is exponential. Its general term is
$2\times3^{n-1}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Continue the doubling pattern $5, 10, 20, 40, \ldots$ by one term.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The multiplier is 2, so multiply the last term by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$40\times2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$80$$

The next term is $80$. Notice that the gap between terms also doubles each time:
$5, 10, 20, 40$. That is the sign of exponential growth.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Continue the tripling pattern $7, 14, 28, \ldots$ by one term.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide to find the multiplier before you continue.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$14\div7=2$ and $28\div14=2$, so the multiplier is 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$28\times2=56$$

The next term is $56$. The pattern doubles, even though it starts at 7.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A pattern is $2, 6, 18, 54, \ldots$, with general term $2\times3^{n-1}$. Work out
the 6th term.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 6th term is 5 steps past the 1st, so multiply by 3 five times.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times3^5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2\times243=486$$

The 6th term is $486$. Work out the power before multiplying by 2.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** At position 5, which is larger, the quadratic pattern $n^2$ or the exponential
pattern $2^{n-1}$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out both values at position 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5^2=25 \qquad 2^4=16$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The quadratic pattern is larger at position 5, because $25$ is bigger than $16$. The
exponential pattern catches up later: at position 8 it is $2^7=128$, while $8^2=64$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Cara's plan has general term $2^{n-1}$ euro. How much does she save in week 8?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put $n=8$ into the rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2^7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$128$$

She saves €128 in week 8. Doubling has taken her past both of the other plans.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** Find the multiplier of the pattern $3, 12, 48, \ldots$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide a term by the one before it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$12\div3=4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The multiplier is $4$. Check it: $48\div12=4$ as well. The same answer twice means the
pattern multiplies by 4 each time.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Work out $10^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A square is a number times itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$10\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$100$$

Powers of 10 are quick to work out, and they show how fast an exponential pattern can
grow.

</details>
</details>
</details>
