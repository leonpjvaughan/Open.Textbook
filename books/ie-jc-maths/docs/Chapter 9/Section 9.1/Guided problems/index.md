# 9.1 Repeating patterns and tables — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $7\times8$.
2. Write down the next three whole numbers after 12.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $56$.
2. $13, 14, 15$. Counting on is the same as adding 1 each time.

Both skills return in this section. Multiplying helps you jump along a pattern, and
counting on is the first pattern of all.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Continue the pattern $3, 7, 11, 15, \ldots$ by two terms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the change between neighbouring terms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$7-3=4$, so the pattern adds 4 each time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$15+4=19 \qquad 19+4=23$$

The next two terms are $19$ and $23$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A pattern repeats $1, 4, 7, 1, 4, 7, \ldots$. What is the 11th term?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The cycle has 3 terms. Divide 11 by 3 and look at the remainder.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$11 = 3\times3 + 2$, so the 11th term is the second term of a cycle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The second term of the cycle is $4$, so the 11th term is $4$.

You can check by counting in threes: positions $1, 4, 7, 10$ are all 1, so position
11 is the next one, which is 4.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A table shows position 1 with term 5, position 2 with term 10, position 3 with
term 15 and position 4 with term 20. What is the term at position 5?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the change between the terms in the table.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10-5=5$ and $15-10=5$, so the terms go up by 5 each time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$20+5=25$$

The term at position 5 is $25$. The table is the 5 times table, so position 5 gives
$5\times5=25$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Continue the pattern $30, 25, 20, \ldots$ by two terms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The pattern goes down, so the change is a subtraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$25-30=-5$, so the pattern subtracts 5 each time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$20-5=15 \qquad 15-5=10$$

The next two terms are $15$ and $10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the missing term: $2, 9, 16, ?, 30$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use two terms that are next to each other and known.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$9-2=7$ and $30-16=14$. The pattern adds 7 each time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$16+7=23$$

The missing term is $23$. Check it against the next term: $23+7=30$, which matches.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A pattern of four shapes repeats: star, moon, sun, cloud, then starts again.
What shape is at position 15?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The cycle has 4 shapes. Divide 15 by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$15 = 4\times3 + 3$, so position 15 is the third shape of a cycle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The third shape of the cycle is the sun, so position 15 is a sun.

Check: positions $4, 8, 12$ are clouds, so position 15 is three shapes later, which is
the sun.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A pattern starts with 8 tiles and adds 6 tiles each time. Write the number of
tiles in designs 1, 2, 3 and 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start at 8, then add 6 for each new design.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8+6=14$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8, 14, 20, 26$$

Each design has 6 more tiles than the one before. Design 1 has 8, design 2 has 14,
design 3 has 20, and design 4 has 26.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Which is larger, the 10th term of $4, 8, 12, \ldots$ or the 10th term of $6, 12,
18, \ldots$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You do not need to list ten terms. Multiply the step by 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first pattern has 10th term $4\times10=40$. The second has 10th term $6\times10=60$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The 10th term of $6, 12, 18, \ldots$ is larger, because $60$ is bigger than $40$.

Both patterns start at their step, so each 10th term is the step multiplied by 10.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Round 47 to the nearest ten.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the two tens that 47 sits between: 40 and 50. Which is it closer to?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The units digit is 7, which is 5 or more, so round up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$50$. Rounding is a useful check on any estimate, and you will use it again when you
read values from a graph in Section 9.5.

</details>
</details>
</details>
