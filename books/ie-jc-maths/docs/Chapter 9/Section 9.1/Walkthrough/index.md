# 9.1 Repeating patterns and tables — Learn

**Prerequisites:** counting, and the multiplication tables.

**Objectives:** describe a pattern in words; continue a repeating pattern; record a
growing pattern in a table; read a table to find the next term.

By the end of this section, you can look at a pattern, say how it works, and put it in
a table.

## Repeating patterns

A **pattern** is a set of things that follow a rule. A **sequence** is a list of
numbers in a pattern. Each number in the list is a **term**.

Some patterns repeat the same block again and again. The block that repeats is the
**cycle**.

$$2, 5, 8, 2, 5, 8, 2, 5, 8, \ldots$$

The cycle here is $2, 5, 8$. It is 3 terms long. After the 8, the cycle starts again
from the 2.

To find the term at a position, divide the position by the length of the cycle. The
remainder tells you which term of the cycle you land on.

**Example 1.** The cycle is $2, 5, 8$. What is the 14th term?

The cycle has 3 terms. Work out how many whole cycles fit into 14.

$$14 = 3\times4 + 2$$

So 14 is 4 whole cycles and 2 more. The 14th term is the second term of a cycle. It
is $5$.

**Example 2.** The cycle is triangle, circle, square. What shape is at position 10?

The cycle has 3 shapes.

$$10 = 3\times3 + 1$$

So position 10 is the first shape of a cycle. It is a triangle.

## Growing patterns and tables

Other patterns do not repeat. They grow or shrink, and every term is different. You
record these in a **table**.

A table has a row for the **position** and a row for the **term**. The position is the
term's place in the list: 1st, 2nd, 3rd.

**Example 3.** A tile design uses 4 tiles in design 1, 7 tiles in design 2 and 10 tiles
in design 3. Record this in a table and find the number of tiles in design 5.

| Position | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tiles | 4 | 7 | 10 | 13 | 16 |

The number of tiles goes up by 3 each time. So design 4 uses $10+3=13$ tiles, and
design 5 uses $13+3=16$ tiles.

**Example 4.** A pattern starts at 20 and goes down by 4 each time: $20, 16, 12, 8,
\ldots$. What is the 5th term?

$$8 - 4 = 4$$

The 5th term is $4$.

## Describing a pattern in words

To describe a growing pattern, say two things: where it starts, and what happens each
step.

For $4, 7, 10, 13, \ldots$: "Start at 4, and add 3 each time."

For $20, 16, 12, 8, \ldots$: "Start at 20, and subtract 4 each time."

> **Watch out.** The first term is not the change between terms. In $4, 7, 10,
> \ldots$, the first term is 4 and the change is 3. To describe the pattern, say both.
> If you say only "add 3", the reader does not know where the pattern starts.
>
> **Re-check:** write the first three terms from your description. If they are $4, 7,
> 10$, the description is complete.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The three savings plans start like this.

| Week | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aoife (€) | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 |
| Ben (€) | 1 | 4 | 9 | 16 |
| Cara (€) | 1 | 2 | 4 | 8 |

1. Describe Aoife's plan in words. Say where it starts and what happens each week.
2. Describe Cara's plan in words.
3. Ben's amounts are the square numbers. Which week first has Ben saving more than
   €20?

In Section 9.2 you will write a rule for Aoife's plan.

## You should now be able to

- describe a repeating pattern and find the term at a position;
- record a growing pattern in a table;
- find the change between terms and use it to find the next term;
- describe a growing pattern in words, naming both the start and the change.
