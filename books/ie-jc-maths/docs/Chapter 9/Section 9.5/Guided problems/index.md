# 9.5 Converting between representations — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $4\times6+1$.
2. A point is 2 across and 5 up. Write its coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $25$. Multiply before you add: $24+1=25$.
2. $(2, 5)$. The first coordinate is the distance across, and the second is the
   distance up.

Multiplying before adding is how you put a position into a rule, and coordinates are
how you plot the points.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Make a table for the rule $2n+1$ at positions 1 to 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put each position into the rule one at a time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2(1)+1=3$ and $2(2)+1=5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

| Position $n$ | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Term $2n+1$ | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 |

The terms are $3, 5, 7, 9$. The common difference is 2, which is the number that
multiplies $n$ in the rule.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A graph passes through $(1, 4)$, $(2, 7)$ and $(3, 10)$. Find the rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the change in the second coordinate as the first coordinate goes up by 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From $(1,4)$ to $(2,7)$ the term rises by 3, so the rule starts with $3n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The rule is $3n+1$, because at position 1, $3(1)=3$ and you need 1 more to reach 4.

Check position 3: $3(3)+1=10$. It matches.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A graph passes through $(1, 5)$, $(2, 9)$ and $(3, 13)$. Find the rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The points rise by the same amount each step, so the rule is linear.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The common difference is 4, so start with $4n$. At position 1, $4(1)=4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

You need 1 more to reach 5, so the rule is $4n+1$.

Check position 2: $4(2)+1=9$. It matches.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A graph curves through $(1, 1)$, $(2, 4)$ and $(3, 9)$. Find the rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The points are the square numbers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1^2=1$, $2^2=4$ and $3^2=9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The rule is $n^2$. The first differences are $3, 5, 7$, which grow, so the graph curves
upwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A graph passes through $(1, 2)$, $(2, 4)$ and $(3, 8)$. Find the rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide each term by the one before it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4\div2=2$ and $8\div4=2$, so the multiplier is 2 and the first term is 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The rule is $2^n$. Check position 1: $2^1=2$. Check position 3: $2^3=8$. Both match.

The multiplier being constant means the pattern is exponential.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A pattern starts at 3 and adds 7 each time. Write its general term.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The common difference is 7, so start with $7n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The $7n$ pattern is $7, 14, 21, \ldots$, and each term here is 4 less.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The general term is $7n-4$.

Check position 1: $7(1)-4=3$. Check position 2: $7(2)-4=10$, which is $3+7$. Both
match.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A table shows position 1 with term 6, position 2 with term 10, position 3 with
term 14 and position 4 with term 18. Find the term at position 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the common difference, then extend the table or use a rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The common difference is 4, so the rule is $4n+2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4(6)+2=26$$

The term at position 6 is $26$. You can check by extending the table: position 5 is
22, and position 6 is 26.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Which rule gives a straight-line graph, $5n-2$ or $n^2$? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the first differences of each rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For $5n-2$, the terms are $3, 8, 13$, and the differences are $5, 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$5n-2$ gives a straight line, because its first differences are constant at 5. The
rule $n^2$ gives $1, 4, 9$, whose differences $3, 5$ grow, so its graph curves.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $8-3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtracting gives a difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8-3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5$$

Reading a graph is mostly about differences, so quick subtraction helps.
