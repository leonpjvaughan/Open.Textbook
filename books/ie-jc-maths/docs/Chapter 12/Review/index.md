# Chapter 12: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The school musical.** Adult tickets cost €12 and student tickets cost €8. On the first
night, 200 tickets are sold and €2040 is taken at the door. The hall costs €150 to hire,
and the hospice is to receive at least €1500.

**Step 1. Name the unknowns.** Let $a$ be the number of adult tickets and $s$ the number
of student tickets.

**Step 2. Write two equations.** The total number of tickets gives one equation, and the
money taken gives the other.

$$a+s=200 \qquad \text{and} \qquad 12a+8s=2040$$

**Step 3. Solve by substitution.** Make $s$ the subject of the first equation, and
substitute into the second.

$$s=200-a$$

$$12a+8(200-a)=2040$$

$$12a+1600-8a=2040 \qquad 4a+1600=2040 \qquad 4a=440 \qquad a=110$$

Then $s=200-110=90$.

**The musical sold 110 adult tickets and 90 student tickets.**

**Step 4. Check the ticket pair.** Substitute both values into the money equation.

$$12\times110+8\times90=1320+720=2040$$

That is the money taken at the door, so the pair is right. The same pair comes from
elimination: multiply $a+s=200$ by 8 to get $8a+8s=1600$, and subtract it from
$12a+8s=2040$ to get $4a=440$.

**Step 5. The money for the hospice.** Subtract the hall hire from the money taken.

$$2040-150=1890$$

**The hospice receives €1890.**

**Step 6. Check the target.** The target was at least €1500, and $1890\geq1500$. The
target is reached, and the amount over it is $1890-1500=390$ euro.

**Step 7. The worst case.** Suppose every ticket sold were a student ticket at €8. The
hospice would receive $8n-150$, and it needs at least €1500.

$$8n-150\geq1500$$

Here $n$ is the number of student tickets.

$$8n\geq1650 \qquad n\geq206.25$$

A ticket count is a whole number, so round up.

$$n\geq207$$

**At least 207 student tickets are needed in the worst case.**

**Step 8. Check the boundary.** Work out the money for 206 and for 207 tickets.

$$8\times206-150=1498 \qquad 8\times207-150=1506$$

The value 206 is short of €1500, and 207 reaches it. So 207 is the smallest whole
number that works.

**Step 9. Draw the solution set.** On a number line for whole numbers, the graph is a
row of filled dots starting at 207. On a number line for real numbers, the graph is a
solid line starting at 206.25.

The musical sold 110 adult and 90 student tickets, the hospice receives €1890, and at
least 207 student tickets would be needed in the worst case. Each step used one of the
sections: a linear equation, an equation with a bracket, simultaneous equations by two
methods, an inequality, a number-line graph, and a chosen method.

## What you can now do

- solve a linear equation by undoing the operations in reverse order;
- solve an equation with a bracket or a fraction;
- solve simultaneous equations by substitution;
- solve simultaneous equations by elimination;
- solve a linear inequality, and turn the sign when you divide by a negative number;
- draw the solution set of an inequality on a number line for whole numbers, integers
  and real numbers;

  trial-and-improvement method;
- check every answer in the original equation or inequality.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you predicted whether more adult or more student tickets
were sold, and whether the hospice received closer to €1500 or €2000.

More student tickets were sold: 90 against 110 adult tickets. The hospice received
€1890, which is closer to €2000 than to €1500. If you reasoned that the money is close
to $200\times10=2000$, your prediction was a good one.

You also wrote down two ways of paying for something and a rule for each. Take those two
rules now. Write each one with letters, and decide whether you have one unknown or two.
If you have two, you need two equations, and you can solve them together. Then check
your answer in both rules. That is the whole of this chapter in one piece of work.
