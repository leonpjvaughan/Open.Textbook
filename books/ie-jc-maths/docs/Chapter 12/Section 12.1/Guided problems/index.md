# 12.1 Solving linear equations — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $12-7$.
2. Work out $15\div3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $5$. Subtracting undoes adding, and it is the first step in many equations here.
2. $5$. Dividing undoes multiplying.

Both answers are 5 by accident, and that is worth noticing. The two equations
$x+7=12$ and $3x=15$ have the same solution, but the operation you undo is different
in each one.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $x+8=15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 8 is added to $x$. What undoes adding?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 8 from both sides.

$$x+8-8=15-8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=7$$

Check: $7+8=15$. The operation is done to both sides, so the balance is kept.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $x-6=9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 6 is subtracted from $x$. What undoes subtracting?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 6 to both sides.

$$x-6+6=9+6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=15$$

Check: $15-6=9$. Adding undoes subtracting.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $7x=42$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$ is multiplied by 7. What undoes multiplying?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide both sides by 7.

$$\frac{7x}{7}=\frac{42}{7}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=6$$

Check: $7\times6=42$. Dividing undoes multiplying.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $2x+5=17$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Undo the addition first, and then the multiplication.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract 5 from both sides: $2x=12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Divide both sides by 2.

$$x=6$$

Check: $2\times6+5=17$. Working in reverse order means the addition goes first.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $4x-3=25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add 3 to both sides first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4x-3+3=25+3 \qquad 4x=28$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Divide both sides by 4.

$$x=7$$

Check: $4\times7-3=25$. Both operations are undone, in reverse order.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Solve $6x+1=3x+16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Collect the letters on one side first. Subtract $3x$ from both sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6x-3x+1=16 \qquad 3x+1=16$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Subtract 1: $3x=15$. Divide by 3.

$$x=5$$

Check: $6\times5+1=31$ and $3\times5+16=31$. Both sides agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Solve $5x-4=2x+11$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract $2x$ from both sides, and then undo the subtraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x-4=11 \qquad 3x=15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=5$$

Check: $5\times5-4=21$ and $2\times5+11=21$. The letters were collected before the numbers.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Solve $8-3x=2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 8 from both sides first. The $x$ term will be negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-3x=2-8 \qquad -3x=-6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Divide both sides by $-3$.

$$x=2$$

Check: $8-3\times2=8-6=2$. Dividing a negative by a negative gives a positive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** The chapter problem has 200 tickets in total, made of 110 adult tickets and $s$
student tickets. Solve $110+s=200$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 110 is added to $s$. Subtract it from both sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$110+s-110=200-110$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s=90$$

So 90 student tickets were sold. Check: $110+90=200$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Is $x=4$ a solution of $2x+3=11$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 10.3. Put 4 in place of $x$ and work out the left side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times4+3=8+3=11$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The left side is 11, which equals the right side. So $x=4$ is a solution.

Solving $2x+3=11$ gives $x=4$ as well. Substituting a value checks an answer, and
solving finds it.

</details>
</details>
</details>
