# 12.1 Solving linear equations — Learn

**Prerequisites:** substituting into expressions from Section 10.2, and the inverse
operations from Section 10.3.

**Objectives:** say what a solution is; solve a one-step equation; solve a two-step
equation; solve an equation with the letter on both sides.

By the end of this section, you can solve a linear equation by undoing the operations
one at a time.

## What a solution is

An **equation** says that two expressions are equal. The equals sign is the middle of
the statement.

$$x+7=12$$

A **solution** is a value of the letter that makes the equation true. For $x+7=12$,
the solution is $x=5$, because $5+7=12$.

A **linear equation** has a letter with no power higher than one. The letter is not
squared, and it is not in the bottom of a fraction. Every equation in this section is
linear.

## Keeping the balance

An equation is like a balance that stays level. If you do something to the left side,
you must do the same thing to the right side. The two sides stay equal, so the
equation stays true.

Each operation is undone by its **inverse**:

| Operation | Inverse |
|---|---|
| add | subtract |
| subtract | add |
| multiply | divide |
| divide | multiply |

To solve an equation, undo the operations that are applied to the letter. Work in the
reverse of the order they were applied. The last operation applied is the first one
you undo.

## One-step equations

**Example 1.** Solve $x+7=12$.

The 7 is added to $x$, so subtract 7 from both sides.

$$x+7-7=12-7$$

$$x=5$$

**Example 2.** Solve $x-9=4$.

The 9 is subtracted from $x$, so add 9 to both sides.

$$x-9+9=4+9$$

$$x=13$$

**Example 3.** Solve $4x=20$.

The $x$ is multiplied by 4, so divide both sides by 4.

$$\frac{4x}{4}=\frac{20}{4}$$

$$x=5$$

## Two-step equations

Two operations are applied to the letter. Undo the addition or subtraction first, and
then undo the multiplication or division.

**Example 4.** Solve $3x-5=10$.

Add 5 to both sides, and then divide by 3.

$$3x-5+5=10+5 \qquad 3x=15$$

$$x=\frac{15}{3}=5$$

**Example 5.** Solve $2x+7=19$.

Subtract 7 from both sides, and then divide by 2.

$$2x=19-7 \qquad 2x=12$$

$$x=6$$

## The letter on both sides

When the letter appears on both sides, collect the letters on one side first. Then the
equation becomes a two-step equation, and you already know how to finish it.

**Example 6.** Solve $5x-2=3x+8$.

Subtract $3x$ from both sides.

$$5x-3x-2=8 \qquad 2x-2=8$$

Add 2 to both sides, and then divide by 2.

$$2x=10 \qquad x=5$$

**Example 7.** Solve $4x+3=2x+11$.

Subtract $2x$ from both sides, and then subtract 3.

$$2x+3=11 \qquad 2x=8 \qquad x=4$$

Check the answer in the original equation: $4\times4+3=19$ and $2\times4+11=19$. Both
sides agree.

**Example 8.** Solve $9-2x=1$.

Subtract 9 from both sides, and then divide by $-2$.

$$-2x=1-9 \qquad -2x=-8 \qquad x=4$$

Check: $9-2\times4=9-8=1$.

> **Watch out.** Whatever you do to one side, do to the other side as well. In
> $3x-5=10$, the common error is to add 5 to the left side only and write $3x=10$.
> That breaks the balance, and the solution is wrong.
>
> **Re-check:** write the operation under both sides before you work it out. The line
> $3x-5+5=10+5$ shows the balance is kept, and the next line $3x=15$ follows from it.
> Put the answer back into the original equation to be sure.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The musical sold 110 adult tickets and some student
tickets, and 200 tickets were sold in total.

1. Let $s$ be the number of student tickets. Write an equation for the total.
2. Solve the equation by subtracting 110 from both sides.
3. How many student tickets were sold?

In Section 12.2 you will use the ticket prices in an equation with a bracket.

## You should now be able to

- say what a solution of an equation is;
- undo an addition, a subtraction, a multiplication or a division on both sides;
- solve a two-step equation by undoing the operations in reverse order;
- collect the letter on one side when it appears on both sides;
- check a solution by putting it back into the original equation.
