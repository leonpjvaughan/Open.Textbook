# 12.4 Simultaneous equations by elimination — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Simplify $4x-2x$.
2. Solve $3x=18$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2x$. Subtracting the like terms leaves $2x$.
2. $x=6$. Divide both sides by 3.

Elimination adds or subtracts whole equations, and then leaves an equation like these.
Collecting like terms is what makes the letter disappear.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $x+y=9$ and $x-y=5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$ terms are $+y$ and $-y$, which are opposite. Add the equations.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x=14$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=7$$

Then $7+y=9$, so $y=2$. Check: $7-2=5$. Adding removed the $y$ terms.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $2x+y=13$ and $2x-y=7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$ terms are opposite, so add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4x=20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=5$$

Then $2\times5+y=13$, so $y=3$. Check: $2\times5-3=7$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $4x+y=17$ and $x+y=8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$ terms are both $+y$, so subtract the second equation from the first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(4x+y)-(x+y)=17-8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x=9 \qquad x=3$$

Then $3+y=8$, so $y=5$. Check: $4\times3+5=17$. The bracket removes the whole $x+y$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $3x+2y=20$ and $3x-2y=4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$ terms are opposite, so add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6x=24$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=4$$

Then $3\times4+2y=20$, so $2y=8$ and $y=4$. Check: $3\times4-2\times4=4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $2x+5y=19$ and $2x+y=7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$ terms match at $2x$, so subtract.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(2x+5y)-(2x+y)=19-7 \qquad 4y=12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y=3$$

Then $2x+3=7$, so $2x=4$ and $x=2$. Check: $2\times2+5\times3=19$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Solve $3x+2y=16$ and $2x+2y=12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$ terms match at $2y$. Subtract the second equation from the first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x=4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Then $2\times4+2y=12$, so $8+2y=12$ and $y=2$. Check: $3\times4+2\times2=16$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Solve $3x+2y=19$ and $2x-y=8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$ terms do not match. Multiply the second equation by 2, so the $y$ terms become
$+2y$ and $-2y$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4x-2y=16$$

Now add this to $3x+2y=19$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7x=35 \qquad x=5$$

Then $2\times5-y=8$, so $y=2$. Check: $3\times5+2\times2=19$. Multiplying by 2 changes
every term in the second equation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Is $(3,2)$ a solution of $x+y=5$ and $2x-y=4$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put $x=3$ and $y=2$ into each equation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3+2=5 \qquad 2\times3-2=4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both statements are true, so $(3,2)$ is a solution of the pair.

A solution of simultaneous equations must satisfy both equations. One is not enough.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** The chapter problem gives $a+s=200$ and $12a+8s=2040$. Solve the pair by
elimination.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply $a+s=200$ by 8 to match the $s$ terms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8a+8s=1600$$

Subtract this from $12a+8s=2040$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4a=440 \qquad a=110$$

Then $110+s=200$, so $s=90$. So 110 adult tickets and 90 student tickets were sold.

Check: $12\times110+8\times90=2040$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**10.** Solve $7x-2=33$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 12.1. Add 2 to both sides first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$7x=35$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=5$$

Elimination leaves an equation in one letter, and solving it is this skill. The two
methods meet at the same final step.
