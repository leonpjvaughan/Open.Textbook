# 12.4 Simultaneous equations by elimination — Learn

**Prerequisites:** simultaneous equations by substitution from Section 12.3, and
collecting like terms from Section 11.1.

**Objectives:** decide whether to add or subtract two equations; remove one letter by
elimination; multiply one or both equations to match the coefficients; solve and check
the pair.

By the end of this section, you can solve a pair of simultaneous equations by
elimination.

## Adding and subtracting to remove a letter

Substitution works for every pair, and sometimes another method is quicker. In
**elimination**, you add or subtract the two equations so that one letter disappears.

Look at the coefficients, which are the numbers in front of the letters.

- If the two coefficients of a letter are **opposite**, such as $+y$ and $-y$, add the
  equations.
- If the two coefficients are **the same**, such as $2x$ and $2x$, subtract one
  equation from the other.

**Example 1.** Solve $x+y=10$ and $x-y=4$.

The $y$ terms are $+y$ and $-y$, which are opposite, so add the equations.

$$(x+y)+(x-y)=10+4$$

$$2x=14 \qquad x=7$$

Substitute $x=7$ into $x+y=10$.

$$7+y=10 \qquad y=3$$

The solution is $x=7$ and $y=3$. Check: $7-3=4$.

**Example 2.** Solve $2x+y=11$ and $2x-y=5$.

The $y$ terms are opposite, so add.

$$4x=16 \qquad x=4$$

Then $2\times4+y=11$, so $y=3$. Check: $2\times4-3=5$.

**Example 3.** Solve $5x+y=21$ and $2x+y=9$.

The $y$ terms are both $+y$, so they are the same. Subtract the second equation from the
first.

$$(5x+y)-(2x+y)=21-9$$

$$5x+y-2x-y=12 \qquad 3x=12 \qquad x=4$$

Then $2\times4+y=9$, so $y=1$. Check: $5\times4+1=21$.

## Multiplying an equation first

Sometimes the coefficients do not match. Multiply one equation, or both, so that one
letter has the same coefficient in each. Multiply **every** term.

**Example 4.** Solve $3x+2y=18$ and $x+2y=10$.

The $y$ terms already match at $2y$. Subtract the second equation from the first.

$$2x=8 \qquad x=4$$

Then $4+2y=10$, so $2y=6$ and $y=3$. Check: $3\times4+2\times3=18$.

**Example 5.** Solve $2x+3y=12$ and $5x-2y=11$.

Neither pair matches. Multiply the first equation by 2 and the second by 3, so that the
$y$ terms become $6y$ and $-6y$.

$$4x+6y=24 \qquad 15x-6y=33$$

Now add, because the $y$ terms are opposite.

$$19x=57 \qquad x=3$$

Substitute $x=3$ into $2x+3y=12$.

$$6+3y=12 \qquad 3y=6 \qquad y=2$$

The solution is $x=3$ and $y=2$. Check: $5\times3-2\times2=11$.

## The chapter problem by elimination

The pair from the musical is $a+s=200$ and $12a+8s=2040$. The coefficients of $s$ are 1
and 8, so multiply the first equation by 8 to match them.

$$8a+8s=1600$$

Now subtract this from the money equation.

$$(12a+8s)-(8a+8s)=2040-1600$$

$$4a=440 \qquad a=110$$

Then $110+s=200$, so $s=90$. So 110 adult tickets and 90 student tickets were sold.
Check: $12\times110+8\times90=1320+720=2040$.

> **Watch out.** When you subtract, subtract the whole equation. In Example 3, the
> common error is to write $5x+y-2x+y=12$, which adds the $y$ terms instead of removing
> them. The correct line keeps the bracket: $(5x+y)-(2x+y)=3x$.
>
> **Re-check:** put both values into the equation you did not use for the final step. If
> the two sides balance, the elimination kept the equations equal.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem.

1. Multiply $a+s=200$ by 8.
2. Subtract the result from $12a+8s=2040$.
3. Solve for $a$, and then find $s$.
4. Check both values in $12a+8s=2040$.

In Section 12.5 you will turn to questions with a range of answers rather than one
answer.

## You should now be able to

- decide whether to add or to subtract the two equations;
- remove one letter by elimination;
- multiply an equation, or both, so that a coefficient matches;
- multiply every term when you multiply an equation;
- find both values and check them in both equations.
