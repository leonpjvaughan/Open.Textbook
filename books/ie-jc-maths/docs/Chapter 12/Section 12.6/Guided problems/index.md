# 12.6 Graphing the solution set of an inequality — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Solve $x+3=8$.
2. Work out $-1+5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x=5$. Subtract 3 from both sides.
2. $4$. Adding a positive number to a negative number moves to the right.

The first is the algebra behind the graph, and the second is the direction you move
along the number line.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Draw the solution set of $x>2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sign is $>$, so the endpoint is not included. Which way do larger values lie?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

An open circle at 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An open circle at 2, with an arrow to the right. Larger values lie to the right, and the
open circle shows that 2 itself is not included.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Draw the solution set of $x\leq-1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sign is $\leq$, so the endpoint is included.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A closed circle at $-1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A closed circle at $-1$, with an arrow to the left. The closed circle shows that $-1$
is included, and the arrow shows that smaller values are in the set.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Draw the solution set of $x\geq3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sign is $\geq$, so the circle is closed and the arrow points right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A closed circle at 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A closed circle at 3, with an arrow to the right. Check the endpoint: $3\geq3$ is true,
so 3 is included.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $2x<10$ and draw its solution set.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide both sides by 2, which is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x<5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An open circle at 5, with an arrow to the left. The sign is $<$, so 5 is not included,
and the solution set is smaller than 5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $x+4\leq1$ and draw its solution set.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 4 from both sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x\leq1-4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x\leq-3$$

A closed circle at $-3$, with an arrow to the left. The sign is $\leq$, so $-3$ is
included.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Solve $-2<x+1\leq4$ and draw its solution set.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 1 from all three parts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-2-1<x\leq4-1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$-3<x\leq3$$

An open circle at $-3$ and a closed circle at 3, with the part between them shaded. The
left sign is $<$, and the right sign is $\leq$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Solve $2\leq3x-1<11$ and draw its solution set.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add 1 to all three parts, and then divide all three by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\leq3x<12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1\leq x<4$$

A closed circle at 1 and an open circle at 4, with the part between them shaded.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A number line shows an open circle at $-2$ with an arrow to the right. Write the
inequality.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The arrow shows the direction, and the circle shows whether the endpoint is included.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The values in the set are larger than $-2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x>-2$$

The open circle means $-2$ is not included, so the sign is $>$ rather than $\geq$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** The chapter problem gives $8n-150\geq1500$, so $n\geq206.25$. Draw the solution
set for whole numbers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A whole number cannot be 206.25. What is the first whole number that works?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first whole number is 207.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Dots at 207, 208, 209 and so on to the right. The dot at 207 is filled, because 207 is
included.

Check: 206 gives $8\times206-150=1498$, which is short. 207 gives $1506$, which reaches
the target.

</details>
</details>
</details>
