# Chapter 12: Equations and inequalities

An equation says that two amounts are equal. You already know how to read one. In
this chapter you learn how to solve one, which means finding the value of the letter
that makes the statement true.

Some questions have two unknowns, and then one equation is not enough. You need two
equations, and you solve them together. Other questions ask for a range of values
rather than one value, and then you use an inequality.

## The chapter problem

**The school musical.** The school is putting on a musical to raise money for the
local hospice.

- An adult ticket costs €12.
- A student ticket costs €8.
- On the first night, 200 tickets are sold.
- The money taken at the door is €2040.
- The hall costs €150 to hire.
- The hospice is to receive at least €1500.

Before you calculate anything, write down a prediction. Were more adult tickets sold,
or more student tickets? Do you think the hospice received closer to €1500 or closer
to €2000?

By the end of the chapter you can find how many of each ticket were sold, work out
what the hospice received, and find the smallest number of tickets that would reach
the target. You will return to this musical in every section.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- solve a linear equation by doing the inverse operation to both sides;
- solve an equation with a bracket or a fraction;
- solve simultaneous equations by substitution;
- solve simultaneous equations by elimination;
- solve a linear inequality, and know when the sign turns around;
- graph the solution set of an inequality on the number line;
- choose between a graphic, a numeric and an algebraic method, and say why.

## Where it is used

A phone plan, a bus fare and a wage are all equations waiting for a value. Two
unknowns appear whenever you compare two plans, because each plan gives one equation.
Inequalities answer the questions with a limit in them: how many people fit, how much
you can spend, how long the battery lasts. Scientists, engineers and business people
choose between a graph, a table and algebra every day, and the choice saves them time.
