# 12.2 Equations with brackets and fractions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** solving linear equations from Section 12.1, and multiplying out a
single bracket from Section 11.2.

**Objectives:** solve an equation that contains a bracket; choose between expanding
the bracket and dividing by the number in front; solve an equation with a fraction;
clear a fraction by multiplying both sides.

By the end of this section, you can solve an equation with a bracket or a fraction by
clearing it first.

## Equations with a bracket

A bracket groups the operations inside it. When an equation contains a bracket, you
have two routes. You can multiply out the bracket, or you can divide both sides by the
number in front of it. Both routes give the same solution.

**Example 1.** Solve $3(x+4)=21$.

Route 1: divide both sides by 3, because the bracket is multiplied by 3.

$$\frac{3(x+4)}{3}=\frac{21}{3} \qquad x+4=7$$

Subtract 4 from both sides.

$$x=3$$

Route 2: multiply out the bracket first.

$$3x+12=21 \qquad 3x=9 \qquad x=3$$

Both routes reach $x=3$. Divide first when the number divides the other side exactly,
and expand when it does not.

**Example 2.** Solve $5(x+2)=3x+16$.

Here the bracket has to be expanded, because $3x+16$ does not divide by 5.

$$5x+10=3x+16$$

Subtract $3x$ from both sides, and then subtract 10.

$$2x+10=16 \qquad 2x=6 \qquad x=3$$

Check: $5(3+2)=25$ and $3\times3+16=25$.

## The number in front multiplies every term

When you expand a bracket, the number in front multiplies every term inside it. The
same is true when the bracket has a minus sign in front, which turns every sign
around.

**Example 3.** Solve $4(x-1)=2x+6$.

$$4x-4=2x+6$$

Subtract $2x$ from both sides, and then add 4.

$$2x-4=6 \qquad 2x=10 \qquad x=5$$

**Example 4.** Solve $2(x+3)=5x-3$.

$$2x+6=5x-3$$

Subtract $2x$ from both sides.

$$6=3x-3$$

Add 3 to both sides, and then divide by 3.

$$9=3x \qquad x=3$$

## Equations with a fraction

The bar of a fraction is a bracket around the whole top and the whole bottom. Work out
the top first, and then divide.

To solve an equation with a fraction, multiply both sides by the number underneath.
That clears the fraction and leaves a simpler equation.

**Example 5.** Solve $\dfrac{x}{3}+2=5$.

Subtract 2 from both sides first.

$$\frac{x}{3}=3$$

Multiply both sides by 3.

$$x=9$$

**Example 6.** Solve $\dfrac{x+1}{4}=3$.

Multiply both sides by 4. The bar groups the whole top, so the 4 multiplies all of
$x+1$.

$$x+1=12 \qquad x=11$$

**Example 7.** Solve $\dfrac{2x+1}{3}=5$.

Multiply both sides by 3, and then undo the operations.

$$2x+1=15 \qquad 2x=14 \qquad x=7$$

Check: $\dfrac{2\times7+1}{3}=\dfrac{15}{3}=5$.

**Example 8.** Solve $\dfrac{x-2}{5}=4$.

$$x-2=20 \qquad x=22$$

> **Watch out.** Clearing a fraction means multiplying the whole of the other side, and
> every term in the bracket. From $\dfrac{2x+1}{3}=5$, the common error is to multiply
> only the $2x$ and write $2x+1=5\times3$ in a way that drops the 1, or to write
> $2x+\frac{1}{3}=15$.
>
> **Re-check:** put the whole top in brackets before you multiply: $3\times\frac{(2x+1)}{3}=3\times5$,
> so $2x+1=15$. Then check the answer in the original fraction.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. Adult tickets cost €12 and student tickets cost €8. Let
$a$ be the number of adult tickets. There are 200 tickets in total, so the number of
student tickets is $200-a$. The money taken is $12a+8(200-a)$, and it equals €2040.

1. Write the equation $12a+8(200-a)=2040$.
2. Expand the bracket and collect the $a$ terms.
3. Solve the equation to find the number of adult tickets.

In Section 12.3 you will solve the same pair of facts by substitution.

## You should now be able to

- solve an equation with a bracket by dividing or by expanding;
- multiply every term inside a bracket by the number in front;
- solve an equation with a fraction by multiplying both sides by the denominator;
- keep a fraction bar as a bracket around the whole top;
- check the answer in the original equation.
