# 12.2 Equations with brackets and fractions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Expand $4(x-2)$.
2. Solve $x+5=12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4x-8$. Multiply each term inside the bracket by 4.
2. $x=7$. Subtract 5 from both sides.

This section joins the two skills. You expand the bracket, and then you solve the
equation that is left.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $2(x+5)=18$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bracket is multiplied by 2. Divide both sides by 2 first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x+5=9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=4$$

Check: $2(4+5)=2\times9=18$. Dividing first keeps the numbers small.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $3(x-2)=15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide both sides by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x-2=5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=7$$

Check: $3(7-2)=3\times5=15$. The bracket keeps the minus sign with the 2.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $5(x+1)=35$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide both sides by 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x+1=7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=6$$

Check: $5(6+1)=35$. Dividing by 5 undoes the multiplication outside the bracket.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $4(x+3)=2x+20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Expand the bracket, because $2x+20$ does not divide by 4 neatly.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4x+12=2x+20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Subtract $2x$: $2x+12=20$. Subtract 12: $2x=8$.

$$x=4$$

Check: $4(4+3)=28$ and $2\times4+20=28$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $3(x-1)=2x+5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Expand the bracket first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x-3=2x+5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Subtract $2x$: $x-3=5$. Add 3.

$$x=8$$

Check: $3(8-1)=21$ and $2\times8+5=21$. The $x$ terms are collected on one side.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Solve $\frac{x}{5}+1=4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 1 from both sides, and then multiply by 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{x}{5}=3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=15$$

Check: $\frac{15}{5}+1=3+1=4$. Multiplying by 5 clears the fraction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Solve $\frac{x+3}{2}=5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bar groups the whole top. Multiply both sides by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x+3=10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=7$$

Check: $\frac{7+3}{2}=\frac{10}{2}=5$. The 2 multiplies all of $x+3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Solve $\frac{2x-2}{3}=4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply both sides by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x-2=12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Add 2: $2x=14$. Divide by 2.

$$x=7$$

Check: $\frac{2\times7-2}{3}=\frac{12}{3}=4$. The bar groups the whole top.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** The chapter problem gives $12a+8(200-a)=2040$. Solve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Expand $8(200-a)$ first: multiply both terms by 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12a+1600-8a=2040 \qquad 4a+1600=2040$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Subtract 1600 from both sides: $4a=440$. Divide by 4.

$$a=110$$

So 110 adult tickets were sold. Check: $12\times110+8\times90=1320+720=2040$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Solve $4x-3=13$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 12.1. Undo the subtraction first, and then the multiplication.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 3 to both sides: $4x=16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=4$$

This is the same two-step idea you used in questions 1 to 5, with a bracket replaced by
a number.
