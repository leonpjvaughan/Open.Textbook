# 12.5 Linear inequalities — Learn

**Prerequisites:** solving linear equations from Section 12.1, and equations with
brackets and fractions from Section 12.2.

**Objectives:** read the four inequality signs; solve a linear inequality; know when
the sign turns around; keep the sign the same when dividing by a positive number.

By the end of this section, you can solve a linear inequality and state its solution
set.

## The four signs

An **inequality** compares two amounts instead of saying they are equal. It has a range
of answers rather than one answer.

| Sign | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $<$ | is less than |
| $>$ | is greater than |
| $\leq$ | is less than or equal to |
| $\geq$ | is greater than or equal to |

The signs $\leq$ and $\geq$ include the endpoint. The signs $<$ and $>$ do not.

## Solving an inequality

You solve an inequality in the same way as an equation. Undo the operations on both
sides, and keep the inequality sign the same.

**Example 1.** Solve $x+5>9$.

Subtract 5 from both sides.

$$x>4$$

Every value greater than 4 is a solution.

**Example 2.** Solve $3x\geq12$.

Divide both sides by 3, which is positive.

$$x\geq4$$

The endpoint 4 is included, because the sign is $\geq$.

**Example 3.** Solve $2x+1<11$.

Subtract 1 from both sides, and then divide by 2.

$$2x<10 \qquad x<5$$

**Example 4.** Solve $5x-3\geq2x+6$.

Subtract $2x$ from both sides, and then undo the rest.

$$3x-3\geq6 \qquad 3x\geq9 \qquad x\geq3$$

## When the sign turns around

Multiplying or dividing by a positive number keeps the sign the same. Multiplying or
dividing by a **negative** number turns the sign around.

This is worth seeing with numbers. Start with a true statement.

$$2<5$$

Multiply both sides by $-1$.

$$-2 \qquad \text{and} \qquad -5$$

Now $-2$ is greater than $-5$, not less. The sign must turn around.

$$-2>-5$$

So when you multiply or divide an inequality by a negative number, reverse the sign.

**Example 5.** Solve $-2x>6$.

Divide both sides by $-2$, and turn the sign around.

$$x<6\div(-2) \qquad x<-3$$

Check with $x=-4$: $-2\times(-4)=8$, and $8>6$, so $-4$ is a solution.

**Example 6.** Solve $10-3x\leq1$.

Subtract 10 from both sides.

$$-3x\leq-9$$

Divide by $-3$, and turn the sign around.

$$x\geq3$$

## Inequalities with brackets and fractions

Clear a bracket or a fraction first, exactly as in Section 12.2. Then solve the simpler
inequality that is left.

**Example 7.** Solve $4(x-1)<12$.

Divide both sides by 4, which is positive, so the sign stays.

$$x-1<3 \qquad x<4$$

**Example 8.** Solve $\dfrac{x+2}{3}>4$.

Multiply both sides by 3, which is positive, so the sign stays.

$$x+2>12 \qquad x>10$$

> **Watch out.** The sign turns around only when you multiply or divide by a negative
> number. In $-2x>6$, the common error is to write $x>-3$ and keep the sign. Dividing by
> $-2$ reverses it, so the correct answer is $x<-3$.
>
> **Re-check:** test a value on each side. For $x<-3$, try $x=-4$: $-2\times(-4)=8>6$,
> so it works. Try $x=0$: $-2\times0=0$, which is not greater than 6, so 0 is not in the
> solution set.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The hospice needs at least €1500. The hall costs €150,
and in the worst case each ticket brings in €8. Let $n$ be the number of tickets.

1. Write an inequality for the money the hospice receives.
2. Add 150 to both sides, and then divide by 8.
3. The boundary is not a whole number. What is the smallest whole number of tickets?

In Section 12.6 you will draw the solution set on a number line.

## You should now be able to

- read the four inequality signs and say which include the endpoint;
- solve a linear inequality in the same way as an equation;
- turn the sign around when you multiply or divide by a negative number;
- keep the sign the same when you multiply or divide by a positive number;
- clear a bracket or a fraction before solving.
