# 12.5 Linear inequalities — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Solve $x+4=9$.
2. Work out $-2\times(-3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x=5$. Subtract 4 from both sides.
2. $6$. The product of two negative numbers is positive.

Both matter here. The first step is the same as solving an equation, and the second is
the sign rule that decides whether the inequality sign turns around.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $x+7>10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 7 from both sides. The sign stays the same.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x>10-7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x>3$$

Every value greater than 3 is a solution. The endpoint 3 is not included, because the
sign is $>$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $x-2\leq5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add 2 to both sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x\leq5+2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x\leq7$$

The endpoint 7 is included, because the sign is $\leq$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $4x\geq20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide both sides by 4, which is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x\geq\frac{20}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x\geq5$$

Dividing by a positive number keeps the sign the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $2x-1<9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add 1 to both sides, and then divide by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x<10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x<5$$

The endpoint 5 is not included, because the sign is $<$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $4x+1\geq2x+7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract $2x$ from both sides first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x+1\geq7 \qquad 2x\geq6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x\geq3$$

Dividing by the positive number 2 keeps the sign the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Solve $-3x>12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide both sides by $-3$. The sign turns around.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x<12\div(-3)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x<-4$$

Check with $x=-5$: $-3\times(-5)=15$, and $15>12$, so $-5$ is a solution.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Solve $8-2x\leq2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 8 from both sides first. The $x$ term becomes negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-2x\leq2-8 \qquad -2x\leq-6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Divide by $-2$, and turn the sign around.

$$x\geq3$$

Check with $x=4$: $8-2\times4=0$, and $0\leq2$, so 4 is a solution.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Solve $3(x+2)<15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide both sides by 3, which is positive, and then subtract 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x+2<5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x<3$$

Dividing by the positive number 3 keeps the sign the same. Check with $x=2$:
$3(2+2)=12$, and $12<15$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** The chapter problem gives $8n-150\geq1500$. Solve it, and state the smallest
whole number of tickets.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add 150 to both sides, and then divide by 8. The boundary is not a whole number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8n\geq1650 \qquad n\geq206.25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A ticket count is a whole number, so round up.

$$n\geq207$$

Check: 206 tickets give $8\times206-150=1498$, which is short. 207 tickets give
$8\times207-150=1506$, which reaches the target.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Solve $2x+5=13$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 12.1. Subtract 5 from both sides, and then divide by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x=8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=4$$

Solving an inequality uses exactly these steps. The only new rule is the one about
dividing by a negative number.

</details>
