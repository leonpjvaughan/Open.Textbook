# 12.3 Simultaneous equations by substitution — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out the value of $x+3$ when $x=4$.
2. Solve $2x=10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $7$, because $4+3=7$.
2. $x=5$. Divide both sides by 2.

Substitution uses both skills. You work out an expression at a value, and then you
solve the equation that is left.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $y=x+2$ and $x+y=10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first equation has $y$ on its own. Substitute $x+2$ for $y$ in the second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x+(x+2)=10 \qquad 2x+2=10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x=8 \qquad x=4$$

Then $y=4+2=6$. Check: $4+6=10$. Both values are found, not only the first.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $y=3x$ and $x+y=16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $3x$ for $y$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x+3x=16 \qquad 4x=16$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=4$$

Then $y=3\times4=12$. Check: $4+12=16$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $y=x-3$ and $2x+y=9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $x-3$ for $y$, and keep the bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x+(x-3)=9 \qquad 3x-3=9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x=12 \qquad x=4$$

Then $y=4-3=1$. Check: $2\times4+1=9$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $x=y+5$ and $x+y=11$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Here $x$ is the subject of the first equation. Substitute $y+5$ for $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(y+5)+y=11 \qquad 2y+5=11$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2y=6 \qquad y=3$$

Then $x=3+5=8$. Check: $8+3=11$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $y=4-x$ and $3x-y=8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $4-x$ for $y$, and keep the bracket after the minus sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x-(4-x)=8 \qquad 3x-4+x=8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4x-4=8 \qquad 4x=12 \qquad x=3$$

Then $y=4-3=1$. Check: $3\times3-1=8$. The bracket turns $-(4-x)$ into $-4+x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Solve $y=2x+1$ and $y=x+6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both expressions equal $y$, so set them equal to each other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x+1=x+6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Subtract $x$: $x+1=6$, so $x=5$. Then $y=5+6=11$. Check: $2\times5+1=11$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Solve $y=x-1$ and $2x+3y=17$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $x-1$ for $y$ in the second equation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x+3(x-1)=17 \qquad 2x+3x-3=17$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5x-3=17 \qquad 5x=20 \qquad x=4$$

Then $y=4-1=3$. Check: $2\times4+3\times3=8+9=17$. The 3 multiplies both terms in the
bracket.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Is $(2,5)$ a solution of $y=x+3$ and $x+y=7$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put $x=2$ and $y=5$ into each equation and check both.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2+3=5 \qquad 2+5=7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both statements are true, so $(2,5)$ is a solution of the pair.

A solution of simultaneous equations must satisfy both equations. Satisfying one of
them is not enough.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** The chapter problem gives $a+s=200$ and $12a+8s=2040$. Solve the pair.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Make $s$ the subject of $a+s=200$, and substitute into the money equation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$s=200-a \qquad 12a+8(200-a)=2040$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$12a+1600-8a=2040 \qquad 4a=440 \qquad a=110$$

Then $s=200-110=90$. So 110 adult tickets and 90 student tickets were sold.

Check: $12\times110+8\times90=2040$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Solve $4x+5=25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 12.1. Undo the addition first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4x=20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x=5$$

Step 3 of the substitution method is exactly this skill. Once one letter is left, the
rest is a linear equation.
