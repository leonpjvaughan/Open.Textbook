# 7.1 Profit, loss and percentage profit — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $10\%$ of €80.
2. Work out $6.00-4.00$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. €8.00. Ten per cent is one tenth, and $80\div10=8$.
2. €2.00. This is a selling price minus a cost price, which is the profit.

A percentage of an amount and a subtraction are the two moves in every profit
question.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A shop buys a phone case for €4.00 and sells it for €6.00. Work out the
profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Profit is the selling price minus the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6.00-4.00$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6.00-4.00=2.00$$

The profit is €2.00. The selling price is the larger number, so the shop makes a
profit rather than a loss.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A shop buys a charger for €8.00 and sells it for €10.00. Work out the
percentage profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the profit. Then divide it by the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The profit is $10.00-8.00=2.00$. Now work out $\frac{2.00}{8.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{2.00}{8.00}\times100=25$$

The percentage profit is $25\%$. The base is the cost price, €8.00.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A shop buys a speaker for €20.00 and sells it for €15.00. Work out the loss
and the percentage loss.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The selling price is smaller than the cost price, so this is a loss.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The loss is $20.00-15.00=5.00$. Now work out $\frac{5.00}{20.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The loss is €5.00, and the percentage loss is
$\frac{5.00}{20.00}\times100=25\%$. A loss divides by the cost price, just as a
profit does.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A shop buys a game for €25.00 and sells it for €30.00. Work out the profit
and the percentage profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the profit first, then divide by the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The profit is $30.00-25.00=5.00$. Now work out $\frac{5.00}{25.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The profit is €5.00, and the percentage profit is
$\frac{5.00}{25.00}\times100=20\%$. Check it: $25.00\times1.20=30.00$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A shop buys a bag for €50.00 and sells it for €40.00 in a sale. Work out
the loss and the percentage loss.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bag sells for less than it cost, so the shop makes a loss.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The loss is $50.00-40.00=10.00$. Now work out $\frac{10.00}{50.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The loss is €10.00, and the percentage loss is
$\frac{10.00}{50.00}\times100=20\%$. A $20\%$ loss means the shop keeps $80\%$ of
the cost price, and $50.00\times0.80=40.00$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A shop buys a tablet for €120.00 and sells it for €150.00. Work out the
percentage profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The profit is $150.00-120.00$. Divide it by €120.00.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{30.00}{120.00}\times100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{30.00}{120.00}\times100=25$$

The percentage profit is $25\%$. The profit is €30.00, and the base is the cost
price of €120.00.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A shop buys 20 phone cases at €4.00 each and sells them all at €6.00 each.
Work out the total profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the profit on one case first, then multiply by 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

One case makes $6.00-4.00=2.00$ euro. Now work out $2.00\times20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2.00\times20=40.00$$

The total profit is €40.00. The percentage profit is still $50\%$, because each
case has the same cost price and the same selling price.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A shop buys a bag for €80.00 and wants a profit of $15\%$. Work out the
selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A profit of $15\%$ multiplies the cost price by $1.15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$80.00\times1.15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$80.00\times1.15=92.00$$

The selling price is €92.00. The profit is $80.00\times0.15=12.00$, and
$80.00+12.00=92.00$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** A shop buys a case for €4.00 and sells it for €6.00. A second shop buys a
case for €5.00 and sells it for €7.00. Which shop makes the better percentage
profit?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the percentage profit for each shop. The profits are both €2.00, but the
cost prices are different.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

First shop: $\frac{2.00}{4.00}\times100$. Second shop:
$\frac{2.00}{5.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first shop makes $\frac{2.00}{4.00}\times100=50\%$. The second shop makes
$\frac{2.00}{5.00}\times100=40\%$.

The first shop makes the better percentage profit. Both shops make €2.00, but the
first shop makes it on a smaller cost price, so the percentage is larger.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Increase €60.00 by $20\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 5.5. Multiply by $1.20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$60.00\times1.20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$60.00\times1.20=72.00$$

The new amount is €72.00. The increase is $60.00\times0.20=12.00$, and
$60.00+12.00=72.00$. This is the same move as finding a selling price from a cost
price and a percentage profit.

</details>
</details>
</details>
