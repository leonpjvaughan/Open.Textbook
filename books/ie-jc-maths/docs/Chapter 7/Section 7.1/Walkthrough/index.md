# 7.1 Profit, loss and percentage profit — Learn

**Prerequisites:** finding a percentage of an amount, and subtracting one decimal
from another.

**Objectives:** work out the profit or loss on a sale; write the profit or loss as
a percentage of the cost price; find a selling price from a cost price and a
percentage profit; tell a profit from a loss.

By the end of this section, you can say what a shop made or lost on a sale, and
express it as a percentage of what the shop paid.

## Cost price and selling price

A shop buys an item and later sells it. Two prices matter.

- The **cost price** is what the shop pays to buy the item.
- The **selling price** is what the customer pays.

When the selling price is higher, the shop makes a **profit**.

$$\text{profit} = \text{selling price} - \text{cost price}$$

When the selling price is lower, the shop makes a **loss**.

$$\text{loss} = \text{cost price} - \text{selling price}$$

The shop in the chapter problem buys a phone case for €4.00 and sells it for
€6.00 before VAT.

$$\text{profit} = 6.00 - 4.00 = 2.00$$

The profit is €2.00 on each case.

**Example 1.** A shop buys a charger for €8.00 and sells it for €10.00. Work out
the profit.

$$10.00 - 8.00 = 2.00$$

The profit is €2.00.

**Example 2.** A shop buys a speaker for €20.00 and sells it for €15.00 in a
sale. Work out the loss.

$$20.00 - 15.00 = 5.00$$

The loss is €5.00.

## Profit as a percentage of the cost

A profit of €2.00 means one thing on a €4.00 case and something else on a €200
phone. To compare two profits, write each one as a percentage of the **cost
price**.

$$\text{percentage profit} = \frac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost price}}\times100$$

For the phone case:

$$\frac{2.00}{4.00}\times100 = 50$$

The shop makes a $50\%$ profit on the case. The cost price is the base, so the
percentage is worked out from what the shop paid.

**Example 3.** A shop buys a charger for €8.00 and sells it for €10.00. Work out
the percentage profit.

The profit is $10.00-8.00=2.00$.

$$\frac{2.00}{8.00}\times100 = 25$$

The percentage profit is $25\%$.

**Example 4.** A shop buys a speaker for €20.00 and sells it for €15.00. Work out
the percentage loss.

The loss is $20.00-15.00=5.00$.

$$\frac{5.00}{20.00}\times100 = 25$$

The percentage loss is $25\%$. The rule is the same for a loss: divide the loss by
the cost price, because the cost price is what the shop started with.

> **Watch out.** The percentage is worked out from the **cost price**, not the
> selling price. For the phone case the profit is €2.00. Dividing by the selling
> price gives $\frac{2.00}{6.00}\times100=33.3\%$, which is a different number and
> answers a different question. That number is the margin, and it has a section of
> its own.
>
> **Re-check:** the percentage profit and the percentage loss both divide by the
> cost price. Ask "what did the shop pay?" before you divide.

## Finding a selling price

If you know the cost price and the percentage profit, you can find the selling
price in one step. A profit of $p\%$ multiplies the cost price by $1+\frac{p}{100}$.

**Example 5.** A shop buys a case for €4.00 and wants a profit of $50\%$. Work out
the selling price.

$$4.00\times1.5 = 6.00$$

The selling price is €6.00, which is the price in the chapter problem.

**Example 6.** A shop buys a bag for €40.00 and wants a profit of $15\%$. Work out
the selling price.

$$40.00\times1.15 = 46.00$$

The selling price is €46.00. The profit itself is $40.00\times0.15=6.00$.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The phone shop buys a case for €4.00 and sells it
for €6.00 before VAT.

1. Work out the profit on one case.
2. Work out the percentage profit.
3. The shop also buys a screen protector for €2.50 and sells it for €3.50. Work
   out the profit and the percentage profit on the protector.
4. Which item gives the better percentage profit, the case or the protector?

You will use both percentages again in Section 7.2.

## You should now be able to

- work out the profit or the loss on a sale by subtracting;
- write a profit or a loss as a percentage of the cost price;
- find a selling price from a cost price and a percentage profit;
- tell the cost price from the selling price, and divide by the right one.
