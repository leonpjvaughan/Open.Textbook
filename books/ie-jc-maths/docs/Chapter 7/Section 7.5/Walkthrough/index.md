# 7.5 Simple and compound interest — Learn

**Prerequisites:** finding a percentage of an amount, and powers from Section 2.1.

**Objectives:** work out simple interest; work out the total with simple
interest; work out compound interest; say which is larger and why.

By the end of this section, you can grow a lump sum two ways, and say which one
the question is asking for.

## Interest and the principal

Interest is money paid for the use of money. A bank pays interest on savings, and
charges interest on a loan.

The **principal** is the amount you start with. The **rate** is the percentage
for one year, and the **term** is the number of years.

## Simple interest

**Simple interest** is worked out on the principal only, once for each year. The
interest does not earn interest of its own.

$$I = P\times r\times t$$

$I$ is the interest, $P$ is the principal, $r$ is the rate as a decimal, and $t$
is the term in years.

**Example 1.** €2,000.00 is saved at $5\%$ simple interest for 3 years. Work out
the interest and the total.

$$I = 2000\times0.05\times3 = 300$$

The interest is €300.00. The total is the principal plus the interest.

$$2000+300 = 2300$$

The total is €2,300.00.

## Compound interest

**Compound interest** is worked out on the principal and on the interest already
added. Each year the interest joins the balance, so the next year's interest is
larger.

$$A = P(1+r)^t$$

$A$ is the total after $t$ years.

**Example 2.** €2,000.00 is saved at $5\%$ compound interest for 2 years. Work
out the total.

$$A = 2000\times1.05^2 = 2000\times1.1025 = 2205$$

The total is €2,205.00.

The first year adds $2000\times0.05=100$ euro. The second year adds $5\%$ of
€2,100.00, which is €105.00. The extra €5.00 is the interest on the first year's
interest.

**Example 3.** €500.00 is saved at $4\%$ compound interest for 2 years. Work out
the total.

$$A = 500\times1.04^2 = 500\times1.0816 = 540.80$$

The total is €540.80.

## Comparing the two

For €2,000.00 at $5\%$:

| Years | Simple total | Compound total |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | €2,100.00 | €2,100.00 |
| 2 | €2,200.00 | €2,205.00 |
| 3 | €2,300.00 | €2,315.25 |

After one year the two totals agree. After that, compound interest is always
larger, because each year's interest earns interest too.

> **Watch out.** Compound interest is not the same as simple interest. For
> €2,000.00 at $5\%$ over 3 years, the simple interest is €300.00 and the
> compound interest is €315.25. The extra €15.25 is the interest on the interest.
> Work out which kind the question asks for before you start.
>
> **Re-check:** simple interest uses the same interest every year. Compound
> interest grows, so the total after several years must be larger than the simple
> total.

**Test.** Set the amount to €2,000.00 and the rate to 5%, then move the number of years

the end of the last year.

**Explain.** Write two or three sentences. Say what the shaded gap measures, and why it
grows larger every year.

## Working to the nearest cent

A compound total is often not a whole number of cent. Round it at the end, and
only once.

**Example 4.** €1,000.00 is saved at $3\%$ compound interest for 3 years. Work
out the total, correct to the nearest cent.

$$A = 1000\times1.03^3 = 1000\times1.092727 = 1092.727$$

The total is €1,092.727, which rounds to €1,092.73.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. You saved €2,000.00 from your pay, and you put it
into a savings account at $5\%$ a year.

1. Work out the total after 2 years at simple interest.
2. Work out the total after 2 years at compound interest.
3. Say which total is larger, and by how much.

You will compare the same €2,000.00 with an online offer in Section 7.6.

## You should now be able to

- work out simple interest with $I=P\times r\times t$;
- add the interest to the principal to get the total;
- work out compound interest with $A=P(1+r)^t$;
- explain why compound interest grows faster than simple interest;
- round a compound total to the nearest cent, at the end and once.
