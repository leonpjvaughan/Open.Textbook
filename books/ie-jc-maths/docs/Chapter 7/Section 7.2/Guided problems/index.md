# 7.2 Mark-up and margin — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $25\%$ as a decimal.
2. Work out $0.4\times60$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.25$. Divide by 100, because per cent means "per hundred".
2. $24$. Multiplying by $0.4$ finds four tenths of the amount.

A percentage becomes a decimal before it multiplies an amount.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A shop buys a mug for €4.00 and sells it for €6.00. Work out the mark-up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The mark-up divides the profit by the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The profit is $6.00-4.00=2.00$. Now work out $\frac{2.00}{4.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{2.00}{4.00}\times100=50$$

The mark-up is $50\%$. The base is the cost price of €4.00.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A shop buys a mug for €4.00 and sells it for €6.00. Work out the margin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The margin divides the same profit by the selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{2.00}{6.00}\times100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{2.00}{6.00}\times100=33\frac{1}{3}$$

The margin is $33\frac{1}{3}\%$. The same €2.00 profit gives a $50\%$ mark-up and a
$33\frac{1}{3}\%$ margin, because the bases are different.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A shop buys a bag for €40.00 and adds a mark-up of $25\%$. Work out the
selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A mark-up of $25\%$ multiplies the cost price by $1.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$40.00\times1.25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$40.00\times1.25=50.00$$

The selling price is €50.00. The profit is $40.00\times0.25=10.00$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A shop buys a lamp for €50.00 and sells it for €60.00. Work out the
mark-up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the profit, then divide by the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The profit is $60.00-50.00=10.00$. Now work out $\frac{10.00}{50.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{10.00}{50.00}\times100=20$$

The mark-up is $20\%$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A shop buys a lamp for €50.00 and sells it for €60.00. Work out the margin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the same €10.00 profit by the selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{10.00}{60.00}\times100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{10.00}{60.00}\times100=16\frac{2}{3}$$

The margin is $16\frac{2}{3}\%$. The mark-up is $20\%$, so the mark-up is the
larger of the two.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A shop buys a radio for €80.00 and sells it for €100.00. Work out the
mark-up and the margin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The profit is $100.00-80.00=20.00$. Divide it by the cost price for the mark-up,
and by the selling price for the margin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{20.00}{80.00}\times100 \qquad \frac{20.00}{100.00}\times100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The mark-up is $25\%$, and the margin is $20\%$. Both come from the same €20.00
profit.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A shop buys a kettle for €35.00 and adds a mark-up of $40\%$. Work out the
selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the cost price by $1.40$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$35.00\times1.40$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$35.00\times1.40=49.00$$

The selling price is €49.00. The profit is $35.00\times0.40=14.00$, and
$35.00+14.00=49.00$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A shop sells a speaker for €80.00 with a margin of $25\%$. Work out the
profit and the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The margin is a percentage of the selling price, so find $25\%$ of €80.00 first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$80.00\times0.25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$80.00\times0.25=20.00$$

The profit is €20.00. The cost price is $80.00-20.00=60.00$, which is €60.00.

Check the mark-up: $\frac{20.00}{60.00}\times100=33.3\%$, which is larger than the
$25\%$ margin.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** A shop buys a desk lamp for €25.00 and adds a mark-up of $60\%$. Work out
the selling price and the margin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the selling price first. Then the profit is the selling price minus €25.00.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$25.00\times1.60=40.00$$

The profit is $40.00-25.00=15.00$. Now work out
$\frac{15.00}{40.00}\times100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The selling price is €40.00. The profit is €15.00, so the margin is
$\frac{15.00}{40.00}\times100=37.5\%$.

A mark-up of $60\%$ gives a margin of $37.5\%$, which is smaller.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Write $\frac{3}{4}$ as a percentage.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 5.4. Multiply the fraction by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{3}{4}\times100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{3}{4}\times100=75$$

So $\frac{3}{4}=75\%$. Writing a fraction as a percentage is the same division a
margin uses, with the profit in the top.

</details>
</details>
</details>
