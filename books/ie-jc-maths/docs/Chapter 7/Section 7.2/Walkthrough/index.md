# 7.2 Mark-up and margin — Learn

**Prerequisites:** percentage profit from Section 7.1, and finding a percentage
of an amount.

**Objectives:** work out a mark-up as a percentage of the cost price; work out a
margin as a percentage of the selling price; tell the two apart; find a selling
price from a cost price and a mark-up.

By the end of this section, you can write the same profit as two different
percentages, and say which question each one answers.

## The same profit, two bases

A shop's profit can be written as a percentage of two different numbers.

- The **mark-up** is the profit as a percentage of the **cost price**.
- The **margin** is the profit as a percentage of the **selling price**.

The phone case costs €4.00, sells for €6.00, and makes a profit of €2.00.

$$\text{mark-up} = \frac{2.00}{4.00}\times100 = 50\%$$

$$\text{margin} = \frac{2.00}{6.00}\times100 = 33\frac{1}{3}\%$$

Both describe the same €2.00. The mark-up is the larger of the two, because the
cost price is the smaller base.

## Which number a shopkeeper uses

A shopkeeper who says "I add a $50\%$ mark-up" is talking about the cost price. A
shopkeeper who says "I make a $33\%$ margin" is talking about the selling price.
The two are not interchangeable, and mixing them up changes the price.

A mark-up of $p\%$ multiplies the cost price by $1+\frac{p}{100}$.

$$\text{selling price} = \text{cost price}\times\left(1+\frac{p}{100}\right)$$

**Example 1.** A shop buys a bag for €40.00 and adds a mark-up of $25\%$. Work out
the selling price.

$$40.00\times1.25 = 50.00$$

The selling price is €50.00. The profit is $40.00\times0.25=10.00$. Now find the
margin.

$$\frac{10.00}{50.00}\times100 = 20$$

The mark-up is $25\%$, and the margin is $20\%$. The same €10.00 profit gives two
different percentages.

**Example 2.** A shop buys a radio for €80.00 and sells it for €100.00. Work out
the mark-up and the margin.

The profit is $100.00-80.00=20.00$.

$$\text{mark-up} = \frac{20.00}{80.00}\times100 = 25\%$$

$$\text{margin} = \frac{20.00}{100.00}\times100 = 20\%$$

The mark-up is $25\%$, and the margin is $20\%$.

**Example 3.** A shop sells a speaker for €80.00 with a margin of $25\%$. Work out
the profit and the cost price.

The margin is a percentage of the selling price, so the profit is

$$80.00\times0.25 = 20.00$$

The profit is €20.00. The cost price is the selling price minus the profit.

$$80.00-20.00 = 60.00$$

The cost price is €60.00. As a check, the mark-up is
$\frac{20.00}{60.00}\times100=33.3\%$, which is larger than the margin.

> **Watch out.** A mark-up of $50\%$ is not a margin of $50\%$. For the phone
> case, the profit is €2.00. The mark-up divides by the cost price, €4.00, and
> gives $50\%$. The margin divides by the selling price, €6.00, and gives
> $33\frac{1}{3}\%$. The mark-up is always the larger of the two, because the cost
> price is smaller than the selling price.
>
> **Re-check:** ask which number is the base. If the base is the cost price, it is
> a mark-up. If the base is the selling price, it is a margin.

## Margin and cost price

A margin is a percentage of the selling price, so it does not tell you the cost
price directly. Find the profit first, then subtract it.

**Example 4.** A shop buys a lamp for €50.00 and sells it for €60.00. Work out the
mark-up and the margin.

The profit is $60.00-50.00=10.00$.

$$\text{mark-up} = \frac{10.00}{50.00}\times100 = 20\%$$

$$\text{margin} = \frac{10.00}{60.00}\times100 = 16\frac{2}{3}\%$$

The mark-up is $20\%$, and the margin is $16\frac{2}{3}\%$.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The phone shop buys a case for €4.00 and sells it
for €6.00 before VAT.

1. Work out the mark-up on the case.
2. Work out the margin on the case.
3. The shop also buys a screen protector for €2.50 and sells it for €3.50. Work
   out the mark-up and the margin on the protector.
4. Explain, in one sentence, why the mark-up is larger than the margin for both
   items.

You will use the €6.00 selling price again in Section 7.3, where VAT is added.

## You should now be able to

- work out a mark-up as the profit divided by the cost price;
- work out a margin as the profit divided by the selling price;
- find a selling price from a cost price and a mark-up;
- find the profit and the cost price from a selling price and a margin;
- explain why the mark-up is larger than the margin.
