# 7.4 Income tax and net pay — Learn

**Prerequisites:** finding a percentage of an amount, and subtracting decimals.

**Objectives:** work out gross pay from an hourly rate and hours; work out income
tax using a standard rate and a higher rate; apply a tax credit; work out PRSI;
work out net pay.

By the end of this section, you can read a payslip from the gross pay at the top
to the net pay at the bottom.

## Gross pay

**Gross pay** is the pay before anything is taken out. It is the hourly rate
multiplied by the hours worked.

$$\text{gross pay} = \text{hourly rate}\times\text{hours}$$

In the chapter problem you are paid €15.00 an hour for 40 hours a week.

$$15.00\times40 = 600.00$$

The gross pay is €600.00 a week.

## Income tax

**Income tax** is a percentage of your pay. It is charged in **bands**, and the
rate rises as pay rises. In this book the weekly bands are:

| Weekly pay | Rate |
|---|---|
| the first €800.00 | $20\%$ |
| anything above €800.00 | $40\%$ |

A **tax credit** is an amount taken off the tax. It does not change your pay. It
reduces the tax you owe. In this book the weekly tax credit is €60.00.

For a gross pay of €600.00, all of the pay is in the first band.

$$600.00\times0.20 = 120.00$$

The tax before the credit is €120.00. Take off the credit.

$$120.00-60.00 = 60.00$$

The income tax is €60.00.

This is a simplified model, so that each line is clear. A real payslip has more
lines than this, such as a universal social charge and a pension.

## PRSI

**PRSI** stands for pay-related social insurance. It is a percentage of gross pay
that pays for social insurance. In this book the rate is $4\%$.

$$600.00\times0.04 = 24.00$$

The PRSI is €24.00.

## Net pay

**Net pay** is what reaches the bank account.

$$\text{net pay} = \text{gross pay} - \text{income tax} - \text{PRSI}$$

$$600.00-60.00-24.00 = 516.00$$

The net pay is €516.00 a week.

## Pay above the cut-off

When gross pay is above €800.00, the pay splits into two parts. The first €800.00
is taxed at $20\%$, and the rest at $40\%$.

**Example 1.** A worker's gross pay is €1,000.00 a week. Work out the net pay.

The first €800.00 is taxed at $20\%$, and the remaining €200.00 at $40\%$.

$$800.00\times0.20 = 160.00 \qquad 200.00\times0.40 = 80.00$$

$$160.00+80.00 = 240.00$$

The tax before the credit is €240.00. Take off the €60.00 credit.

$$240.00-60.00 = 180.00$$

The income tax is €180.00. The PRSI is $1,000.00\times0.04=40.00$. Then

$$1,000.00-180.00-40.00 = 780.00$$

The net pay is €780.00.

**Example 2.** A worker's gross pay is €900.00 a week. Work out the net pay.

The first €800.00 is taxed at $20\%$, and the remaining €100.00 at $40\%$.

$$800.00\times0.20 = 160.00 \qquad 100.00\times0.40 = 40.00$$

$$160.00+40.00 = 200.00$$

Take off the credit: $200.00-60.00=140.00$. The PRSI is
$900.00\times0.04=36.00$. Then

$$900.00-140.00-36.00 = 724.00$$

The net pay is €724.00.

> **Watch out.** A tax credit cannot make the tax negative. If the tax before the
> credit is €50.00 and the credit is €60.00, the tax is €0.00, not minus €10.00.
> You do not receive money for unused credit.
>
> **Re-check:** work out the tax before the credit first. If it is smaller than
> the credit, the tax is zero.

**Example 3.** A worker's gross pay is €250.00 a week. Work out the income tax and
the net pay.

The tax before the credit is $250.00\times0.20=50.00$. The credit is €60.00, which
is larger, so the income tax is €0.00. The PRSI is $250.00\times0.04=10.00$. Then

$$250.00-10.00 = 240.00$$

The net pay is €240.00. The only deduction is the PRSI.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. Your gross pay is €600.00 a week.

1. Work out the income tax before the credit.
2. Work out the income tax after the €60.00 credit.
3. Work out the PRSI.
4. Work out the net pay.
5. Check the payslip: do the income tax and the PRSI add up to the difference
   between the gross pay and the net pay?

Part of your net pay becomes the €2,000.00 you save, which you will use in
Section 7.5.

## You should now be able to

- work out gross pay from an hourly rate and the hours worked;
- work out income tax in bands, using $20\%$ up to the cut-off and $40\%$ above it;
- take a tax credit off the tax, and stop the tax at zero when the credit is
  larger;
- work out PRSI as a percentage of gross pay;
- work out net pay by subtracting the deductions from the gross pay;
- check that the deductions account for the difference between gross and net pay.
