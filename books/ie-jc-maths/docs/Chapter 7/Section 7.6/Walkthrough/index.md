# 7.6 Value for money and comparing offers — Learn

**Prerequisites:** division, and comparing two decimals.

**Objectives:** work out the price of one unit; compare two offers with the same
unit price; decide which offer is better value; explain why the total price alone
can mislead.

By the end of this section, you can compare two offers even when the amounts are
different.

## The price of one unit

Two offers are hard to compare when the amounts are different. The way through is
to find the **price of one unit**.

$$\text{price per unit} = \frac{\text{total price}}{\text{quantity}}$$

The unit might be one GB of data, one kilogram, one litre, or one item. The unit
must be the same on both sides of the comparison.

**Example 1.** A phone plan costs €20.00 for 10 GB of data. Work out the price of
one GB.

$$20.00\div10 = 2.00$$

The price is €2.00 for each GB.

**Example 2.** A second plan costs €24.00 for 15 GB. Work out the price of one
GB, and say which plan is better value.

$$24.00\div15 = 1.60$$

The second plan is €1.60 for each GB. That is less than €2.00, so the second plan
is better value.

Notice that the second plan has the larger total price. The larger total is not
the same as the worse value.

## Comparing offers with different amounts

**Example 3.** A shop sells coffee in two sizes: 500 g for €4.50, and 750 g for
€6.00. Which is better value?

The price of one gram is small and hard to read. Compare the price of a kilogram
instead. There are two 500 g amounts in a kilogram, and 0.75 kilograms in 750 g.

$$4.50\div0.5 = 9.00 \qquad 6.00\div0.75 = 8.00$$

The 500 g bag costs €9.00 for each kilogram, and the 750 g bag costs €8.00 for
each kilogram. The 750 g bag is better value.

**Example 4.** Three phone cases cost €21.00 in a bundle, and one case costs €7.38
on its own. Which is better value?

$$21.00\div3 = 7.00$$

The bundle works out at €7.00 for each case, which is less than €7.38. The bundle
is better value.

**Example 5.** A 2-litre bottle of juice costs €3.60, and a 1.5-litre bottle costs
€2.85. Which is better value for each litre?

$$3.60\div2 = 1.80 \qquad 2.85\div1.5 = 1.90$$

The 2-litre bottle costs €1.80 for each litre, and the 1.5-litre bottle costs
€1.90 for each litre. The 2-litre bottle is better value, even though it has the
larger total price.

> **Watch out.** The larger pack is not always better value. A large pack can
> cost more for each unit than a small one. Work out the price of one unit for
> each offer, and compare those two numbers.
>
> **Re-check:** keep the units the same on both sides. Compare euro for each
> kilogram with euro for each kilogram, or euro for each GB with euro for each GB.

## A discount is another way to compare

A discount changes the price, and the price of one unit can be worked out after
the discount.

**Example 6.** A shop sells a pack of 12 pens for €4.80, and a pack of 20 pens for
€7.60. Work out the price of one pen for each pack.

$$4.80\div12 = 0.40 \qquad 7.60\div20 = 0.38$$

The 12-pack costs €0.40 for each pen, which is 40 cent. The 20-pack costs €0.38
for each pen, which is 38 cent. The 20-pack is better value.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The shop sells the phone case at a shelf price of
€7.38. An online shop sells the same case at 8.00 US dollars, with a delivery
charge of 2.50 US dollars.

1. Change 8.00 US dollars to euro at €1 = 1.09 US dollars, correct to the nearest
   cent.
2. Compare that with €7.38. Which is cheaper?
3. The shop sells three cases for €21.00. Work out the price of one case.
4. Put the three offers in order, from the best value to the worst value.

You will meet the exchange rate in Section 7.7, and the Review brings all four
offers together.

## You should now be able to

- work out the price of one unit by dividing the total price by the quantity;
- compare two offers by comparing their unit prices;
- keep the unit the same on both sides of the comparison;
- explain why the larger total price can still be the better value.
