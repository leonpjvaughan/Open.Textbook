# 7.3 VAT and bills — Learn

**Prerequisites:** increasing an amount by a percentage from Section 5.5, and
dividing by a decimal.

**Objectives:** add VAT to a price; take VAT off a price that already includes
it; read a bill with a subtotal and VAT; check a VAT calculation.

By the end of this section, you can move between a price before VAT and a price
with VAT, in either direction.

## What VAT is

VAT stands for **value added tax**. It is a tax added to the price of most goods
and services. The shop collects it and passes it to the government.

In Ireland the standard rate of VAT is $23\%$. Some goods and services carry a
lower rate, but the standard rate is the one used in this chapter.

## Adding VAT

To add VAT at $23\%$, multiply the price before VAT by $1.23$.

$$\text{price with VAT} = \text{price before VAT}\times1.23$$

For the phone case, the price before VAT is €6.00.

$$6.00\times1.23 = 7.38$$

The VAT is $6.00\times0.23=1.38$, and the shelf price is €7.38.

**Example 1.** A phone bill is €30.00 before VAT. Work out the bill with VAT at
$23\%$.

$$30.00\times1.23 = 36.90$$

The bill is €36.90. The VAT is $30.00\times0.23=6.90$.

**Example 2.** A price is €50.00 before VAT. Work out the VAT and the price with
VAT.

$$50.00\times0.23 = 11.50$$

$$50.00+11.50 = 61.50$$

The VAT is €11.50, and the price with VAT is €61.50.

## Taking VAT off a price

The shelf price already includes VAT. To find the price before VAT, divide by
$1.23$.

$$\text{price before VAT} = \frac{\text{price with VAT}}{1.23}$$

**Example 3.** A shelf price is €24.60, including VAT at $23\%$. Work out the
price before VAT.

$$24.60\div1.23 = 20.00$$

The price before VAT is €20.00. The VAT is $24.60-20.00=4.60$, which is $23\%$ of
€20.00.

**Example 4.** A receipt shows €73.80, including VAT at $23\%$. Work out the price
before VAT and the VAT.

$$73.80\div1.23 = 60.00$$

The price before VAT is €60.00. The VAT is $73.80-60.00=13.80$.

> **Watch out.** Do not take $23\%$ off the price with VAT. For the €24.60 shelf
> price, $24.60\times0.77=18.94$, which is wrong. The €24.60 is $123\%$ of the
> price before VAT, so you divide by $1.23$. The price before VAT is €20.00, and
> the VAT is $23\%$ of €20.00, which is €4.60.
>
> **Re-check:** add the VAT back to the price before VAT. If it returns to the
> shelf price, the division was right. $20.00+4.60=24.60$.

## A bill with more than one line

A bill often has more than one line before VAT. Add the lines to get the
**subtotal**, then add VAT to the subtotal.

**Example 5.** A phone bill has a line rental of €30.00 and call charges of
€15.00. VAT is added at $23\%$. Work out the subtotal and the total.

$$\text{subtotal} = 30.00+15.00 = 45.00$$

$$45.00\times1.23 = 55.35$$

The subtotal is €45.00, and the total is €55.35. The VAT is
$45.00\times0.23=10.35$.

**Example 6.** A repair bill is €110.00 before VAT, and the standard rate of
$23\%$ applies. Work out the total.

$$110.00\times1.23 = 135.30$$

The total is €135.30. The VAT is $110.00\times0.23=25.30$.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The phone case sells for €6.00 before VAT, and the
shop adds VAT at $23\%$.

1. Work out the VAT on one case.
2. Work out the shelf price of one case.
3. A customer buys a case and a charger. The charger is €10.00 before VAT. Work
   out the subtotal before VAT, the VAT on the subtotal, and the total the
   customer pays.

The shelf price of €7.38 is used again in Section 7.6, where the shop's offer is
compared with an online one.

## You should now be able to

- add VAT to a price by multiplying by $1.23$;
- find the VAT itself as $23\%$ of the price before VAT;
- take VAT off a price by dividing by $1.23$;
- add the lines of a bill to get the subtotal, then add VAT;
- check a VAT answer by adding the VAT back to the price before VAT.
