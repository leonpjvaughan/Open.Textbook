# Chapter 7: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The first payslip.** You work in a phone shop for €15.00 an hour, 40 hours a
week. Your payslip shows income tax at $20\%$ on the first €800.00 of weekly pay,
a weekly tax credit of €60.00, and PRSI at $4\%$ of gross pay. The shop also buys
a phone case for €4.00 and sells it for €6.00 before VAT, and the shelf price
includes VAT at $23\%$.

Work through it in the order the chapter taught.

**Step 1. Gross pay.**

$$15.00\times40 = 600.00$$

**The gross pay is €600.00 a week.**

**Step 2. Income tax.** All of the €600.00 is below the €800.00 cut-off, so it is
taxed at $20\%$.

$$600.00\times0.20 = 120.00$$

The tax before the credit is €120.00. Take off the €60.00 credit.

$$120.00-60.00 = 60.00$$

**The income tax is €60.00.**

**Step 3. PRSI.**

$$600.00\times0.04 = 24.00$$

**The PRSI is €24.00.**

**Step 4. Net pay.**

$$600.00-60.00-24.00 = 516.00$$

**The net pay is €516.00 a week.** That is what reaches the bank account.

**Step 5. The phone case.** The shop pays €4.00 and sells for €6.00, so the profit
is

$$6.00-4.00 = 2.00$$

euro. As a percentage of the cost price, the percentage profit is

$$\frac{2.00}{4.00}\times100 = 50\%$$

The mark-up is the same $50\%$, because the base is the cost price. The margin
uses the selling price.

$$\frac{2.00}{6.00}\times100 = 33\frac{1}{3}\%$$

**Step 6. VAT and the shelf price.**

$$6.00\times0.23 = 1.38$$

The VAT is €1.38, and the shelf price is

$$6.00+1.38 = 7.38$$

euro. **The case sells for €7.38.**

**Checks.** The deductions account for the whole difference between the gross pay
and the net pay: $60.00+24.00=84.00$ and $600.00-516.00=84.00$. The shelf price
checks the other way: $7.38\div1.23=6.00$, which is the price before VAT.

So the payslip runs from €600.00 gross to €516.00 net, and the case runs from
€4.00 cost to €7.38 on the shelf. Every figure has a reason.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and
say why in a few words. The idea might be a percentage profit, a mark-up, a
margin, VAT, income tax, interest, a unit price, an exchange rate, or something
from an earlier chapter.

1. A shop buys a case for €4.00 and sells it for €6.00. Work out the percentage
   profit.
2. A shop buys a lamp for €50.00 and sells it for €60.00. Work out the mark-up and
   the margin.
3. A price is €40.00 before VAT. Work out the price with VAT at $23\%$.
4. A shelf price is €61.50, including VAT at $23\%$. Work out the price before
   VAT.
5. A worker's gross pay is €750.00 a week. Work out the income tax before the
   credit, the income tax after the €60.00 credit, the PRSI at $4\%$, and the net
   pay.
6. €1,500.00 is saved at $4\%$ simple interest for 2 years. Work out the interest
   and the total.
7. €1,500.00 is saved at $4\%$ compound interest for 2 years. Work out the total.
8. A 2-litre bottle costs €3.60, and a 1.5-litre bottle costs €2.85. Which is
   better value for each litre?
9. Change €250.00 to US dollars at €1 = 1.09 US dollars.
10. Change 109.00 US dollars to euro at €1 = 1.09 US dollars.
11. A jacket costs €80.00 and is reduced by $25\%$. Work out the sale price.
12. Divide 84 in the ratio $3:4$.
13. Work out $2^3\times3^2$.
14. Write 4,700,000 in standard form.
15. **H** A shop buys a coat for €60.00 and adds a mark-up of $40\%$. It then takes
    $20\%$ off in a sale. Work out the sale price, and say whether the shop still
    makes a profit on the coat.
16. **H** €2,000.00 is saved at $5\%$ compound interest for 3 years. Work out the
    total, and work out how much more that is than the simple-interest total.

## What you can now do

- work out a profit or a loss, and write it as a percentage of the cost price;
- tell a mark-up from a margin, and work out each one;
- add VAT to a price, and take VAT off a price that already includes it;
- work out gross pay, income tax in bands, a tax credit, PRSI and net pay;
- work out simple interest and compound interest, and say which is larger;
- compare two offers by working out the price of one unit;
- change an amount from one currency to another, and back again;
- check a payslip by making the deductions account for the difference between
  gross and net pay.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you predicted whether the net pay would be nearer
€400, €500 or €600 a week. Look back at that prediction now.

The net pay is €516.00, so it is nearer €500. The deductions took €84.00 from
€600.00, which is a little over one seventh of the gross pay.

You also wrote about a place where understanding interest or a payslip would help
you or someone you know. Take that example now, and work it out with the ideas
from this chapter. If it is a payslip, find the gross pay and the deductions. If
it is a savings account, find the interest and the total. If it is a price, find
the VAT or the percentage change.

One question is worth asking about anything you found. Was the percentage worked
out from the cost price, the selling price, or the amount before tax? Writing down
which number was the base is what makes the answer mean something.
