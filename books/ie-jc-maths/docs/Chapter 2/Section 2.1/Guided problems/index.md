# 2.1 Index form and powers — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $6\times6$.
2. Work out $3+4\times5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $36$. A number times itself is a square number.
2. $23$. Multiply before you add, so work out $4\times5=20$ first. Then
   $3+20=23$. If you added first, revisit Section 1.4.

Both answers are the moves this section uses. A power is a product, and the order
of the multiplication does not change the value.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write $6\times6\times6$ in index form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count how many copies of 6 are multiplied together. That count is the index.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The base is 6, and it repeats three times.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6\times6\times6 = 6^3$$

The base is 6 and the index is 3. The value is $216$, which you can check on a
calculator.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Work out $3^4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The index 4 tells you to multiply four copies of the base 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3^4 = 3\times3\times3\times3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3^4 = 3\times3\times3\times3 = 81$$

Work from the left: $3\times3=9$, then $9\times3=27$, then $27\times3=81$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out $2^7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the table of powers of 2 from the Learn page, or keep multiplying by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2^6 = 64$, so $2^7$ is one more doubling.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^7 = 128$$

You can build up: 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128. The last two entries are $2^6=64$ and
$2^7=128$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Write $10\times10\times10\times10\times10$ in index form, and work out its
value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The base is 10. Count the copies to find the index.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are five copies of 10, so the index is 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10\times10\times10\times10\times10 = 10^5 = 100000$$

The index counts the zeros: five zeros after the 1.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Work out $5^3$ and $2^5$. Which value is larger?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out each power separately before you compare them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$5^3 = 5\times5\times5$ and $2^5 = 2\times2\times2\times2\times2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5^3 = 125 \qquad 2^5 = 32$$

So $5^3$ is larger. The base matters as much as the index: a larger base with a
smaller index can still give a larger value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Write $7\times7\times7\times7\times7\times7$ in index form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the copies of 7 carefully. There are more than four.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Count them one at a time: 7, 7, 7, 7, 7, 7. That is six copies.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7\times7\times7\times7\times7\times7 = 7^6$$

The base is 7 and the index is 6. The value is $117649$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Work out $12^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$12^2$ means $12\times12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply 12 by itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$12^2 = 12\times12 = 144$$

This is a square number, because a square of side 12 has area 144.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A square has side 9 cm. Work out its area, and write your answer using an
index.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area of a square is side times side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Area $= 9\times9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{Area} = 9^2 = 81\ \text{cm}^2$$

The unit is squared as well as the number, because the area is measured in square
centimetres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $8-3\times(-2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply before you subtract.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3\times(-2) = -6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8-3\times(-2) = 8-(-6) = 8+6 = 14$$

Subtracting a negative number is the same as adding its positive value.

</details>
</details>
</details>
