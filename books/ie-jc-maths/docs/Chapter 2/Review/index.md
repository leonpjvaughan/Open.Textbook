# Chapter 2: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**An A4 sheet is 210 mm wide and 297 mm tall. The photocopier's 141% button turns
it into an A3 sheet. Why is the button marked 141, and what does each button do?**

Start with the area. An A3 sheet has twice the area of an A4 sheet, so the button
multiplies the area by 2.

$$2 = 2^1$$

The width and the height are both multiplied by the same number $f$, because the
shape stays the same. The area is width times height, so the area is multiplied
by $f\times f = f^2$.

$$f^2 = 2$$

That equation asks for the number whose square is 2. It is the square root of 2.

$$f = 2^{\frac12} = \sqrt2 \approx 1.414$$

A multiplier of 1.414 is 141%. **That is why the button carries that number.**

Now the measurements. Multiply each side of the A4 sheet by 1.414.

$$210\times1.414 \approx 297\ \text{mm} \qquad 297\times1.414 \approx 420\ \text{mm}$$

So an A3 sheet is 297 mm by 420 mm. **The 141% button turns A4 into A3.**

Two checks, and both are free. The A3 area is $297\times420 = 124740\ \text{mm}^2$
and the A4 area is $210\times297 = 62370\ \text{mm}^2$, which is exactly half. And
the shape is unchanged: $420\div297 \approx 1.414$, the same ratio as
$297\div210 \approx 1.414$.

Now the other buttons. Each one multiplies the width by a number, and the area by
the square of that number.

| Button | Width multiplier | As a power of 2 | Area multiplier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 141% | $\sqrt2 \approx 1.414$ | $2^{\frac12}$ | $2^1 = 2$ |
| 100% | 1 | $2^0$ | $2^0 = 1$ |
| 71% | $\frac{1}{\sqrt2} \approx 0.707$ | $2^{-\frac12}$ | $2^{-1} = \frac12$ |
| 50% | $\frac12$ | $2^{-1}$ | $2^{-2} = \frac14$ |
| 25% | $\frac14$ | $2^{-2}$ | $2^{-4} = \frac{1}{16}$ |

Two presses of 141% multiply the width by
$\left(2^{\frac12}\right)^2 = 2^1 = 2$, so the width doubles. The area is
multiplied by $2^2 = 4$. That takes A4 to A2, which is 420 mm by 594 mm.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which law or idea
applies**, and say why in a few words. Then calculate. The arithmetic follows from
the choice, so making the choice is the skill.

1. Write $4\times4\times4\times4\times4$ in index form.
2. Work out $2^6$.
3. Simplify $a^5\times a^3$.
4. Simplify $\dfrac{6^8}{6^5}$.
5. Simplify $(x^4)^3$.
6. Work out $9^0$.
7. Write $2^{-4}$ as a fraction, and work out its value.
8. Work out $10^{-2}$.
9. Work out $16^{\frac12}$.
10. Work out $27^{\frac13}$.
11. Work out $8^{\frac23}$.
12. Work out $4^{-\frac12}$.
13. The photocopier's 200% button doubles the width and the height. What does it
    do to the area? Give your answer as a power of 2.
14. A square has area 49 cm². Write the length of one side using an index.
15. Which idea do you need for each of these, and why: $3^4\times3^2$, $(3^4)^2$,
    and $3^{-2}$?
16. Work out $-5+2\times(-3)$.
17. Work out $(-4)\times(-6)$.

## What you can now do

- write a repeated product in index form, and name the base and the index;
- work out the value of a power, including squares and cubes;
- multiply powers with the same base, divide powers with the same base, and raise
  a power to a power;
- explain why any non-zero number to the power zero is 1;
- write a negative index as a reciprocal, and keep the answer positive;
- read a fractional index as a root, and work out a value such as
  $8^{\frac23}$;
- work out what a multiplier does to a length and to an area.

## Thinking back

Look back at what you wrote at the start of the chapter, where you found a small
raised number or something that grows by multiplying by the same amount each time.

Now work out the mathematics of your example. Write the multiplier as a power, and
say what the index counts. If your example doubles, the index counts the doublings,
and $2^{10} = 1024$ gives you a quick estimate for ten of them. If the number you
found carries a negative or a fractional index, say what that index means in your
example. A negative index means going backwards, and a fractional index means a
root.
