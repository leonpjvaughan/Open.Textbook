# 2.3 Zero and negative indices — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the laws of indices from Section 2.2. Dividing a whole number
by a larger one.

**Objectives:** explain why a non-zero base to the power zero is 1; write a
negative index as a reciprocal; work out powers of 10 with zero and negative
indices.

By the end of this section, you can work out any whole-number index, including
zero and negative ones.

## The power zero

Use the quotient law on a power divided by itself. Any non-zero number divided by
itself gives 1.

$$\frac{2^3}{2^3} = 1$$

Now work out the same quotient with the index law, which subtracts the indices.

$$\frac{2^3}{2^3} = 2^{3-3} = 2^0$$

Both routes describe the same number, so the two results must agree.

$$2^0 = 1$$

The same argument works for any base except zero, because dividing by zero is not
allowed.

$$a^0 = 1\quad(a\ne0)$$

**Example 1.** Work out $7^0$, $100^0$ and $a^0$.

$$7^0 = 1 \qquad 100^0 = 1 \qquad a^0 = 1$$

The base does not matter, as long as it is not zero. Every non-zero number to the
power zero is 1.

## Negative indices

Now divide a smaller power by a larger one. The index law gives a negative index.

$$\frac{2^3}{2^5} = 2^{3-5} = 2^{-2}$$

Writing the division out shows what that number really is.

$$\frac{2^3}{2^5} = \frac{2\times2\times2}{2\times2\times2\times2\times2} = \frac{1}{2\times2} = \frac{1}{2^2}$$

So $2^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{2^2} = \dfrac14$. A negative index means a **reciprocal**.
Flip the base over and keep the index positive.

$$a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\quad(a\ne0)$$

**Example 2.** Work out $5^{-2}$.

$$5^{-2} = \frac{1}{5^2} = \frac{1}{25}$$

**Example 3.** Work out $10^{-3}$.

$$10^{-3} = \frac{1}{10^3} = \frac{1}{1000} = 0.001$$

## Powers of 10 and place value

Powers of 10 with negative indices are the place values below 1.

$$10^2 = 100 \qquad 10^1 = 10 \qquad 10^0 = 1$$

$$10^{-1} = 0.1 \qquad 10^{-2} = 0.01 \qquad 10^{-3} = 0.001$$

Each step down divides by 10, and subtracting 1 from the index does the same
thing.

## The answer stays positive

> **Watch out.** A negative index does not make the answer negative. $2^{-3}$ is
> the positive number $\frac18 = 0.125$, and it is not $-8$. The minus sign in the
> index tells you to take the reciprocal. It says nothing about the sign of the
> answer. Read $2^{-3}$ as "one over two cubed".

**Example 4.** Work out $3^{-1}$ and $4^{-2}$.

$$3^{-1} = \frac{1}{3} \qquad 4^{-2} = \frac{1}{4^2} = \frac{1}{16}$$

Both answers are positive, even though both indices are negative.

## Progress check

Back to the photocopier. The button marked 50% makes the sheet smaller.

1. A button that changes nothing is marked 100%. Write the width multiplier 1 as
   a power of 2, and say which index gives it.
2. The 50% button halves the width. Write the width multiplier $\frac12$ as a
   power of 2 with a negative index.
3. The 25% button halves the width twice. Write its width multiplier as a power
   of 2 with a negative index, and work out its value.

## You should now be able to

- explain why $a^0 = 1$ for any non-zero $a$;
- write $a^{-n}$ as $\frac{1}{a^n}$ and work out its value;
- work out the powers of 10 with zero and negative indices;
- say why a negative index still gives a positive answer.
