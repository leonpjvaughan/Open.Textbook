# 2.3 Zero and negative indices — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Simplify $2^3\times2^4$.
2. Work out $3^5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2^7$. The bases match, so add the indices: $3+4 = 7$.
2. $243$. Multiply five copies of 3: $3\times3\times3\times3\times3$.

Both answers use Section 2.2. This section pushes the same law one step further,
to an index of zero and to negative indices. If either answer felt unsure, look
back at Section 2.2 before you continue.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Work out $8^0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Any non-zero number to the power zero has the same value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $a^0 = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8^0 = 1$$

The base is 8, and the index is 0. The base does not change the answer here,
because dividing $8^3$ by $8^3$ gives both 1 and $8^0$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Write $3^{-2}$ as a fraction, and work out its value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A negative index means a reciprocal. Move the power underneath a 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{3^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3^{-2} = \frac{1}{3^2} = \frac{1}{9}$$

The answer is positive. The minus sign in the index only tells you to flip the
power over.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out $10^{-4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the power as a reciprocal, then work out the denominator.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10^{-4} = \dfrac{1}{10^4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10^{-4} = \frac{1}{10^4} = \frac{1}{10000} = 0.0001$$

For a power of 10, the index counts the zeros. A negative index puts those zeros
after the decimal point.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $2^{-5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out $2^5$ first, then take the reciprocal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2^5 = 32$, so $2^{-5} = \dfrac{1}{32}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^{-5} = \frac{1}{2^5} = \frac{1}{32}$$

As a decimal that is $0.03125$. The answer is much smaller than 1, but it is
still positive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Write $x^{-3}$ as a fraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The same rule works with a letter as the base.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Move $x^3$ underneath a 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^{-3} = \frac{1}{x^3}$$

The base stays as $x$. Only the index changes sign, and the power moves to the
denominator.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $6^{-1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An index of $-1$ gives the reciprocal of the base.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{6^1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6^{-1} = \frac{1}{6}$$

The answer is a fraction. It is about $0.167$ as a decimal, and it is positive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Work out $10^0$ and $10^{-1}$, and say what each means in place value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One of these is the units place, and the other is the tenths place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10^0 = 1$ and $10^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{10}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10^0 = 1 \qquad 10^{-1} = \frac{1}{10} = 0.1$$

The index 0 gives the units place. The index $-1$ gives the tenths place, which is
one place to the right of the decimal point.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Work out $\dfrac{2^2}{2^6}$ using the index law, and check it by writing the
division out.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the indices first, then write the result as a fraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2^2\div2^6 = 2^{2-6} = 2^{-4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^{2-6} = 2^{-4} = \frac{1}{2^4} = \frac{1}{16}$$

Written out, the division cancels two factors of 2 on top and leaves four
underneath: $\dfrac{1}{16}$. Both methods agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Simplify $(2^3)^2\times2^4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Deal with the bracket first, then multiply the two powers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(2^3)^2 = 2^6$, so the expression is $2^6\times2^4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^6\times2^4 = 2^{10} = 1024$$

The bracket multiplies the indices to give 6. Then the product adds the indices to
give 10.

</details>
</details>
</details>
