# 18.6 Angles of elevation and depression — Learn

**Prerequisites:** finding a missing side with a ratio from Section 18.4, and
finding a missing angle from Section 18.5.

**Objectives:** draw a right-angled triangle for a situation above or below you;
use the angle of elevation or depression to find a length.

By the end of this section, you can turn a description of something above or below
you into a triangle and solve it.

## Elevation and depression

Both angles are measured from a horizontal line.

- The **angle of elevation** is the angle up from the horizontal to an object above
  you.
- The **angle of depression** is the angle down from the horizontal to an object
  below you.

A surveyor on the ground looks up to a rooftop: that is an angle of elevation. A
person on the rooftop looks down to the ground: that is an angle of depression.

The two horizontal lines are parallel. That makes the angle of elevation from the
ground to the top equal to the angle of depression from the top to the ground. So
you can use whichever angle the question gives you.

**Example 1.** A person stands 20 m from a tree. The angle of elevation to the top
of the tree is 30°. Find the height of the tree.

The height is opposite the 30° angle, and 20 m is adjacent to it. Use tangent.

$$\tan 30^\circ = \frac{h}{20}$$

$$h = 20\tan 30^\circ = 20\times0.5773\ldots = 11.547\ldots$$

The tree is about 11.5 m tall.

**Example 2.** From the top of a cliff 40 m above the sea, the angle of depression
to a boat is 25°. Find the horizontal distance from the cliff to the boat.

The height of 40 m is opposite the 25° angle, and the distance is adjacent to it.
Use tangent.

$$\tan 25^\circ = \frac{40}{d}$$

$$d = \frac{40}{\tan 25^\circ} = \frac{40}{0.4663\ldots} = 85.78\ldots$$

The boat is about 85.8 m from the cliff.

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** The angle of depression is measured from the horizontal, not from
> the vertical. The common error is to put the angle at the bottom of the cliff,
> next to the vertical face.
>
> In Example 2 the 25° angle belongs at the top of the cliff, between the line of
> sight and the horizontal. The triangle still has a right angle at the foot of the
> cliff.
>
> **Re-check:** draw the horizontal line first, then put the angle between it and
> the line of sight.

A second trap is to use the wrong ratio. Name the opposite and the adjacent side
for the angle you were given.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. You stand on the ground, 12 m from the foot of the
school building, and look up to the top.

1. What is the name for the angle between the horizontal ground and your line of
   sight?
2. The angle is 37°. Which two sides of the triangle does it link?

In Section 18.7 you will decide when to use a ratio and when to use Pythagoras.

## You should now be able to

- name an angle above you as an angle of elevation, and one below you as an angle
  of depression;
- draw the horizontal line and the right-angled triangle for a situation;
- use the angle of elevation and a distance to find a height;
- use the angle of depression and a height to find a horizontal distance.
