# 18.4 Finding a missing side using a ratio — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $8\times0.7$.
2. Work out $12\times0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $5.6$. Work out $8\times7=56$, then move the decimal point one place.
2. $6$. Multiplying by 0.5 is the same as halving.

Every missing-side question ends with a multiplication like these. The ratio value
comes from the calculator, and you multiply it by a side.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 35°. The adjacent side is 8 cm. Find
the opposite side, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two sides are the opposite and the adjacent, so use tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan 35^\circ=\frac{x}{8}$, so $x=8\tan 35^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 8\times0.7002\ldots = 5.601\ldots$$

The opposite side is 5.6 cm, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 40°. The opposite side is 6 m. Find
the hypotenuse, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two sides are the opposite and the hypotenuse, so use sine. The unknown is on
the bottom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin 40^\circ=\frac{6}{h}$, so $h=\frac{6}{\sin 40^\circ}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h = \frac{6}{0.6427\ldots} = 9.334\ldots$$

The hypotenuse is 9.3 m, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 50°. The hypotenuse is 12 cm. Find
the adjacent side, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two sides are the adjacent and the hypotenuse, so use cosine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos 50^\circ=\frac{a}{12}$, so $a=12\cos 50^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = 12\times0.6427\ldots = 7.713\ldots$$

The adjacent side is 7.7 cm, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 30° and a hypotenuse of 10 m. Find
the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use sine. The exact value of $\sin 30^\circ$ is 0.5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = 10\sin 30^\circ = 10\times0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 5$$

The opposite side is 5 m. This is a whole-number answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 45° and an adjacent side of 9 cm.
Find the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use tangent. The exact value of $\tan 45^\circ$ is 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = 9\tan 45^\circ = 9\times1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 9$$

The opposite side is 9 cm. In a 45° right-angled triangle the opposite and adjacent
sides are equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ramp rises at an angle of 20°. Its sloping surface is 4 m long. Find the
height it rises, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sloping surface is the hypotenuse. The height is opposite the 20° angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h = 4\sin 20^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h = 4\times0.3420\ldots = 1.368\ldots$$

The ramp rises about 1.4 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A kite string makes an angle of 55° with the ground. The string is 20 m
long. Find the height of the kite above the ground, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The string is the hypotenuse. The height is opposite the angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h = 20\sin 55^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h = 20\times0.8191\ldots = 16.383\ldots$$

The kite is about 16.4 m above the ground.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. You stand 12 m from the school building. The angle of
elevation to the top is 37°. Find the height of the building, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The height is opposite the angle, and 12 m is adjacent to it. Use tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h = 12\tan 37^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h = 12\times0.7536\ldots = 9.04\ldots$$

The building is about 9.0 m tall. This agrees with the 9 m you found in Section
18.2, using Pythagoras' theorem.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out 15% of 80.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ten per cent is one tenth. Five per cent is half of that.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10\%$ of 80 is 8, so $5\%$ is 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$12$. Add $8+4=12$. You can check with decimals: $0.15\times80=12$.

Percentages appear in surveying questions, where an answer may be reported to a
given accuracy.

</details>
</details>
