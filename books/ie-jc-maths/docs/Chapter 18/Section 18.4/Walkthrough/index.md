# 18.4 Finding a missing side using a ratio — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the trigonometric ratios from Section 18.3, and rearranging a
simple equation.

**Objectives:** choose the ratio that uses the two sides in the question; set up an
equation; solve it to find a missing side.

By the end of this section, you can find a missing side when you know one angle and
one other side.

## The method

Four steps, in order:

1. Mark the right angle and the hypotenuse.
2. Mark the side you know and the side you want, relative to the angle.
3. Choose the ratio that uses those two sides.
4. Write the equation and solve it.

The ratio gives a fraction. To find the missing side, multiply both sides by the
denominator.

**Example 1.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 35°. The side adjacent to
the angle is 8 cm. Find the side opposite the angle.

The two sides in the question are the opposite and the adjacent, so use tangent and
call the opposite side $x$.

$$\tan 35^\circ = \frac{x}{8}$$

Multiply both sides by 8.

$$x = 8\tan 35^\circ = 8\times0.7002\ldots = 5.601\ldots$$

The opposite side is 5.6 cm, to one decimal place.

**Example 2.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 40°. The side opposite the
angle is 6 m. Find the hypotenuse.

The two sides are the opposite and the hypotenuse, so use sine and call the
hypotenuse $h$.

$$\sin 40^\circ = \frac{6}{h}$$

Multiply both sides by $h$, then divide by $\sin 40^\circ$.

$$h = \frac{6}{\sin 40^\circ} = \frac{6}{0.6427\ldots} = 9.334\ldots$$

The hypotenuse is 9.3 m, to one decimal place.

**Example 3.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 50°. The hypotenuse is
12 cm. Find the side adjacent to the angle.

The two sides are the adjacent and the hypotenuse, so use cosine and call the
adjacent side $a$.

$$\cos 50^\circ = \frac{a}{12}$$

$$a = 12\cos 50^\circ = 12\times0.6427\ldots = 7.713\ldots$$

The adjacent side is 7.7 cm, to one decimal place.

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** Check which side is on the bottom of the fraction. The common
> error is to divide when the unknown is on the top, or to multiply when the
> unknown is on the bottom.
>
> In $\tan 35^\circ=\frac{x}{8}$ the unknown $x$ is on the top, so multiply by 8:
> $x=8\tan 35^\circ$. In $\sin 40^\circ=\frac{6}{h}$ the unknown $h$ is on the
> bottom, so divide: $h=\frac{6}{\sin 40^\circ}$.
>
> **Re-check:** the hypotenuse is the longest side. If you found a leg longer than
> the hypotenuse, you swapped the top and the bottom.

A second trap is using the wrong ratio. Match the ratio to the two sides you have,
not to the angle on its own.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. You stand 12 m from the foot of the school building.
The angle of elevation to the top is 37°. You want the height.

1. The height is opposite the 37° angle, and 12 m is adjacent to it. Which ratio
   uses those two sides?
2. Write the equation and solve it for the height.

In Section 18.5 you will check the answer a different way.

## You should now be able to

- choose tangent, sine or cosine from the two sides in the question;
- write the ratio as an equation with the unknown in it;
- multiply or divide to make the unknown the subject;
- round the final answer to a sensible number of decimal places;
- check that no leg came out longer than the hypotenuse.
