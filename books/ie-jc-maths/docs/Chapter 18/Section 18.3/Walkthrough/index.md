# 18.3 The trigonometric ratios — Learn

**Prerequisites:** right-angled triangles from Section 18.1, and division of whole
numbers and decimals.

**Objectives:** label the opposite, the adjacent and the hypotenuse for a chosen
angle; write the sine, cosine and tangent ratios; use a calculator to find a ratio
for a given angle.

By the end of this section, you can name the three sides of a right-angled triangle
from the point of view of an angle, and write down the three ratios.

## Naming the sides

A right-angled triangle has two acute angles. Choose one of them and call it
$\theta$ (the Greek letter theta).

- The **opposite** side is the side across from $\theta$.
- The **adjacent** side is the side next to $\theta$ that is not the hypotenuse.
- The **hypotenuse** is the longest side, opposite the right angle.

The labels depend on which angle you chose. Swap to the other acute angle and the
opposite and adjacent sides swap over.

## The three ratios

For the angle $\theta$, the three **trigonometric ratios** are:

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad
\cos\theta = \frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}} \qquad
\tan\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$$

You can remember them as **SOH CAH TOA**:

- **SOH** — sine is opposite over hypotenuse;
- **CAH** — cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse;
- **TOA** — tangent is opposite over adjacent.

For a fixed angle, each ratio is the same in every right-angled triangle. A bigger
triangle is a scaled copy, so the two sides in the ratio grow together. The ratio
depends only on the size of the angle.

**Example 1.** A right-angled triangle has sides of 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. The angle
$\theta$ is opposite the 3 cm side. Write down $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and
$\tan\theta$.

The hypotenuse is 5 cm. The side opposite $\theta$ is 3 cm, and the adjacent side
is 4 cm.

$$\sin\theta = \frac{3}{5} \qquad \cos\theta = \frac{4}{5} \qquad
\tan\theta = \frac{3}{4}$$

**Example 2.** Use a calculator to find $\tan 37^\circ$, correct to three decimal
places.

Press the tan key, then 37, then equals. Make sure the calculator is in degree mode.

$$\tan 37^\circ = 0.7536\ldots \approx 0.754$$

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** The hypotenuse is never the adjacent side. The adjacent side is the
> one next to the angle, and the hypotenuse is the long side opposite the right
> angle.
>
> The common error is to use the hypotenuse as the adjacent side. For the triangle
> in Example 1 the adjacent side is 4 cm, so $\cos\theta=\frac{4}{5}$, not
> $\frac{3}{5}$.
>
> **Re-check:** mark the hypotenuse first, then mark the side opposite the angle.
> The one left over is the adjacent side.

A second trap is leaving the calculator in the wrong angle mode. Check for a small
`D` or the word `DEG` on the screen before you start.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The school building, the ground and the line to the
top make a right-angled triangle. The angle of elevation is 37°, measured from the
ground.

1. Which side is opposite the 37° angle?
2. Which side is adjacent to the 37° angle?
3. Which ratio uses those two sides?

In Section 18.4 you will use that ratio to find the height.

## You should now be able to

- label the opposite, the adjacent and the hypotenuse for a chosen angle;
- write $\sin\theta$, $\cos\theta$ and $\tan\theta$ as ratios of two sides;
- use SOH CAH TOA to choose the right ratio;
- find the value of a ratio for a given angle with a calculator in degree mode.
