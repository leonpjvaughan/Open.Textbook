# 18.7 Choosing Pythagoras or trigonometry — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\sqrt{169}$.
2. Work out $\sin^{-1}(0.5)$ in degrees.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $13$. Check it by squaring: $13^2=169$.
2. $30^\circ$. The inverse sine asks which angle has a sine of 0.5.

The first is the last step of a Pythagoras problem. The second is the last step of
an angle problem. Choosing between them is the whole of this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two sides are known and no angle is given, so use Pythagoras.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 9^2+12^2 = 81+144$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = \sqrt{225} = 15$$

The hypotenuse is 15 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 30° and a hypotenuse of 10 m. Find
the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One side and one angle are known, so use a ratio. The sides are the opposite and
the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = 10\sin 30^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 10\times0.5 = 5$$

The opposite side is 5 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 6 m and an adjacent side of
8 m. Find the angle, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two sides are known and an angle is wanted, so use an inverse ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{6}{8}\right)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.75) = 36.869\ldots^\circ$$

The angle is 36.9°, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 cm and one leg of 5 cm. Find
the other leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two sides are known and no angle is given, so use Pythagoras.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$b^2 = 13^2-5^2 = 169-25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$b = \sqrt{144} = 12$$

The other leg is 12 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A right-angled triangle has an angle of 45° and an adjacent side of 9 cm.
Find the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One side and one angle are known, so use a ratio. The sides are the opposite and
the adjacent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = 9\tan 45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 9\times1 = 9$$

The opposite side is 9 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 7 m and 24 m. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Pythagoras. Look for a square number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 49+576 = 625$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = \sqrt{625} = 25$$

The hypotenuse is 25 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 8 m and a hypotenuse of
16 m. Find the angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the inverse sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\theta=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{8}{16}\right)=\sin^{-1}(0.5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = 30^\circ$$

The angle is 30°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. You know the 15 m line to the top of the building and
the 12 m distance along the ground. You want the height. Which method do you use,
and why?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the known sides. Is an angle given?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two sides are known and no angle is given.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Use Pythagoras' theorem, because two sides of the right-angled triangle are known
and the third is wanted. The height is $h$, and $h^2+12^2=15^2$, so $h=9$ m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Solve $3x-5=16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Undo the subtraction first, then the multiplication.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 5 to both sides to get $3x=21$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x=21 \qquad x=7$$

Check it: $3\times7-5=21-5=16$.

Rearranging is the skill underneath every ratio equation in this chapter.

</details>
</details>
</details>
