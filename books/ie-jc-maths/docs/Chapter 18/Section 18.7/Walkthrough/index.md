# 18.7 Choosing Pythagoras or trigonometry — Learn

**Prerequisites:** everything in Sections 18.1 to 18.6.

**Objectives:** decide which method a question needs; solve a problem that uses
both methods; give a reason for the choice.

By the end of this section, you can look at a right-angled triangle and choose the
method that fits it.

## Three questions

Ask what you know and what you want.

| You know | You want | Use |
|---|---|---|
| two sides | the third side | Pythagoras' theorem |
| one side and one angle | another side | a trigonometric ratio |
| two sides | an angle | an inverse ratio |

The presence of an angle decides it. If the question gives an angle and a side, use
a ratio. If it gives two sides and no angle, use Pythagoras.

## Using both

Some problems need two steps. Find a missing side with one method, then use that
side in the other.

**Example 1.** A ladder is 6 m long and reaches 5 m up a wall. Find how far the
foot of the ladder is from the wall, and the angle the ladder makes with the
ground.

**Step 1.** The ladder is the hypotenuse, so use Pythagoras for the distance $d$
along the ground.

$$d^2 = 6^2-5^2 = 36-25 = 11$$

$$d = \sqrt{11} = 3.316\ldots$$

The foot is about 3.3 m from the wall.

**Step 2.** Now two sides are known. Use an inverse ratio for the angle $\theta$.

$$\sin\theta = \frac{5}{6} = 0.8333\ldots$$

$$\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.8333\ldots) = 56.44\ldots^\circ$$

The ladder makes an angle of about 56.4° with the ground.

**Example 2.** A flagpole stands on level ground. From a point 8 m away, the angle
of elevation to the top is 55°. Find the height of the flagpole.

An angle and a side, so use a ratio and call the height $h$.

$$\tan 55^\circ = \frac{h}{8}$$

$$h = 8\tan 55^\circ = 8\times1.4281\ldots = 11.42\ldots$$

The flagpole is about 11.4 m tall.

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** Pythagoras needs two sides, and a ratio needs an angle. The common
> error is to reach for Pythagoras when only one side and one angle are known.
>
> If a question gives a 30° angle and a hypotenuse of 10 m, there is only one side,
> so Pythagoras cannot start. Use $\sin 30^\circ=\frac{x}{10}$ instead.
>
> **Re-check:** count the known sides. Two sides means Pythagoras. One side and one
> angle means a ratio.

A second trap is using the wrong ratio when an angle is given. Name the sides for
that angle first.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. You know the 15 m line to the top and the 12 m
distance along the ground. You want the height.

1. Which method finds the height, and why?
2. The angle of elevation is also 37°. Which method would you use if you knew only
   the angle and the 12 m distance?

## You should now be able to

- choose Pythagoras when two sides are known and the third is wanted;
- choose a ratio when one side and one angle are known;
- choose an inverse ratio when two sides are known and an angle is wanted;
- solve a problem in two steps, using both methods;
- give a reason for the method you chose.
