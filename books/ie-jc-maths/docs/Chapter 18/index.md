# Chapter 18: Pythagoras and trigonometry

Some lengths you can measure with a ruler. Some you cannot: the height of a
building, the width of a river, the distance to a boat. This chapter gives you two
ways to find a length you cannot reach, and shows you how to choose between them.

## The chapter problem

**How tall is the school building?**

You cannot climb the building, so you measure from the ground. You stand 12 m from
the foot of the building. A long tape from your position to the top reads 15 m, and
a clinometer gives the angle up to the top as 37°.

Two measurements, two methods, one height. By the end of the chapter you can find
the height both ways, check that the two answers agree, and say why you chose each
method.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- state Pythagoras' theorem and use it to find a missing side of a right-angled
  triangle;
- label the opposite, the adjacent and the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle;
- write the sine, cosine and tangent ratios and use them to find a missing side;
- use the inverse ratios to find a missing angle;
- use angles of elevation and depression in a real situation;
- choose between Pythagoras' theorem and a trigonometric ratio, and say why.

## Where it is used

Surveying, construction, navigation, and any height or distance you cannot reach.
