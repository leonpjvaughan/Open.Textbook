# 18.2 Finding a missing side — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\sqrt{81}$ and $\sqrt{169}$.
2. Work out $13^2-5^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $9$ and $13$. Check each answer by squaring it: $9^2=81$ and $13^2=169$.
2. $144$. Work out each square first: $169-25=144$.

The second question is the shape of a missing-leg problem. Subtract the squares,
then take the square root.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 8 cm and 15 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The hypotenuse is missing, so add the squares of the two legs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 8^2+15^2 = 64+225$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c^2 = 289$$

$$c = \sqrt{289} = 17$$

The hypotenuse is 17 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 7 m and 24 m. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the squares, then take the square root.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 49+576$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c^2 = 625$$

$$c = \sqrt{625} = 25$$

The hypotenuse is 25 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 10 cm and one leg of 6 cm. Find
the other leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The hypotenuse is known, so subtract the known leg squared.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$b^2 = 10^2-6^2 = 100-36$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$b^2 = 64$$

$$b = \sqrt{64} = 8$$

The other leg is 8 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 17 m and one leg of 15 m. Find
the other leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the squares. The answer is a whole number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$b^2 = 289-225$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$b^2 = 64$$

$$b = \sqrt{64} = 8$$

The other leg is 8 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 4 cm and 9 cm. Find the hypotenuse,
correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The answer is not a whole number. Keep the value on the calculator, then round at
the end.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 16+81 = 97$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = \sqrt{97} = 9.848\ldots$$

The hypotenuse is 9.8 cm, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ladder is 13 m long. Its foot is 5 m from a wall. How far up the wall does
the ladder reach?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The ladder is the hypotenuse. The height up the wall is a leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h^2 = 13^2-5^2 = 169-25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h^2 = 144$$

$$h = \sqrt{144} = 12$$

The ladder reaches 12 m up the wall.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A rectangle is 12 cm long and 5 cm wide. Find the length of a diagonal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A diagonal makes a right-angled triangle with the length and the width. The
diagonal is the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$d^2 = 12^2+5^2 = 144+25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d^2 = 169$$

$$d = \sqrt{169} = 13$$

The diagonal is 13 cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. You stand 12 m from the school building, and the top is
15 m away in a straight line. Find the height of the building.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 15 m line is the hypotenuse, so subtract the squares.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h^2 = 15^2-12^2 = 225-144$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h^2 = 81$$

$$h = \sqrt{81} = 9$$

The building is 9 m tall. You will find the same height from the angle in Section
18.4.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Write 2.4 m in centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 100 cm in 1 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$240$ cm. Moving from metres to centimetres multiplies by 100, so $2.4\times100=240$.

Lengths in this chapter need their unit written with the answer.

</details>
</details>
