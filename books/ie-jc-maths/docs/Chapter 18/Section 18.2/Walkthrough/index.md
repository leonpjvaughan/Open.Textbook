# 18.2 Finding a missing side — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Pythagoras' theorem from Section 18.1, and square roots.

**Objectives:** find the hypotenuse from the two legs; find a leg from the
hypotenuse and the other leg; round an answer to a sensible number of decimal
places.

By the end of this section, you can use Pythagoras' theorem in both directions.

## Finding the hypotenuse

When the hypotenuse is the missing side, add the squares of the two legs, then take
the square root.

$$c = \sqrt{a^2+b^2}$$

**Example 1.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the
hypotenuse.

$$c^2 = 9^2+12^2 = 81+144 = 225$$

$$c = \sqrt{225} = 15$$

The hypotenuse is 15 cm.

**Example 2.** A right-angled triangle has legs of 5 m and 7 m. Find the
hypotenuse, correct to one decimal place.

$$c^2 = 5^2+7^2 = 25+49 = 74$$

$$c = \sqrt{74} = 8.602\ldots$$

The hypotenuse is 8.6 m, to one decimal place.

Use the square-root key on your calculator. Write down the full value first, then
round once at the end.

## Finding a leg

When the hypotenuse is known and one leg is missing, subtract. Take the known leg
away from the hypotenuse.

$$b = \sqrt{c^2-a^2}$$

**Example 3.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of 13 cm and one leg of
5 cm. Find the other leg.

$$b^2 = 13^2-5^2 = 169-25 = 144$$

$$b = \sqrt{144} = 12$$

The other leg is 12 cm.

**Example 4.** A ladder is 5 m long. Its foot is 3 m from a wall. How far up the
wall does the ladder reach?

The ladder is the hypotenuse. The distance along the ground is one leg, and the
height up the wall is the other leg.

$$h^2 = 5^2-3^2 = 25-9 = 16$$

$$h = \sqrt{16} = 4$$

The ladder reaches 4 m up the wall.

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** Add the squares only when you are finding the hypotenuse. The
> common error is to add when a leg is missing, and get a leg longer than the
> hypotenuse.
>
> If the hypotenuse is 10 cm and one leg is 6 cm, the other leg is
> $\sqrt{10^2-6^2}=\sqrt{64}=8$ cm. Adding would give $\sqrt{136}\approx11.7$ cm,
> which is longer than the hypotenuse. A leg can never be longer than the
> hypotenuse.
>
> **Re-check:** find the longest known side. If it is the hypotenuse, subtract.

A second trap is rounding in the middle of the work. Keep the full value, then round
the final answer.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. You stand 12 m from the foot of the school building.
The top is 15 m away in a straight line.

Find the height of the building. The 15 m line is the hypotenuse, so subtract.

$$h^2 = 15^2-12^2 = 225-144 = 81$$

In Section 18.4 you will find the same height using the angle of 37°.

## You should now be able to

- find the hypotenuse by adding the squares of the two legs, then taking the square
  root;
- find a leg by subtracting the known leg squared from the hypotenuse squared, then
  taking the square root;
- keep the hypotenuse alone on one side, and never let a leg come out longer than
  it;
- round once, at the end, to the accuracy the question asks for.
