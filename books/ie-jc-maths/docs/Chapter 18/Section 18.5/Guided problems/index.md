# 18.5 Finding a missing angle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $3\div5$.
2. Work out $5\div12$ as a decimal, correct to three decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.6$. Dividing 3 by 5 gives a decimal that stops.
2. $0.417$. The calculator shows $0.4166\ldots$, and the fourth decimal digit is 6,
   so the third digit rounds up.

The inverse ratios start from a fraction like these and give back the angle.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 3 cm and a hypotenuse of
5 cm. Find the angle $\theta$, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use sine, then the inverse sine key.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin\theta=0.6$, so $\theta=\sin^{-1}(0.6)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = 36.869\ldots^\circ$$

The angle is 36.9°, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 5 m and an adjacent side of
12 m. Find the angle $\theta$, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two sides are the opposite and the adjacent, so use tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan\theta=\frac{5}{12}=0.4166\ldots$, so $\theta=\tan^{-1}(0.4166\ldots)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = 22.619\ldots^\circ$$

The angle is 22.6°, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right-angled triangle has an adjacent side of 8 cm and a hypotenuse of
10 cm. Find the angle $\theta$ between them, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use cosine, then the inverse cosine key.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos\theta=0.8$, so $\theta=\cos^{-1}(0.8)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = 36.869\ldots^\circ$$

The angle is 36.9°, to one decimal place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 7 m and an adjacent side of
7 m. Find the angle $\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two sides are equal. What is $\tan\theta$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan\theta=\frac{7}{7}=1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$$

The angle is 45°. An isosceles right-angled triangle has two 45° angles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 4 cm and a hypotenuse of
8 cm. Find the angle $\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use sine. The ratio is a familiar one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin\theta=\frac{4}{8}=0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ$$

The angle is 30°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A right-angled triangle has an adjacent side of 5 m and a hypotenuse of
10 m. Find the angle $\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use cosine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos\theta=\frac{5}{10}=0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.5) = 60^\circ$$

The angle is 60°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** One acute angle of a right-angled triangle is 36.9°. Find the other acute
angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two acute angles add to 90°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$90^\circ-36.9^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$90^\circ-36.9^\circ = 53.1^\circ$$

The other acute angle is 53.1°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The chapter problem. The height of the building is 9 m, and you stand 12 m
from the foot. Find the angle of elevation, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The height is opposite the angle, and 12 m is adjacent to it. Use the inverse
tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan\theta=\frac{9}{12}=0.75$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.75) = 36.869\ldots^\circ$$

The angle of elevation is 36.9°, to one decimal place. The clinometer in the
chapter problem read 37°, which agrees.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $90-37$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is the subtraction that finds the second acute angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Take 37 from 90.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$53$. If one acute angle is 37°, the other is 53°, because the two add to 90°.

</details>
</details>
