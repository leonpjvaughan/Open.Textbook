# 18.5 Finding a missing angle — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the trigonometric ratios from Section 18.3, and finding a
missing side with a ratio from Section 18.4.

**Objectives:** use the inverse ratios to find an angle from two sides; find the
second acute angle of a right-angled triangle.

By the end of this section, you can work backwards from two sides to the angle
between them.

## Inverse ratios

The ratios turn an angle into a fraction. The **inverse ratios** turn a fraction
back into an angle. On a calculator they are the keys marked $\sin^{-1}$,
$\cos^{-1}$ and $\tan^{-1}$.

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}$$

This gives

$$\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}}\right)$$

The same pattern holds for cosine and tangent.

**Example 1.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 3 cm and a
hypotenuse of 5 cm. Find the angle $\theta$ opposite the 3 cm side.

$$\sin\theta = \frac{3}{5} = 0.6$$

$$\theta = \sin^{-1}(0.6) = 36.869\ldots^\circ$$

The angle is 36.9°, to one decimal place.

**Example 2.** A right-angled triangle has an opposite side of 5 m and an adjacent
side of 12 m. Find the angle $\theta$.

$$\tan\theta = \frac{5}{12} = 0.4166\ldots$$

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.4166\ldots) = 22.619\ldots^\circ$$

The angle is 22.6°, to one decimal place.

**Example 3.** A right-angled triangle has an adjacent side of 8 cm and a
hypotenuse of 10 cm. Find the angle $\theta$ between them.

$$\cos\theta = \frac{8}{10} = 0.8$$

$$\theta = \cos^{-1}(0.8) = 36.869\ldots^\circ$$

The angle is 36.9°, to one decimal place.

## The second angle

The three angles of a triangle add to 180°, and one of them is 90°. So the two
acute angles add to 90°.

Once you have one acute angle, subtract it from 90° to find the other.

**Example 4.** In Example 1 the angle is 36.9°. Find the other acute angle.

$$90^\circ - 36.9^\circ = 53.1^\circ$$

The other acute angle is 53.1°.

## Watch out

> **Watch out.** The inverse key is not the same as the ratio key. The common error
> is to read $\sin^{-1}(0.6)$ as $\frac{1}{\sin 0.6}$, which is a different number.
>
> On a calculator, $\sin^{-1}$ is the key marked with the small $-1$, often reached
> with the shift or second-function key. It answers the question "which angle has
> this sine?".
>
> **Re-check:** the answer to an inverse ratio is an angle, so it should be between
> 0° and 90° in a right-angled triangle. If your answer is a small decimal such as
> 0.6, you found the ratio, not the angle.

A second trap is choosing the wrong ratio. Check which two sides you have before
you press the key.

## You should now be able to

- use $\sin^{-1}$, $\cos^{-1}$ and $\tan^{-1}$ to turn a ratio into an angle;
- choose the inverse ratio that matches the two sides you have;
- find the second acute angle by subtracting the first from 90°;
- recognise that an angle answer must sit between 0° and 90° here.
