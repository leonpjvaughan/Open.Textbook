# 5.5 Percentage increase and decrease — Learn

**Prerequisites:** percentages from Section 5.4, and multiplying by a decimal.

**Objectives:** increase an amount by a percentage; decrease an amount by a
percentage; work out a percentage change from an old value and a new value.

By the end of this section, you can change a price or a quantity by a percentage, and
say what percentage change has happened.

## One multiplier for a rise

A percentage increase adds a part of the amount to the amount itself. You can do both
steps at once with a single multiplier.

To increase an amount by $p\%$, multiply by $1+\frac{p}{100}$.

$$15\% \text{ rise} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \times1.15$$

The 1 keeps the whole amount, and the $0.15$ adds the extra part.

**Example 1.** Increase 40 by $15\%$.

$$40\times1.15=46$$

You can check it in two steps. The increase is $40\times0.15=6$, and
$40+6=46$.

## One multiplier for a fall

A percentage decrease takes a part away. The multiplier is less than 1.

To decrease an amount by $p\%$, multiply by $1-\frac{p}{100}$.

$$25\% \text{ fall} \quad \longrightarrow \quad \times0.75$$

**Example 2.** Decrease 80 by $25\%$.

$$80\times0.75=60$$

The fall is $80\times0.25=20$, and $80-20=60$.

**Example 3.** A jacket costs €60. It is reduced by $20\%$ in a sale. Work out the
sale price.

$$60\times0.8=48$$

The sale price is €48. The reduction is $60\times0.2=12$, and $60-12=48$.

## A rise followed by a fall

Each change is applied to the new amount, not to the original one. So a rise followed
by a fall of the same percentage does not bring you back to the start.

**Example 4.** A bottle of orange juice costs €2.50. The shop raises the price by
$10\%$. Then the committee uses a voucher for $20\%$ off. Work out the price it pays.

First apply the rise.

$$2.50\times1.1=2.75$$

The bottle now costs €2.75. Then apply the discount to the new price.

$$2.75\times0.8=2.20$$

The committee pays €2.20. Notice that the discount is larger than the rise, because
the discount is worked out on the higher price.

**Example 5.** A phone costs €320, and the price rises by $5\%$.

$$320\times1.05=336$$

The new price is €336. Five per cent is half of ten per cent: one tenth of €320 is
€32, so the rise is €16.

**Example 6.** A car worth €12000 loses $15\%$ of its value in a year.

$$12000\times0.85=10200$$

After one year the car is worth €10200. Ten per cent is €1200, and five per cent is
€600, so the fall is €1800.

## Finding the percentage change

Sometimes you know the old value and the new value, and you want the percentage
change. Divide the change by the **original** value, then multiply by 100.

$$\text{percentage change}=\frac{\text{change}}{\text{original}}\times100$$

**Example 7.** A price rises from €40 to €46. Work out the percentage increase.

The change is $46-40=6$, and the original price is €40.

$$\frac{6}{40}\times100=15$$

The price rose by $15\%$.

> **Watch out.** A percentage increase gives a larger answer than you started with,
> and a percentage decrease gives a smaller one. From "increase 40 by $15\%$", the
> common error is to work out $40\times0.15=6$ and stop, which is only the increase.
> The amount itself must be added back.
>
> **Re-check:** decide which way the answer should move before you calculate.
> A rise on 40 must give more than 40, so 46 passes and 6 fails. A fall on 80 must
> give less than 80, so 60 passes.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The committee plans to charge 80 cent for a cup of
punch, and a 2-litre bottle of orange juice costs €2.50.

1. The committee raises the cup price by $25\%$ so that it covers the cost. Work out
   the new price in cent.
2. The shop raises the bottle price by $10\%$. Work out the new price.
3. The committee then uses a voucher for $20\%$ off that new price. Work out what it
   pays for one bottle.

The Review brings the number of jugs, the bottles of juice and the cost together.

## You should now be able to

- increase an amount by a percentage using a single multiplier;
- decrease an amount by a percentage using a single multiplier;
- apply a rise and then a fall, working each one on the new amount;
- work out a percentage change by dividing the change by the original value.
