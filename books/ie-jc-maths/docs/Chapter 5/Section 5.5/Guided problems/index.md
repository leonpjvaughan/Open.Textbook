# 5.5 Percentage increase and decrease — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $20\%$ as a decimal.
2. Work out $50\times0.8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.2$. Divide by 100, because per cent means "per hundred".
2. $40$. Multiplying by $0.8$ takes away one fifth, because $1-0.2=0.8$.

The first is the multiplier for a change. The second is how you apply it in one step.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Increase 60 by $10\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A rise of $10\%$ multiplies the amount by $1.1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$60\times1.1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$60\times1.1=66$$

Check it in two steps. One tenth of 60 is 6, and $60+6=66$. The answer is larger
than 60, as a rise must be.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Decrease 90 by $20\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A fall of $20\%$ multiplies the amount by $0.8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$90\times0.8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$90\times0.8=72$$

One fifth of 90 is 18, and $90-18=72$. The answer is smaller than 90, as a fall must
be.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A coat costs €25. It is reduced by $40\%$ in a sale. Work out the sale price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The multiplier for a $40\%$ fall is $0.6$, because $1-0.4=0.6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$25\times0.6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$25\times0.6=15$$

The sale price is €15. Check it: the reduction is $25\times0.4=10$, and
€25 - €10 = €15.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Increase 150 by $20\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $1.2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$150\times1.2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$150\times1.2=180$$

One fifth of 150 is 30, and $150+30=180$. The two methods agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ticket costs €4.80. The price goes up by $25\%$. Work out the new price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A rise of $25\%$ multiplies the price by $1.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4.80\times1.25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4.80\times1.25=6.00$$

The new price is €6.00. A quarter of €4.80 is €1.20, and €4.80 + €1.20 = €6.00.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A price rises from €50 to €55. Work out the percentage increase.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the change by the original price, then multiply by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{5}{50}\times100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{5}{50}\times100=10$$

The price rose by $10\%$. Check it: $50\times1.1=55$. Divide by the original price,
not the new one.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A coat costs €200. It is reduced by $15\%$. Work out the new price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $0.85$, because $1-0.15=0.85$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$200\times0.85$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$200\times0.85=170$$

The new price is €170. Ten per cent of €200 is €20, and five per cent is €10, so the
fall is €30. Then €200 - €30 = €170.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The committee plans to charge 80 cent for a cup of punch, and a bottle of
orange juice costs €2.50. It raises the cup price by $25\%$, and the shop raises the
bottle price by $10\%$. Work out both new prices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use a multiplier for each rise. Keep the cup price in cent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$80\times1.25 \qquad 2.50\times1.1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$80\times1.25=100 \qquad 2.50\times1.1=2.75$$

The cup now costs 100 cent, which is €1.00. The bottle now costs €2.75. A rise of
$25\%$ is a quarter of the original, and a rise of $10\%$ is one tenth of it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Write $\frac{3}{4}$ as a percentage.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 5.4. Multiply the fraction by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{3}{4}\times100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{3}{4}\times100=75$$

So $\frac{3}{4}=75\%$. A percentage change is easier to apply when the amount is
already written as a decimal or a percentage.

</details>
</details>
</details>
