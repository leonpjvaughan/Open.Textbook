# 5.2 Adding and subtracting fractions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $\frac{1}{2}$ with the denominator 6.
2. Find the lowest common multiple of 4 and 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac{3}{6}$. The denominator 2 was multiplied by 3, so the numerator is
   multiplied by 3 as well.
2. $20$. The multiples of 5 are 5, 10, 15, 20, and 20 is the first one that 4 also
   divides.

Both are the steps you need before you can add fractions with different denominators.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Work out $\frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The denominators already match, so add the numerators.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{2+3}{7}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7}=\frac{5}{7}$$

The denominator stays as 7, because the size of the parts did not change.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Work out $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lowest common multiple of 2 and 5 is 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{5}{10} \qquad \frac{1}{5}=\frac{2}{10}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{5}{10}+\frac{2}{10}=\frac{7}{10}$$

Check the answer is sensible. Both fractions are less than one half, so the total
must be less than 1. And $\frac{7}{10}=0.7$, which is indeed less than 1.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out $\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lowest common multiple of 4 and 6 is 12.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{12} \qquad \frac{1}{6}=\frac{2}{12}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{9}{12}+\frac{2}{12}=\frac{11}{12}$$

The answer is close to 1, which is sensible. The first fraction is $\frac{3}{4}$ and
the second adds a little more.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $\frac{5}{8}-\frac{1}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write $\frac{1}{2}$ with the denominator 8, because 2 divides 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{4}{8}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{5}{8}-\frac{4}{8}=\frac{1}{8}$$

Check with decimals: $0.625-0.5=0.125$, and $\frac{1}{8}=0.125$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Work out $\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lowest common multiple of 3 and 4 is 12.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{12} \qquad \frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{8}{12}+\frac{3}{12}=\frac{11}{12}$$

There is no factor common to 11 and 12, so the fraction is already in its simplest
form.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The denominator 6 works for all three, because both 2 and 3 divide 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{6} \qquad \frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{6}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{6}+\frac{1}{6}=\frac{6}{6}=1$$

The three fractions fill the whole exactly. That is why the answer is 1.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Work out $1-\frac{3}{8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the whole number 1 with the denominator 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1=\frac{8}{8}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{8}{8}-\frac{3}{8}=\frac{5}{8}$$

Subtracting a fraction from 1 leaves the rest of the whole. Here the rest is five
eighths.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** One jug of punch uses $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of orange juice, $\frac{1}{2}$
of a litre of apple juice and $\frac{1}{4}$ of a litre of sparkling water. Work out
the total volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write every fraction with the denominator 4, then add the numerators.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{4}+\frac{1}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{6}{4}=\frac{3}{2}$$

One jug holds $\frac{3}{2}$ litres, which is 1.5 litres. The top is larger than the
bottom, so the amount is more than one whole litre.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Estimate $19.6\times4.1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 4.2. Round each number to one significant figure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$19.6$ rounds to 20, and $4.1$ rounds to 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$20\times4=80$. The exact answer is 80.36, so the estimate is very close.

Rounding each number to one significant figure is a quick way to check that an
answer is sensible. You will use the same idea to check a fraction answer.

</details>
</details>
