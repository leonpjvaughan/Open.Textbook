# 5.2 Adding and subtracting fractions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** equivalent fractions from Section 5.1, and the lowest common
multiple from Section 3.3.

**Objectives:** add and subtract fractions with the same denominator; find a common
denominator and add or subtract fractions with different denominators; add three
fractions.

By the end of this section, you can add and subtract any fractions, and say why the
denominators must match first.

## The same denominator

When two fractions have the same denominator, the parts are the same size. You add
the numerators and keep the denominator.

$$\frac{1}{5}+\frac{2}{5}=\frac{1+2}{5}=\frac{3}{5}$$

One fifth plus two fifths is three fifths. The denominator says what kind of part it
is, and that does not change when you count them.

Subtraction works in the same way.

$$\frac{4}{7}-\frac{1}{7}=\frac{4-1}{7}=\frac{3}{7}$$

## Different denominators

When the denominators differ, the parts are different sizes. You cannot add them
until they match. Give both fractions the same denominator first.

The lowest common denominator is the lowest common multiple of the two denominators.
Rewrite each fraction as an equivalent fraction with that denominator, and then add.

**Example 1.** Work out $\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{3}$.

The lowest common multiple of 4 and 3 is 12.

$$\frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12} \qquad \frac{1}{3}=\frac{4}{12}$$

Now the parts match, so add the numerators.

$$\frac{3}{12}+\frac{4}{12}=\frac{7}{12}$$

**Example 2.** Work out $\frac{3}{5}+\frac{1}{4}$.

The lowest common multiple of 5 and 4 is 20.

$$\frac{3}{5}=\frac{12}{20} \qquad \frac{1}{4}=\frac{5}{20}$$

$$\frac{12}{20}+\frac{5}{20}=\frac{17}{20}$$

**Example 3.** Work out $\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4}$.

The lowest common multiple of 6 and 4 is 12.

$$\frac{5}{6}=\frac{10}{12} \qquad \frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12}$$

$$\frac{10}{12}-\frac{3}{12}=\frac{7}{12}$$

## Adding three fractions

Find a denominator that all three fractions can be written with, then add the
numerators.

**Example 4.** Work out $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4}$.

The denominator 4 works for all three.

$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2}{4}$$

$$\frac{2}{4}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4}=\frac{4}{4}=1$$

The answer is a whole number. That happens when the parts add up to fill the whole.

## Whole numbers and fractions

A whole number can be written as a fraction. The number 1 is $\frac{5}{5}$, or
$\frac{8}{8}$, or any fraction where the top and the bottom match.

**Example 5.** Work out $1-\frac{2}{5}$.

Write the 1 with the denominator 5.

$$1=\frac{5}{5}$$

$$\frac{5}{5}-\frac{2}{5}=\frac{3}{5}$$

**Example 6.** Work out $\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}$.

The denominator 4 works for all three.

$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2}{4}$$

$$\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{4}+\frac{1}{4}=\frac{6}{4}=\frac{3}{2}$$

The answer is $\frac{3}{2}$, which is 1.5. The top is larger than the bottom, so the
value is more than one whole.

**Example 7.** The punch recipe uses $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of orange juice and
$\frac{1}{2}$ of a litre of apple juice. How much more orange juice than apple juice
is that?

Subtract the smaller amount from the larger one. Write $\frac{1}{2}$ as
$\frac{2}{4}$.

$$\frac{3}{4}-\frac{2}{4}=\frac{1}{4}$$

The recipe uses $\frac{1}{4}$ of a litre more orange juice than apple juice.

## Investigate the common denominator

**Predict.** Two strips are cut into different numbers of parts. Before you move the
controls, predict whether the total changes when you cut both strips into more equal
parts.

:::figure fraction-strip

**Test.** The figure shows each fraction on its own strip, and a third strip cut into a
number of parts that suits both. Start with 2 parts in 3 and 1 part in 4, then change
the part counts and watch the third strip.

**Explain.** Write two or three sentences. Say why the third strip is cut into 12 parts
for thirds and quarters, and why the total does not change when you cut the strips into
more parts. Use the words "common denominator" and "equivalent".

> **Watch out.** Never add the denominators. From $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}$, the
> common error is to add the tops and the bottoms and write $\frac{2}{5}$. Check it
> with decimals: $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}=0.5+0.333\ldots=0.833\ldots$, but
> $\frac{2}{5}=0.4$. The correct answer is $\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{6}=\frac{5}{6}$.
>
> **Re-check:** the denominator names the size of the part, so it cannot change when
> you add. Match the denominators first, then add only the numerators.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. One jug of punch uses $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of
orange juice, $\frac{1}{2}$ of a litre of apple juice and $\frac{1}{4}$ of a litre of
sparkling water.

1. Write all three fractions with the denominator 4.
2. Add the three numerators.
3. Work out the total volume in one jug, in litres.

You will use this total in Section 5.3, when you work out how many jugs the stall
needs.

## You should now be able to

- add and subtract fractions with the same denominator;
- find the lowest common denominator of two fractions and rewrite them;
- add and subtract fractions with different denominators, and simplify the answer;
- write a whole number as a fraction, and add three fractions.
