# Chapter 5: Fractions, decimals and percentages

A fraction, a decimal and a percentage can all describe the same amount. One half is
$0.5$, and it is also $50\%$. In this chapter you learn to move between the three
forms, and to add, subtract, multiply and divide with fractions.

## The chapter problem

**The fruit punch for the school fair.** The class is making fruit punch for the
school fair. One jug holds 1.5 litres, and the recipe uses three amounts:

- $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of orange juice,
- $\frac{1}{2}$ of a litre of apple juice,
- $\frac{1}{4}$ of a litre of sparkling water.

The stall needs 12 litres of punch in total. Orange juice comes in 2-litre bottles
that cost €2.50 each.

Before you calculate anything, write down a prediction. Do you think the class will
need nearer 4 jugs, 8 jugs or 16 jugs? A prediction costs nothing, and it gives you a
number to check your final answer against.

By the end of the chapter you can work out how many jugs to make, how much of each
juice to buy, what share of the punch is orange juice, and what the juice costs. You
will return to this punch in every section.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- write a fraction in its simplest form, and order fractions by giving them a common
  denominator;
- add and subtract fractions, including fractions with different denominators;
- multiply and divide fractions, and find a fraction of a quantity;
- change between fractions, decimals and percentages;
- increase and decrease an amount by a percentage, and work out a percentage change.

## Where it is used

Prices, discounts and interest are written as percentages. Recipes and measurements
are written as fractions. A phone battery, a match report and a bank statement all mix
the three forms, so moving between them is a skill you use every week. The next
chapter uses fractions for ratio and proportion.

## Your turn to connect it

Where in your own week do you see a part of a whole? Think of a recipe, a battery
level, a sale in a shop, or a score in a match. Write down one example, and say
whether it is written as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage.
