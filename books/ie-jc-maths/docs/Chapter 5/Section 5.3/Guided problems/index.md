# 5.3 Multiplying and dividing fractions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $12\div4$.
2. Write $\frac{3}{4}$ with the denominator 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3$. Dividing by a whole number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal, so
   $12\div4=12\times\frac{1}{4}$.
2. $\frac{6}{8}$. The denominator was multiplied by 2, so the numerator is multiplied
   by 2 as well.

The first is the rule for dividing by a whole number. The second is how you prepare
a fraction for a calculation.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Work out $\frac{1}{3}\times\frac{6}{7}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the numerators, then multiply the denominators.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{1\times6}{3\times7}=\frac{6}{21}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{6}{21}=\frac{2}{7}$$

Divide the top and the bottom by 3 to simplify the answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Work out $\frac{2}{5}\times\frac{3}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply straight across, then look for a common factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{2\times3}{5\times4}=\frac{6}{20}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{6}{20}=\frac{3}{10}$$

Both parts divide by 2. The answer is less than one half, which is sensible: two
fifths is less than one half, and multiplying by three quarters makes it smaller
again.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out $\frac{3}{4}$ of 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The word "of" means multiply, so this is $\frac{3}{4}\times20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$20\div4=5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

One quarter of 20 is 5. So three quarters is $3\times5=15$.

Check it by multiplying: $\frac{3}{4}\times20=\frac{60}{4}=15$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $\frac{2}{3}\div\frac{1}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Turn the second fraction upside down and multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{6}{1}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{6}{1}=\frac{12}{3}=4$$

There are four sixths in two thirds, which is why the answer is 4. The answer is
larger than $\frac{2}{3}$, as expected when you divide by a fraction less than 1.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Work out $\frac{5}{8}\div\frac{5}{16}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Turn $\frac{5}{16}$ upside down and multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{5}{8}\times\frac{16}{5}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{5}{8}\times\frac{16}{5}=\frac{80}{40}=2$$

The two fractions have the same numerator, so the answer is the ratio of the
denominators: $16\div8=2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Work out $9\div\frac{3}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the 9 as $\frac{9}{1}$, then turn $\frac{3}{4}$ upside down.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{9}{1}\times\frac{4}{3}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{9}{1}\times\frac{4}{3}=\frac{36}{3}=12$$

The answer is larger than 9, because three quarters fit into 9 twelve times.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Work out $\frac{1}{4}\div3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Dividing by 3 is the same as multiplying by $\frac{1}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{1}{4}\times\frac{1}{3}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{1}{4}\times\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{12}$$

Sharing one quarter into three equal parts gives one twelfth in each part.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** One jug of punch holds $\frac{3}{2}$ litres, and each jug uses $\frac{1}{2}$ of
a litre of apple juice. The stall needs 12 litres. How much apple juice is needed?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the number of jugs, then multiply by the apple juice in one jug.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12\div\frac{3}{2}=12\times\frac{2}{3}=8 \text{ jugs}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Eight jugs are needed. Each uses $\frac{1}{2}$ of a litre of apple juice, so the
total is

$$8\times\frac{1}{2}=4$$

Four litres of apple juice are needed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Work out $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 5.2. The denominators differ, so find a common denominator
first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{3}{6} \qquad \frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{6}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{6}=\frac{5}{6}$$

Compare this with the multiplication $\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{6}$.
Addition and multiplication give very different answers, so read the sign with care.

</details>
</details>
</details>
