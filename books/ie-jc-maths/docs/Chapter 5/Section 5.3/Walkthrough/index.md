# 5.3 Multiplying and dividing fractions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplying and dividing whole numbers, and equivalent fractions
from Section 5.1.

**Objectives:** multiply two fractions; find a fraction of a quantity; divide by a
fraction by turning it upside down; divide a fraction by a whole number.

By the end of this section, you can multiply and divide with fractions, and say why
dividing by a small fraction gives a large answer.

## Multiplying two fractions

Multiplying fractions is the most straightforward operation. Multiply the numerators
together, and multiply the denominators together.

$$\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{4}=\frac{2\times3}{3\times4}=\frac{6}{12}$$

Then simplify the answer. Here both parts divide by 6.

$$\frac{6}{12}=\frac{1}{2}$$

You can cancel before multiplying instead, which keeps the numbers small. In
$\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{4}$, the 3 on the top and the 3 on the bottom cancel.

**Example 1.** Work out $\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{10}{9}$.

Multiply straight across.

$$\frac{3\times10}{5\times9}=\frac{30}{45}$$

Simplify by dividing the top and the bottom by 15.

$$\frac{30}{45}=\frac{2}{3}$$

**Example 2.** Work out $\frac{4}{5}\times15$.

Write the whole number as a fraction with denominator 1.

$$\frac{4}{5}\times\frac{15}{1}=\frac{60}{5}=12$$

## A fraction of a quantity

The word **of** means multiply. So $\frac{3}{8}$ of 24 means
$\frac{3}{8}\times24$.

**Example 3.** Work out $\frac{3}{8}$ of 24.

$$\frac{3}{8}\times24=\frac{72}{8}=9$$

Divide by the bottom first to see why. Since $24\div8=3$, one eighth of 24 is 3. Then
three eighths is $3\times3=9$.

## Dividing by a fraction

Dividing asks how many of one amount fit into another. To divide by a fraction, turn
that fraction upside down and multiply. The turned fraction is called the
**reciprocal**.

$$\frac{1}{2}\div\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{4}{1}=\frac{4}{2}=2$$

There are two quarters in one half, which is why the answer is 2.

**Example 4.** Work out $\frac{3}{4}\div\frac{3}{8}$.

Turn the second fraction upside down and multiply.

$$\frac{3}{4}\times\frac{8}{3}=\frac{24}{12}=2$$

**Example 5.** Work out $6\div\frac{3}{4}$.

Write the 6 as a fraction, then turn the second fraction upside down.

$$6\div\frac{3}{4}=\frac{6}{1}\times\frac{4}{3}=\frac{24}{3}=8$$

The answer is larger than 6, because three quarters fit into 6 more than six times.

## Dividing a fraction by a whole number

Dividing by a whole number is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. Dividing by
2 is the same as multiplying by $\frac{1}{2}$.

**Example 6.** Work out $\frac{1}{3}\div2$.

$$\frac{1}{3}\div2=\frac{1}{3}\times\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{6}$$

Sharing one third into two equal parts gives one sixth in each part.

## The chapter problem

**Example 7.** One jug of punch holds $\frac{3}{2}$ litres. How many jugs fill
12 litres?

$$12\div\frac{3}{2}=12\times\frac{2}{3}=\frac{24}{3}=8$$

Eight jugs hold 12 litres.

**Example 8.** Each jug uses $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of orange juice. How much
orange juice is needed for 8 jugs?

$$8\times\frac{3}{4}=\frac{24}{4}=6$$

Six litres of orange juice are needed.

> **Watch out.** Only the **second** fraction is turned upside down. From
> $\frac{1}{2}\div\frac{1}{4}$, the common error is to turn the first fraction and
> write $\frac{2}{1}\div\frac{1}{4}=8$. The correct step keeps the first fraction and
> turns the second: $\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{4}{1}=2$.
>
> **Re-check:** dividing by a number smaller than 1 gives an answer larger than you
> started with. So $\frac{1}{2}\div\frac{1}{4}$ must be more than $\frac{1}{2}$. The
> answer 2 passes that test, and the answer 8 does not fit the question.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The stall needs 12 litres of punch, and one jug holds
$\frac{3}{2}$ litres.

1. Work out $12\div\frac{3}{2}$ to find the number of jugs.
2. Each jug uses $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of orange juice. Multiply to find the
   orange juice needed for that many jugs.
3. How much apple juice is needed for the same number of jugs?

You will write these amounts as percentages of a jug in Section 5.4.

## You should now be able to

- multiply two fractions and simplify the answer;
- find a fraction of a quantity by multiplying;
- divide by a fraction by turning it upside down and multiplying;
- divide a fraction by a whole number, and check that the size of the answer is
  sensible.
