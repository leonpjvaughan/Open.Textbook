# 5.1 Equivalent fractions and ordering — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the highest common factor of 12 and 18.
2. Work out $7\times4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $6$, because 6 divides both 12 and 18, and no larger number does.
2. $28$. You will use multiplication facts like this to change a denominator.

Both skills are used in this section. The highest common factor simplifies a
fraction, and a multiplication fact builds an equivalent one.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Write $\frac{8}{12}$ in its simplest form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the highest common factor of 8 and 12, then divide both numbers by it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The highest common factor is 4, so divide the top and the bottom by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{8}{12}=\frac{8\div4}{12\div4}=\frac{2}{3}$$

Check that 2 and 3 have no factor in common other than 1, so the fraction is in its
simplest form.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Write $\frac{10}{25}$ in its simplest form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both numbers end in 0 or 5, so 5 divides them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the top and the bottom by 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{10}{25}=\frac{10\div5}{25\div5}=\frac{2}{5}$$

The highest common factor of 10 and 25 is 5, so one division is enough.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Fill in the missing number. $\frac{2}{3}=\frac{?}{15}$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What number multiplies 3 to give 15?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3\times5=15$, so the numerator is multiplied by 5 as well.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2\times5=10 \qquad \frac{2}{3}=\frac{10}{15}$$

Check it the other way round. Dividing 10 and 15 by 5 brings you back to
$\frac{2}{3}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Are $\frac{3}{4}$ and $\frac{9}{12}$ equivalent fractions? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the top and the bottom of the first fraction by the same number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3\times3=9$ and $4\times3=12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Yes. Multiplying the top and the bottom of $\frac{3}{4}$ by 3 gives
$\frac{9}{12}$. Both numbers changed by the same factor, so the value did not
change.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Which is larger, $\frac{3}{5}$ or $\frac{5}{8}$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lowest common multiple of 5 and 8 is 40.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac{3}{5}=\frac{24}{40}$ and $\frac{5}{8}=\frac{25}{40}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$\frac{5}{8}$. With the denominator 40, the numerators are 25 and 24. Since $25$ is
more than $24$, $\frac{5}{8}$ is larger. It is larger by $\frac{1}{40}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Write $\frac{1}{2}$, $\frac{3}{8}$ and $\frac{5}{8}$ in order, smallest
first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The denominator 8 works for all three, because 2 divides 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{4}{8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{3}{8}, \quad \frac{1}{2}, \quad \frac{5}{8}$$

With the denominator 8 they are $\frac{3}{8}$, $\frac{4}{8}$ and $\frac{5}{8}$. The
numerators are 3, 4 and 5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Write down three fractions equivalent to $\frac{2}{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Choose a whole number and multiply the top and the bottom by it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiplying by 2, 3 and 4 gives three fractions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

For example $\frac{4}{10}$, $\frac{6}{15}$ and $\frac{8}{20}$. Each one simplifies
back to $\frac{2}{5}$, which is how you know they are equivalent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** One jug of punch uses $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of orange juice and
$\frac{2}{3}$ of a litre of apple juice. Which amount is larger?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write both fractions with the denominator 12.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{12}$ and $\frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{12}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The orange juice. With the denominator 12, the amounts are $\frac{9}{12}$ and
$\frac{8}{12}$. So the recipe uses $\frac{1}{12}$ of a litre more orange juice than
apple juice.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Find the lowest common multiple of 6 and 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 3.3. List the multiples of the larger number and stop at the
first one that 6 also divides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The multiples of 8 are 8, 16, 24, 32, and so on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$24$. It is a multiple of 8, and $24=6\times4$, so it is a multiple of 6 as well.
No smaller multiple of 8 divides by 6.

You use this in the same way in this section: the lowest common multiple of two
denominators is the lowest common denominator.

</details>
</details>
</details>
