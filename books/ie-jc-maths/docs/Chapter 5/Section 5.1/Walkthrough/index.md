# 5.1 Equivalent fractions and ordering — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication and division facts, and the highest common factor
from Section 3.3.

**Objectives:** recognise equivalent fractions; write a fraction in its simplest
form; compare two fractions; put a list of fractions in order.

By the end of this section, you can write the same amount in different ways, and say
which of two fractions is larger.

## What a fraction is

A **fraction** names a part of a whole. It is written with one number above another.

$$\frac{3}{4}$$

The bottom number is the **denominator**. It says how many equal parts the whole is
cut into. The top number is the **numerator**. It says how many of those parts you
take.

In $\frac{3}{4}$, the whole is cut into 4 equal parts, and you take 3 of them. Three
quarters is a little less than one whole.

## Equivalent fractions

Two fractions are **equivalent** when they name the same amount. Multiply the
numerator and the denominator by the same number, and the fraction keeps its value.

$$\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1\times2}{2\times2}=\frac{2}{4}$$

The whole is now cut into 4 parts instead of 2, and you take twice as many parts. The
amount is unchanged.

You can also divide the numerator and the denominator by the same number. That makes
the numbers smaller and easier to work with.

**Example 1.** Write $\frac{12}{18}$ in its simplest form.

A fraction is in its **simplest form** when the only common factor of the top and the
bottom is 1. The highest common factor of 12 and 18 is 6, so divide both by 6.

$$\frac{12}{18}=\frac{12\div6}{18\div6}=\frac{2}{3}$$

**Example 2.** Fill in the missing number. $\frac{3}{5}=\frac{?}{20}$

The denominator has changed from 5 to 20, so it was multiplied by 4. Do the same to
the numerator.

$$3\times4=12 \qquad \frac{3}{5}=\frac{12}{20}$$

**Example 3.** Are $\frac{4}{6}$ and $\frac{6}{9}$ equivalent?

Simplify each fraction. Divide the top and the bottom of $\frac{4}{6}$ by 2, and the
top and the bottom of $\frac{6}{9}$ by 3.

$$\frac{4}{6}=\frac{2}{3} \qquad \frac{6}{9}=\frac{2}{3}$$

Both simplify to $\frac{2}{3}$, so they are equivalent.

## Comparing two fractions

Two fractions are hard to compare when their denominators differ. Give them the same
denominator first. The lowest common denominator is the lowest common multiple of the
two denominators.

**Example 4.** Which is larger, $\frac{3}{4}$ or $\frac{2}{3}$?

The lowest common multiple of 4 and 3 is 12. Write both fractions with denominator 12.

$$\frac{3}{4}=\frac{9}{12} \qquad \frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{12}$$

Now the denominators match, so compare the numerators. Since $9$ is more than $8$,
$\frac{3}{4}$ is larger.

## Ordering a list of fractions

Use the same method for a list. Find a denominator that every fraction can be written
with, then compare the numerators.

**Example 5.** Write $\frac{2}{3}$, $\frac{5}{6}$ and $\frac{7}{12}$ in order,
smallest first.

Every denominator divides 12, so 12 works for all three.

$$\frac{2}{3}=\frac{8}{12} \qquad \frac{5}{6}=\frac{10}{12} \qquad \frac{7}{12}=\frac{7}{12}$$

The numerators are 8, 10 and 7. In order from smallest, that is 7, 8, 10.

$$\frac{7}{12}, \quad \frac{2}{3}, \quad \frac{5}{6}$$

**Example 6.** Which is larger, $\frac{5}{8}$ or $\frac{3}{5}$?

The lowest common multiple of 8 and 5 is 40.

$$\frac{5}{8}=\frac{25}{40} \qquad \frac{3}{5}=\frac{24}{40}$$

Since $25$ is more than $24$, $\frac{5}{8}$ is larger.

> **Watch out.** Adding the same number to the top and the bottom does **not** give an
> equivalent fraction. From $\frac{1}{2}$, the common error is to add 1 to each and
> write $\frac{2}{3}$. Check it with decimals: $\frac{1}{2}=0.5$, but
> $\frac{2}{3}=0.666\ldots$, so the two are not the same amount.
>
> **Re-check:** multiply or divide the top and the bottom by the same number, and
> never add or subtract. The correct partner of $\frac{1}{2}$ with a 4 on the bottom
> is $\frac{2}{4}$, because $1\times2=2$ and $2\times2=4$.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. One jug of punch needs $\frac{3}{4}$ of a litre of
orange juice. The committee has $\frac{2}{3}$ of a litre left in the fridge.

1. Write both fractions with the denominator 12.
2. Which of the two amounts is larger?
3. Is there enough orange juice for one jug? Write one sentence saying why.

You will add the three amounts in the recipe in Section 5.2.

## You should now be able to

- say what the numerator and the denominator of a fraction tell you;
- make an equivalent fraction by multiplying or dividing the top and the bottom by the
  same number;
- write a fraction in its simplest form using the highest common factor;
- compare and order fractions by giving them a common denominator.
