# 15.4 Choosing sensible units and estimating — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Round 4.67 to 1 decimal place.
2. Round 127 to the nearest 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4.7$. The digit after the 6 is 7, so the 6 goes up to 7.
2. $130$. The digit in the tens place is 2, and the units digit is 7, so the 2 goes up
   to 3.

An estimate is rounded to a sensible accuracy. These two skills decide how many
figures you keep.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Which unit fits the mass of a full school bag: grams, kilograms or tonnes? Give
a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Picture the bag beside a bag of sugar, which has a mass of 1 kg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The bag holds a few books, so it is a few kilograms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The kilogram fits the mass of a school bag.

A full bag has a mass of about 5 kg. In grams that is 5000 g, which is a long number. A
tonne is a thousand kilograms, which is far too large.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A pace is about 0.7 m. Estimate the length of a corridor that takes 20 paces.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the length of one pace by the number of paces.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.7\times20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The corridor is about $14$ m long.

Twenty paces at 0.7 m each give 14 m. An estimate is a sensible value, so a whole
number of metres is enough.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Six identical books each have a mass of about 0.5 kg. Estimate their total mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the mass of one book by the number of books.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6\times0.5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The books have a total mass of about $3$ kg.

Six half-kilograms make three whole kilograms. Each mass is given to one decimal place,
so the total is a round number.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A bucket holds about 10 litres. A bath holds about 15 buckets of water. Estimate
the capacity of the bath.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the capacity of one bucket by the number of buckets.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$15\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The bath holds about $150$ litres.

Fifteen buckets at 10 litres each give 150 litres. A bath is a large container, so
litres suit it better than millilitres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A pupil estimates the width of a room as 7.28 m. Round this to the nearest 0.1 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Keep one decimal place. Look at the digit after the 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The digit after the 2 is 8, which is 5 or more.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The estimate is $7.3$ m.

The 2 goes up to 3 because the next digit is 8. An estimate to the nearest centimetre
is more accurate than a guess from pacing can be.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Estimate the height of a classroom door. Use an adult of about 1.8 m as your
reference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A door is taller than most adults, but not by very much.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A door is a little taller than 1.8 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The door is about $2$ m tall.

An adult is about 1.8 m, and a door leaves a little space above the head. Two metres is
a sensible estimate, and it is a round number.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A tap fills a 1 litre bottle in 8 seconds. Estimate how much water flows from the
tap in 1 minute. Give your answer in millilitres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the water in 1 second first, and then multiply by 60.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1000\div8=125 \qquad 125\times60$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

About $7500$ mL flows in one minute.

The bottle holds 1000 mL, so the tap gives $1000\div8=125$ mL each second. In 60
seconds that is $125\times60=7500$ mL, which is 7.5 litres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** On the school tour, a suitcase holds clothes and a few other items. Estimate its
mass, and say whether it is inside the 20 kg baggage limit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add a mass for the empty case to a mass for the clothes inside it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3+15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The suitcase has a mass of about $18$ kg, so it is inside the 20 kg limit.

An empty case has a mass of about 3 kg, and the clothes add about 15 kg. The total of
18 kg is under the limit, with 2 kg to spare.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** A suitcase is estimated at 18.4 kg. Round this to the nearest kilogram.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Keep the whole number of kilograms. Look at the digit after the 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The digit after the 8 is 4, which is less than 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The mass is $18$ kg to the nearest kilogram.

The 8 stays because the next digit is 4. A mass to the nearest kilogram is accurate
enough for a luggage estimate.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Convert 3.5 kg to grams.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 15.2. The gram is the smaller unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3.5\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 3.5 kg is $3500$ g.

An estimate is often written in one unit and needed in another. Converting is the same
skill, whichever section the question comes from.

</details>
</details>
</details>
