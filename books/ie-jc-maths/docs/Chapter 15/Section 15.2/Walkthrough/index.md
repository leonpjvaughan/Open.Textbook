# 15.2 Converting between units — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the unit links from Section 15.1, and multiplying and dividing by
10, 100 and 1000.

**Objectives:** convert a measurement to a smaller unit by multiplying; convert to a
larger unit by dividing; choose the right factor; convert between units of area and of
volume.

By the end of this section, you can change a measurement from one unit to another and
say which way the number moves.

## Two directions

A **conversion** changes a measurement from one unit to another. The size of the thing
measured does not change. Only the number and the unit change together.

The rule has two directions.

- To a **smaller** unit, **multiply**. A smaller unit needs more of them.
- To a **larger** unit, **divide**. A larger unit needs fewer of them.

A length of 1 m is 100 cm. The centimetre is the smaller unit, so the number is bigger:

100. The metre is the larger unit, so the number is smaller: 1.

## The factors to learn

These six links cover most questions.

| From | To | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| km | m | multiply by 1000 |
| m | cm | multiply by 100 |
| cm | mm | multiply by 10 |
| kg | g | multiply by 1000 |
| t | kg | multiply by 1000 |
| L | mL | multiply by 1000 |

Going the other way divides by the same number. To change metres to kilometres, divide
by 1000.

**Example 1.** Convert 3 km to metres.

The metre is the smaller unit, so multiply by 1000.

$$3 \times 1000 = 3000$$

So 3 km is 3000 m.

**Example 2.** Convert 250 cm to metres.

The metre is the larger unit, so divide by 100.

$$250 \div 100 = 2.5$$

So 250 cm is 2.5 m.

**Example 3.** Convert 4.5 kg to grams.

The gram is the smaller unit, so multiply by 1000.

$$4.5 \times 1000 = 4500$$

So 4.5 kg is 4500 g.

## Moving the decimal point

Multiplying or dividing by 10, 100 or 1000 moves the decimal point. No long
multiplication is needed.

- To multiply by 10, move the point one place right.
- To multiply by 100, move it two places right.
- To multiply by 1000, move it three places right.

**Example 4.** Convert 2.5 litres to millilitres.

The millilitre is the smaller unit, so multiply by 1000. Move the point three places
right.

$$2.5 \to 2500$$

So 2.5 L is 2500 mL.

Dividing works the same way, with the point moving left. To change 750 mL to litres,
divide by 1000 and move the point three places left.

$$750 \to 0.75$$

So 750 mL is 0.75 L.

## Area and volume

A unit of **area** is a length squared, and a unit of **volume** is a length cubed. The
factor is squared or cubed with the unit.

$$1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm} \quad \text{so} \quad 1\ \text{m}^2 = 100^2\ \text{cm}^2 = 10000\ \text{cm}^2$$

$$1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm} \quad \text{so} \quad 1\ \text{m}^3 = 100^3\ \text{cm}^3 = 1000000\ \text{cm}^3$$

Capacity and volume are linked as well. One millilitre fills one cubic centimetre.

$$1\ \text{mL} = 1\ \text{cm}^3 \qquad 1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{cm}^3$$

**Example 5.** Convert 3.5 litres to cubic centimetres.

One litre is 1000 cm³, so multiply by 1000.

$$3.5 \times 1000 = 3500$$

So 3.5 L is 3500 cm³.

## Watch out

The number grows when the unit shrinks. Dividing makes a number smaller, so dividing
is the wrong move when the unit gets smaller.

> **Watch out.** Check the direction before you multiply or divide.
>
> Change 4.5 kg to grams. The gram is smaller than the kilogram, so there are more
> grams than kilograms.
>
>
>
> $$4.5 \times 1000 = 4500$$
>
>
>
> An answer of 0.0045 g is a warning sign. **Re-check:** ask whether the unit became
> smaller or larger. A smaller unit needs a larger number.

2. Write the capacity of the bottle in litres.

## You should now be able to

- convert to a smaller unit by multiplying;
- convert to a larger unit by dividing;
- use the factors 10, 100 and 1000 for length, mass and capacity;
- move the decimal point instead of doing long multiplication;
- convert units of area and volume, where the factor is squared or cubed;
- check the direction by asking whether the unit became smaller or larger.
