# 15.2 Converting between units — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $2.5\times1000$.
2. Work out $840\div100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2500$. Multiplying by 1000 moves the decimal point three places right.
2. $8.4$. Dividing by 100 moves the decimal point two places left.

Every conversion in this section is one of these two moves. Decide the direction
first, and then pick the factor.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Convert 5 km to metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The metre is the smaller unit, so the number grows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 5 km is $5000$ m.

There are 1000 m in 1 km. To a smaller unit you multiply, and the number gets bigger.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Convert 300 cm to metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The metre is the larger unit, so the number shrinks.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$300\div100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 300 cm is $3$ m.

There are 100 cm in 1 m. To a larger unit you divide, and the number gets smaller.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Convert 3.4 kg to grams.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The gram is smaller than the kilogram, so multiply by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3.4\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 3.4 kg is $3400$ g.

Move the decimal point three places right. The 4 moves into the tens column.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Convert 5600 g to kilograms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The kilogram is larger than the gram, so divide by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5600\div1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 5600 g is $5.6$ kg.

Move the decimal point three places left. The answer sits between 5 kg and 6 kg, which
is a sensible check.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Convert 1.8 litres to millilitres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The millilitre is the smaller unit, so the number grows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1.8\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 1.8 L is $1800$ mL.

There are 1000 mL in 1 L. The factor is 1000, the same as for kilograms and grams.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Convert 4 square metres to square centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The unit is squared, so the factor is squared as well.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$100\times100=10000 \qquad 4\times10000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 4 m² is $40000$ cm².

One metre is 100 cm, so one square metre is $100\times100=10000$ cm². Then multiply by
4. Using 100 here instead of 10000 would make the answer 100 times too small.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Convert 0.6 tonnes to kilograms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The kilogram is the smaller unit, so multiply by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.6\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 0.6 t is $600$ kg.

There are 1000 kg in 1 tonne. The answer is less than 1000 kg, because 0.6 of a tonne
is less than a whole tonne.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The baggage limit on the school tour is 20 kg. Write it in grams.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The gram is smaller than the kilogram, so multiply by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$20\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The limit is $20000$ g.

The gram is a much smaller unit, so the number is much larger. Writing the limit in
grams makes the comparison clearer with a bag that weighs a few hundred grams over.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** Convert 250 mL to litres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The litre is the larger unit, so divide by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$250\div1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 250 mL is $0.25$ L.

One quarter of a litre is 250 mL. Move the decimal point three places left to write it
in litres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** How many millimetres are in 7 cm?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 15.1. There are 10 mm in 1 cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$7\times10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

There are $70$ mm in 7 cm.

The millimetre is the smaller unit, so the number is ten times bigger. This is the
same multiply-to-a-smaller-unit rule, with a factor of 10.

</details>
</details>
</details>
