# 15.3 Time, timetables and time zones — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the unit links from Section 15.1, and addition and subtraction
from Chapter 1.

**Objectives:** use the links between seconds, minutes, hours and days; read a 12-hour
and a 24-hour clock; find a time interval; read a timetable; change a time between two
time zones.

By the end of this section, you can work out a time from a timetable and move a time
from one time zone to another.

## The units of time

Three links cover most questions about time.

$$60\ \text{seconds} = 1\ \text{minute} \qquad 60\ \text{minutes} = 1\ \text{hour}
\qquad 24\ \text{hours} = 1\ \text{day}$$

Unlike length and mass, the steps are 60 and 24. They are not 10, 100 or 1000, so the
decimal-point trick from Section 15.2 does not work here.

**Example 1.** Convert 2.5 hours to minutes.

There are 60 minutes in 1 hour, so multiply by 60.

$$2.5 \times 60 = 150$$

So 2.5 hours is 150 minutes.

## The two clocks

A **12-hour clock** runs from midnight to noon and then from noon to midnight. The
first half is am and the second half is pm.

- 7:35 in the morning is written 7:35 am.
- Half past five in the evening is written 5:45 pm.

A **24-hour clock** counts every hour of the day from 00:00 to 23:59. The morning hours
keep their number, so 7:35 am is 07:35. The afternoon and evening hours add 12, so
5:45 pm is 17:45.

To change a 24-hour time in the afternoon or evening to a 12-hour time, subtract 12
from the hour. The minutes do not change.

$$17:45 \to 17 - 12 = 5 \to 5{:}45\ \text{pm}$$

To go the other way, add 12 to the hour of a pm time.

## Finding a time interval

A **time interval** is how long something lasts. Subtract the start time from the end
time, or count on from the start in whole steps.

**Example 2.** A train leaves at 08:15 and arrives at 10:05. How long is the journey?

Count on in whole steps.

- From 08:15 to 09:15 is 1 hour.
- From 09:15 to 10:05 is another 50 minutes.

So the journey takes 1 hour 50 minutes. In minutes, that is $60+50=110$ minutes.

## Timetables

A **timetable** lists departure and arrival times. Read along one row to follow a
single journey, and check that the two times are in the same clock.

**Example 3.** A bus leaves the school at 09:20. The next bus leaves 45 minutes later.
What time does the next bus leave?

From 09:20, another 40 minutes reaches 10:00. Five minutes more reaches 10:05.

So the next bus leaves at 10:05.

## Time zones

The Earth turns, so the Sun rises at different times in different places. A **time
zone** gives each place its own local time.

- **Paris is 1 hour ahead of Dublin.** When it is 09:30 in Dublin, it is 10:30 in
  Paris.
- **New York is 5 hours behind Dublin in the winter.** When it is 18:00 in Dublin, it
  is 13:00 in New York.

**Example 4.** A flight leaves Dublin at 07:35 and takes 1 hour 55 minutes. Paris is
1 hour ahead of Dublin. What is the local time in Paris when the flight lands?

First find the landing time in Dublin. From 07:35, add 1 hour to get 08:35, and then
add 55 minutes to get 09:30.

Paris is 1 hour ahead, so add one more hour.

$$09:30 + 1\ \text{hour} = 10:30$$

The flight lands at 10:30 local time in Paris.

## Watch out

Time is not decimal. The minutes are sixtieths of an hour, not hundredths.

> **Watch out.** Do not write 1 hour 30 minutes as 1.30 hours.
>
>
>
> $$1\ \text{h}\ 30\ \text{min} = 1 + \frac{30}{60} = 1.5\ \text{h}$$
>
>
>
> An answer of 1.30 h is wrong by 18 minutes.
>
> The same trap appears when you subtract. In ordinary subtraction you borrow 10 or
>
> 100. In time you borrow **60**.
>
> **Re-check:** after a time subtraction, check that the minutes are below 60.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The coach journey takes 25 minutes, the flight leaves
Dublin at 07:35 and takes 1 hour 55 minutes, and the class must be at the airport
2 hours before departure.

1. Write the 25-minute coach journey in hours.
2. Work out the time the flight lands in Dublin.
3. Work out the time the class must arrive at the airport.

for the tour.

## You should now be able to

- use the links $60\ \text{s}=1\ \text{min}$, $60\ \text{min}=1\ \text{h}$ and
  $24\ \text{h}=1\ \text{day}$;
- read a time on a 12-hour clock and on a 24-hour clock;
- change a time from one clock to the other;
- find a time interval by counting on or by subtracting;
- read a departure and an arrival time from a timetable;
- move a time from one time zone to another, and say which way the clock moves.
