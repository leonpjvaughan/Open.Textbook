# 15.3 Time, timetables and time zones — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $3\times60$.
2. Work out $135-60$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $180$. There are 60 minutes in an hour, so three hours is 180 minutes.
2. $75$. Taking away one hour from 135 minutes leaves 75 minutes.

Time arithmetic uses 60, not 10 or 100. Keep the number 60 in mind for every question
in this section.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Convert 1.5 hours to minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 60 minutes in 1 hour. The minute is the smaller unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1.5\times60$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 1.5 hours is $90$ minutes.

Half an hour is 30 minutes, so one and a half hours is $60+30=90$ minutes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Convert 240 seconds to minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 60 seconds in 1 minute. The minute is the larger unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$240\div60$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 240 seconds is $4$ minutes.

Four minutes at 60 seconds each give 240 seconds. To a larger unit you divide.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A television programme starts at 18:50 and lasts 40 minutes. What time does it
end?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count on from 18:50. Another 10 minutes reaches the next hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$18:50+10\ \text{min} = 19:00$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The programme ends at 19:30.

Ten of the 40 minutes take the clock to 19:00. The remaining 30 minutes take it to
19:30. Check that the minutes are below 60.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A train leaves at 07:20 and arrives at 09:05. How long is the journey?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count on in whole steps. From 07:20, one hour reaches 08:20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$07:20 \to 08:20 = 1\ \text{hour}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The journey takes 1 hour 45 minutes.

From 08:20 to 09:05 is another 45 minutes. Add the parts: 1 hour and 45 minutes. In
minutes, that is $60+45=105$ minutes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A 24-hour clock shows 20:15. Write this as a 12-hour time with am or pm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A time from 13:00 to 23:59 is in the afternoon or evening. Subtract 12 from the hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$20-12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The time is 8:15 pm.

The minutes do not change, so $20-12=8$ and the minutes stay at 15. An evening time
takes pm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Convert 45 minutes to hours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 60 minutes in 1 hour. The hour is the larger unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$45\div60$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

So 45 minutes is $0.75$ hours.

Three quarters of an hour is 45 minutes, and three quarters is 0.75. The answer is
less than 1, because 45 minutes is less than a whole hour.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A flight leaves at 06:40 and takes 3 hours 25 minutes. What time does it land?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the whole hours first, and then add the minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$06:40+3\ \text{h} = 09:40$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The flight lands at 10:05.

From 09:40, another 20 minutes reaches 10:00, and 5 minutes more reaches 10:05. The
extra 25 minutes splits into 20 and 5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The flight on the school tour takes 1 hour 55 minutes. Write this time in
minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the whole hour to minutes first, and then add the 55 minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$60+55$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The flight takes $115$ minutes.

One hour is 60 minutes, and $60+55=115$. Working in minutes makes the arithmetic
easier, as long as you change back at the end.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** It is 09:30 in Dublin. Paris is 1 hour ahead of Dublin. What time is it in Paris?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A place ahead of Dublin has a later local time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$09:30+1\ \text{hour}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

It is 10:30 in Paris.

Paris is ahead, so add the hour. If a place is behind Dublin, you subtract instead.
The minutes do not change when you add whole hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Round 3.47 to 1 decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 4.1. The digit after the first decimal place decides the rounding.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first decimal digit is 4, and the digit after it is 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The answer is $3.5$.

The digit 7 is 5 or more, so the 4 goes up to 5. Rounding a time or a measurement to a
sensible accuracy uses the same rule.

</details>
</details>
</details>
