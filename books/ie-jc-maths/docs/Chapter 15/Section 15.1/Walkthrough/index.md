# 15.1 Units of length, mass and capacity — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the four operations from Chapter 1, and place value.

**Objectives:** name the metric units of length, mass and capacity; write their
symbols; say what each unit measures; read a measurement from a scale; use the facts
that link the units.

By the end of this section, you can name the right unit for a measurement and read it
from a scale.

## Length

**Length** is the distance from one end of an object to the other. The **metre** is the
main unit of length, and its symbol is m.

Three other units of length are common.

- The **millimetre** (mm) is the smallest. A pin head is about 1 mm across.
- The **centimetre** (cm). A fingernail is about 1 cm wide.
- The **kilometre** (km). A walk of 1 km takes about 12 minutes.

The links between them are worth learning.

$$1\ \text{cm} = 10\ \text{mm} \qquad 1\ \text{m} = 100\ \text{cm} \qquad 1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m}$$

A ruler shows centimetres with long marks and millimetres with short marks. Read a
length from the 0 mark, not from the edge of the ruler.

**Example 1.** A pencil starts at the 0 mark and ends at the 70 mm mark. Write its
length in centimetres.

There are 10 mm in 1 cm, so divide by 10.

$$70 \div 10 = 7$$

The pencil is 7 cm long.

## Mass

**Mass** is the amount of matter in an object. The **gram** is the main unit of mass,
and its symbol is g.

- The **kilogram** (kg) is 1000 g. A bag of sugar has a mass of 1 kg.
- The **tonne** (t) is 1000 kg. A small car has a mass of about 1 tonne.

$$1\ \text{kg} = 1000\ \text{g} \qquad 1\ \text{t} = 1000\ \text{kg}$$

**Example 2.** A bag of flour has a mass of 2 kg. Write its mass in grams.

There are 1000 g in 1 kg, so multiply by 1000.

$$2 \times 1000 = 2000$$

The bag has a mass of 2000 g. A kitchen scale often shows kilograms, so press the
unit button to read grams when you need them.

## Capacity

**Capacity** is how much a container holds. The **litre** is the main unit of capacity,
and its symbol is L.

- The **millilitre** (mL) is 1000 times smaller than a litre. A teaspoon holds about
  5 mL.

$$1\ \text{L} = 1000\ \text{mL}$$

A measuring jug is marked in millilitres, often every 100 mL. Read the volume at eye
level, and read up from the 0 mark at the bottom.

**Example 3.** A jug is marked every 100 mL. Water is poured in up to the sixth mark
above zero. Write the volume in millilitres.

Six marks at 100 mL each:

$$6 \times 100 = 600$$

The volume is 600 mL.

## The tour measurements

The chapter problem has one measurement for each of the three kinds of unit.

- The coach journey is **12 km**. That is a length.
- The baggage limit is **20 kg**. That is a mass.
- The liquid limit is **100 mL**. That is a capacity.

Each one names its unit, so the number has a meaning. In Section 15.2 you will change
each of these into another unit.

## Watch out

A bigger number does not always mean a bigger measurement. The unit matters as much as
the number.

Take a desk that is 1.5 m long. The same desk is 150 cm long. The number 150 is bigger
than 1.5, but the desk has not grown. The centimetre is the smaller unit, so it takes
more of them to cover the same length.

> **Watch out.** Do not compare two measurements until they are in the same unit.
>
> The desk is 1.5 m, and a bench is 140 cm. The number 140 looks bigger than 1.5.
>
> Write both in centimetres: the desk is 150 cm and the bench is 140 cm. The desk is
> longer.
>
> **Re-check:** when the units differ, convert one measurement first. A smaller unit
> always needs a larger number.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The coach journey is 12 km, each bag may have a mass of
up to 20 kg, and each liquid container may hold up to 100 mL.

1. Write the coach journey in metres.
2. Write the baggage limit in grams.
3. Write the liquid limit in litres.

In Section 15.2 you will use these three conversions to check the class's bags.

## You should now be able to

- name the metric units of length, mass and capacity, and write their symbols;
- say what each unit measures;
- use the links $1\ \text{cm}=10\ \text{mm}$, $1\ \text{m}=100\ \text{cm}$,
  $1\ \text{km}=1000\ \text{m}$, $1\ \text{kg}=1000\ \text{g}$,
  $1\ \text{t}=1000\ \text{kg}$ and $1\ \text{L}=1000\ \text{mL}$;
- read a length from a ruler, a mass from a scale and a capacity from a jug;
- explain why the number alone does not fix the size of a measurement.
