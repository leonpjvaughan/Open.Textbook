# 15.1 Units of length, mass and capacity — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $7\times100$.
2. Work out $4500\div1000$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $700$. Multiplying by 100 makes the number one hundred times bigger.
2. $4.5$. Dividing by 1000 makes the number one thousand times smaller.

These two moves appear in every conversion in this chapter. A smaller unit needs a
bigger number, so you multiply. A larger unit needs a smaller number, so you divide.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A ruler is marked in millimetres. An object starts at the 0 mark and ends at the
40 mm mark. Write its length in centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The answer is wanted in centimetres, and there are 10 mm in 1 cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$40\div10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The length is $4$ cm.

Ten millimetres make one centimetre, so you divide the millimetres by 10. The
centimetre is the larger unit, so the number is smaller.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A measuring jug is marked every 200 mL. Water is poured in up to the third mark
above zero. Write the volume in millilitres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each mark is 200 mL. Count how many marks the water has reached.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times200$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volume is $600$ mL.

Three marks at 200 mL each give 600 mL. Read a jug from the 0 mark at the bottom, and
look at the level at eye level.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A kitchen scale shows 1.25 kg. Write the mass in grams.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 1000 g in 1 kg. The gram is the smaller unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1.25\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The mass is $1250$ g.

The gram is smaller than the kilogram, so the number grows. Moving the decimal point
three places right turns 1.25 into 1250.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Which unit fits the length of a bus: centimetres, metres or kilometres? Give a
reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Picture the bus beside a person. Is the bus a few metres long, a few centimetres long,
or a few kilometres long?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A bus is a few metres longer than a car.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The metre fits the length of a bus.

A bus is about 12 m long. In centimetres that is 1200 cm, which is an awkward number.
A kilometre is a thousand metres, which is far too long for a bus.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A bag of sugar has a mass of 1 kg. A recipe uses 400 g of it. How many grams of
sugar are left?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the mass of the full bag in grams first, so both masses share a unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1000-400$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

There are $600$ g left.

One kilogram is 1000 g. Taking away the 400 g used leaves $1000-400=600$ g. You
cannot subtract grams from kilograms directly.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A desk is 3 m long and a bench is 280 cm long. Which is longer?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write both lengths in the same unit. Centimetres suit both.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times100=300$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The desk is longer.

The desk is $300$ cm and the bench is $280$ cm. Since $300$ is bigger than $280$, the
desk is longer. The numbers 3 and 280 cannot be compared until the units match.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A bottle holds 2 litres. How many 250 mL glasses can it fill?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the capacity of the bottle in millilitres, so both amounts share a unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times1000=2000 \qquad 2000\div250$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The bottle fills $8$ glasses.

Two litres is $2000$ mL, and $2000\div250=8$. Each glass takes one quarter of a litre,
and four glasses make one litre.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The coach journey on the school tour is 12 km. Write the distance in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 1000 m in 1 km. The metre is the smaller unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12\times1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The journey is $12000$ m.

The metre is smaller than the kilometre, so the number grows. Multiplying by 1000
moves the decimal point three places right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Write down the value of the digit 5 in the number 3520.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the position of the 5. Count the places from the right: units, tens, hundreds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The 5 sits in the hundreds column.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The digit 5 has the value $500$.

It sits in the hundreds column, so it stands for five hundreds. Place value is the
same idea you use when a decimal point moves during a conversion.

</details>
</details>
</details>
