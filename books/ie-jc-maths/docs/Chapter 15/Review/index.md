# Chapter 15: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The school tour to Paris.** The coach journey is 12 km and takes 25 minutes. The
class must be at Dublin Airport 2 hours before the 07:35 flight. The flight takes
1 hour 55 minutes, and Paris is 1 hour ahead of Dublin. Find the time the coach must
leave school, the local time in Paris on landing, and check the tour measurements.

**Step 1. The time the class must be at the airport.** The flight leaves at 07:35, and
the class must arrive 2 hours earlier. Take 2 hours from 07:35.

$$07:35 - 2\ \text{hours} = 05:35$$

**The class must be at the airport at 05:35.**

**Step 2. The time the coach must leave school.** The coach takes 25 minutes. Take
25 minutes from 05:35.

$$05:35 - 25\ \text{minutes} = 05:10$$

**The coach must leave school at 05:10.** Check it forwards: $05:10+25$ minutes is
05:35, and 05:35 plus 2 hours is 07:35. The check gives the departure time, so the
answer is right.

**Step 3. The landing time in Dublin.** The flight leaves at 07:35 and takes 1 hour
55 minutes. Add 1 hour to get 08:35, and then add 55 minutes.

$$08:35 + 55\ \text{minutes} = 09:30$$

**The flight lands at 09:30 Dublin time.**

**Step 4. The local time in Paris.** Paris is 1 hour ahead of Dublin, so add one more
hour.

$$09:30 + 1\ \text{hour} = 10:30$$

**The class lands at 10:30 local time in Paris.**

**Step 5. The coach journey in metres.** One kilometre is 1000 m, and the metre is the
smaller unit, so multiply.

$$12 \times 1000 = 12000$$

**The coach journey is 12000 m.**

**Step 6. The baggage.** Each bag may have a mass of up to 20 kg. Write the limit in
grams.

$$20 \times 1000 = 20000$$

The limit is 20000 g. A suitcase of 18.4 kg has a mass of 18400 g, because
$18.4\times1000=18400$. That is under the limit by $20000-18400=1600$ g, which is
1.6 kg.

**Step 7. The liquids.** Each liquid container may hold at most 100 mL. Write the limit
in litres.

$$100 \div 1000 = 0.1$$

The limit is 0.1 L. A 500 mL bottle is five times the limit, so it belongs in the
checked bag, not in the hand luggage.

**Step 8. The estimate.** A full school bag has a mass of about 5 kg. That is well
inside the 20 kg limit, so the estimate gives no cause for worry.

Every step used one of the four sections: naming the units, converting between them,
working with times and time zones, and choosing a unit with a sensible estimate.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and say
why in a few words. The idea might be a unit conversion, a time interval, a time zone,
a choice of unit, an estimate, a percentage, or an equation.

1. Convert 6 km to metres.
2. Convert 420 cm to metres.
3. Convert 2.75 kg to grams.
4. Convert 900 mL to litres.
5. Convert 1.5 hours to minutes.
6. A film starts at 20:15 and lasts 1 hour 50 minutes. What time does it end?
7. A bus leaves at 11:40 and arrives at 13:05. How long is the journey?
8. It is 08:20 in Dublin. New York is 5 hours behind Dublin. What time is it in
   New York?
9. Convert 3 square metres to square centimetres.
10. Convert 2.4 litres to cubic centimetres.
11. Which unit fits the mass of a bicycle: grams, kilograms or tonnes? Give a reason.
12. A room takes 7 paces to cross, and each pace is about 0.8 m. Estimate the length
    of the room.
13. Round 5.38 kg to the nearest 0.1 kg.
14. Work out 45% of 80.
15. Solve $3x+7=22$.
16. A car travels 150 km in 2 hours. Find its average speed.

<summary>Answers and concise solutions</summary>

1. $6000$ m. The metre is smaller, so multiply: $6\times1000=6000$.
2. $4.2$ m. The metre is larger, so divide: $420\div100=4.2$.
3. $2750$ g. Multiply by 1000: $2.75\times1000=2750$.
4. $0.9$ L. Divide by 1000: $900\div1000=0.9$.
5. $90$ minutes. Multiply by 60: $1.5\times60=90$.
6. 22:05. Add 1 hour to 20:15 to get 21:15, and then add 50 minutes.
7. 1 hour 25 minutes. From 11:40, another 20 minutes reaches 12:00, and 1 hour 5
   minutes more reaches 13:05.
8. 03:20. New York is behind Dublin, so subtract 5 hours. The time crosses midnight,
   which is why it reads 03:20.
9. $30000$ cm². The factor is squared: $3\times10000=30000$.
10. $2400$ cm³. One litre is 1000 cm³, so $2.4\times1000=2400$.
11. Kilograms. A bicycle has a mass of about 12 kg. In grams that is 12000 g, and a
    tonne is far too large.
12. About $5.6$ m. Multiply: $0.8\times7=5.6$. An estimate can be reported as about
    6 m.
13. $5.4$ kg. The digit after the 3 is 8, so the 3 goes up to 4.
14. $36$. Ten per cent of 80 is 8, so 45% is $4\times8+4=36$.
15. $x=5$. Subtract 7 from both sides to get $3x=15$, and then divide by 3.
16. $75$ km/h. Speed is distance divided by time: $150\div2=75$.

## What you can now do

- name the metric units of length, mass and capacity, and write their symbols;
- read a measurement from a ruler, a scale or a jug;
- convert to a smaller unit by multiplying and to a larger unit by dividing;
- convert units of area and volume, where the factor is squared or cubed;
- read a 12-hour clock and a 24-hour clock, and change between them;
- find a time interval by counting on or by subtracting;
- move a time from one time zone to another;
- choose a unit that keeps a measurement between 1 and 1000;
- estimate a length, a mass or a capacity from a known reference;
- round an estimate to a sensible accuracy.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you predicted whether the coach would leave school before
5 am or after. The answer is after: the coach leaves at 05:10, ten minutes past five.
The prediction was close if you reasoned that two hours at the airport plus a
25-minute journey would take the start time back near 5 am, but not past it.

You also wrote down one measurement from your own day, with its unit and what it
measures. Take that measurement now. Write it in a second unit, and say which of the
two is easier to read. Then round it to a sensible accuracy.

That is the whole of this chapter in one small piece of work: a number, a unit, a
conversion and a sensible value.
