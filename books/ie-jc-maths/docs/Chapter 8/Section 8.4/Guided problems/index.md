# 8.4 Venn diagrams with two and three sets — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $15 + 12 - 6$.
2. Write down the multiples of 4 that are less than 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $21$. Add first: $15 + 12 = 27$, then subtract 6.
2. $4, 8, 12$ and $16$. The next multiple, 20, is not less than 20.

Subtraction like this is what fills a Venn diagram: you take a pair total and
remove the students already counted in the middle.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** In a class of 20 students, 12 like mathematics, 9 like science, and 4 like
both. How many like mathematics only, and how many like science only?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with the 4 who like both. Then remove them from each total.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$12 - 4$ for mathematics only, and $9 - 4$ for science only.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$12 - 4 = 8 \text{ like mathematics only}$$

$$9 - 4 = 5 \text{ like science only}$$

The four who like both are already counted inside the 12 and inside the 9, so you
remove them from each.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the same class, how many students like at least one of the two subjects?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the three regions inside the circles: mathematics only, both, and science only.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8 + 4 + 5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8 + 4 + 5 = 17$$

So 17 students like at least one subject. The union formula gives the same answer:
$12 + 9 - 4 = 17$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** For the same class, how many students like neither subject?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The class has 20 students. Subtract the 17 who like at least one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$20 - 17$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$20 - 17 = 3$$

So 3 students like neither subject. They are the region outside both circles, and
they are the complement of the union.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** In a three-set diagram, $n(A \cap B \cap C) = 3$, $n(A \cap B) = 8$,
$n(A \cap C) = 7$ and $n(B \cap C) = 6$. Find the number in each pair region.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each pair total includes the 3 students in the middle. Subtract 3 from each pair
total.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A \cap B$ only: $8 - 3$. $A \cap C$ only: $7 - 3$. $B \cap C$ only: $6 - 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A \cap B \text{ only} = 5 \qquad A \cap C \text{ only} = 4 \qquad B \cap C \text{ only} = 3$$

Each pair total counted the middle 3, so removing 3 leaves the students who are in
that pair and in no other set.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** For the same diagram, $n(A) = 15$, $n(B) = 14$ and $n(C) = 13$. Find the
number in each single region.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For $A$, subtract the three regions that lie inside $A$ but are not in $A$ alone:
the two pair regions and the middle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A$ only: $15 - (5 + 4 + 3)$. Do the same for $B$ and for $C$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A \text{ only} = 15 - 12 = 3$$

$$B \text{ only} = 14 - (5 + 3 + 3) = 14 - 11 = 3$$

$$C \text{ only} = 13 - (4 + 3 + 3) = 13 - 10 = 3$$

Each single region is what remains when the students shared with another set are
removed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** For the same diagram, how many students are in at least one of the three
sets?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add all seven regions that lie inside at least one circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 24$$

So 24 students are in at least one set. Check with the union rule:
$15 + 14 + 13 - 8 - 7 - 6 + 3 = 24$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The universal set for the same diagram has 30 students. How many are outside
all three circles?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the 24 students who are in at least one set.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$30 - 24$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$30 - 24 = 6$$

So 6 students are outside all three circles. They belong to none of the three
sets, and they are the complement of the union.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** For the same diagram, find $n(A \cup B)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add every region inside the $A$ or $B$ circle. The $C$-only region and the outside
region are not part of the union.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The regions inside $A$ or $B$ are $A$ only (3), $A \cap B$ only (5), $B$ only (3),
$A \cap C$ only (4), $B \cap C$ only (3) and the middle (3).

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$n(A \cup B) = 3 + 5 + 3 + 4 + 3 + 3 = 21$$

The $C$-only region is not inside $A$ or $B$, so it is left out. Check with the
union rule: $n(A) + n(B) - n(A \cap B) = 15 + 14 - 8 = 21$, which agrees.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Round 4.76 to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the two tenths it sits between, and look at the hundredths digit.
Section 4.1 has the method.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

It sits between 4.7 and 4.8, and the hundredths digit is 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$4.8$. The digit after the tenths is 6, which is 5 or more, so round up from 4.7
to 4.8.

Estimating is a useful check on a Venn diagram too. If a region looks too large for
the universal set, round the numbers and compare.

</details>
</details>
</details>
