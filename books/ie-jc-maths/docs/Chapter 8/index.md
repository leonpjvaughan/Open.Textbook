# Chapter 8: Sets

A set is a well-defined collection of things. The things can be numbers, letters,
people or clubs. In this chapter you learn how to write a set down, how to compare
two sets, and how to combine them. Venn diagrams show the same ideas as a picture.

## The chapter problem

**The club fair.** A year group of 32 students signs up for three clubs: Chess,
Drama and Science. Every student may join any number of clubs. The sign-up sheet
gives these totals.

- 15 students joined Chess.
- 13 students joined Drama.
- 12 students joined Science.
- 6 students joined Chess and Drama.
- 5 students joined Chess and Science.
- 4 students joined Drama and Science.
- 2 students joined all three clubs.

Before you calculate anything, make a prediction and write it down. Do you think
every student joined at least one club? How many students do you think joined
none?

By the end of the chapter you can find how many students joined each club on its
own, how many joined at least one club, and how many joined none. You will return
to this sign-up sheet in every section.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- write a set in roster form, name its elements and count them;
- use the symbols $\in$ and $\notin$, and tell an element from a set;
- say whether one set is a subset of another, and use the universal set and the
  null set;
- find the intersection, the union and the difference of two sets;
- read and draw Venn diagrams for two and three sets;
- work out an expression such as $(A \cap B) \cup C$ in the correct order;
<!-- tier:h -->
- use the commutative and associative properties of union and intersection.

## Where it is used

Surveys, membership lists, search filters and databases all work with sets. A
shop's stock, a music service's playlists and a school's club lists are all
collections. Combining them is how a question such as "who is in both groups?"
gets an answer.

## Your turn to connect it

Where in your own week does something belong to more than one group? Think of a
song that is on two playlists, a friend who plays on two teams, or a subject that
appears twice on a timetable. Write down one example and say what the two groups
are.
