# 6.3 Direct proportion — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $1.50\div3$.
2. Simplify the ratio $6:9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.50$. Dividing by 3 is the same as finding a third, and a third of 1.50 is
   0.50.
2. $2:3$.

Both answers are used in this section. The division finds the value of one unit,
and the ratio shows how two quantities are compared.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Four pens cost 2 euro. Find the cost of nine pens.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the cost of one pen first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2\div4=0.50$ euro for one pen.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

One pen costs 0.50 euro, so nine pens cost

$$0.50\times9 = 4.50$$

The answer is 4.50 euro, which is more than 2 euro, as it must be for more pens.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Six kilograms of potatoes cost 9 euro. Find the cost of ten kilograms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide by 6 to find the cost of one kilogram.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$9\div6=1.50$ euro for one kilogram.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1.50\times10 = 15$$

Ten kilograms cost 15 euro. The cost per kilogram is the constant here, and it is
the same for every mass.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A car travels 180 km in 3 hours at a steady speed. How far does it travel in
5 hours?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How far does it travel in one hour?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$180\div3=60$ km in one hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$60\times5 = 300$$

The car travels 300 km in 5 hours. The distance is directly proportional to the
time because the speed stays the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A recipe for 6 people uses 450 g of rice. How much rice is needed for 10
people?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the amount for one person.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$450\div6=75$ g for one person.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$75\times10 = 750$$

Ten people need 750 g. Each person receives the same amount, which is what makes
the two quantities directly proportional.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Five tickets cost 40 euro. Find the cost of eight tickets.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the price of one ticket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$40\div5=8$ euro for one ticket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8\times8 = 64$$

Eight tickets cost 64 euro. You can check it another way: 8 tickets are 3 more than
5, and $3\times8=24$, so the total is $40+24=64$ euro. Both routes agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A machine makes 240 bottles in 4 minutes. How many bottles does it make in 9
minutes?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How many bottles in one minute?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$240\div4=60$ bottles in one minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$60\times9 = 540$$

The machine makes 540 bottles in 9 minutes. The answer is more than twice 240,
which fits, because 9 minutes is more than twice 4 minutes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** On the school tour, 48 packed lunches cost 168 euro. Find the cost of 72
packed lunches.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the cost of one packed lunch first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$168\div48=3.50$ euro for one packed lunch.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3.50\times72 = 252$$

Seventy-two packed lunches cost 252 euro. Every lunch has the same price, so the
total cost is directly proportional to the number of lunches.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A shop sells 3 bars for 2.10 euro and 7 bars for 4.90 euro. Is the cost
directly proportional to the number of bars? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the cost of one bar in each offer and compare.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2.10\div3=0.70$ and $4.90\div7=0.70$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Yes, the cost is directly proportional. One bar costs 0.70 euro in both offers, so
the value of one unit is the same each time. Direct proportion needs exactly that:
the same value for one unit, whichever pair of amounts you use.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** A map has a scale of 1 cm to 3 km. Two towns are 7 cm apart on the map. Find
the real distance between them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Every centimetre on the map stands for 3 km in real life.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$7\times3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The real distance is 21 km. The scale is a direct proportion: map length and real
distance grow together, and the constant is 3 km for every 1 cm. A scale written as
$1:300000$ means the same thing, with both lengths in the same unit.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**10.** Divide 60 euro in the ratio $2:3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the parts, find the value of one part, then multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2+3=5$, and $60\div5=12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shares are $2\times12=24$ euro and $3\times12=36$ euro, and $24+36=60$ euro.

Sharing in a ratio and direct proportion both work by finding the value of one
part. In a ratio the one part is a share of an amount; in direct proportion it is
the value of one unit, such as the price of a single pen.

</details>
</details>
</details>
