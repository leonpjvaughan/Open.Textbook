# 6.1 Ratio notation and simplifying — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication tables, dividing one whole number by another,
and finding the highest common factor of two numbers from Chapter 3.

**Objectives:** read and write a ratio; explain what the order of the parts means;
simplify a ratio by dividing every part by a common factor; write an equivalent
ratio with a given part.

By the end of this section, you can write a comparison as a ratio and reduce it to
its simplest form.

## What a ratio is

A **ratio** compares two amounts of the same kind. You write it with a colon, like
this:

$$8:12$$

You read it as "8 to 12". It says that for every 8 of the first thing there are 12
of the second thing.

**Example 1.** A bowl holds 8 red counters and 12 blue counters. Write the ratio of
red counters to blue counters.

The red counters are mentioned first, so they come first in the ratio.

$$8:12$$

That is the ratio. It is correct as it stands, but it can be written in a simpler
way, which is the next idea in this section.

## The order of the parts matters

The two numbers in a ratio name two different things, and the order tells you
which is which. The ratio of red to blue is not the same as the ratio of blue to
red.

$$8:12 \qquad\text{is not the same as}\qquad 12:8$$

Read the words of the question carefully. "Girls to boys" puts girls first. "The
ratio of teachers to students" puts teachers first. If you swap the words, you must
swap the numbers.

## Simplifying a ratio

A ratio can be written in many ways, just as a fraction can. The ratio $8:12$ and
the ratio $2:3$ describe the same comparison, because both numbers have been
divided by 4.

To simplify a ratio, divide every part by a common factor. Stop when the parts
share no common factor other than 1. This is exactly the highest common factor
idea from Chapter 3.

**Example 2.** Simplify the ratio $24:36$.

Find the highest common factor of 24 and 36.

$$\text{HCF}(24,36) = 12$$

Divide both parts by 12.

$$24:36 = 2:3$$

You can check it. For every 24 of the first thing there are 36 of the second, and
$24=12\times2$ while $36=12\times3$. So the comparison is the same as 2 to 3.

Dividing by the highest common factor in one step is the quickest route. Dividing
by 2 and then by 6 gives the same answer, so a smaller factor is not wrong — it
takes a little longer.

**Example 3.** Simplify the ratio $200:150$.

$$\text{HCF}(200,150) = 50$$

$$200:150 = 4:3$$

The units have to match before you compare. Both amounts here are in grams. If one
amount were in kilograms, you would change it to grams first.

## Investigate simplifying

**Predict.** Before you move the controls, predict whether $8:12$ and $2:3$ describe the
same comparison.

**Test.** The figure draws each amount as a bar of unit parts. Set the first amount to 8
and the second to 12, then change the group size and watch the bars break into equal
groups.

**Explain.** Write two or three sentences. Say what happens when the group size divides
both amounts, and what happens when it does not. Use the words "common factor" and
"simplest form".

## Ratios with three or more parts

A ratio can compare more than two amounts. Write all the parts in order, separated
by colons.

**Example 4.** A bag holds 4 red, 8 blue and 12 green beads. Write the ratio of red
to blue to green in its simplest form.

$$4:8:12$$

All three parts divide by 4, and 4 is the highest common factor of 4, 8 and 12.

$$4:8:12 = 1:2:3$$

The simplified ratio still carries the whole picture: twice as many blue beads as
red, and three times as many green as red.

## Equivalent ratios

Multiplying every part of a ratio by the same number gives an **equivalent ratio**.
It looks different but compares the same two amounts.

**Example 5.** Write the ratio $3:7$ as an equivalent ratio whose first part is 15.

The first part went from 3 to 15, which is a multiplication by 5.

$$3\times5 = 15 \qquad 7\times5 = 35$$

$$3:7 = 15:35$$

Multiplying both parts by 5 keeps the comparison the same. This is useful when you
want two ratios to have matching first parts so you can compare them.

> **Watch out.** Simplifying is division, not subtraction. The ratio $12:18$ does
> **not** simplify to $11:17$ by taking 1 from each part. Subtract 1 from both
> parts and you have changed the comparison: $12:18$ is 2 to 3, but $11:17$ is not.
> Divide both parts by the same number instead, so $12:18=2:3$.

> **Watch out.** Do not divide only one part. The ratio $6:9$ simplifies to $2:3$
> because both parts are divided by 3. Dividing only the first part would give
> $2:9$, which is a different comparison altogether.

## Progress check

Back to the school tour. There are 4 teachers and 48 students going, so the ratio
of teachers to students is $4:48$.

Write this ratio in its simplest form. Then write one sentence saying what the
simplified ratio tells you about the tour.

## You should now be able to

- write a comparison as a ratio, with the parts in the order the question gives;
- explain why swapping the two words swaps the two numbers;
- simplify a ratio with two or three parts by dividing every part by a common
  factor;
- write an equivalent ratio with one part given;
- check that the units of the amounts you are comparing match.
