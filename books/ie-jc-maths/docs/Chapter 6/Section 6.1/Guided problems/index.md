# 6.1 Ratio notation and simplifying — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the highest common factor of 12 and 18.
2. Work out $24\div6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $6$. The factors of 12 are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12$ and the factors of 18 are
   $1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18$. The largest number in both lists is 6.
2. $4$.

Both answers are used in this section. The highest common factor is the number you
divide both parts of a ratio by, and the division is how you do it.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Simplify the ratio $10:15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the highest common factor of 10 and 15, then divide both parts by it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The highest common factor of 10 and 15 is 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10:15 = 2:3$$

Both parts divide by 5, and 5 is the highest common factor, so nothing is left to
divide. Check it: $10=5\times2$ and $15=5\times3$, so the comparison is the same
as 2 to 3.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Simplify the ratio $18:24$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What is the highest common factor of 18 and 24?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$18=2\times3\times3$ and $24=2\times2\times2\times3$, so the highest common factor
is $2\times3=6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$18:24 = 3:4$$

Dividing both parts by 6 gives $3:4$, and 3 and 4 share no common factor. You
could divide by 2 first to get $9:12$, then by 3 to get $3:4$ — the same answer,
in two steps.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Simplify the ratio $5:20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One of these numbers divides the other. What does that tell you about the highest
common factor?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

5 divides 20 exactly, so the highest common factor is 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5:20 = 1:4$$

When one part divides the other exactly, the smaller part becomes 1. The ratio
$1:4$ says there are four times as many of the second thing as the first.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Write the ratio $3:4$ as an equivalent ratio whose first part is 12.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How many times does 3 go into 12?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$12\div3=4$, so multiply both parts by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3:4 = 12:16$$

The first part was multiplied by 4, so the second part is multiplied by 4 as well:
$4\times4=16$. Multiplying both parts by the same number keeps the comparison the
same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A recipe uses 400 g of flour and 250 g of sugar. Write the ratio of flour to
sugar in its simplest form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both amounts are in grams, so they already match. Find the highest common factor
of 400 and 250.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The highest common factor is 50.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$400:250 = 8:5$$

Divide both parts by 50. The simplified ratio says that for every 8 g of flour
there are 5 g of sugar. The units are the same on both sides, so the ratio has no
unit of its own.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A class has 16 girls and 24 boys. Write the ratio of girls to boys in its
simplest form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Girls are mentioned first, so they come first in the ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The highest common factor of 16 and 24 is 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$16:24 = 2:3$$

Divide both parts by 8. The ratio is girls to boys, so the 2 stands for girls and
the 3 stands for boys. The class does not have 2 girls and 3 boys; the ratio
compares, it does not count.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Simplify the ratio $3:9:15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

All three parts divide by the same number. Find the highest common factor of 3, 9
and 15.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The highest common factor is 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3:9:15 = 1:3:5$$

Divide every part by 3. A ratio with three parts is simplified in the same way as
one with two: divide every part by the same number, and stop when the parts share
no common factor.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Write the ratio $7:5$ in the form $1:n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first part must become 1. What do you divide 7 by?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide both parts by 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7:5 = 1:\frac{5}{7}$$

As a decimal that is about $1:0.71$. The first part is now 1, so the ratio tells
you how much of the second thing there is for every 1 of the first. This form is
useful for comparing several ratios with different first parts.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Find the highest common factor of 36 and 60.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write both numbers as a product of their prime factors.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$36=2^2\times3^2$ and $60=2^2\times3\times5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{HCF} = 2^2\times3 = 12$$

The highest common factor takes the smaller power of each prime that appears in
both numbers.

This is the number you would divide both parts of the ratio $36:60$ by, so
$36:60=3:5$. The highest common factor from Chapter 3 is the tool that makes
simplifying a ratio a one-step job.

</details>
</details>
</details>
