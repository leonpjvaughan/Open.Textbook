# 6.6 Speed, distance and time — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. How many minutes are there in 1.5 hours?
2. Work out $120\div2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $90$ minutes. One hour is 60 minutes, and half an hour is 30, so
   $60+30=90$.
2. $60$.

You will need both in this section. Changing a time into the unit the answer wants
comes first, and the division is the speed calculation itself.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A car travels 180 km in 3 hours. Find its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Speed is distance divided by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$180\div3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The average speed is 60 km/h. The distance is in kilometres and the time is in
hours, so the unit is km/h.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A cyclist rides at 15 km/h for 4 hours. How far does the cyclist travel?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Distance is speed multiplied by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$15\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The cyclist travels 60 km. At 15 km/h the cyclist covers 15 km each hour, so in 4
hours the distance is $4\times15=60$ km.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A train travels 300 km at an average speed of 75 km/h. How long does the
journey take?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Time is distance divided by speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$300\div75$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The journey takes 4 hours. Check it: at 75 km/h the train covers 75 km in one
hour, and $4\times75=300$ km.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A runner covers 300 m in 60 seconds. Find the runner's average speed in
metres per second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both measurements are already in metres and seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$300\div60$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The average speed is 5 m/s. The distance is in metres and the time is in seconds,
so the unit is m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A bus travels 36 km in 45 minutes. Find its average speed in km/h.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed is wanted in km/h, so change 45 minutes to hours first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$45\div60=0.75$ hours, then $36\div0.75$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The average speed is 48 km/h. At 48 km/h the bus covers 48 km in a full hour, and
three quarters of that is 36 km, which fits the 45 minutes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The coach for the school tour travels 96 km, and the journey takes 1 hour 30
minutes. Find its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change 1 hour 30 minutes to hours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1+30\div60=1.5$ hours, then $96\div1.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The average speed is 64 km/h. At 64 km/h the coach covers 64 km in the first hour
and 32 km in the remaining half hour, and $64+32=96$ km.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A plane flies at 750 km/h for 4 hours. How far does it fly?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Distance is speed multiplied by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$750\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The plane flies 3000 km. The answer is large, which it must be for a plane, and it
is the product of a large speed and a whole number of hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A walker walks 15 km at a steady 5 km/h. How long does the walk take?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Time is distance divided by speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$15\div5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The walk takes 3 hours. At 5 km/h the walker covers 5 km each hour, so 15 km takes
three hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**9.** Convert a speed of 54 km/h into metres per second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One kilometre is 1000 m and one hour is 3600 seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$54\times1000\div3600$, or $54\div3.6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The speed is 15 m/s. Dividing by 3.6 is the quick route: $54\div3.6=15$. To change
m/s back into km/h, multiply by 3.6.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**10.** A car travels 60 km at 30 km/h and then a further 90 km at 90 km/h. Find
its average speed for the whole journey.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the time for each part, then the totals.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$60\div30=2$ hours and $90\div90=1$ hour, so the totals are 150 km and 3 hours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$150\div3 = 50$$

The average speed for the whole journey is 50 km/h. The answer is not the mean of
30 and 90, which is 60, because the car spends two hours at the slower speed and
only one hour at the faster speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**11.** A car travels 120 km in 2 hours at a steady speed. How far does it travel
in 5 hours?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the distance travelled in one hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$120\div2=60$ km in one hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$60\times5 = 300$$

The car travels 300 km in 5 hours.

The distance is directly proportional to the time when the speed stays the same,
so the unitary method from Section 6.3 works here too. The distance travelled in
one hour is the same as the speed, 60 km/h.

</details>
</details>
</details>
