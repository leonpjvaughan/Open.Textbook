# 6.6 Speed, distance and time — Learn

**Prerequisites:** units of length and time, division, and direct proportion from
Section 6.3.

**Objectives:** use the relationship between speed, distance and time; rearrange
it for distance and for time; keep the units consistent; convert between km/h and
m/s.

By the end of this section, you can answer any question about a steady journey and
say what the units of your answer mean.

## Speed is a rate

**Speed** tells you how far something travels in a given time. It compares a
distance with a time, so it is a **rate**. You write it with a slash: km/h means
kilometres per hour, and m/s means metres per second.

A car that travels 120 km in 2 hours covers 60 km in each hour. Its average speed
is 60 km/h. The distance and the time are directly proportional when the speed
stays the same, so doubling the time doubles the distance.

## The three formulas

One relationship gives you all three formulas.

$$\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$

Rearranged for the other two:

$$\text{distance} = \text{speed}\times\text{time} \qquad \text{time} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}}$$

You do not need to memorise three separate formulas. Learn the first one and move
the letters when a question asks for distance or time.

**Example 1.** A car travels 150 km in 3 hours. Find its average speed.

$$150\div3 = 50$$

The average speed is 50 km/h.

**Example 2.** A train travels 240 km at an average speed of 80 km/h. How long does
the journey take?

This time the question asks for time, so divide the distance by the speed.

$$240\div80 = 3$$

The journey takes 3 hours.

## Keeping the units consistent

The unit of the answer comes from the units you put in. Kilometres divided by
hours gives km/h. Metres divided by seconds gives m/s.

When a time is given in minutes and the speed is wanted in km/h, change the
minutes to hours first.

**Example 3.** The coach for the school tour travels 96 km, and the journey takes
1 hour 30 minutes. Find its average speed.

Change the time to hours.

$$1\ \text{hour}\ 30\ \text{minutes} = 1 + \frac{30}{60} = 1.5\ \text{hours}$$

Then divide.

$$96\div1.5 = 64$$

The average speed is 64 km/h.

You can check it in your head. At 64 km/h the coach covers 64 km in the first
hour, leaving 32 km, which is half of 64. So the remaining distance takes half an
hour, and the whole journey takes 1 hour 30 minutes.

## Changing between km/h and m/s

Science often uses m/s, and road signs use km/h. To change between them, use the
two conversions.

$$1\ \text{km} = 1000\ \text{m} \qquad 1\ \text{hour} = 3600\ \text{seconds}$$

**Example 4.** Convert a speed of 72 km/h into metres per second.

$$72\times1000\div3600 = 20$$

So 72 km/h is 20 m/s.

There is a shortcut: divide the speed in km/h by 3.6 to get m/s, and multiply by
3.6 to go the other way. Both methods give the same answer because
$1000\div3600$ is $\frac{1}{3.6}$.

## Average speed over a whole journey

Average speed means the total distance divided by the total time, not the average
of two speeds.

**Example 5.** A car travels 60 km at 40 km/h and then a further 60 km at 60 km/h.
Find the average speed for the whole journey.

Find the time for each part.

$$60\div40 = 1.5\ \text{hours} \qquad 60\div60 = 1\ \text{hour}$$

The whole journey is 120 km in 2.5 hours.

$$120\div2.5 = 48$$

The average speed for the whole journey is 48 km/h.

The answer is not 50 km/h, which is the mean of 40 and 60. The car spends longer
travelling at the slower speed, so the average is pulled down towards 40.

> **Watch out.** Do not mix kilometres with minutes. If the distance is in
> kilometres and the time is in minutes, change the minutes to hours before you
> divide, or change the kilometres to metres and the minutes to seconds. Mixing
> the units gives an answer with no sensible unit at all.

> **Watch out.** Average speed is not the mean of two speeds when the two parts
> take different times. Add the distances, add the times, and divide the totals.
> The mean of the speeds only works when both parts take the same time.

## Progress check

Back to the school tour. The coach travels 96 km to the museum, and the journey
takes 1 hour 30 minutes.

Find the average speed in km/h. Then write one sentence saying why the answer is
not $96\div1$, which would come from reading the time as one hour.

## You should now be able to

- use $\text{speed}=\text{distance}\div\text{time}$ and rearrange it for distance
  and time;
- change a time given in minutes into hours before dividing;
- convert a speed between km/h and m/s;
- find an average speed for a whole journey by dividing the total distance by the
  total time;
- check that the unit of your answer matches the units you used.
