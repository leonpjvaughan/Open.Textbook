# 6.4 Inverse proportion — Learn

**Prerequisites:** direct proportion from Section 6.3, and multiplying and
dividing whole numbers and decimals.

**Objectives:** recognise when two quantities are inversely proportional; use the
constant product to find a missing value; tell direct proportion from inverse
proportion.

By the end of this section, you can answer questions where more of one thing means
less of the other.

## Two quantities that move in opposite directions

Two quantities are in **inverse proportion** when one goes up and the other goes
down by the same factor. Double one and the other halves. Treble one and the other
falls to a third.

Four workers build a wall in 6 hours. With more workers, the wall goes up faster,
so the time is shorter. The number of workers and the time move in opposite
directions. This is inverse proportion.

The constant that links them is the **product**. For the wall, the product of the
workers and the hours is the total amount of work.

$$4\times6 = 24\ \text{worker-hours}$$

That product does not change. Whatever number of workers you use, workers times
hours must come to 24.

## The method in two steps

1. Multiply the two given values to find the constant product.
2. Divide the constant by the new value.

**Example 1.** Four workers build a wall in 6 hours. How long would three workers
take, working at the same rate?

**Step 1.** Find the constant product.

$$4\times6 = 24$$

The job takes 24 worker-hours.

**Step 2.** Divide by the new number of workers.

$$24\div3 = 8$$

Three workers take 8 hours.

Check the direction. Three workers are fewer than four, so the job must take
longer. Eight hours is longer than 6 hours, so the answer is the right way round.

## Speed and time

Speed and time for a fixed journey are inversely proportional. Going faster means
arriving sooner, and the distance stays the same.

**Example 2.** A car takes 4 hours to make a journey at 60 km/h. How long does the
same journey take at 80 km/h?

$$60\times4 = 240$$

The journey is 240 km.

$$240\div80 = 3$$

At 80 km/h the journey takes 3 hours.

The speed went up, so the time came down, and the distance of 240 km is the same
in both parts of the calculation.

## The school tour

**Example 3.** Six people share a taxi and each pays 8 euro. How much would each
pay if four people shared the same taxi?

$$6\times8 = 48$$

The taxi fare is 48 euro.

$$48\div4 = 12$$

Each of four people pays 12 euro.

Fewer people means more each, because the same fare is shared between fewer
people. The number of people and the cost per person are inversely proportional.

## Direct or inverse?

The two ideas look similar on the page, so decide which one fits the situation.

| The question says | The quantities are | The constant is |
|---|---|---|
| More of one gives more of the other | in direct proportion | the value of one unit |
| More of one gives less of the other | in inverse proportion | the product of the two |

**Example 4.** Decide whether each pair is in direct or inverse proportion.

- The cost of pens and the number of pens. More pens cost more, so this is
  **direct** proportion.
- The time to paint a room and the number of painters. More painters take less
  time, so this is **inverse** proportion.
- The number of people sharing a taxi and the cost each pays. More people means
  less each, so this is **inverse** proportion.

> **Watch out.** Do not use the unitary method for an inverse proportion question.
> If 4 workers take 6 hours, one worker does not take $6\div4=1.5$ hours — one
> worker takes much longer, in fact 24 hours. Multiplying first, then dividing,
> keeps the product constant.

> **Watch out.** Check the direction of the answer. In inverse proportion, more of
> the first quantity must give a smaller answer for the second. If you get a bigger
> answer when the other quantity went up, you have divided where you should have
> multiplied, or the other way round.

## Progress check

Back to the school tour. The coach costs 480 euro.

If 40 students go, each pays 12 euro. Find what each pays if 48 students go. Then
write one sentence saying why the cost per student is smaller when more students
travel.

## You should now be able to

- say whether two quantities are in direct or inverse proportion;
- find the constant product for an inverse proportion question;
- divide the constant by the new value to find the missing quantity;
- check that your answer moved in the right direction.
