# 6.2 Dividing a quantity in a given ratio — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Simplify the ratio $15:25$.
2. Work out $96\div8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3:5$. Divide both parts by 5, the highest common factor of 15 and 25.
2. $12$.

You will need both in this section. Simplifying a ratio gives you the smallest
whole-number parts to work with, and the division is how you find the value of one
part.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Divide 40 euro in the ratio $1:3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the parts, then divide the total by that number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1+3=4$, so $40\div4=10$ euro in one part.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shares are $1\times10=10$ euro and $3\times10=30$ euro.

Check: $10+30=40$ euro. The share with 3 parts is three times the share with 1
part, which is what the ratio says.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Divide 72 in the ratio $5:4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How many parts are there altogether?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$5+4=9$, and $72\div9=8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shares are $5\times8=40$ and $4\times8=32$.

Check: $40+32=72$. The first share is the larger one, because 5 parts is more than
4 parts.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Divide 90 kg in the ratio $2:3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the parts first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2+3=5$, and $90\div5=18$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shares are $2\times18=36$ kg and $3\times18=54$ kg.

Check: $36+54=90$ kg. The unit stays with the answer, because you divided a mass
in kilograms.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Divide 180 in the ratio $1:2:3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are three parts to add this time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1+2+3=6$, and $180\div6=30$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shares are 30, 60 and 90.

Check: $30+60+90=180$. The middle share is twice the first and the last is three
times the first, which is exactly what $1:2:3$ says.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Share 320 euro in the ratio $3:5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the value of one part, then multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3+5=8$, and $320\div8=40$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shares are $3\times40=120$ euro and $5\times40=200$ euro.

Check: $120+200=320$ euro. The larger share goes with the 5, so 200 euro is the
bigger amount.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A piece of wood 1.5 m long is cut in the ratio $2:3$. Find the length of
each piece in centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The answer is asked for in centimetres, so change 1.5 m to centimetres first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1.5\times100=150$ cm. Then $2+3=5$, and $150\div5=30$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The pieces are $2\times30=60$ cm and $3\times30=90$ cm.

Check: $60+90=150$ cm. Working in centimetres from the start avoids a conversion at
the end, which is where a unit error usually creeps in.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Divide 100 in the ratio $1:1:3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two of the parts are the same size. Add all three parts anyway.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1+1+3=5$, and $100\div5=20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shares are 20, 20 and 60.

Check: $20+20+60=100$. The first two shares are equal because their parts are
equal. A ratio with a repeated part shares the amount equally between those two.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Two amounts are in the ratio $4:1$, and the difference between them is 27.
Find the two amounts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This time you are given the difference, not the total. The difference is how many
parts?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4-1=3$ parts, and those 3 parts are worth 27. So one part is $27\div3=9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The amounts are $4\times9=36$ and $1\times9=9$.

Check: $36-9=27$, which is the difference you were given. When the question gives
the difference, divide the difference by the difference in the parts, not by the
sum of the parts.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Simplify the ratio $24:36$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the highest common factor of 24 and 36.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The highest common factor is 12.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$24:36 = 2:3$$

Simplify the ratio before you share anything. A ratio such as $20:28$ from the
school tour is much easier to work with as $5:7$, and the shares are identical
either way.

</details>
</details>
</details>
