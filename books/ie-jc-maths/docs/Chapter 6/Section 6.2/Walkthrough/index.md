# 6.2 Dividing a quantity in a given ratio — Learn

**Prerequisites:** simplifying ratios from Section 6.1, and the four operations on
whole numbers and decimals.

**Objectives:** divide a quantity in a given ratio; check that the shares add back
to the whole; handle a ratio with three parts.

By the end of this section, you can share any amount in a ratio and prove that
your shares are right.

## The method in three steps

Dividing an amount in a ratio is a fair-share question. The ratio tells you how
many parts each person receives, not how much money or how many sweets.

**Example 1.** Divide 60 euro in the ratio $2:3$.

**Step 1.** Add the parts.

$$2+3=5$$

There are 5 equal parts in total.

**Step 2.** Find the value of one part.

$$60\div5=12$$

One part is 12 euro.

**Step 3.** Multiply by the number of parts in each share.

$$2\times12=24 \qquad 3\times12=36$$

The shares are 24 euro and 36 euro.

**Check.** The shares must add back to the whole.

$$24+36=60$$

They do, so the answer is right.

## A second example

**Example 2.** Divide 45 kg in the ratio $4:5$.

$$4+5=9 \qquad 45\div9=5$$

One part is 5 kg. Then

$$4\times5=20 \qquad 5\times5=25$$

The shares are 20 kg and 25 kg, and $20+25=45$ kg.

Notice that the share with the larger number of parts is the larger share. That is
a quick way to see whether your answer is the right way round.

## A ratio with three parts

The method does not change when the ratio has three parts. Add all the parts.

**Example 3.** Divide 84 in the ratio $1:2:4$.

$$1+2+4=7 \qquad 84\div7=12$$

One part is 12. Then

$$1\times12=12 \qquad 2\times12=24 \qquad 4\times12=48$$

The three shares are 12, 24 and 48, and $12+24+48=84$.

## Sharing money

**Example 4.** Share 250 euro between two people in the ratio $7:3$.

$$7+3=10 \qquad 250\div10=25$$

One part is 25 euro. Then

$$7\times25=175 \qquad 3\times25=75$$

The shares are 175 euro and 75 euro, and $175+75=250$.

You can also read the difference straight off. The difference between the shares
is $7-3=4$ parts, and $4\times25=100$ euro, which matches $175-75=100$.

> **Watch out.** The two numbers in the ratio are **not** the two shares. In
> Example 1 the ratio is $2:3$, but the shares are 24 euro and 36 euro, not 2 euro
> and 3 euro. The ratio tells you how many parts each share gets; one part is
> worth 12 euro.

> **Watch out.** Add the parts before you divide. If you divide 60 by 2 and by 3
> you get 30 and 20, which add to only 50 and are not the right shares. The total
> number of parts is $2+3=5$, and every part is worth the same.

## Progress check

Back to the school tour. The coach costs 480 euro, and the two classes share the
cost in the ratio of their numbers. Class A has 20 students and Class B has 28, so
the ratio is $20:28$, which simplifies to $5:7$.

Find each class's share of the 480 euro. Then check that your two shares add back
to 480 euro.

## You should now be able to

- divide a quantity in a two-part or three-part ratio;
- find the value of one part by dividing the whole by the total number of parts;
- check your shares by adding them back to the whole;
- use the size of the parts to see which share should be larger.
