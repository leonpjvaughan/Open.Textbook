# Chapter 6: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The school tour.** The coach holds 53 seats and costs 480 euro to hire. There
are 48 students and 4 teachers going. Class A has 20 students and Class B has 28,
and the two classes share the coach cost in the ratio of their numbers. The museum
is 96 km away, and the journey takes 1 hour 30 minutes. Each packed lunch costs
3.50 euro.

Work through it one question at a time. Each part uses one idea from the chapter.

**The ratio of teachers to students.** There are 4 teachers and 48 students, so the
ratio is $4:48$. Divide both parts by 4, the highest common factor.

$$4:48 = 1:12$$

There is one teacher for every 12 students.

**The two classes' shares of the coach cost.** The class sizes are 20 and 28, so
the ratio is $20:28$. Simplify it by dividing both parts by 4.

$$20:28 = 5:7$$

Add the parts and find the value of one part.

$$5+7=12 \qquad 480\div12=40$$

One part is 40 euro. Then

$$5\times40=200 \qquad 7\times40=280$$

Class A pays 200 euro and Class B pays 280 euro, and $200+280=480$ euro.

**The cost per student for the coach.** Divide the coach cost by the number of
students.

$$480\div48=10$$

Each student pays 10 euro towards the coach.

**The packed lunches.** Each lunch costs 3.50 euro, so 48 lunches cost

$$3.50\times48=168$$

euro. The total cost per student is the coach share plus one lunch.

$$10+3.50=13.50$$

**Each student pays 13.50 euro altogether.**

**The average speed of the coach.** Change 1 hour 30 minutes to 1.5 hours, then
divide.

$$96\div1.5=64$$

The coach averages 64 km/h.

**This year against last year.** Last year the coach cost 450 euro. The absolute
increase is

$$480-450=30$$

euro. The relative increase is

$$\frac{30}{450}=0.0666\ldots$$

which is 6.7% to 1 decimal place.

So the tour costs each student 13.50 euro, the coach averages 64 km/h, and the
coach hire is up by 30 euro, or 6.7%, on last year.

Two checks are worth making. The two class shares add back to 480 euro, so nothing
was lost in the sharing. And the coach cost per student is only 10 euro, because
48 students share the cost — a smaller group would each pay more.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and
say why in a few words. Then calculate. Choosing between ratio, direct proportion,
inverse proportion, percentage change and speed is the skill; the arithmetic
follows from it.

1. Write the ratio $45:60$ in its simplest form.
2. Divide 84 in the ratio $3:4$.
3. Five kilograms of rice cost 8 euro. Find the cost of twelve kilograms.
4. Six workers take 10 hours to finish a job. How long would four workers take?
5. A price rises from 60 euro to 66 euro. Find the absolute increase and the
   percentage increase.
6. A car travels 210 km in 2.5 hours. Find its average speed.
7. Write the ratio $48:36$ in its simplest form, and say which of the two amounts
   is larger.
8. A recipe for 6 people uses 480 g of flour. How much flour is needed for 15
   people?
9. Eight people share a prize equally and receive 35 euro each. How much would
   each of fourteen people receive from the same prize?
10. The coach costs 480 euro. If 40 students go, each pays 12 euro. Find what each
    pays if 60 students go.
11. Round 0.007349 to 2 significant figures.
12. Find the highest common factor of 84 and 126.
13. Work out $2^{3}\times3^{2}$.
14. Write 5200000 in standard form.
15. **H** Two amounts are in the ratio $7:4$, and their difference is 18 euro. Find
    the two amounts.

## What you can now do

- write a comparison as a ratio, and simplify it by dividing every part by a
  common factor;
- divide a quantity in a given ratio, and check the shares add back to the whole;
- recognise direct proportion and use the unitary method to find a missing value;
- recognise inverse proportion and use the constant product;
- find an absolute change and a relative change, and use the percentage to compare
  two changes;
- use the relationship between speed, distance and time, keeping the units
  consistent.

## Thinking back

Look back at what you wrote at the start of the chapter, where you described
something in your own week that gets shared out or compared with something else.

Now work it out with numbers. If it is a shared bill, find the total and the ratio,
then divide it. If it is a price change, find the absolute change and the
percentage change. If it is a journey, find the distance and the time, then divide
to get the speed.

One question is worth asking about anything you found. Was the change you looked
at an absolute change or a relative one? A price that goes up by 2 euro is a small
change on a phone and a large one on a bus fare. Writing down which kind of change
you measured is what makes the number mean something.
