# 6.5 Absolute and relative comparison — Learn

**Prerequisites:** fractions, decimals and percentages, and ratio notation from
Section 6.1.

**Objectives:** find an absolute change; find a relative change as a percentage;
use both to compare two changes; compare two proportions.

By the end of this section, you can say both how much something changed and how
big that change is compared with where it started.

## Two ways to describe a change

Suppose the price of a bus fare rises from 2 euro to 2.50 euro. There are two
honest ways to describe the change.

- The fare rose by **50 cent**. This is the **absolute change**. It keeps the
  units of the thing being measured.
- The fare rose by **25%**. This is the **relative change**. It compares the
  change with the original fare, and it has no units.

Both statements are true, and they answer different questions. The absolute change
says how much. The relative change says how big that amount is compared with the
starting value.

## The absolute change

The absolute change is the new value minus the original value.

$$\text{absolute change} = \text{new value} - \text{original value}$$

A positive answer is an increase. A negative answer is a decrease. The units are
the same as the values you started with.

**Example 1.** A price rises from 40 euro to 44 euro. Find the absolute increase.

$$44-40 = 4$$

The absolute increase is 4 euro.

## The relative change

The relative change is the absolute change divided by the **original** value.

$$\text{relative change} = \frac{\text{absolute change}}{\text{original value}}$$

Write the result as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage. A percentage is the
usual form for comparing two changes.

**Example 2.** Using the same prices, find the percentage increase.

$$\frac{4}{40} = 0.1 = 10\%$$

The price rose by 10%.

You can put the two steps together as one formula:

$$\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{new} - \text{original}}{\text{original}}\times100\%$$

## A decrease

The method is the same when the value falls. Divide by the original value, which
is the larger number.

**Example 3.** A price falls from 80 euro to 60 euro. Find the absolute decrease
and the percentage decrease.

$$80-60 = 20 \qquad \frac{20}{80} = 0.25 = 25\%$$

The price fell by 20 euro, which is a decrease of 25%.

## Comparing two changes

Two changes can have the same absolute size and very different relative sizes.
Percentages are how you compare them.

**Example 4.** Which is the bigger increase, 5 euro on 20 euro or 5 euro on 50
euro?

$$\frac{5}{20} = 25\% \qquad \frac{5}{50} = 10\%$$

The increase of 5 euro on 20 euro is the bigger change in relative terms. The
absolute increase is the same 5 euro in both cases, so only the percentage can
tell them apart.

## Comparing two proportions

The same idea compares two proportions, such as the share of students who walk to
school in two classes. Divide each part by its own total, then compare the
results.

**Example 5.** In Class A, 15 of 20 students walk to school. In Class B, 18 of 25
students walk to school. Which class has the higher proportion of walkers?

$$\frac{15}{20} = 75\% \qquad \frac{18}{25} = 72\%$$

Class A has the higher proportion. Class B has more walkers in total, but its
class is larger, so the proportion is slightly lower.

## The school tour

**Example 6.** Last year the coach cost 450 euro. This year it costs 480 euro.
Find the absolute increase and the percentage increase, correct to 1 decimal
place.

$$480-450 = 30$$

The absolute increase is 30 euro.

$$\frac{30}{450} = 0.0666\ldots = 6.7\%$$

The percentage increase is 6.7% to 1 decimal place. The fraction $\frac{30}{450}$
simplifies to $\frac{1}{15}$, so the exact percentage is $6\frac{2}{3}$%.

> **Watch out.** Divide by the **original** value, not the new one. For a rise
> from 40 to 44, the increase is $4\div40=10\%$, not $4\div44=9.1\%$. The original
> value is the one you started with, and it is the base the percentage describes.

> **Watch out.** A rise and a fall of the same percentage do not cancel out. A
> price of 50 euro rises by 10% to 55 euro, and then falls by 10% to 49.50 euro,
> not back to 50 euro. The second percentage is taken from a different starting
> value.

## Progress check

Back to the school tour. The coach cost 450 euro last year and 480 euro this year.

Find the absolute increase and the percentage increase. Then write one sentence
saying which of the two best describes the change to a reader who has never seen
the old price.

## You should now be able to

- find an absolute change by subtracting, and keep its units;
- find a relative change by dividing by the original value;
- write a relative change as a percentage;
- compare two changes or two proportions using percentages;
- explain why an absolute change and a percentage can tell different stories.
