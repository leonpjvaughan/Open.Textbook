# 14.4 Graphing quadratic functions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $(-3)^2$.
2. Solve $x-2=0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $9$, because $(-3)\times(-3)=9$.
2. $x=2$. Add 2 to both sides.

Both skills appear in this section. The first one fills a table for a quadratic, and
the second one finds a root from a bracket.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Make a table of values for $y=x^2$ for $x=-2$, $-1$, $0$, $1$ and $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square each input. The square of a negative number is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(-2)^2 \quad (-1)^2 \quad 0^2 \quad 1^2 \quad 2^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The values are 4, 1, 0, 1 and 4.

The curve is a U-shape with its lowest point at $(0,0)$, and it is symmetrical.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the value of $y$ when $x=-5$, using $y=x^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put the input in brackets before you square it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(-5)^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = 25$$

The output is positive, even though the input is negative. This is why the left side of
the curve rises.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the value of $y$ when $x=3$, using $y=x^2+1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the square before the addition.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3^2+1 = 9+1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = 10$$

Adding 1 moves every point of the curve up by 1, so the lowest point is $(0,1)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the values of $x$ where the graph of $y=x^2-9$ crosses the horizontal axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The curve crosses where $y=0$. Solve $x^2-9=0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 = 9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 3$$

Both values make $y=0$, so the curve crosses the axis at $x=-3$ and at $x=3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A quadratic graph crosses the horizontal axis at $x=3$ and $x=4$. Write its rule
in factorised form, and expand it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A root at $x=3$ gives the factor $(x-3)$. A root at $x=4$ gives the factor $(x-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$y = (x-3)(x-4)$$

Multiply the two brackets.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = x^2-7x+12$$

Check: at $x=3$, $9-21+12=0$. At $x=4$, $16-28+12=0$. Both roots work.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A quadratic graph crosses the horizontal axis at $x=-1$ and $x=2$. Write its
rule in factorised form, and expand it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A root at $x=-1$ gives the factor $(x+1)$, because substituting $-1$ makes it zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$y = (x+1)(x-2)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = x^2-x-2$$

Check: at $x=-1$, $1+1-2=0$. At $x=2$, $4-2-2=0$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The garden bed has area $A(x)=12x-2x^2$ square metres. Find $A(1)$ and $A(5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute each value and work out the index before the subtraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A(1) = 12-2 = 10 \qquad A(5) = 60-50 = 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both areas are $10\ \text{m}^2$.

The two widths are at the same height on the curve, because the curve is symmetrical
about its maximum. The maximum is halfway between them, at a width of 3 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The garden bed has area $A(x)=12x-2x^2$ square metres. Find the greatest area,
and the width that gives it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the highest point of the curve. The table for $x=0$ to $6$ has one largest value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A(3) = 12\times3-2\times3^2 = 36-18$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A(3) = 18$$

The greatest area is $18\ \text{m}^2$, and it comes from a width of 3 m. The values on
either side are 16 and 16, so the curve is highest at 3.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Factorise $x^2-7x+12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 11.5. Find two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to $-7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The numbers are $-3$ and $-4$, because $(-3)\times(-4)=12$ and $-3+-4=-7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2-7x+12 = (x-3)(x-4)$$

Setting each bracket to zero gives the roots 3 and 4. Factorising and finding the roots
of a graph are the same piece of mathematics.

</details>
</details>
</details>
