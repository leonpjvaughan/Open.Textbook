# 14.5 Interpreting graphs in context — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $60\div2$.
2. Work out $(-1)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $30$. Dividing a distance by a time gives a speed.
2. $1$, because $(-1)\times(-1)=1$.

Both skills appear in this section. A rate is a division, and a square appears when a
graph is a curve.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A distance-time graph shows a cyclist travelling 30 km in 2 hours. Find the
speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the distance by the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{30}{2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The speed is 15 km/h.

The line is straight, so the speed is the same throughout the two hours.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** The same cyclist rests for one hour. Describe the graph during that hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What happens to the distance from home while the cyclist is resting?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The distance does not change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The graph is **flat** during the rest.

The horizontal axis still moves forward, because time passes. The vertical value stays
the same, because the distance from home does not change.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A tap fills a tank with 150 litres in 3 minutes. Find the rate at which the water
flows in.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the volume by the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{150}{3}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The rate is 50 litres per minute.

The graph of the volume against the time is a straight line, because the rate is
steady.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A ball is thrown straight up. Its height in metres after $t$ seconds is
$h(t)=20t-5t^2$. Find the greatest height and the time it takes to get there.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Make a table for $t=0$, $1$, $2$, $3$ and $4$, and look for the highest value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$h(1) = 15 \qquad h(2) = 40-20 = 20 \qquad h(3) = 15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The greatest height is 20 m, reached after 2 seconds.

The curve is symmetrical, so the ball is at 15 m on the way up at $t=1$ and at 15 m on
the way down at $t=3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** The ball has height $h(t)=20t-5t^2$ metres. Find the time at which the ball
lands, that is, the time when the height is 0.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Solve $20t-5t^2=0$. Take out the common factor $5t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5t(4-t) = 0$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad t = 4$$

The ball is on the ground at $t=0$, when it is thrown, and again at $t=4$. So the ball
lands after 4 seconds. The two values are the roots of the function.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The garden bed has area $A(x)=12x-2x^2$ square metres. Read the greatest area
from the graph, and the width that gives it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the highest point of the curve. The table has one largest value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A(3) = 36-18$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A(3) = 18$$

The greatest area is $18\ \text{m}^2$, at a width of 3 m. This is the maximum point,
$(3,18)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The garden bed has area $A(x)=12x-2x^2$ square metres. Find the two widths for
which the area is 0.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Solve $12x-2x^2=0$. Take out the common factor $2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x(6-x) = 0$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 0 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 6$$

These are the two roots. They are the places where the graph meets the horizontal axis,
and they mean a bed of no area at a width of 0 m and at a width of 6 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The graphs of $y=3x-2$ and $y=x+4$ cross at $x=3$. Show that both graphs have
the same height there.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put $x=3$ into each rule and compare the two answers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times3-2 = 7 \qquad 3+4 = 7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both graphs have height 7, so the point $(3,7)$ lies on both lines.

The crossing point is the solution of $3x-2=x+4$. Subtract $x$ and add 2 to see that
$2x=6$, so $x=3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** Solve $x^2-5x+6=0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look back at Section 11.5 and Section 12.7. Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add
to $-5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(x-2)(x-3) = 0$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 2 \quad \text{or} \quad x = 3$$

These are the roots of the quadratic, so they are the two places where the graph of
$y=x^2-5x+6$ crosses the horizontal axis.

</details>
</details>
</details>
