# Chapter 14: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The garden bed against the wall.** The club has 12 m of fencing for a rectangular
bed. One long side is the school wall, so only three sides are fenced. The width is
$x$ metres. Find the width that gives the greatest area, and find that area.

**Step 1. The fenced length.** The two widths use $2x$ metres of fence, so the fenced
length is what is left.

$$L(x) = 12-2x$$

This is a linear function, and its graph is a straight line that falls by 2 for each
extra metre of width.

**Step 2. The area.** The area of a rectangle is its length times its width.

$$A(x) = x(12-2x) = 12x-2x^2$$

This is a quadratic function, so its graph is a curve.

**Step 3. The table of values.**

| $x$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $A(x)$ | 0 | 10 | 16 | 18 | 16 | 10 | 0 |

**Step 4. The greatest area.** The largest value in the table is 18, at a width of
3 m. Check it in the rule.

$$A(3) = 12\times3-2\times3^2 = 36-18 = 18$$

**The greatest area is $18\ \text{m}^2$, and the width that gives it is 3 m.**

**Step 5. The length.** Substitute the width into the length function.

$$L(3) = 12-2\times3 = 12-6 = 6$$

**The bed is 3 m wide and 6 m long.** Check the area: $3\times6=18$, which agrees with
Step 4. The club uses $2\times3+6=12$ metres of fencing, which is all of it.

**Step 6. The roots.** The area is 0 when $A(x)=0$.

$$12x-2x^2 = 0 \qquad 2x(6-x) = 0$$

So $x=0$ or $x=6$. These are the two places where the graph meets the horizontal axis.
A width of 0 m gives no bed, and a width of 6 m leaves no fence for the length. Between
those two roots every width gives a positive area.

**Step 7. The shape.** The curve rises to a maximum at $(3,18)$ and then falls. It is
symmetrical, so the widths 2 m and 4 m give the same area of $16\ \text{m}^2$. The
greatest area is at the top of the curve, and it is not at either end.

Every step used one of the five sections: the idea of a function, function notation,
the graph of a line, the graph of a curve, and the meaning of a maximum.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and say
why in a few words. The idea might be finding an output, finding an input, a table of
values, a graph, a root, a maximum, or a rate.

1. A machine triples the input and then adds 1. Find the output when the input is 4.
2. The function $f$ is given by $f(x)=5x-3$. Find $f(2)$.
3. The function $f$ is given by $f(x)=x^2+1$. Find $f(-2)$.
4. The function $f$ is given by $f(x)=4x+1$. Find the input $x$ for which $f(x)=17$.
5. Make a table of values for $y=3x-2$ for $x=0$, $1$ and $2$.
6. Find the value of $y$ when $x=-2$, using $y=x+4$.
7. Find the values of $x$ where the graph of $y=x^2-16$ crosses the horizontal axis.
8. A quadratic graph has roots 1 and 6. Write its rule in factorised form, and expand
   it.
9. A distance-time graph shows a train travelling 80 km in 2 hours. Find its speed.
10. A distance-time graph is flat for 30 minutes. What is happening during that time?
11. The garden bed has area $A(x)=12x-2x^2$ square metres. Find $A(2)$.
12. The garden bed has area $A(x)=12x-2x^2$ square metres. Find the two widths for
    which the area is $10\ \text{m}^2$.
13. Expand $(x+2)(x-5)$.
14. Solve $3x-7=11$.
15. A ball is thrown straight up. Its height in metres after $t$ seconds is
    $h(t)=30t-5t^2$. Find the greatest height the ball reaches.
16. Make $x$ the subject of the formula $y=3x+2$.

- name the input, the output, the domain, the range and the co-domain;
- use function notation to find an output, including for a negative input;
- find the input that gives a chosen output by solving an equation;
- make a table of values and draw the graph of a linear function;
- draw the curve of a quadratic function and read its roots;
- read the maximum or the minimum of a graph and say what it means;
- build a quadratic from its roots and expand it;

- find where two graphs meet, and give an approximate reading from a graph.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you predicted whether the biggest bed would be wide and
short, or narrow and long. Look back at that prediction now.

The biggest area comes from a bed that is 3 m wide and 6 m long. The bed is twice as
long as it is wide, and the width sits exactly halfway between the two roots, 0 and 6.
If you predicted a very wide bed, the graph shows why that fails: the area falls back
to 0 at a width of 6 m.

You also wrote down one pair of amounts from your own week where one depends on the
other. Take that pair now. Name the input and the output, draw a table of values, and
sketch the graph. Then say where the maximum or the minimum is, and what it means for
the situation you chose. That is the whole of this chapter in one piece of work.
