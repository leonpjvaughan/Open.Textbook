# 11.4 Common factors and factorising — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the factors of 12.
2. Work out $6x \div 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $1, 2, 3, 4, 6$ and $12$. These are the numbers that divide 12 exactly.
2. $2x$. Divide the 6 by 3, and the letter stays.

Factorising asks which number and which letter divide every term. Dividing each term
by the common factor is how the bracket is built.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Factorise $5x+10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both terms divide by 5. Take the 5 outside a bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5(\quad + \quad)$$

What is left in each place when you divide by 5?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5(x + 2)$$

The $5x$ becomes $x$ and the 10 becomes 2. Check: $5(x+2)=5x+10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Factorise $8x+12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The highest common factor of 8 and 12 is 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4(\quad + \quad)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4(2x + 3)$$

The $8x$ becomes $2x$ and the 12 becomes 3. Check: $4(2x+3)=8x+12$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Factorise $3x^2+6x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both terms divide by 3 and by $x$, so the highest common factor is $3x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x(\quad + \quad)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x(x + 2)$$

The $3x^2$ becomes $x$ and the $6x$ becomes 2. Check: $3x(x+2)=3x^2+6x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Factorise $x^2+4x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both terms divide by $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x(\quad + \quad)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x(x + 4)$$

The $x^2$ becomes $x$ and the $4x$ becomes 4. Check: $x(x+4)=x^2+4x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Factorise $7a-14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both terms divide by 7. Keep the minus sign inside the bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$7(\quad - \quad)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7(a - 2)$$

The $7a$ becomes $a$ and the 14 becomes 2. Check: $7(a-2)=7a-14$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Factorise $6x^2-9x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The numbers 6 and 9 share the factor 3, and both terms contain $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x(\quad - \quad)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x(2x - 3)$$

The $6x^2$ becomes $2x$ and the $9x$ becomes 3. Check: $3x(2x-3)=6x^2-9x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Factorise $2x^2+8x+4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The highest common factor of 2, 8 and 4 is 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(\quad + \quad + \quad)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2(x^2 + 4x + 2)$$

Every term is divided by 2. The last term 4 becomes 2.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Factorise $4x+14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both terms divide by 2. This is the perimeter of the garden plot.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(\quad + \quad)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2(2x + 7)$$

The $4x$ becomes $2x$ and the 14 becomes 7. Check: $2(2x+7)=4x+14$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** Find the highest common factor of 18 and 24.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 3.1: list the factors of each number, and take the largest one they
share.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The factors of 18 are $1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18$. The factors of 24 are
$1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 24$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The highest common factor is 6. It is the largest number in both lists.

</details>
</details>
