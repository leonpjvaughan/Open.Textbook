# 11.7 Dividing a cubic or quadratic by a linear expression — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $12 \div 3$.
2. Simplify $\frac{6x}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4$. Division undoes multiplication.
2. $2x$. Divide the 6 by 3, and the letter stays.

Dividing an expression works the same way. Divide the numbers, and divide the letters.
Multiplying back is how you check the answer.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Divide $6x^2+9x$ by $3x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide each term by $3x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{6x^2}{3x} + \frac{9x}{3x}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x + 3$$

Check by multiplying back: $3x(2x+3)=6x^2+9x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Divide $x^2+5x+6$ by $x+2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look for a factor that multiplies $x+2$ to give $x^2+5x+6$. Start with $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(x+2)(x+3) = x^2 + 5x + 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x+3$$

The $x^2$ term comes from $x\times x$, and the constant 6 comes from $2\times3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Divide $x^2+9x+20$ by $x+4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The quotient starts with $x$. The constant is $20\div4=5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(x+4)(x+5) = x^2 + 9x + 20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x+5$$

Check by multiplying back: $(x+4)(x+5)=x^2+5x+4x+20=x^2+9x+20$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Divide $x^2-4x-12$ by $x-6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The constant is $(-12)\div(-6)=2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(x-6)(x+2) = x^2 - 4x - 12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x+2$$

Check by multiplying back: $(x-6)(x+2)=x^2+2x-6x-12=x^2-4x-12$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Divide $x^3+6x^2+11x+6$ by $x+1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The quotient starts with $x^2$. Try a quadratic that multiplies $x+1$ to give the
cubic.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(x+1)(x^2+5x+6) = x^3 + 6x^2 + 11x + 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2+5x+6$$

Check by multiplying back. The $x^3$ comes from $x\times x^2$, and the constant 6 comes
from $1\times6$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Divide $x^3-8$ by $x-2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There is no $x^2$ term and no $x$ term. Keep their places in the quotient.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Try the quotient $x^2+2x+4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2+2x+4$$

Check by multiplying back: $(x-2)(x^2+2x+4)=x^3+2x^2+4x-2x^2-4x-8=x^3-8$. The
$x^2$ and $x$ terms cancel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Divide $x^3+7x^2+10x$ by $x+5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The quotient starts with $x^2$. The last term is $10x\div5=2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(x+5)(x^2+2x) = x^3 + 7x^2 + 10x$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2+2x$$

This is the volume of the raised bed divided by its length in the chapter problem.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**8.** Factorise $x^2+8x+15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 11.5: find two numbers that multiply to 15 and add to 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times5=15 \qquad 3+5=8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+3)(x+5)$$

This factorisation is the same skill that division reverses.

</details>
</details>
</details>
