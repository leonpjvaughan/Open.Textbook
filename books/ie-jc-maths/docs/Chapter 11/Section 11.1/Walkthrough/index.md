# 11.1 Adding and subtracting algebraic expressions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** writing expressions and naming their parts from Section 10.1, and
the distributive property from Section 1.5.

**Objectives:** name the terms of an expression; collect like terms; add two
expressions; subtract one expression from another, including when a bracket follows a
minus sign.

By the end of this section, you can simplify an expression by collecting its like
terms.

## Terms and like terms

An expression is built from **terms**. A term is a part joined to the rest by a plus
sign or a minus sign.

$$3x + 5y - 7$$

This expression has three terms: $3x$, $5y$ and $-7$. The number in front of a letter
is the **coefficient**. In $3x$ the coefficient is 3.

**Like terms** are terms with exactly the same letter part. The terms $4x$ and $9x$
are like terms. The terms $4x$ and $4y$ are not like terms, because the letters
differ.

You collect like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients. The letter part
stays the same.

$$4x + 9x = 13x \qquad 7a - 2a = 5a$$

Think of it as counting: 4 copies of $x$ plus 9 copies of $x$ is 13 copies of $x$.

## Adding expressions

To add two expressions, write them one after the other and collect the like terms.

**Example 1.** Simplify $(3x+4)+(2x+5)$.

$$3x + 4 + 2x + 5$$

The bracket is not needed for an addition, because adding a group of terms adds each
one. Now collect the like terms.

$$3x + 2x = 5x \qquad 4 + 5 = 9$$

So the answer is $5x + 9$.

**Example 2.** Simplify $(4a+3b)+(2a-b)$.

$$4a + 3b + 2a - b$$

$$4a + 2a = 6a \qquad 3b - b = 2b$$

The answer is $6a + 2b$.

## Subtracting expressions

A minus sign in front of a bracket changes the sign of **every** term inside it. That
is the distributive property with $-1$.

$$-(2x+3) = -2x - 3$$

**Example 3.** Simplify $(5x+2)-(3x+4)$.

$$5x + 2 - 3x - 4$$

$$5x - 3x = 2x \qquad 2 - 4 = -2$$

The answer is $2x - 2$.

**Example 4.** Simplify $(7y+5)-(2y-3)$.

The minus sign turns the $-3$ into $+3$.

$$7y + 5 - 2y + 3$$

$$7y - 2y = 5y \qquad 5 + 3 = 8$$

The answer is $5y + 8$.

## A number in front of a bracket

Sometimes a bracket is multiplied by a number before it is added or subtracted.
Multiply out the bracket first, and then collect the like terms. Section 11.2 teaches
the multiplication in full; here you need it for the last step.

**Example 5.** Simplify $3(x+2) + 2(x+5)$.

$$3x + 6 + 2x + 10$$

$$5x + 16$$

**Example 6.** Simplify $4(x+3) - 2(x+1)$.

$$4x + 12 - 2x - 2$$

$$2x + 10$$

> **Watch out.** A minus sign in front of a bracket applies to the whole bracket. From
> $(5x+2)-(3x-4)$, the common error is to write $5x+2-3x-4$, changing only the first
> term. The correct line is $5x+2-3x+4$, because the $-4$ becomes $+4$.
>
> **Re-check:** put a number in for $x$ and compare. With $x=1$:
> $(5+2)-(3-4)=7-(-1)=8$, and $2(1)+6=8$. The wrong line gives $2(1)-2=0$, which does
> not match.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The garden plot is $(x+5)$ metres long and $(x+2)$
metres wide.

1. Write an expression for the length plus the width, and simplify it.
2. Write an expression for the length take away the width, and simplify it.

In Section 11.2 you will multiply out a bracket to find the perimeter of the plot.

## You should now be able to

- name the terms of an expression and the coefficient of a term;
- collect like terms by adding or subtracting their coefficients;
- add two expressions by writing their terms in one line;
- subtract one expression from another, changing the sign of every term in the second
  bracket.
