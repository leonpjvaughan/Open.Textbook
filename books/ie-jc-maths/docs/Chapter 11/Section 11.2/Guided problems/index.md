# 11.2 Multiplying out a single bracket — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $3(4+5)$.
2. Work out $2\times3+2\times4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $27$, because $4+5=9$ and $3\times9=27$.
2. $14$, because $2\times3=6$ and $2\times4=8$, and $6+8=14$.

The two answers are equal, and that is the distributive property at work:
$3(4+5)=3\times4+3\times5$. The same idea lets you multiply out an algebraic bracket.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Expand $4(x+3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 4 multiplies both terms inside the bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4\times x + 4\times3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4x + 12$$

The 4 multiplies the $x$ and the 3. Both terms are changed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Expand $6(2x-1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the $2x$ by 6 and the $-1$ by 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6\times2x - 6\times1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$12x - 6$$

The 6 multiplies both terms, so the 1 becomes 6.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Expand $x(x+7)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$ outside multiplies each term. Remember that $x\times x=x^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x\times x + x\times7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 + 7x$$

The first product is $x^2$, because a letter times itself is that letter squared.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Expand $3x(2x+1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the $2x$ by $3x$, and then the 1 by $3x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x\times2x + 3x\times1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6x^2 + 3x$$

The numbers give $3\times2=6$, and $x\times x=x^2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Expand $-(x+5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The minus sign is the same as multiplying by $-1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-1\times x - 1\times5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$-x - 5$$

Both signs inside the bracket change.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Expand $-3(2x-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply both terms by $-3$. The product of two negative numbers is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-3\times2x + 3\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$-6x + 12$$

The $-3$ times the $-4$ is $+12$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Expand and simplify $2(x+5)+3(x+1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Expand each bracket first, and then collect the like terms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x + 10 + 3x + 3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5x + 13$$

The $x$ terms give $2x+3x=5x$, and the numbers give $10+3=13$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Expand and simplify $4(x+3)-2(x+5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The minus sign applies to the whole second bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4x + 12 - 2x - 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x + 2$$

The $x$ terms give $4x-2x=2x$, and the numbers give $12-10=2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** A rectangle is $x$ centimetres long and 5 centimetres wide. Write an expression
for its perimeter, and expand it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 10.1: the perimeter is twice the length plus twice the width.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2(x+5)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x + 10$$

The 2 multiplies both terms inside the bracket.

</details>
</details>
</details>
