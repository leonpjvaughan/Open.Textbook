# 11.6 The difference of two squares — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $9^2-5^2$.
2. Simplify $x\times x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $56$, because $9^2=81$ and $5^2=25$, and $81-25=56$.
2. $x^2$. A letter times itself is that letter squared.

A difference of two squares is exactly what question 1 describes, but with letters.
Question 2 reminds you that $x^2$ is a square.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Factorise $x^2-16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both terms are squares: $x^2$ and $4^2$. Use $a^2-b^2=(a+b)(a-b)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a=x \qquad b=4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+4)(x-4)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2-4x+4x-16=x^2-16$. The middle terms cancel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Factorise $x^2-36$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$36$ is $6^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a=x \qquad b=6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+6)(x-6)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2-6x+6x-36=x^2-36$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Factorise $x^2-49$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$49$ is $7^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a=x \qquad b=7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+7)(x-7)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2-7x+7x-49=x^2-49$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Factorise $4x^2-9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$4x^2$ is $(2x)^2$, and $9$ is $3^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a=2x \qquad b=3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2x+3)(2x-3)$$

Check by expanding: $4x^2-6x+6x-9=4x^2-9$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Factorise $25x^2-1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$25x^2$ is $(5x)^2$, and $1$ is $1^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a=5x \qquad b=1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(5x+1)(5x-1)$$

Check by expanding: $25x^2-5x+5x-1=25x^2-1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Factorise $x^4-16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$x^4$ is $(x^2)^2$, and 16 is $4^2$. Factorise once, and then look at the second
bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(x^2+4)(x^2-4)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x^2+4)(x+2)(x-2)$$

The bracket $x^2-4$ is itself a difference of two squares, so it factorises again. The
bracket $x^2+4$ has a plus sign, so it does not factorise.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Factorise $9x^2-64$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$9x^2$ is $(3x)^2$, and $64$ is $8^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a=3x \qquad b=8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3x+8)(3x-8)$$

Check by expanding: $9x^2-24x+24x-64=9x^2-64$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Expand $(x+6)(x-6)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The middle terms will cancel, so only the two squares are left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 - 6x + 6x - 36$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 - 36$$

This is the expansion that the factorisation in question 2 reverses.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** Factorise $x^2+7x+12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 11.5: find two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times4=12 \qquad 3+4=7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+3)(x+4)$$

This is an ordinary quadratic, so both brackets have a plus sign. A difference of two
squares has two terms, not three.

</details>
</details>
</details>
