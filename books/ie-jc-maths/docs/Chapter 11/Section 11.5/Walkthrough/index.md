# 11.5 Factorising quadratic expressions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplying out two brackets from Section 11.3, and taking out a
common factor from Section 11.4.

**Objectives:** factorise an expression of the form $x^2+bx+c$; find the two numbers
that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$; handle positive and negative signs.

By the end of this section, you can write a quadratic expression as a product of two
brackets.

## A quadratic expression

A **quadratic expression** has an $x^2$ term as its highest power. The form
$x^2+bx+c$ has three terms, and the coefficient of $x^2$ is 1.

You already know how to expand $(x+5)(x+2)$.

$$(x+5)(x+2) = x^2 + 7x + 10$$

The number 7 is $5+2$, and the number 10 is $5\times2$. That is the pattern to
reverse.

## The two numbers

To factorise $x^2+bx+c$, find two numbers that **multiply to $c$** and **add to $b$**.
Then write the expression as $(x+\text{first})(x+\text{second})$.

**Example 1.** Factorise $x^2+7x+12$.

You need two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7.

$$3\times4=12 \qquad 3+4=7$$

So

$$x^2 + 7x + 12 = (x+3)(x+4)$$

Check by expanding: $(x+3)(x+4)=x^2+4x+3x+12=x^2+7x+12$.

**Example 2.** Factorise $x^2+9x+20$.

$$4\times5=20 \qquad 4+5=9$$

$$x^2 + 9x + 20 = (x+4)(x+5)$$

**Example 3.** Factorise $x^2+2x-15$.

The product is negative, so one number is positive and one is negative. The sum is
positive, so the positive number has the larger size.

$$5\times(-3)=-15 \qquad 5+(-3)=2$$

$$x^2 + 2x - 15 = (x+5)(x-3)$$

**Example 4.** Factorise $x^2-6x+8$.

The product is positive and the sum is negative, so both numbers are negative.

$$(-2)\times(-4)=8 \qquad (-2)+(-4)=-6$$

$$x^2 - 6x + 8 = (x-2)(x-4)$$

**Example 5.** Factorise $x^2-5x-24$.

The product is negative, so the numbers have opposite signs. The sum is negative, so
the negative number has the larger size.

$$3\times(-8)=-24 \qquad 3+(-8)=-5$$

$$x^2 - 5x - 24 = (x+3)(x-8)$$

> **Watch out.** The two numbers must give the right product **and** the right sum.
> From $x^2+7x+12$, the pair 2 and 6 multiplies to 12, so it looks right, but it adds
> to 8, not 7. The pair is 3 and 4.
>
> **Re-check:** expand the brackets you wrote. If the middle term is wrong, the pair
> is wrong.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The total area of the garden plot is $x^2+7x+10$ square
metres.

1. Find two numbers that multiply to 10 and add to 7.
2. Factorise $x^2+7x+10$.
3. The plot is $(x+5)$ metres long. Use your factorisation to write down its width.

In Section 11.6 you will meet a special quadratic with only two terms.

## You should now be able to

- find two numbers that multiply to $c$ and add to $b$;
- factorise $x^2+bx+c$ into two brackets;
- use the signs of $b$ and $c$ to tell the signs of the two numbers;
- check a factorisation by expanding it again.
