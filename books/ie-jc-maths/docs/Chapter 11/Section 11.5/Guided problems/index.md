# 11.5 Factorising quadratic expressions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $4\times(-3)$.
2. Work out $(-2)+(-5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $-12$. A positive times a negative is negative.
2. $-7$. Two negative numbers add to a negative number.

These two sign rules decide the signs of the two numbers you need in a factorisation.
The product gives the sign of $c$, and the sum gives the sign of $b$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Factorise $x^2+5x+6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times3=6 \qquad 2+3=5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+2)(x+3)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2+3x+2x+6=x^2+5x+6$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Factorise $x^2+8x+15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find two numbers that multiply to 15 and add to 8.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times5=15 \qquad 3+5=8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+3)(x+5)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2+5x+3x+15=x^2+8x+15$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Factorise $x^2+10x+21$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find two numbers that multiply to 21 and add to 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times7=21 \qquad 3+7=10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+3)(x+7)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2+7x+3x+21=x^2+10x+21$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Factorise $x^2+x-12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The product is negative, so the numbers have opposite signs. The sum is positive, so
the positive number is larger.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4\times(-3)=-12 \qquad 4+(-3)=1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+4)(x-3)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2-3x+4x-12=x^2+x-12$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Factorise $x^2-3x-10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The product is negative, so the signs differ. The sum is negative, so the negative
number is larger.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(-5)\times2=-10 \qquad (-5)+2=-3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x-5)(x+2)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2+2x-5x-10=x^2-3x-10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Factorise $x^2-8x+15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The product is positive and the sum is negative, so both numbers are negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(-3)\times(-5)=15 \qquad (-3)+(-5)=-8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x-3)(x-5)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2-5x-3x+15=x^2-8x+15$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Factorise $x^2-7x+12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both numbers are negative. Find a pair that multiplies to 12 and adds to $-7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(-3)\times(-4)=12 \qquad (-3)+(-4)=-7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x-3)(x-4)$$

Check by expanding: $x^2-4x-3x+12=x^2-7x+12$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Factorise $x^2+7x+10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is the total area of the garden plot. Find two numbers that multiply to 10 and
add to 7.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5\times2=10 \qquad 5+2=7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x+5)(x+2)$$

The two brackets are the length and the width of the plot. Check by expanding:
$x^2+2x+5x+10=x^2+7x+10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** Expand $(x+3)(x+5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 11.3: every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the
second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 + 5x + 3x + 15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 + 8x + 15$$

The middle terms collect to $8x$. This is the expansion that the factorisation in
question 2 reverses.

</details>
</details>
