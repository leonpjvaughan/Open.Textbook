# 11.3 Multiplying two brackets — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplying out a single bracket from Section 11.2, and collecting
like terms from Section 11.1.

**Objectives:** multiply out two brackets; use a grid to keep track of the four
products; simplify the result by collecting the middle terms; expand a bracket
squared.

By the end of this section, you can write a product of two brackets as a single
expression.

## A grid for two brackets

To expand $(x+2)(x+3)$, every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the
second. A grid keeps the four products in place.

|  | $x$ | $+3$ |
|---|---|---|
| $x$ | $x^2$ | $3x$ |
| $+2$ | $2x$ | $6$ |

Add the four products.

$$x^2 + 3x + 2x + 6 = x^2 + 5x + 6$$

The two middle terms, $3x$ and $2x$, are like terms, so they collect to $5x$.

**Example 1.** Expand $(x+4)(x+5)$.

$$x^2 + 5x + 4x + 20 = x^2 + 9x + 20$$

**Example 2.** Expand $(x+7)(x-2)$.

$$x^2 - 2x + 7x - 14 = x^2 + 5x - 14$$

**Example 3.** Expand $(x-3)(x-4)$.

$$x^2 - 4x - 3x + 12 = x^2 - 7x + 12$$

The product of two negative numbers is positive, so the last term is $+12$.

## A coefficient on the first term

When the first term has a coefficient, the grid still works.

**Example 4.** Expand $(2x+1)(x+3)$.

$$2x^2 + 6x + x + 3 = 2x^2 + 7x + 3$$

**Example 5.** Expand $(3x-2)(2x+5)$.

$$6x^2 + 15x - 4x - 10 = 6x^2 + 11x - 10$$

## A bracket squared

$(x+3)^2$ means $(x+3)(x+3)$, so it is a product of two brackets.

**Example 6.** Expand $(x+3)^2$.

$$(x+3)(x+3) = x^2 + 3x + 3x + 9 = x^2 + 6x + 9$$

**Example 7.** Expand $(x-5)^2$.

$$(x-5)(x-5) = x^2 - 5x - 5x + 25 = x^2 - 10x + 25$$

> **Watch out.** A bracket squared is not the square of each term. From $(x+3)^2$, the
> common error is to write $x^2+9$, squaring both terms. The correct expansion is
> $x^2+6x+9$, because the two middle terms $3x$ and $3x$ also appear.
>
> **Re-check:** put a number in for $x$ and compare. With $x=1$: $(1+3)^2=16$, and
> $1+6+9=16$. The wrong form gives $1+9=10$, which does not match.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The garden plot is $(x+5)$ metres long and $(x+2)$
metres wide.

1. Write the area of the plot as $(x+5)(x+2)$.
2. Expand the product.
3. Write down the total area of the plot with the brackets removed.

In Section 11.4 you will go the other way and take out a common factor.

## You should now be able to

- multiply out two brackets by making every term in the first bracket multiply every
  term in the second;
- use a grid to keep track of the four products;
- collect the two middle terms into one;
- expand a bracket squared.
