# 11.3 Multiplying two brackets — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Expand $3(x+2)$.
2. Simplify $4x+7x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3x+6$. The 3 multiplies both terms inside the bracket.
2. $11x$. The two terms are like terms, so their coefficients add.

Both skills return here. Multiplying out two brackets creates four terms, and then you
collect the like terms.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Expand $(x+2)(x+5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Every term in the first bracket multiplies every term in the second. There are four
products.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 + 5x + 2x + 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 + 7x + 10$$

The two middle terms $5x$ and $2x$ are like terms, so they collect to $7x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Expand $(x+6)(x+1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use a grid, or write the four products in a line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 + x + 6x + 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 + 7x + 6$$

The middle terms $x$ and $6x$ collect to $7x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Expand $(x+4)(x-3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The minus sign is attached to the 3, so one of the four products is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 - 3x + 4x - 12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 + x - 12$$

The middle terms are $-3x$ and $4x$, which collect to $+x$. The last term is $4\times(-3)=-12$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Expand $(x-2)(x-5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both numbers are negative, so the last product is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 - 5x - 2x + 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 - 7x + 10$$

The middle terms are both negative, so they add to $-7x$. The last term is $(-2)\times(-5)=+10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Expand $(2x+3)(x+1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $2x$ multiplies both terms in the second bracket, and the 3 multiplies both too.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x^2 + 2x + 3x + 3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x^2 + 5x + 3$$

The $x^2$ term is $2x\times x=2x^2$, and the middle terms collect to $5x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Expand $(3x-1)(x+4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Watch the sign on the $-1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x^2 + 12x - x - 4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x^2 + 11x - 4$$

The middle terms are $12x$ and $-x$, which collect to $11x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Expand $(x+5)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$(x+5)^2$ means $(x+5)(x+5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 + 5x + 5x + 25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 + 10x + 25$$

The two middle terms are equal, so they give $10x$. Do not forget them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** Expand $(x-4)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$(x-4)^2$ means $(x-4)(x-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x^2 - 4x - 4x + 16$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x^2 - 8x + 16$$

The two middle terms give $-8x$, and the last term is $(-4)\times(-4)=+16$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from earlier work

**9.** Expand $4(2x+3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

From Section 11.2: the 4 multiplies both terms inside the bracket.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4\times2x + 4\times3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8x + 12$$

The 4 multiplies the $2x$ and the 3.

</details>
</details>
</details>
