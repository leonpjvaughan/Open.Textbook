# Chapter 11: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The school garden plot.** The plot is $(x+5)$ metres long and $(x+2)$ metres wide.
A square pond of side $x$ metres sits in one corner, and the rest is planted. The
planted area is 45 square metres.

**Step 1. The total area of the plot.** Multiply the length by the width.

$$(x+5)(x+2) = x^2 + 2x + 5x + 10 = x^2 + 7x + 10$$

**The total area is $x^2+7x+10$ square metres.**

**Step 2. The area of the pond.** The pond is a square of side $x$.

$$x \times x = x^2$$

**The pond area is $x^2$ square metres.**

**Step 3. The planted area.** Subtract the pond from the plot.

$$(x^2+7x+10) - x^2 = 7x + 10$$

**The planted area is $7x+10$ square metres.**

**Step 4. Find $x$.** The planted area is 45 square metres.

$$7x + 10 = 45$$

$$7x = 35 \qquad x = 5$$

**Step 5. The dimensions.** The length is $x+5=10$ metres and the width is $x+2=7$
metres. The pond is 5 metres by 5 metres.

**Step 6. Check the answer.** The plot is 10 m by 7 m, so its area is 70 m². The pond
is 5 m by 5 m, so its area is 25 m². The planted area is $70-25=45$ m², which matches
the problem.

The factorisation from Section 11.5 checks the dimensions another way. The total area
$x^2+7x+10$ factorises to $(x+5)(x+2)$, so the two brackets are the length and the
width.

<!-- tier:h -->
**Step 7. The patio.** The patio is a square of side $(x+2)$ metres, with a square
flower bed of side $(x-2)$ metres cut from it. Its area is a difference of two
squares.

$$(x+2)^2 - (x-2)^2 = \big((x+2)+(x-2)\big)\big((x+2)-(x-2)\big) = (2x)(4) = 8x$$

At $x=5$, the patio area is $8\times5=40$ square metres.

**Step 8. The raised bed.** The volume is $x^3+7x^2+10x$ cubic metres. Divide by the
length $x+5$.

$$(x^3+7x^2+10x) \div (x+5) = x^2 + 2x$$

Then divide by the width $x$.

$$(x^2+2x) \div x = x + 2$$

At $x=5$, the height of the raised bed is 7 metres.
<!-- /tier:h -->

The garden is 10 metres by 7 metres, the pond is 5 metres by 5 metres, and the planted
area is 45 square metres. Each section played a part: collecting terms, expanding one
bracket, expanding two brackets, taking out a common factor, factorising the
quadratic, and the difference of two squares.

## What you can now do

- collect like terms and simplify an expression;
- add and subtract expressions, including expressions in brackets;
- multiply out a single bracket;
- multiply out two brackets and collect the middle terms;
- take out the highest common factor from an expression;
- factorise a quadratic expression $x^2+bx+c$ into two brackets;
- recognise and factorise the difference of two squares;
<!-- tier:h -->
- divide a quadratic or cubic expression by a linear expression.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you predicted whether $x$ was greater than 5 or less than
5, and whether the plot was longer than 10 metres. Look back at that prediction now.

The planted area is $7x+10=45$, so $x=5$. The plot is 10 metres by 7 metres, so the
length is exactly 10 metres. The prediction about the plot was a good one if you
reasoned that the length is $x+5$, so it is always 5 metres more than $x$.

You also wrote down a rectangle from your own life and two expressions for its area.
Take that rectangle now. Expand the product form, and factorise the sum form back
again. If the two expressions match, you have checked your own work in both
directions.
