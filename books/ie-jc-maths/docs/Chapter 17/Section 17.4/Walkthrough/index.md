# 17.4 Parallel and perpendicular lines — Learn

**Prerequisites:** finding the slope of a line from Section 17.3.

**Objectives:** decide whether two lines are parallel, perpendicular or neither;
find the slope of a line parallel to a given line; find the slope of a line
perpendicular to a given line.

By the end of this section, you can compare two lines from their slopes alone.

## Parallel lines

Two lines are **parallel** when they run in the same direction and never meet.
Their slopes $m_1$ and $m_2$ are equal.

$$m_1 = m_2 \quad \text{means the lines are parallel}$$

Two parallel lines rise at the same rate, so the distance between them stays the
same. Train tracks are parallel.

**Example 1.** Line A has slope 3 and line B has slope 3. Are the lines parallel?

The two slopes are equal, so the lines are parallel. They rise at the same rate.

**Example 2.** Line A has slope 2 and line B has slope 3. Are the lines parallel?

The slopes are different, so the lines are not parallel. The two lines will meet
somewhere, because one is steeper than the other.

## Perpendicular lines

Two lines are **perpendicular** when they meet at a right angle. Their slopes
multiply to $-1$.

$$m_1 \times m_2 = -1 \quad \text{means the lines are perpendicular}$$

Each slope is the **negative reciprocal** of the other. To find it, flip the slope
and change its sign.

- The negative reciprocal of 2 is $-\tfrac{1}{2}$.
- The negative reciprocal of $-\tfrac{2}{3}$ is $\tfrac{3}{2}$.

**Example 3.** Line A has slope 4 and line B has slope $-\tfrac{1}{4}$. Are the
lines perpendicular?

Multiply the slopes.

$$4 \times \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = -1$$

The product is $-1$, so the lines are perpendicular. They meet at a right angle.

**Example 4.** A line has slope 5. Find the slope of a line perpendicular to it.

Flip 5 to get $\tfrac{1}{5}$, then change the sign.

$$m = -\frac{1}{5}$$

Check it: $5 \times \left(-\tfrac{1}{5}\right) = -1$. The perpendicular slope is
$-\tfrac{1}{5}$.

**Example 5.** A line has slope $-\tfrac{2}{3}$. Find the slope of a line
perpendicular to it.

Flip $-\tfrac{2}{3}$ to get $-\tfrac{3}{2}$, then change the sign.

$$m = \frac{3}{2}$$

Check it: $-\tfrac{2}{3} \times \tfrac{3}{2} = -1$. The perpendicular slope is
$\tfrac{3}{2}$.

## Neither parallel nor perpendicular

Most pairs of lines are neither. Check the slopes in order.

1. Are the slopes equal? If yes, the lines are parallel.
2. Is the product of the slopes $-1$? If yes, the lines are perpendicular.
3. Otherwise, the lines are neither.

**Example 6.** Line A has slope 3 and line B has slope $\tfrac{1}{3}$. What are the
lines?

The slopes are not equal, so the lines are not parallel. The product is
$3 \times \tfrac{1}{3} = 1$, which is not $-1$, so the lines are not perpendicular
either.

The lines are neither parallel nor perpendicular.

## Horizontal and vertical lines

A horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular. Their slopes are 0 and
undefined, and they meet at a right angle.

Two vertical lines are parallel, and two horizontal lines are parallel.

**Example 7.** Are the line through $(1,1)$ and $(4,1)$ and the line through
$(2,0)$ and $(2,5)$ perpendicular?

The first line is horizontal, because both points have $y=1$. The second line is
vertical, because both points have $x=2$. A horizontal line and a vertical line
meet at a right angle, so the lines are perpendicular.

## Watch out

The negative reciprocal is not the same as the negative.

> **Watch out.** Slopes 2 and $-2$ are not perpendicular. The common error is to
> change the sign and stop.
>
> Their product is $2 \times (-2) = -4$, not $-1$. The perpendicular slope to 2 is
> $-\tfrac{1}{2}$, because $2 \times \left(-\tfrac{1}{2}\right) = -1$.
>
> **Re-check:** multiply the two slopes. Only a product of exactly $-1$ means
> perpendicular.

A second trap is a pair of equal negative slopes. Slopes $-3$ and $-3$ are
parallel, not perpendicular. Parallel lines have equal slopes, whatever the sign.

## You should now be able to

- decide whether two lines are parallel by comparing their slopes;
- decide whether two lines are perpendicular using the product of their slopes;
- find the slope of a line parallel to a given line;
- find the slope of a line perpendicular to a given line, using the negative
  reciprocal;
- say when a pair of lines is neither parallel nor perpendicular;
- explain why a horizontal line and a vertical line are perpendicular.
