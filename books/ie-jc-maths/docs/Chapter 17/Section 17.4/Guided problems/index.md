# 17.4 Parallel and perpendicular lines — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Work these from memory. They are the skills this section uses.

1. Work out $2\times\frac{1}{2}$.
2. Write the reciprocal of 4.
3. Work out $\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $1$. A number times its reciprocal is 1.
2. $\tfrac{1}{4}$. Flip the fraction upside down.
3. $1$. The 2 and the 3 cancel, so the product is 1.

A perpendicular slope is a negative reciprocal, so flipping a fraction returns in
every question here.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Line A has slope 5 and line B has slope 5. Are the lines parallel,
perpendicular or neither?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the two slopes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5 = 5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The lines are parallel. Equal slopes mean the lines rise at the same rate and
never meet.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Line A has slope 3 and line B has slope $-\tfrac{1}{3}$. Are the lines
parallel, perpendicular or neither?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the two slopes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The lines are perpendicular.

$$3\times\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)=-1$$

A product of $-1$ means a right angle. Each slope is the negative reciprocal of
the other.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Line A has slope 2 and line B has slope 4. Are the lines parallel,
perpendicular or neither?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The slopes are not equal, so check the product.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The lines are neither parallel nor perpendicular. The slopes are not equal, so the
lines are not parallel. Their product is $2\times4=8$, which is not $-1$, so they
are not perpendicular either.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A line has slope $-3$. Find the slope of a line perpendicular to it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Flip the slope, then change its sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\text{flip } -3 \text{ to } -\frac{1}{3}, \text{ then change the sign}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The perpendicular slope is $\tfrac{1}{3}$.

$$-3\times\frac{1}{3}=-1$$

Check the product. The two slopes multiply to $-1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A line has slope $\tfrac{2}{5}$. Find the slope of a line perpendicular to
it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Flip $\tfrac{2}{5}$ to get $\tfrac{5}{2}$, then change the sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-\frac{5}{2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The perpendicular slope is $-\tfrac{5}{2}$.

$$\frac{2}{5}\times\left(-\frac{5}{2}\right)=-1$$

The product of the two slopes is $-1$, which is the test for perpendicular lines.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The line through $(0,0)$ and $(3,6)$ and the line through $(0,0)$ and
$(2,4)$ are drawn on one grid. Are the lines parallel?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find both slopes and compare them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{6-0}{3-0} \qquad \frac{4-0}{2-0}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The lines are parallel.

$$\frac{6}{3}=2 \qquad \frac{4}{2}=2$$

Both slopes are 2, so the lines rise at the same rate. They pass through the same
point here, but two parallel lines can also be drawn apart.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The park main path has slope $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and the second path has slope
$-\tfrac{4}{3}$. Work out the product of the slopes and say whether the paths are
perpendicular.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the two fractions. The 3 and the 4 cancel.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{3}{4}\times\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The product is $-1$, so the two paths are perpendicular.

$$\frac{3}{4}\times\left(-\frac{4}{3}\right)=-1$$

The paths meet at the kiosk at a right angle. The guess you wrote at the start of
the chapter is answered here.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**8.** Write the reciprocal of $\tfrac{2}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Swap the top and the bottom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{3}{2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The reciprocal is $\tfrac{3}{2}$.

$$\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{2}=1$$

A negative reciprocal is the reciprocal with the sign changed, which is how a
perpendicular slope is found.

</details>
</details>
</details>
