# Chapter 17: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The park on the grid.** The gate is at $G(1,2)$, the kiosk is at $K(9,8)$ and
the pond is at $P(3,16)$. A straight main path joins the gate to the kiosk. A
bench sits at the midpoint of the main path. A second straight path leaves the
kiosk and reaches the pond. Each unit on the grid is 1 metre.

**Step 1. Plot the points.** The gate $G$ is 1 across and 2 up. The kiosk $K$ is 9
across and 8 up. The pond $P$ is 3 across and 16 up. All three points have
positive coordinates, so all three lie in the first quadrant.

**Step 2. The length of the main path.** Find the two steps from $G$ to $K$.

$$\text{across} = 9-1 = 8 \qquad \text{up} = 8-2 = 6$$

Square the steps, add, and take the root.

$$d = \sqrt{8^2+6^2} = \sqrt{64+36} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

**The main path is 10 m long.**

**Step 3. The bench.** The bench is at the midpoint of the main path. Average the
coordinates.

$$M = \left(\frac{1+9}{2},\ \frac{2+8}{2}\right) = (5,5)$$

**The bench is at $(5,5)$.**

**Step 4. The slope of the main path.** Divide the rise by the run.

$$m = \frac{8-2}{9-1} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$$

**The main path has slope $\tfrac{3}{4}$.**

**Step 5. The slope of the second path.** From $K(9,8)$ to $P(3,16)$, the run is
negative.

$$m = \frac{16-8}{3-9} = \frac{8}{-6} = -\frac{4}{3}$$

**The second path has slope $-\tfrac{4}{3}$.**

**Step 6. Are the paths perpendicular?** Multiply the two slopes.

$$\frac{3}{4} \times \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = -1$$

The product is $-1$, so the paths meet at a right angle.

**The two paths are perpendicular.** If your guess at the start of the chapter was
"yes", the slopes have now proved it.

**Step 7. The equation of the main path.** The path passes through $G(1,2)$ with
slope $\tfrac{3}{4}$. Find the intercept.

$$c = y - mx = 2 - \frac{3}{4} \times 1 = \frac{8}{4}-\frac{3}{4} = \frac{5}{4}$$

**The main path is $y=\tfrac{3}{4}x+\tfrac{5}{4}$.** Check it with the kiosk:
$\tfrac{3}{4}\times9+\tfrac{5}{4}=\tfrac{27}{4}+\tfrac{5}{4}=8$.

**Step 8. The general form.** Multiply every term by 4 to clear the fractions.

$$4y = 3x + 5 \qquad 3x - 4y + 5 = 0$$

**The main path is $3x-4y+5=0$ in general form.** Check the gate: $3-8+5=0$. Check
the kiosk: $27-32+5=0$. Both points lie on the line.

**Step 9. A rate in the park.** The park keeper walks 10 m in the first 2 minutes
and then 24 m in the next 4 minutes. Divide each distance by its time.

$$\frac{10}{2}=5 \qquad \frac{24}{4}=6$$

**The keeper walks at 5 m per minute, and then at 6 m per minute.**

Every step used one of the seven sections: plotting, distance and midpoint, slope,
perpendicular lines, the equation of a line, the general form, and a rate from a
graph.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and
say why in a few words. The idea might be plotting, distance, midpoint, slope,
parallel or perpendicular lines, the equation of a line, the general form, a rate
of change, a percentage, an equation, an area, or a unit.

1. Plot the point $(3,-2)$ and name its quadrant.
2. Find the distance between $(2,1)$ and $(5,5)$.
3. Find the midpoint of the segment joining $(1,3)$ and $(7,11)$.
4. Find the slope of the line through $(2,4)$ and $(6,10)$.
5. A line has slope $\tfrac{3}{2}$. A second line has slope $-\tfrac{2}{3}$. Are
   the lines perpendicular?
6. Write the equation of the line with slope 4 that passes through $(2,3)$.
7. Write the line $y=2x-5$ in the form $ax+by+c=0$.
8. Find the $y$-intercept of the line $3x+2y-8=0$.
9. Find the distance between the origin and $(5,12)$.
10. A graph passes through $(0,3)$ and $(4,15)$. Find its rate of change and its
    starting value.
11. The midpoint of a segment is $(5,6)$, and one end is $(2,3)$. Find the other
    end.
12. Work out 15% of 240.
13. Solve $4x-3=17$.
14. Find the area of a circle of radius 5 cm, in terms of $\pi$.
15. A car travels 180 km in 2.5 hours. Find its average speed.
16. Write $0.00045$ in standard form.

<summary>Answers and concise solutions</summary>

1. The fourth quadrant. A positive $x$ is to the right and a negative $y$ is below
   the axis.
2. $5$. The steps are 3 and 4, and $3^2+4^2=25$, so $\sqrt{25}=5$.
3. $(4,7)$. Average the coordinates: $(1+7)\div2=4$ and $(3+11)\div2=7$.
4. $\tfrac{3}{2}$. The rise is $10-4=6$ and the run is $6-2=4$, so $6\div4=\tfrac{3}{2}$.
5. Yes. The product is $\tfrac{3}{2}\times\left(-\tfrac{2}{3}\right)=-1$, which is
   the test for perpendicular lines.
6. $y=4x-5$. The intercept is $c=3-4\times2=-5$.
7. $2x-y-5=0$. Move every term to the left.
8. $4$. Set $x=0$: $2y-8=0$, so $y=4$.
9. $13$. The steps are 5 and 12, and $5^2+12^2=25+144=169$, so $\sqrt{169}=13$.
10. The rate is $3$ and the starting value is $3$. The rate is
    $(15-3)\div(4-0)=3$, and the graph meets the $y$-axis at $(0,3)$.
11. $(8,9)$. Double the midpoint and subtract the end: $10-2=8$ and $12-3=9$.
12. $36$. Ten per cent of 240 is 24, so 5% is 12. Add: $24+12=36$.
13. $x=5$. Add 3 to get $4x=20$, then divide by 4.
14. $25\pi$ cm². Use $A=\pi r^2$: $\pi\times5^2=25\pi$.
15. $72$ km/h. Speed is distance divided by time: $180\div2.5=72$.
16. $4.5\times10^{-4}$. Move the decimal point 4 places right, so the power is
    $-4$.

## What you can now do

- plot a point from its coordinates, and read the coordinates of a point;
- name the quadrant from the signs of the coordinates;
- find the distance between two points using
  $d=\sqrt{(x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2}$;
- find the midpoint of a segment by averaging the coordinates;
- find the slope of a line from two points or from a graph;
- decide whether two lines are parallel, perpendicular or neither;
- write the equation of a line as $y=mx+c$, as $y-y_1=m(x-x_1)$, or as
  $ax+by+c=0$;
- read a straight-line graph in context and work out its rate of change.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you guessed whether the two park paths meet at a right
angle. Look back at that guess now.

The main path has slope $\tfrac{3}{4}$ and the second path has slope
$-\tfrac{4}{3}$. Their product is $-1$, so the paths are perpendicular. If you
guessed "yes" by looking at the grid, you read the drawing well. If you guessed
"no", you now have a method that answers the question exactly, with no drawing
needed.

You also wrote down two places on a map of your own and a rough pair of numbers
for each. Take that map now. Find the slope of the straight route between your two
places, and write the equation of that route. Then find the midpoint, which is
where you would stop halfway.

That is the whole of this chapter in one small piece of work: two points, a
distance, a slope, and a line written as an equation.
