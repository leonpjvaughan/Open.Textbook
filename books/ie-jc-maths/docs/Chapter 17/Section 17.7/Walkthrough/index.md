# 17.7 Graphs in context and rate of change — Learn

**Prerequisites:** reading a graph in context from Section 14.5, the slope of a
line from Section 17.3, and the equation of a line from Section 17.5.

**Objectives:** draw the graph of a line segment in context; work out a rate of
change from a graph; read the starting value from the $y$-intercept; compare two
rates; read values from a line segment.

By the end of this section, you can read a straight-line graph and say what its
slope means.

## A line segment on a graph

A real journey or process often lasts only for a short time, so its graph is a
**line segment** rather than a whole line. The segment starts at one time and
ends at another.

- The graph is read only between its endpoints.
- The **slope** of the segment is the **rate of change**.
- The **$y$-intercept** is the starting value, the amount at time 0.

For a distance-time graph, the rate of change is the speed. For a cost graph, it
is the price of one item.

## Working out the rate

The rate is the change in the vertical amount divided by the change in the
horizontal amount. That is the slope.

$$\text{rate} = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$

Write the units with the answer. A rate always has units of the form
"$y$-unit per $x$-unit".

**Example 1.** A graph shows distance in metres against time in minutes. A walker
covers 240 m in 4 minutes at a steady pace. Find the rate of change.

Divide the change in distance by the change in time.

$$\text{rate} = \frac{240}{4} = 60$$

The rate is 60 m per minute. The walker moves 60 m every minute.

**Example 2.** From minute 6 to minute 10 the same walker moves from 240 m to
400 m. Find the rate of change.

The change in distance is $400-240=160$ m, and the change in time is
$10-6=4$ minutes.

$$\text{rate} = \frac{160}{4} = 40$$

The rate is 40 m per minute. This part of the graph is less steep, so the walker
is slower.

**Example 3.** A walker rests from minute 4 to minute 6, staying 240 m from the
start. Find the rate of change.

The distance does not change.

$$\text{rate} = \frac{240-240}{6-4} = 0$$

The rate is 0 m per minute. The graph is flat, and a flat segment means no change.

## The starting value

The $y$-intercept is the amount at the start. It is the height of the graph where
it meets the $y$-axis.

**Example 4.** A phone bill graph passes through $(0,10)$ and $(5,25)$, with the
cost in euro and the time in hours. Find the rate of change and the starting value.

The rate is the slope.

$$\text{rate} = \frac{25-10}{5-0} = \frac{15}{5} = 3$$

The rate is 3 euro per hour. The starting value is the $y$-intercept, which is the
cost at 0 hours.

$$c = 10$$

The starting value is 10 euro. The equation of the line is $y=3x+10$.

## Comparing two rates

When two segments are drawn on the same axes, the steeper one has the larger rate.

**Example 5.** Two lines are drawn on one distance-time graph. Line A goes through
$(0,0)$ and $(2,10)$. Line B goes through $(0,0)$ and $(4,10)$. Which line shows
the faster speed?

Find each rate.

$$\text{rate of A} = \frac{10}{2} = 5 \qquad \text{rate of B} = \frac{10}{4} = 2.5$$

Line A has the larger rate, so line A is faster. Both lines reach 10 m, but line A
does it in 2 s and line B takes 4 s. Line A is steeper.

## Drawing the segment

To draw a line segment in context, plot the two endpoints and join them with a
straight line. Do not carry the line on past the endpoints, because the situation
may not continue.

**Example 6.** A tank fills at a steady rate. The depth is 0 cm at 0 minutes and
45 cm at 9 minutes. Find the rate and draw the segment.

$$\text{rate} = \frac{45}{9} = 5$$

The rate is 5 cm per minute. Plot $(0,0)$ and $(9,45)$, and join them with a
straight line. The segment ends at 9 minutes, when the tank is full.

## The park keeper's walk

The park keeper walks from the gate at a steady pace.

- In the first 2 minutes the keeper walks 10 m.
- In the next 4 minutes the keeper walks 24 m.

**Example 7.** Find the rate of change in each part.

First part:

$$\frac{10}{2} = 5$$

Second part:

$$\frac{24}{4} = 6$$

The keeper walks at 5 m per minute and then 6 m per minute. The second part of the
graph is steeper.

## Watch out

The $y$-intercept and the rate are different numbers.

> **Watch out.** A graph passes through $(0,10)$ and $(5,25)$. The common error is
> to say the rate is 10, because that is where the graph starts.
>
> The starting value is 10, but the rate is the slope:
> $\frac{25-10}{5-0}=3$. The rate is 3 per unit.
>
> **Re-check:** a rate is a change divided by a change. If the graph is flat, the
> rate is 0 even when the starting value is large.

A second trap is reading past the ends of the segment. The graph stops where the
data stops. A value beyond the last point is not on the graph.

## Progress check

Return to the chapter problem. The park keeper walks 10 m in the first 2 minutes,
then 24 m in the next 4 minutes.

1. Find the rate of change in the first part.
2. Find the rate of change in the second part.
3. Which part of the graph is steeper, and why?

This is the last piece of the park. The Review brings every section together.

## You should now be able to

- read a line segment in context, between its endpoints;
- work out a rate of change as the slope of the segment;
- write the units of a rate;
- read the starting value from the $y$-intercept;
- compare two rates by comparing the steepness of two segments;
- draw a line segment from two points and stop at the endpoints;
- explain what a flat part of a graph means.
