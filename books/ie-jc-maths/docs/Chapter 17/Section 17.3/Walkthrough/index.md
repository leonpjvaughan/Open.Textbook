# 17.3 Slope of a line — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the horizontal and vertical steps from Section 17.2, and
dividing with negative numbers from Section 1.3.

**Objectives:** find the slope of a line from two points; find the slope from a
graph; tell positive, negative, zero and undefined slopes apart; say what the sign
of a slope means.

By the end of this section, you can measure how steep a line is.

## Slope is rise over run

The **slope** of a line measures how steep it is. It compares the vertical change
to the horizontal change.

$$\text{slope} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$$

The **rise** is the vertical step, and the **run** is the horizontal step. Divide
the rise by the run.

The slope of a line is the same between any two points on it. That is what makes a
straight line straight.

**Example 1.** Find the slope of the line through $A(1,2)$ and $B(5,10)$.

The rise is the change in $y$, and the run is the change in $x$.

$$\text{rise} = 10-2 = 8 \qquad \text{run} = 5-1 = 4$$

Divide the rise by the run.

$$\text{slope} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$$

The slope is 2. The line goes up 2 for every 1 across.

**Predict.** Before you move the sliders, predict whether the slope changes if you swap
which point is A and which is B.

**Test.** The figure draws two points, the line through them, and the rise and the run as
the two legs of a triangle. Move the points and watch the slope and the equation change.

**Explain.** Write two or three sentences. Say why the slope is the rise divided by the

## The sign of the slope

The sign of the slope tells you the direction of the line.

- A **positive** slope means the line goes up as you move right.
- A **negative** slope means the line goes down as you move right.
- A **zero** slope means the line is horizontal.
- An **undefined** slope means the line is vertical.

**Example 2.** Find the slope of the line through $A(2,7)$ and $B(6,3)$.

The rise is $3-7=-4$, and the run is $6-2=4$.

$$\text{slope} = \frac{-4}{4} = -1$$

The slope is $-1$. The rise is negative, so the line falls as you move right.

**Example 3.** Find the slope of the line through $A(1,5)$ and $B(4,5)$.

The rise is $5-5=0$, and the run is $4-1=3$.

$$\text{slope} = \frac{0}{3} = 0$$

The slope is 0. Both points have the same $y$-coordinate, so the line is
horizontal.

**Example 4.** Find the slope of the line through $A(3,1)$ and $B(3,9)$.

The run is $3-3=0$. Division by 0 has no answer, so the slope is undefined.

The line is vertical. A vertical line has no slope.

## Slope from a graph

You can read a slope straight from a graph. Choose two points on the line, and
count the rise and the run.

**Example 5.** A line passes through $(0,1)$ and $(2,5)$. Find its slope.

From $(0,1)$ to $(2,5)$, the rise is $5-1=4$ and the run is $2-0=2$.

$$\text{slope} = \frac{4}{2} = 2$$

The slope is 2. You can use any two points on the line and you will get the same
answer.

## Slope as a rate

A slope is a rate. It says how much the vertical amount changes for each unit of
the horizontal amount.

For a distance-time graph, the slope is the speed. For a cost graph, the slope is
the price of one item. You will use this in Section 17.7.

**Example 6.** A path rises 6 m over a horizontal run of 8 m. Find its slope.

$$\text{slope} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$$

The slope is $\tfrac{3}{4}$. The path rises 3 m for every 4 m across.

## Watch out

A common mistake is to swap the rise and the run.

> **Watch out.** A line goes up 4 and across 8. The common error is to divide the
> run by the rise and write $8\div4=2$.
>
> The slope is rise over run: $\frac{4}{8}=\frac{1}{2}$. A slope of 2 would be
> much steeper than this line.
>
> **Re-check:** a slope greater than 1 is steeper than 45°, and a slope less than
> 1 is gentler. This line rises less than it runs, so its slope is less than 1.

A second trap is the sign. When one point is left of the other, the run is
negative, and the slope can be negative. Keep the order of the points the same in
the top and the bottom of the fraction.

## You should now be able to

- find the slope of a line using $\text{slope}=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$;
- read the rise and the run from a graph;
- tell a positive slope from a negative one;
- explain why a horizontal line has slope 0;
- explain why a vertical line has an undefined slope;
- say what a slope means as a rate.
