# 17.6 The general form ax + by + c = 0 — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the equation of a line from Section 17.5, and rearranging a
formula from Section 13.2.

**Objectives:** write a line in the form $ax+by+c=0$; change between the general
form and $y=mx+c$; read the slope and the intercepts from the general form; test
whether a point lies on a line.

By the end of this section, you can work with a line in any of its forms.

## The general form

A straight line can be written with all its terms on one side.

$$ax + by + c = 0$$

Here $a$, $b$ and $c$ are numbers, and $a$ and $b$ are not both 0. This is called
the **general form** of the equation of a line.

Every line has a general form, including vertical lines. A vertical line has
$b=0$, so the $y$ term disappears. For example, $x-3=0$ is the vertical line
$x=3$.

**Example 1.** Write the line $y=3x+2$ in general form.

Move every term to the left. Subtract $y$ from both sides.

$$0 = 3x - y + 2$$

The general form is $3x-y+2=0$.

**Example 2.** Write the line $y=-2x+4$ in general form.

Add $2x$ to both sides and subtract 4.

$$2x + y - 4 = 0$$

## Changing back to y = mx + c

To find the slope and the intercept, rearrange the general form so that $y$ is on
its own.

**Example 3.** Write $2x+3y-6=0$ in the form $y=mx+c$.

Move the $x$ term and the constant to the right.

$$3y = 6 - 2x$$

Divide every term by 3.

$$y = 2 - \frac{2}{3}x$$

The slope is $-\tfrac{2}{3}$ and the intercept is 2.

## Reading the slope and intercepts

When $b$ is not 0, the slope and the $y$-intercept can be read straight from the
general form.

$$\text{slope} = -\frac{a}{b} \qquad \text{$y$-intercept} = -\frac{c}{b}$$

The $x$-intercept is found by setting $y=0$, which gives $x=-\frac{c}{a}$.

**Example 4.** Find the slope of the line $4x-2y+6=0$.

Use the slope rule with $a=4$ and $b=-2$.

$$-\frac{a}{b} = -\frac{4}{-2} = 2$$

The slope is 2. Check by rearranging: $2y=4x+6$, so $y=2x+3$.

**Example 5.** Find the $y$-intercept of the line $3x+4y-12=0$.

Set $x=0$, because the $y$-axis is the line $x=0$.

$$4y - 12 = 0$$

$$4y = 12 \qquad y = 3$$

The $y$-intercept is 3. The line crosses the $y$-axis at $(0,3)$.

**Example 6.** Find the $x$-intercept of the line $5x+2y-10=0$.

Set $y=0$, because the $x$-axis is the line $y=0$.

$$5x - 10 = 0$$

$$5x = 10 \qquad x = 2$$

The $x$-intercept is 2. The line crosses the $x$-axis at $(2,0)$.

## Testing a point

A point lies on a line when substituting its coordinates makes the left-hand side
equal to 0.

**Example 7.** Does the point $(2,3)$ lie on the line $2x+y-7=0$?

Substitute $x=2$ and $y=3$.

$$2 \times 2 + 3 - 7 = 4 + 3 - 7 = 0$$

The result is 0, so the point lies on the line.

## Clearing a fraction

When a line has a fractional slope, multiply every term by the denominator to get
whole-number coefficients.

**Example 8.** Write $y=\tfrac{1}{2}x+3$ in general form with whole-number
coefficients.

Multiply every term by 2.

$$2y = x + 6$$

Move every term to the left.

$$x - 2y + 6 = 0$$

## Watch out

The sign of the constant is the trap.

> **Watch out.** The line $y=2x+1$ becomes $2x-y+1=0$. The common error is to keep
> the $+1$ on the right and write $2x+y+1=0$, which changes the sign of the $y$
> term.
>
> Move every term to one side and keep its sign. From $y=2x+1$: subtract $y$ from
> both sides to get $0=2x-y+1$.
>
> **Re-check:** substitute a point on the line. For $(0,1)$: $0-1+1=0$, which
> works. For the wrong form: $0+1+1=2$, which does not.

A second trap is a line with $b=0$. The line $x-3=0$ is vertical, so it has no
$y$-intercept and no slope in the form $y=mx+c$.

## You should now be able to

- write a line in the form $ax+by+c=0$;
- rearrange the general form into $y=mx+c$;
- read the slope as $-\frac{a}{b}$ and the $y$-intercept as $-\frac{c}{b}$;
- find the $x$-intercept by setting $y=0$;
- test whether a point lies on a line;
- clear a fraction so the coefficients are whole numbers;
- recognise a vertical line, where $b=0$.
