# 17.1 The coordinate plane and plotting points — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Work these from memory. They are the skills this section uses.

1. Work out $-3+7$.
2. Work out $4-9$.
3. In $y=2x+1$, find the value of $y$ when $x=0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4$. Start at $-3$ and move 7 to the right.
2. $-5$. The answer is negative, because 9 is larger than 4.
3. $y=1$. Substitute $x=0$: $2\times0+1=1$.

Moving left and down makes a coordinate negative, so these skills return in every
question about the grid.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write the coordinates of the point that is 4 units to the right of the
origin and 6 units up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first number is the distance across, and the second is the distance up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(4,\ 6)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The point is $(4,6)$. Move 4 to the right, then 6 up. Both numbers are positive,
so the point is in the first quadrant.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A point is at $(5,3)$. A second point is 2 units to the left of it. Write
the coordinates of the second point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Moving left makes the $x$-coordinate smaller. The height does not change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5-2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The second point is $(3,3)$.

$$5-2=3$$

The $y$-coordinate stays 3, because the move is sideways.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A point is at $(-2,6)$. It moves 4 units down. Write its new coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Moving down makes the $y$-coordinate smaller. The $x$-coordinate does not change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6-4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The new point is $(-2,2)$.

$$6-4=2$$

The $x$-coordinate stays $-2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** In which quadrant does the point $(5,-3)$ lie?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the signs of the two coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Positive $x$, negative $y$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The point is in the fourth quadrant. A positive $x$ puts it to the right of the
$y$-axis. A negative $y$ puts it below the $x$-axis. Right and down is the fourth
quadrant.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** On which axis does the point $(0,-6)$ lie?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One coordinate is 0. Decide which axis that puts the point on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The $x$-coordinate is 0.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The point lies on the $y$-axis. A point is on the $y$-axis when $x=0$. It is 6
units below the origin.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The points $A(2,2)$, $B(6,2)$ and $C(6,5)$ are three corners of a rectangle
$ABCD$. Write the coordinates of the fourth corner $D$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rectangle is 4 units wide and 3 units tall. Use those measurements from the
corners you know.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6-4 \qquad 2+3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The fourth corner is $D(2,5)$.

$$6-4=2 \qquad 2+3=5$$

From $C(6,5)$, move 4 units left to get $x=2$. From $A(2,2)$, move 3 units up to
get $y=5$. Both steps give the same point.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The park gate is at $G(1,2)$. A signpost stands 5 m to the right of the gate
and 3 m above it. Write the coordinates of the signpost.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start from the gate and add the two moves.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1+5 \qquad 2+3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The signpost is at $(6,5)$.

$$1+5=6 \qquad 2+3=5$$

Each unit is 1 m, so the grid coordinates are the distances in metres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**8.** In $y=3x-1$, find the value of $y$ when $x=2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute the value of $x$ into the expression.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\times2-1$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = 3\times2-1 = 5$$

Substitution returns in every section of this chapter, because a point lies on a
line when its coordinates fit the equation.

</details>
</details>
</details>
