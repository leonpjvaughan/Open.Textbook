# 17.2 Distance and midpoint — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Work these from memory. They are the skills this section uses.

1. Work out $3^2+4^2$.
2. Work out $\sqrt{49}$.
3. Find the average of 4 and 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $25$. The squares are 9 and 16, and $9+16=25$.
2. $7$. Since $7\times7=49$.
3. $7$. Add them and halve: $(4+10)\div2=7$.

The distance formula squares the two steps, and the midpoint formula averages the
coordinates. Both skills return in every question here.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the distance between $(1,1)$ and $(4,5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the step across and the step up first. Then square each step and add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{(4-1)^2+(5-1)^2} = \sqrt{3^2+4^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The distance is $5$.

$$3^2+4^2 = 9+16 = 25 \qquad \sqrt{25}=5$$

The steps are 3 across and 4 up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the distance between $(2,3)$ and $(6,6)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The step across is $6-2$ and the step up is $6-3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{4^2+3^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The distance is $5$.

$$4^2+3^2 = 16+9 = 25 \qquad \sqrt{25}=5$$

The order of the points does not matter, because each step is squared.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the distance between $(0,1)$ and $(2,4)$, leaving your answer as a
surd.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The steps are 2 across and 3 up. Add the squares and take the root.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{2^2+3^2} = \sqrt{4+9}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The distance is $\sqrt{13}$.

$$2^2+3^2 = 4+9 = 13$$

The number 13 is not a square number, so the exact answer stays as $\sqrt{13}$,
which is about 3.61.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the midpoint of the segment joining $(2,4)$ and $(6,10)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average the two $x$-coordinates, then average the two $y$-coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\left(\frac{2+6}{2},\ \frac{4+10}{2}\right)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The midpoint is $(4,7)$.

$$\frac{2+6}{2}=4 \qquad \frac{4+10}{2}=7$$

The midpoint lies on the segment, halfway between the two ends.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the midpoint of the segment joining $(-2,3)$ and $(6,-1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the coordinates and halve, even when one is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\left(\frac{-2+6}{2},\ \frac{3+(-1)}{2}\right)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The midpoint is $(2,1)$.

$$\frac{-2+6}{2}=2 \qquad \frac{3-1}{2}=1$$

The average can still be positive when one coordinate is negative.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The midpoint of a segment is $(3,4)$, and one end is $(1,2)$. Find the other
end.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two ends add to twice the midpoint.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\times3-1 \qquad 2\times4-2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The other end is $(5,6)$.

$$2\times3=6 \qquad 6-1=5$$

$$2\times4=8 \qquad 8-2=6$$

Double the midpoint, then subtract the end you know.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The park gate is $G(1,2)$ and the kiosk is $K(9,8)$. Find the length of the
main path, and the coordinates of the bench at its midpoint.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the distance for the length, and the average for the midpoint.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{(9-1)^2+(8-2)^2} = \sqrt{8^2+6^2}$$

$$\left(\frac{1+9}{2},\ \frac{2+8}{2}\right)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The main path is $10$ m long and the bench is at $(5,5)$.

$$8^2+6^2 = 64+36 = 100 \qquad \sqrt{100}=10$$

$$\frac{1+9}{2}=5 \qquad \frac{2+8}{2}=5$$

The bench is the same distance from the gate as from the kiosk.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**8.** Find the mean of the numbers 8, 11 and 14.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the numbers and divide by how many there are.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{8+11+14}{3}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The mean is $11$.

$$\frac{33}{3}=11$$

A mean is an average, and the midpoint of a segment is the same idea applied to
two coordinates.

</details>
</details>
</details>
