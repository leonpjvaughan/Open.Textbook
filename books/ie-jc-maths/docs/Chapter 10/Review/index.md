# Chapter 10: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The sponsored walk.** Each sponsor pays €5 to take part plus €2 for every
kilometre. Find what one sponsor pays for 8 km, and find how far a walker must go
for one sponsor to pay €25.

**Step 1. Write the expression.** The €5 is charged once, so it is the constant.
The €2 is charged for each kilometre, so it multiplies $k$.

$$5 + 2k$$

**Step 2. The payment for 8 km.** Substitute $k=8$.

$$5 + 2\times8 = 5 + 16 = 21$$

**One sponsor pays €21 for 8 km.**

**Step 3. The equation for €25.** The class wants the payment to be €25, so the
expression equals 25.

$$5 + 2k = 25$$

**Step 4. Find the distance by testing.** Start near the 8 km you already know.

- $k=8$: $5+2\times8=21$, which is too small.
- $k=10$: $5+2\times10=25$, which is true.

**A walker must complete 10 km for one sponsor to pay €25.**

**Step 5. Check the answer.** The payment for 8 km was €21, and €25 is more than
€21, so the distance must be more than 8 km. The answer, 10 km, is more than
8 km. That agrees.

The whole chapter is in those five steps. Section 10.1 wrote the expression,
Section 10.2 put a number into it, and Section 10.3 built the equation and found
the value that makes it true.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which idea applies** and
say why in a few words. The idea might be writing an expression, substituting a
value, testing a solution, or the order of operations.

1. Write an expression for the cost of $n$ sandwiches at €4 each.
2. Write an expression for a number $x$ with 9 taken away.
3. Find the value of $6x-1$ when $x=3$.
4. Find the value of $a^2+3a$ when $a=-2$.
5. Is $x=5$ a solution of $2x+3=13$? Give a reason.
6. Write an equation for "three times a number $n$ is 24", and find $n$.
7. A pattern has general term $5n-2$. Find the 20th term.
8. Work out $(-4)^2$ and $-4^2$. Are the two values the same? Explain.
9. A phone plan costs $15+0.10m$ euro for $m$ minutes. Find the cost of 90
   minutes.
10. Explain the difference between an expression and an equation, using an
    example of each.

## What you can now do

- say what a letter stands for in an expression, and name the terms, the
  coefficient and the constant;
- write an expression from a rule in words, and build one from a table or a
  pattern;
- substitute a whole number, a negative number or a decimal into an expression
  and work out the value;
- work out an index before the operations around it;
- say what the equals sign means, and tell an expression from an equation;
- write an equation from a sentence, test whether a value is a solution, and
  find a solution by testing.

## Thinking back

At the start of the chapter you wrote down something in your own week where one
amount depends on another. You may have chosen a phone bill, a taxi fare, a
recipe, or the cost of some tickets.

Take that example now. Write the rule as an expression, and say what the letter
stands for and what each number means. Then choose one value and work out the
result. If your rule has a constant and a coefficient, you have written exactly
the same kind of expression as the sponsored walk.

Finally, look back at your prediction for the chapter problem. The walker needs
10 km, and the payment for 8 km was €21. If you predicted a distance of more than
10 km, the test at $k=10$ is what settled it: the payment there is exactly €25.
Estimating first, and checking afterwards, is a habit worth keeping.
