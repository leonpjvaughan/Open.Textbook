# 21.6 Independent events and the multiplication rule — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\frac12\times\frac13$.
2. Work out $\frac34\times\frac23$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac16$. Multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms: $\frac{1\times1}{2\times3}=\frac16$.
2. $\frac12$. The result is $\frac{6}{12}$, which simplifies to $\frac12$. Cancel
   before multiplying if you can: the 3 and the 2 share a factor.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of two tails.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two tosses are independent, so multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac12\times\frac12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(TT)=\frac12\times\frac12=\frac14$$

One of the four outcomes, $TT$, is two tails. The first toss does not change the
chance of a tail on the second, so the tosses are independent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair six-sided die is rolled twice. Find the probability of a 5 on both
rolls.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two rolls are independent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac16\times\frac16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(5\text{ and }5)=\frac16\times\frac16=\frac{1}{36}$$

One of the 36 equally likely outcomes is a 5 on both rolls. The first roll does
not change the die.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** In the open-day game, $P(6)=\frac16$ and $P(A)=\frac14$. The die and the
spinner are independent. Find the probability of the star prize, a 6 and an A.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the two probabilities.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac16\times\frac14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(6\text{ and }A)=\frac16\times\frac14=\frac{1}{24}$$

One of the 24 equally likely outcomes is a 6 with an A. The count and the
multiplication rule agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A bag holds 2 red and 3 blue counters. A counter is taken, put back, and a
second counter is taken. Find the probability that both are red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Putting the counter back restores the bag, so the draws are independent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The probability of red is $\frac25$ on each draw.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{red and red})=\frac25\times\frac25=\frac{4}{25}$$

The bag has 5 counters and 2 of them are red. Putting the first counter back means
the second draw faces the same bag, so the two draws are independent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. Find the probability that both dice show
an odd number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the probability of an odd number on one die first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The odd results are 1, 3 and 5, so the probability is $\frac36=\frac12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{both odd})=\frac12\times\frac12=\frac14$$

Each die is odd with probability $\frac12$, and the dice are independent. Counting
agrees: 9 of the 36 outcomes have two odd numbers.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A fair coin is tossed and a fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the
probability of a tail and a 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The coin and the die are independent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac12\times\frac16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{tail and }3)=\frac12\times\frac16=\frac{1}{12}$$

One of the 12 equally likely outcomes is a tail with a 3. The coin does not change
the die.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A fair coin is tossed three times. Find the probability of three tails.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each toss is independent, so multiply three probabilities.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac12\times\frac12\times\frac12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(TTT)=\frac12\times\frac12\times\frac12=\frac18$$

One of the 8 equally likely outcomes is $TTT$. Each toss is independent of the
others.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** A bag holds 2 red and 3 blue counters. Two counters are taken without
replacement. Are the two draws independent? Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the bag before the first draw with the bag before the second draw.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

After the first counter is taken, the bag holds 4 counters, not 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

No. The first draw changes the bag, so the second draw faces different chances.
If the first counter is red, then 1 red and 3 blue remain, so the probability of a
second red is $\frac14$ instead of $\frac25$.

The first event changes the second, so the draws are not independent and the
multiplication rule with $\frac25$ does not apply.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**9.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of an even number or a
number greater than 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The result 6 is in both events, so subtract its probability once.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac36+\frac26-\frac16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac36+\frac26-\frac16=\frac46=\frac23$$

The even results are 2, 4 and 6, and the results greater than 4 are 5 and 6. The
result 6 is in both, so it is counted once after the subtraction. The matching
results are 2, 4, 5 and 6.

</details>
</details>
</details>
