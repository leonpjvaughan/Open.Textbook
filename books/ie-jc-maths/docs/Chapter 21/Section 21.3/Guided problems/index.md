# 21.3 Equally likely outcomes — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $\frac{3}{6}$ in its simplest form.
2. Write down the even numbers from 1 to 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac12$. Divide the top and the bottom by 3, the highest common factor of 3
   and 6.
2. $2, 4$ and $6$. The numbers 1, 3 and 5 are odd.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of an odd number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the odd results, then divide by 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The odd results are 1, 3 and 5, so 3 outcomes match.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{odd})=\frac{3}{6}=\frac12$$

The die is fair, so the 6 results are equally likely. Three of them are odd, and
the fraction simplifies to one half.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of a 3 or a 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the outcomes that match, then divide by the total.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The outcomes 3 and 4 both match, so the count is 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{3 or 4})=\frac{2}{6}=\frac13$$

Two of the 6 equally likely results match, and $\frac{2}{6}$ simplifies to
$\frac13$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A spinner has 10 equal sectors and 4 of them are red. Find the probability
that the spinner lands on red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 10 sectors are equal, so the outcomes are equally likely.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Four of the 10 sectors are red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{red})=\frac{4}{10}=\frac25$$

The fraction simplifies to $\frac25$, which is $0.4$. Four of the 10 equal sectors
are red.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A bag holds 3 red, 3 blue and 4 green counters. One counter is taken at
random. Find the probability that it is green.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count all the counters first. Each counter is equally likely.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are $3+3+4=10$ counters and 4 of them are green.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{green})=\frac{4}{10}=\frac25$$

Every counter is equally likely, so count counters and not colours. Four of the 10
counters are green.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** The open-day game has 24 equally likely outcomes. Find the probability that
the die shows a 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 6 row of the table holds one outcome for each of the 4 spinner sectors.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are 4 outcomes with a 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{die shows 6})=\frac{4}{24}=\frac16$$

The 6 row has 4 outcomes and the table has 24. The answer is the same as the
probability from the die alone, which is $\frac16$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The open-day game has 24 equally likely outcomes. Find the probability that
the die shows an even number and the spinner lands on A.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The even results on the die are 2, 4 and 6, and each pairs with the A sector.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are 3 matching outcomes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{even and A})=\frac{3}{24}=\frac18$$

The three outcomes are 2A, 4A and 6A, so 3 of the 24 equally likely outcomes
match.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A bag holds 2 red counters and 6 blue counters. Explain why the probability
of red is not one half.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the counters of each colour, not the number of colours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are 8 counters in total and only 2 of them are red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The probability of red is $\frac{2}{8}=\frac14$, not one half.

One half would need equal numbers of red and blue counters. There are 2 red and 6
blue, so the two colours are not equally likely. The 8 counters are equally
likely, because each counter is one object.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**8.** Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. How many outcomes are in the sample
space?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each die has 6 results, and the two dice are separate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6\times6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6\times6=36$$

There are 36 outcomes. A table with the first die down the side and the second
across the top has 6 rows and 6 columns.

</details>
</details>
</details>
