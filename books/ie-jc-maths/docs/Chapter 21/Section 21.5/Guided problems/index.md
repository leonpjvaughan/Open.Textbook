# 21.5 Two events and the addition rule — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\frac16+\frac14$.
2. Can the results "the die shows 1" and "the die shows 2" both happen on the same
   roll?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac{5}{12}$. Use the common denominator 12: $\frac{2}{12}+\frac{3}{12}=\frac{5}{12}$.
2. No. One roll gives one result, so the two events cannot both happen. They are
   mutually exclusive.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of a 5 or a 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two results cannot both happen, so the events are mutually exclusive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the two probabilities: $\frac16+\frac16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(5\text{ or }6)=\frac16+\frac16=\frac26=\frac13$$

Two of the 6 equally likely results match. There is no overlap to subtract,
because one roll cannot give both a 5 and a 6.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of an even number or a
number greater than 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

List both events and look for the results that are in both.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The even results are 2, 4 and 6. The results greater than 3 are 4, 5 and 6. The
results 4 and 6 are in both.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{even or greater than }3)=\frac36+\frac36-\frac26=\frac46=\frac23$$

The two overlapping results, 4 and 6, are counted twice when you add, so subtract
$\frac26$ once. Counting the matching results gives 2, 4, 5 and 6, which is 4 of
the 6 outcomes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** In the open-day game, $P(6)=\frac16$ and $P(A)=\frac14$. The probability of
both is $\frac{1}{24}$. Find the probability of a prize, which needs a 6 or an A.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the two probabilities with denominator 24 before adding.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac16=\frac{4}{24}$ and $\frac14=\frac{6}{24}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(6\text{ or }A)=\frac{4}{24}+\frac{6}{24}-\frac{1}{24}=\frac{9}{24}=\frac38$$

The star prize outcome, a 6 with an A, is in both events, so subtract it once.
Nine of the 24 equally likely outcomes give a prize.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A card is chosen at random from cards numbered 1 to 10. Find the
probability that the number is even or a multiple of 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The even numbers are 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. The multiples of 5 are 5 and 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The number 10 is in both events, so subtract its probability once.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{even or multiple of }5)=\frac{5}{10}+\frac{2}{10}-\frac{1}{10}=\frac{6}{10}=\frac35$$

The overlapping number is 10. Counting the matching numbers gives 2, 4, 5, 6, 8
and 10, which is 6 of the 10 cards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two fair dice are rolled. Find the probability that the total is 7 or 11.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the outcomes for each total. Can one roll give both totals?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Six outcomes total 7 and two outcomes total 11, out of 36 outcomes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{7 or 11})=\frac{6}{36}+\frac{2}{36}=\frac{8}{36}=\frac29$$

One roll cannot give a total of both 7 and 11, so the events are mutually
exclusive and there is no overlap to subtract.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A bag holds 3 red, 2 blue and 5 green counters. One counter is taken at
random. Find the probability that it is red or blue.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count all the counters first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are 10 counters, so $P(\text{red})=\frac{3}{10}$ and
$P(\text{blue})=\frac{2}{10}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{red or blue})=\frac{3}{10}+\frac{2}{10}=\frac{5}{10}=\frac12$$

A counter cannot be both red and blue, so the events are mutually exclusive. Five
of the 10 counters are red or blue.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Two events have probabilities $0.5$ and $0.4$, and the probability that both
happen is $0.2$. Find the probability that at least one of them happens.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At least one means the first or the second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.5+0.4-0.2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.5+0.4-0.2=0.7$$

The overlap of $0.2$ was counted in both probabilities, so it is subtracted once.
The probability that at least one event happens is $0.7$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**8.** A spinner has 8 equal sectors and 3 of them are red. Find the probability
that the spinner lands on red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The 8 sectors are equal, so the outcomes are equally likely.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Three of the 8 sectors are red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{red})=\frac38$$

The 8 equal sectors are equally likely, and 3 of them are red. The fraction does
not simplify.

</details>
</details>
