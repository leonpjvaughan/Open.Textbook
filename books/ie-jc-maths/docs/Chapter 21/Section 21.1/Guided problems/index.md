# 21.1 Listing outcomes and the sample space — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $5\times4$.
2. A fair coin has two sides. Write down the two results of one toss.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $20$. Counting in fives four times gives $5, 10, 15, 20$.
2. Heads and tails. There are exactly two outcomes, and each is equally likely.
   This is the starting point for every coin question in this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A fair coin is tossed three times. How many outcomes are in the sample
space?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each toss doubles the number of outcomes. Start with 2 outcomes for one toss.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For two tosses there are $2\times2=4$ outcomes. Multiply by 2 again for the third
toss.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2\times2\times2=8$$

There are 8 outcomes, from $HHH$ to $TTT$. You can build the list by taking each
pair from the first two tosses and adding $H$, then adding $T$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair six-sided die is rolled and a coin is tossed. Write down the sample
space.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put the die down the side of a table and the coin across the top.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The table has 6 rows and 2 columns. Each cell is one outcome, such as 3H.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The outcomes are $1H$, $2H$, $3H$, $4H$, $5H$, $6H$, $1T$, $2T$, $3T$, $4T$,
$5T$ and $6T$.

There are $6\times2=12$ outcomes. The coin side is the second part of each
outcome, so $3H$ and $3T$ are different.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A cafe offers 4 starters and 5 main courses. How many different two-course
meals are possible?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each starter can be paired with each main course.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the two numbers of choices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4\times5=20$$

There are 20 meals. For each of the 4 starters there are 5 main courses, which
gives $4\times5=20$ pairs. Adding the numbers would give 9 and would miss most of
the meals.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two fair six-sided dice are rolled. How many outcomes have a total of 8?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work along the pairs in order. Start with a 2 on the first die, then a 3, and so
on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The pairs are $(2,6)$, $(3,5)$, $(4,4)$, $(5,3)$ and $(6,2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

There are 5 outcomes: $(2,6)$, $(3,5)$, $(4,4)$, $(5,3)$ and $(6,2)$.

Each pair adds to 8. The pair $(4,4)$ is allowed, because the two dice are
separate. The pair $(6,2)$ is not the same as $(2,6)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A code is made from one letter chosen from A, B, C, D and one digit chosen
from 1, 2, 3. How many different codes are possible?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the letters and the digits, then multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are 4 letters and 3 digits.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4\times3=12$$

There are 12 codes. Each of the 4 letters can be followed by each of the 3 digits,
which gives A1, A2, A3, B1 and so on, up to D3.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Explain why the sample space has 4 outcomes
and not 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

List the four outcomes and look at the two middle ones.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The outcomes are $HH$, $HT$, $TH$ and $TT$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The sample space has 4 outcomes because the order of the two tosses matters. The
outcome $HT$ means a head first and then a tail, and $TH$ means a tail first and
then a head. They are different, so they are counted separately.

If you counted $HT$ and $TH$ as one outcome, you would get 3, and one of the
four possible results would be missing.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**7.** List the elements of the set of even numbers from 1 to 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An even number divides by 2 with no remainder. The number 1 is not even.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Start at 2 and add 2 each time until you reach 10.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The set is $\{2, 4, 6, 8, 10\}$.

The numbers from 1 to 10 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 10, and the even ones
are 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10. Write a set in braces, with the elements separated by
commas.

</details>
</details>
</details>
