# 21.7 Probability from a table or a diagram — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $\frac{12}{40}$ in its simplest form.
2. Work out $\frac25\times\frac35$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac{3}{10}$. Divide the top and the bottom by 4, the highest common factor
   of 12 and 40.
2. $\frac{6}{25}$. Multiply the tops and multiply the bottoms:
   $\frac{2\times3}{5\times5}=\frac{6}{25}$.

</details>

## Practise with help

The table shows how 100 students in a school travel to school. Use it for
questions 1 to 3.

| | Cycle | Walk | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | 20 | 30 | 50 |
| Year 2 | 15 | 35 | 50 |
| Total | 35 | 65 | 100 |

**1.** A student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student is in
Year 1 and cycles to school.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Read the cell where the Year 1 row meets the cycle column, then divide by the
grand total.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The cell holds 20 and the grand total is 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{Year 1 and cycles})=\frac{20}{100}=\frac15$$

The grand total of the table is 100, so that is the denominator. The cell where
the Year 1 row meets the cycle column holds 20.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student walks
to school.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the total of the walk column.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The walk column holds $30+35=65$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{walks})=\frac{65}{100}=\frac{13}{20}$$

The walk column total is 65 and the grand total is 100. The fraction
$\frac{65}{100}$ simplifies by dividing both numbers by 5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student is in
Year 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the total of the Year 2 row.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The Year 2 row total is 50.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{Year 2})=\frac{50}{100}=\frac12$$

The Year 2 row total is 50 and the grand total is 100, so the probability is one
half.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Use a tree diagram to find the probability of
a head then a head.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply along the branch head then head.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Each branch for the coin carries probability $\frac12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(H\text{ then }H)=\frac12\times\frac12=\frac14$$

Multiply along the branch. The first branch has probability $\frac12$ for a head,
and the second branch from that point also has probability $\frac12$ for a head.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Use a tree diagram to find the probability of
exactly one head.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two paths give exactly one head: head then tail, and tail then head.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Each path has probability $\frac14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{exactly one head})=\frac14+\frac14=\frac12$$

Multiply along each path to get $\frac14$, then add the two paths, because both
match the event.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The open-day game has 24 equally likely outcomes in a table. Find the
probability that the spinner lands on A.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The A column holds one outcome for each of the 6 die results.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are 6 outcomes in the A column.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(A)=\frac{6}{24}=\frac14$$

The A column holds 6 outcomes and the table holds 24. The answer is the same as
the probability from the spinner alone, which is $\frac14$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The open-day game has 24 equally likely outcomes in a table. Nine of the
outcomes have a 6 or an A. Find the probability of a prize.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the 6 row and the A column, taking care not to count the 6 with an A twice.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The 6 row has 4 outcomes and the A column has 6, and one outcome is in both.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4+6-1=9 \qquad P(\text{prize})=\frac{9}{24}=\frac38$$

The overlap of 1 is subtracted once, because the 6 with an A is in both the row
and the column. Nine of the 24 equally likely outcomes give a prize.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**8.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of a 1 or a 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two results cannot both happen on one roll.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $\frac16$ and $\frac16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(1\text{ or }2)=\frac16+\frac16=\frac26=\frac13$$

The results 1 and 2 are mutually exclusive, so there is no overlap to subtract.
Two of the 6 equally likely results match.

</details>
</details>
</details>
