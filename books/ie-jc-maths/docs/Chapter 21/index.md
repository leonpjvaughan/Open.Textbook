# Chapter 21: Probability

Chance is part of every day. A weather forecast gives a percentage. A game gives
a chance of winning. A doctor speaks about risk.

Probability puts a number on that chance. This chapter shows you how to find the
number, and how to combine two chances into one.

## The chapter problem

**The open-day game.**

At the school open day there is a stall. Each play has two parts:

1. roll a fair six-sided die, numbered 1 to 6;
2. spin a spinner with four equal sectors, labelled A, B, C and D.

You win a prize if the die shows a 6 **or** the spinner lands on A. The star prize
goes to the player who gets a 6 **and** an A.

Before you calculate anything, look at the two parts of the game. Do you think a
player is more likely to win a prize, or to win nothing? Write down your guess.
There is no score for it.

By the end of the chapter you can list every possible result, find the probability
of a prize and of no prize, find the chance of the star prize, and say how many
prizes the organiser should expect in 240 plays.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- list the outcomes of an experiment in a systematic way, and use the
  fundamental principle of counting to count them;
- write a probability as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage, and place it on
  the scale from 0 to 1;
- find the probability of an event when the outcomes are equally likely;
- use the complement to find the probability that an event does not happen;
- use the addition rule for two events, and say when the events cannot both
  happen;
- use the multiplication rule for two independent events, and say when two events
  are independent;
- read a probability from a table or a tree diagram;
<!-- tier:h -->
- work out an expected frequency, and compare it with what actually happens.

## Where it is used

Weather forecasts, insurance, medical tests and games of chance all use
probability. A shop uses it to decide how many prizes to buy. A scientist uses it
to judge whether a result is real or a run of luck. A player uses it to decide
whether a game is worth the entry fee.
