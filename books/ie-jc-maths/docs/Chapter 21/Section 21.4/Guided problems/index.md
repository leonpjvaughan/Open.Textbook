# 21.4 The complement of an event — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $1-\frac14$.
2. Work out $1-0.2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac34$. Write the 1 as $\frac44$, then $\frac44-\frac14=\frac34$.
2. $0.8$. Take 2 tenths from 10 tenths to get 8 tenths, which is $0.8$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** The probability that a team wins a match is $0.35$. Find the probability
that the team does not win.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Winning and not winning are complements, so the two probabilities add to 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1-0.35$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1-0.35=0.65$$

The probability that the team does not win is $0.65$. A draw counts as not
winning, so the complement covers both a draw and a loss.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability that the result is not
a 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the probability of a 5 first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$P(5)=\frac16$, so the complement is $1-\frac16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{not }5)=1-\frac16=\frac56$$

The five results 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 are not a 5, so 5 of the 6 equally likely
outcomes match. The complement gives the same answer in one step.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The probability of taking a blue counter from a bag is $\frac38$. Find the
probability of not taking a blue counter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the probability from 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1-\frac38$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{not blue})=1-\frac38=\frac58$$

Write the 1 as $\frac88$ to subtract: $\frac88-\frac38=\frac58$. The two
probabilities add to 1, which checks the answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of at least one tail.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The complement of at least one tail is no tails at all.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

No tails at all means $HH$, whose probability is $\frac12\times\frac12=\frac14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{at least one tail})=1-\frac14=\frac34$$

The only way to avoid a tail is $HH$. So at least one tail has probability
$\frac34$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A fair coin is tossed three times. Find the probability of at least one
head.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The complement is no heads at all, which is $TTT$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$P(TTT)=\frac12\times\frac12\times\frac12=\frac18$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{at least one head})=1-\frac18=\frac78$$

Only one of the 8 outcomes, $TTT$, has no head. The other 7 have at least one
head, so the probability is $\frac78$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** In the open-day game the probability of winning a prize is $\frac38$. Find
the probability of winning no prize.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Winning a prize and winning nothing cover every play.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1-\frac38$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{no prize})=1-\frac38=\frac58$$

Write the 1 as $\frac88$: $\frac88-\frac38=\frac58$. The two probabilities add to
1. Because $\frac58$ is more than one half, a player is more likely to win nothing
than to win a prize.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A spinner lands on red with probability $0.4$. Find the probability that it
does not land on red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the complement with the decimal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1-0.4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1-0.4=0.6$$

The probability that the spinner does not land on red is $0.6$. The two
probabilities add to 1: $0.4+0.6=1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from an earlier section

**8.** A bag holds 4 green counters and 1 yellow counter. Find the probability of
taking a green counter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count all the counters first. Each counter is equally likely.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are 5 counters and 4 of them are green.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{green})=\frac45$$

Four of the 5 equally likely counters are green. The colours are not equally
likely, because there are more green counters than yellow ones.

</details>
</details>
</details>
