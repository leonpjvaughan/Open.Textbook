# 12.10 Bearings and three-dimensional problems — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the bearing of due west.
2. In a right triangle, the side opposite the angle is $3$ cm and the adjacent side is
   $4$ cm. Find the angle, correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $270^\circ$. West is three-quarters of a turn clockwise from north. If this was not
   immediate, count the quarter turns: east is $090^\circ$, south is $180^\circ$, west is
   $270^\circ$.
2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{3}{4}$, so $\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.75) = 36.9^\circ$. The opposite
   side is shorter, so the angle is below $45^\circ$, which the answer is.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** The bearing of $A$ from $B$ is $125^\circ$. Find the bearing of $B$ from $A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two bearings are opposite directions along the same line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $180^\circ$ to the bearing of $A$ from $B$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$125^\circ + 180^\circ = 305^\circ$$

The answer is below $360^\circ$, so there is nothing to subtract. The bearing of $B$ from
$A$ is $305^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A ship is $12$ km from port on a bearing of $060^\circ$. How far north and how far
east of the port is it? Give each answer correct to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw a north line at the port. The bearing makes an angle of $60^\circ$ with it, and the
north and east distances are the two legs of a right triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The north distance is $12\cos 60^\circ$, and the east distance is $12\sin 60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{north} = 12\cos 60^\circ = 12 \times 0.5 = 6 \text{ km}$$

$$\text{east} = 12\sin 60^\circ = 12 \times 0.8660 = 10.39 \text{ km}$$

The north distance is shorter, because the bearing is closer to east than to north.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A walker goes $6$ km north, then $8$ km east. Find the distance and the bearing of
the walker from the start. Give the bearing correct to the nearest degree.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two legs are perpendicular, so use Pythagoras' theorem for the distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$d = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2}$, and the bearing is $\tan^{-1}\!\left(\dfrac{8}{6}\right)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10 \text{ km}$$

$$\text{bearing} = \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{8}{6}\right) = 53.1^\circ$$

The bearing is $053^\circ$. It is a little more than $045^\circ$, because the walker
went further east than north.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two boats leave the same station. The first is $5$ km away on a bearing of
$030^\circ$. The second is $8$ km away on a bearing of $100^\circ$. Find the distance
between the boats, correct to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle between the two bearings is the angle at the station.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle is $100^\circ - 30^\circ = 70^\circ$. Use the cosine rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d^2 = 5^2 + 8^2 - 2(5)(8)\cos 70^\circ$$

$$d^2 = 25 + 64 - 80 \times 0.3420 = 89 - 27.36 = 61.64$$

$$d = \sqrt{61.64} = 7.85 \text{ km}$$

The distance is between the two given distances, which fits an angle of $70^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain how to find the bearing of one point from another, and how the north lines
help.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Every north line on the diagram points the same way.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A back bearing is the bearing of the other point plus $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw a north line at each point. The north lines are parallel, so the angle a bearing
makes with one north line can be moved to the other. This is what turns the picture into
a triangle you can solve.

For a back bearing, add $180^\circ$. If the result is $360^\circ$ or more, subtract
$360^\circ$. For example, the back bearing of $048^\circ$ is $228^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A cuboid is $6$ cm long, $2$ cm wide and $3$ cm high. Find the angle between the
space diagonal and the base, correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The projection of the space diagonal on the base is the base diagonal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The base diagonal is $\sqrt{6^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{40}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The right triangle has height $3$ and base $\sqrt{40}$.

$$\tan\theta = \frac{3}{\sqrt{40}} \qquad \theta = 25.4^\circ$$

The angle is small, because the cuboid is long and flat.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A square-based pyramid has base side $10$ m and vertical height $12$ m. Find the
angle between a sloping edge and the base, correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A sloping edge runs from the apex to a base vertex, and its projection is half the
diagonal of the base.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The half-diagonal is $\dfrac{10\sqrt{2}}{2} = 5\sqrt{2} = 7.07$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\tan\theta = \frac{12}{5\sqrt{2}} \qquad \theta = 59.5^\circ$$

The pyramid is steep, so the edge rises at nearly $60^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** For the same pyramid, find the angle between a sloping face and the base, correct
to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A face meets the base at the midpoint of a side, $5$ m from the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The right triangle has height $12$ and base $5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\tan\phi = \frac{12}{5} \qquad \phi = 67.4^\circ$$

The face is steeper than the edge. Its horizontal distance, $5$ m, is smaller than the
edge's $7.07$ m, so the same height gives a larger angle.

</details>
</details>
</details>
