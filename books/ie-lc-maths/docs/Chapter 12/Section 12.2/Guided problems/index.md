# 12.2 Pythagoras' theorem — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. In a right triangle, $\tan\theta = \dfrac{3}{4}$ and the side adjacent to $\theta$ is
   $8$ cm. Find the side opposite $\theta$.
2. Write $\sqrt{18}$ in the form $k\sqrt{2}$, where $k$ is a whole number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$, so
   $\text{opposite} = 8 \times \dfrac{3}{4} = 6$ cm.
2. $\sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \times 2} = 3\sqrt{2}$.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A right triangle has legs of $6$ cm and $8$ cm. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the squares of the two legs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = \sqrt{100} = 10 \text{ cm}$$

This is the $6$-$8$-$10$ triangle, which is a $3$-$4$-$5$ triangle doubled. The
hypotenuse is the longest side, and $10$ is the longest of the three. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A right triangle has hypotenuse $17$ cm and one leg $8$ cm. Find the other leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The hypotenuse is known, so subtract the known square.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$b^2 = 17^2 - 8^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$b^2 = 289 - 64 = 225 \qquad b = 15 \text{ cm}$$

The other leg is shorter than the hypotenuse, which it must be. Adding instead of
subtracting would give $\sqrt{353} = 18.8$, which is longer than the hypotenuse, so the
error would be visible.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right triangle has legs of $4$ cm and $9$ cm. Find the hypotenuse, leaving your
answer as a surd.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the squares, then simplify the surd if you can.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 16 + 81 = 97$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = \sqrt{97} \text{ cm}$$

$97$ is prime, so the surd does not simplify. The answer is exact as it stands.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A square has a diagonal of $10$ cm. Find the length of one side, correct to two
decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diagonal splits the square into two right triangles with two equal legs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$s^2 + s^2 = 10^2$, so $2s^2 = 100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s^2 = 50 \qquad s = \sqrt{50} = 7.07 \text{ cm}$$

The side is shorter than the diagonal, as it must be. Check:
$7.07^2 + 7.07^2 = 99.97$, which is $100$ to two decimal places.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A triangle has sides of $9$ cm, $40$ cm and $41$ cm. Is it right-angled? Give a
reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Test the two shorter sides against the longest one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$9^2 + 40^2 = 81 + 1600 = 1681$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$41^2 = 1681$$

The two totals agree, so the triangle is right-angled. The right angle lies between the
sides of length $9$ cm and $40$ cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A Coast Guard station is $60$ m above sea level. A buoy lies $378.8$ m
horizontally from the foot of the cliff. Find the direct distance from the station to
the buoy, correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The direct distance is the hypotenuse of a right triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$d^2 = 60^2 + 378.8^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d^2 = 3600 + 143489.44 = 147089.44 \qquad d = 383.5 \text{ m}$$

The answer is only $4.7$ m longer than the horizontal distance. A tall-looking cliff
makes very little difference over almost $400$ m, and that is worth noticing.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 12.1

**7.** In a right triangle the side opposite $\theta$ is $7$ cm and the side adjacent to
$\theta$ is $9$ cm. Find $\theta$, correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You do not need Pythagoras here. Which ratio uses the opposite and the adjacent?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{7}{9}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{7}{9}\right) = 37.9^\circ$$

Pythagoras finds a **side** from two other sides. Trigonometry finds a **side or an
angle** from one side and one angle, or an angle from two sides. Choosing between them
is the skill this section and the last one build together.

</details>
</details>
</details>
