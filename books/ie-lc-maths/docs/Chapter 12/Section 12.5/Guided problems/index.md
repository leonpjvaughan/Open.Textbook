# 12.5 Graphs of the trigonometric functions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the exact values of $\sin 90^\circ$ and $\cos 180^\circ$.
2. How many radians are there in a full turn?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sin 90^\circ = 1$ and $\cos 180^\circ = -1$. These are the coordinates of the points
   at the top and at the left of the unit circle.
2. $2\pi$ radians. A full turn is $360^\circ$, and $360 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = 2\pi$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write down the period and the amplitude of $y = \sin\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Read them from the shape of the graph, or from the formula with $b = 1$ and $c = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The wave repeats after one full turn, and it rises $1$ above the middle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The period is $360^\circ$ and the amplitude is $1$.

The range is $-1 \le y \le 1$, so the curve reaches $1$ and $-1$ but no further.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the period of $y = \sin 3\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The period is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{c}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c = 3$, so the period is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The period is $120^\circ$. Three full waves fit into one turn, which is why the period is
a third of $360^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the amplitude and the range of $y = 4\sin\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There is no vertical shift, so the middle line is the $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The amplitude is $4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The amplitude is $4$, so the range is $-4 \le y \le 4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the amplitude, the period and the range of $y = 1 + 2\sin 2\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the formula with $a + b\sin c\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = 1$, $b = 2$ and $c = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The amplitude is $2$, the period is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{2} = 180^\circ$, and the vertical
shift is $1$. The range is $1 - 2 = -1$ up to $1 + 2 = 3$.

The maximum is $3$, not $2$. The amplitude is measured from the middle line.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Sketch the graph of $y = \cos\theta$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$, and
mark where it is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start at the maximum, because $\cos 0^\circ = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The cosine is zero where the point on the unit circle lies on the vertical axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The curve starts at $1$ when $\theta = 0^\circ$, falls through $0$ at $90^\circ$, reaches
$-1$ at $180^\circ$, passes through $0$ again at $270^\circ$, and returns to $1$ at
$360^\circ$.

It is zero at $90^\circ$ and $270^\circ$. Those are the two places where the radius of
the unit circle points straight up or straight down.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The depth of water over a sandbank is $d = 1.4 + 1.2\sin(30^\circ t)$ metres,
where $t$ is in hours. Find the amplitude, the period and the range, and say what the
amplitude means here.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The middle line is $d = 1.4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The amplitude is $1.2$, so the depth varies by $1.2$ m either side of $1.4$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The amplitude is $1.2$ m, the period is $\dfrac{360^\circ}{30^\circ} = 12$ hours, and
the range is $0.2$ m to $2.6$ m.

The amplitude is the amount by which the depth rises above and falls below its middle
value of $1.4$ m. It is half the difference between high and low water:
$\dfrac{2.6 - 0.2}{2} = 1.2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 12.4

**7.** Find the exact value of $\cos 240^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The reference angle is $240^\circ - 180^\circ = 60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$240^\circ$ is in the third quadrant, where cosine is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\cos 240^\circ = -\cos 60^\circ = -\frac{1}{2}$$

On the cosine graph, $\theta = 240^\circ$ is two thirds of the way along one wave, where
the curve is below the axis and rising back towards zero. The negative answer fits.

</details>
</details>
</details>
