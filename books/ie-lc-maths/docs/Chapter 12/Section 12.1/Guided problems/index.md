# 12.1 The trigonometric ratios in a right triangle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $\sqrt{200}$ in the form $k\sqrt{2}$, where $k$ is a whole number.
2. Round $12.2036$ to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sqrt{200} = \sqrt{100 \times 2} = 10\sqrt{2}$.
2. $12.20$. The third decimal is $3$, so the second decimal stays as it is.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** In a right triangle the angle $\theta = 30^\circ$ and the side opposite it is
$6$ cm. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The opposite and the hypotenuse belong to the sine ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{6}{h}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin 30^\circ = \frac{6}{h} \quad\Rightarrow\quad h = \frac{6}{\sin 30^\circ} = \frac{6}{0.5} = 12 \text{ cm}$$

The answer is exact here, because $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$. The hypotenuse is twice the
opposite side, and $12$ is twice $6$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** In a right triangle the angle $\theta = 50^\circ$ and the hypotenuse is $20$ cm.
Find the side opposite $\theta$, correct to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Opposite and hypotenuse again, so the ratio is sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = 20 \sin 50^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 20 \sin 50^\circ = 20 \times 0.7660 = 15.32 \text{ cm}$$

The opposite side is shorter than the hypotenuse, which is a useful check.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right triangle has hypotenuse $9$ m and an angle of $65^\circ$. Find the side
adjacent to that angle, correct to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two sides involved are the adjacent and the hypotenuse, so the ratio is cosine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = 9 \cos 65^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 9 \cos 65^\circ = 9 \times 0.4226 = 3.80 \text{ m}$$

The angle is steep, so the adjacent side is much shorter than the hypotenuse. The answer
fits.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** In a right triangle the side opposite $\theta$ is $9$ cm and the side adjacent to
$\theta$ is $12$ cm. Find $\theta$, correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Opposite and adjacent together give tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{9}{12} = 0.75$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \tan^{-1}(0.75) = 36.9^\circ$$

The opposite side is shorter than the adjacent side, so the angle is below $45^\circ$.
Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A right triangle has an angle of $28^\circ$, a side opposite it of $4$ cm, and a
hypotenuse of $8.5$ cm. Which ratio would you use to find the angle, and why?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You already know the angle. Which pair of sides do you know?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

You know the opposite and the hypotenuse, so the ratio is sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The opposite and the hypotenuse give the sine ratio. Here
$\sin 28^\circ = 0.4695$, and $\dfrac{4}{8.5} = 0.4706$. The two agree to two decimal
places, so the numbers are consistent.

This question is a reminder that you can use a ratio to **check** a triangle as well as
to find a missing part.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A Coast Guard station stands on a cliff $60$ m above the sea. The angle of
depression from the station to a marker buoy at sea level is $9^\circ$. Find the
horizontal distance from the foot of the cliff to the buoy, correct to one decimal
place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The height and the horizontal distance are the opposite and the adjacent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan 9^\circ = \dfrac{60}{x}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = \frac{60}{\tan 9^\circ} = \frac{60}{0.1584} = 378.8 \text{ m}$$

The distance is much larger than the height, which is what a shallow angle of
depression produces.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** The exact value of $\sin 45^\circ$ is $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$. Write this in the
form $\dfrac{\sqrt{k}}{k}$, where $k$ is a whole number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the top and the bottom by $\sqrt{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \times \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

Multiplying by $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}}$ does not change the value, because that
fraction equals $1$. Writing a surd with a whole-number denominator is the usual way to
give an exact answer, and Section 12.4 uses it for angles of $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and
$60^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
