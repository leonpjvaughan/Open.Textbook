# 12.3 Radians and degrees — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the circumference of a circle of radius $5$ cm, in terms of $\pi$.
2. In a right triangle the hypotenuse is $10$ cm and an angle is $40^\circ$. Find the
   side opposite that angle, correct to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $C = 2\pi r = 10\pi$ cm.
2. $10\sin 40^\circ = 6.43$ cm. The opposite and the hypotenuse give the sine ratio.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Change $120^\circ$ into radians, giving the answer as a multiple of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$120 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$120 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{120\pi}{180} = \frac{2\pi}{3}$$

Divide the $120$ and the $180$ by $60$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Change $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ radians into degrees.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{5\pi}{6} \times \dfrac{180}{\pi}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{5\pi}{6} \times \frac{180}{\pi} = \frac{5 \times 180}{6} = 150^\circ$$

The $\pi$ cancels, so the answer is exact.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A sector has radius $8$ cm and angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ radians. Find the arc length,
giving the answer as a multiple of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is in radians, so use $l = r\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$l = 8 \times \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$l = 8 \times \frac{\pi}{4} = 2\pi \approx 6.28 \text{ cm}$$

The sector is one eighth of the circle, and one eighth of the circumference $16\pi$ is
$2\pi$. That checks the answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A sector has radius $8$ cm and angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ radians. Find the area of the
sector, giving the answer as a multiple of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 64 \times \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 64 \times \frac{\pi}{4} = 8\pi \approx 25.13 \text{ cm}^2$$

The whole circle has area $64\pi$, and one eighth of that is $8\pi$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A sector has radius $9$ cm and angle $40^\circ$. Find the arc length, correct to
two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is in degrees, so use the form with $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$l = 2\pi \times 9 \times \dfrac{40}{360}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$l = 2\pi \times 9 \times \frac{40}{360} = 2\pi \approx 6.28 \text{ cm}$$

The $2\pi$ and the $\dfrac{40}{360}$ combine to give exactly $2\pi$. Leaving the answer
as $2\pi$ is exact; $6.28$ cm is the decimal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A radar sweeps through $48^\circ$. Change this angle into radians, correct to two
decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The exact answer is a multiple of $\pi$. Divide by $\pi$ at the end for the decimal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{48\pi}{180} = \dfrac{4\pi}{15}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{4\pi}{15} = 0.84 \text{ radians (to 2 d.p.)}$$

Keep the exact form $\dfrac{4\pi}{15}$ unless the question asks for a decimal. The
decimal loses precision, and a later step would carry the error forward.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 12.1

**7.** In a right triangle the side opposite $\theta$ is $5$ cm and the hypotenuse is
$13$ cm. Find $\theta$, correct to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Opposite and hypotenuse give the sine ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin\theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \sin^{-1}\left(\frac{5}{13}\right) = 22.6^\circ$$

The answer is in degrees, so the calculator must be in **DEG** mode. If you want the
answer in radians it is $0.39$ radians, and the calculator must be in **RAD** mode. The
number alone is not an answer until the unit is named.

</details>
</details>
</details>
