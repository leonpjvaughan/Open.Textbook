# 12.3 Radians and degrees — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 12.1 — the trigonometric ratios; the circumference and area
of a circle from Chapter 3.

**Objectives:** convert between degrees and radians; find the length of an arc; find the
area of a sector.

By the end of this section you can measure an angle two ways and use either measure to
find an arc length or a sector area.

## Two ways to measure an angle

A degree is one three-hundred-and-sixtieth of a full turn. That number comes from
Babylonian arithmetic, and it has no mathematical reason behind it. A **radian** does.

> **One radian** is the angle at the centre of a circle that cuts off an arc equal in
> length to the radius.

The circumference of a circle is $2\pi r$, which is $2\pi$ radii. So a full turn is
$2\pi$ radians. Half a turn is $\pi$ radians, and half a turn is also $180^\circ$.

> **The conversion.**
>
>
> $$\pi \text{ radians} = 180^\circ$$

To change degrees into radians, multiply by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$. To change radians into
degrees, multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$.

**Example 1.** Change $60^\circ$ into radians.

$$60 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{3}$$

**Example 2.** Change $\dfrac{3\pi}{4}$ radians into degrees.

$$\frac{3\pi}{4} \times \frac{180}{\pi} = \frac{3 \times 180}{4} = 135^\circ$$

Keep the answer as a multiple of $\pi$ when you can. It is exact, and $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$
is easier to read back than $1.047$.

The angles you will use most often are worth knowing by sight:

| Degrees | $0^\circ$ | $30^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ | $180^\circ$ | $360^\circ$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radians | $0$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ | $\pi$ | $2\pi$ |

## Arc length and sector area

A **sector** is a slice of a circle, like a slice of cake. Its curved edge is an **arc**.

The Formulae and Tables booklet gives both formulas, and it gives each one twice — once
for radians and once for degrees.

> **With the angle in radians.** For a circle of radius $r$ and a sector angle $\theta$,
>
>
> $$l = r\theta \qquad A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$$
>
>
>
> Here $l$ is the arc length and $A$ is the area of the sector.

**Example 3.** A sector has radius $6$ cm and angle $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ radians. Find the
arc length and the area.

$$l = 6 \times \frac{\pi}{3} = 2\pi \approx 6.28 \text{ cm}$$

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 36 \times \frac{\pi}{3} = 6\pi \approx 18.85 \text{ cm}^2$$

**Example 4.** A sector has radius $10$ cm and angle $72^\circ$. Find the arc length and
the area.

Use the degree forms from the booklet. The angle is $\dfrac{72}{360}$ of a full turn.

$$l = 2\pi r \times \frac{\theta}{360} = 2\pi \times 10 \times \frac{72}{360} = 4\pi \approx 12.57 \text{ cm}$$

$$A = \pi r^2 \times \frac{\theta}{360} = \pi \times 100 \times \frac{72}{360} = 20\pi \approx 62.83 \text{ cm}^2$$

Both answers are multiples of $\pi$, which is what an exact answer looks like here.

> **Watch out.** The short formulas $l = r\theta$ and $A = \tfrac12 r^2\theta$ work only
> when $\theta$ is in **radians**. Put $60$ into $l = r\theta$ and you get an arc sixty
> times too long.
>
> **Re-check:** decide the unit before you choose the formula. If the angle is in
> degrees, use the form with $\dfrac{\theta}{360}$. If it is in radians, use the short
> form.

## Radians and the calculator

Your calculator has a **DEG** and a **RAD** setting. It must match the unit of the angle
you are using.

- In DEG mode, $\sin 30 = 0.5$.
- In RAD mode, $\sin\left(\dfrac{\pi}{6}\right) = 0.5$.

Both statements are true, and each is false in the other mode. Check the display before
you start a question.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Two angles in it are worth writing in radians.

The radar at the Coast Guard station sweeps from north round to the yacht, which is a
turn of $48^\circ$. Change $48^\circ$ into radians, giving the exact answer as a
multiple of $\pi$ and a decimal to two places.

The depth of the tide is modelled by $d = 1.4 + 1.2\sin(30^\circ t)$. The angle
$30^\circ$ is the amount the tide phase advances each hour. Change it into radians.

Keep both answers. Section 12.5 uses the second one to find the period of the tide, and
Section 12.9 uses it to solve the depth equation.

## You should now be able to

- say what a radian is, and state that $\pi$ radians equals $180^\circ$;
- convert an angle in either direction, exactly as a multiple of $\pi$ or as a decimal;
- find an arc length and a sector area with the radian formulas;
- find an arc length and a sector area with the degree formulas;
- set the calculator to match the unit you are using.
