# 12.8 Trigonometric identities — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the exact values of $\sin 30^\circ$ and $\cos 30^\circ$.
2. Make $x$ the subject of $y = 2x - 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. These are the
   exact values from Section 12.4. If they did not come back at once, look again at the
   table of special angles there.
2. $x = \dfrac{y + 5}{2}$. Add $5$ to both sides, then divide by $2$. The same
   rearranging is what isolates $\cos A$ in the identity.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Given that $\sin A = 0.8$ and $A$ is acute, find $\cos A$ and $\tan A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The identity links the sine and the cosine. The tangent is their ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos^2A = 1 - 0.8^2 = 1 - 0.64 = 0.36$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A \text{ is acute, so } \cos A = 0.6$$

$$\tan A = \frac{\sin A}{\cos A} = \frac{0.8}{0.6} = \frac{4}{3} = 1.33 \text{ (to 2 d.p.)}$$

The identity gives the size of the cosine. The acute angle gives the positive sign.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Given that $\cos A = \dfrac{5}{13}$ and $A$ is acute, find $\sin A$ and $\tan A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the square of the cosine from $1$ to find the square of the sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin^2A = 1 - \left(\dfrac{5}{13}\right)^2 = 1 - \dfrac{25}{169} = \dfrac{144}{169}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A \text{ is acute, so } \sin A = \frac{12}{13}$$

$$\tan A = \frac{\sin A}{\cos A} = \frac{12/13}{5/13} = \frac{12}{5} = 2.4$$

The fraction $\dfrac{12/13}{5/13}$ simplifies because the $13$s cancel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Given that $\sin A = 0.6$ and $A$ is obtuse, find $\cos A$ and $\tan A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The identity gives the size. The obtuse angle decides the sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos^2A = 1 - 0.36 = 0.64$, so $\cos A = \pm 0.8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An obtuse angle lies in the second quadrant, where the cosine is negative.

$$\cos A = -0.8$$

$$\tan A = \frac{0.6}{-0.8} = -0.75$$

The negative cosine is what makes the tangent negative as well.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** $A$ and $B$ are acute, with $\sin A = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\cos B = \dfrac{5}{13}$. Find
$\sin(A + B)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The formula needs all four values. Find the two that are missing first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From the identity, $\cos A = \dfrac{4}{5}$ and $\sin B = \dfrac{12}{13}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin(A + B) = \sin A\cos B + \cos A\sin B$$

$$= \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{5}{13} + \frac{4}{5} \times \frac{12}{13} = \frac{15}{65} + \frac{48}{65} = \frac{63}{65}$$

The answer is close to $1$, so $A + B$ is close to $90^\circ$. That fits two acute
angles that are both fairly large.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Given that $\cos A = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $A$ is acute, find $\sin 2A$ and $\cos 2A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The double angle formulae need both $\sin A$ and $\cos A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From the identity, $\sin A = \dfrac{4}{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin 2A = 2\sin A\cos A = 2 \times \frac{4}{5} \times \frac{3}{5} = \frac{24}{25}$$

$$\cos 2A = \cos^2A - \sin^2A = \frac{9}{25} - \frac{16}{25} = -\frac{7}{25}$$

Check with the other form: $1 - 2\sin^2A = 1 - \dfrac{32}{25} = -\dfrac{7}{25}$. Both
agree. The cosine is negative, so $2A$ is obtuse.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Show that $(\sin A + \cos A)^2 = 1 + 2\sin A\cos A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Expand the bracket, then look for the identity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(\sin A + \cos A)^2 = \sin^2A + 2\sin A\cos A + \cos^2A$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Expand the square:

$$(\sin A + \cos A)^2 = \sin^2A + 2\sin A\cos A + \cos^2A$$

Group the first and last terms, and use $\sin^2A + \cos^2A = 1$:

$$= 1 + 2\sin A\cos A$$

The identity is true for every angle, because $\sin^2A + \cos^2A = 1$ is.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The angle at the Coast Guard station is $42^\circ$, and $\sin 42^\circ = 0.6691$.
Use the identity to find $\cos 42^\circ$, then find $\tan 42^\circ$. Give each to four
decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is acute, so the cosine is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos^2 42^\circ = 1 - 0.6691^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\cos^2 42^\circ = 1 - 0.4477 = 0.5523$$

$$\cos 42^\circ = \sqrt{0.5523} = 0.7432$$

$$\tan 42^\circ = \frac{\sin 42^\circ}{\cos 42^\circ} = \frac{0.6691}{0.7432} = 0.9003$$

A calculator agrees with both values. Keep them: the Review uses them to check the
triangle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** Make $t$ the subject of $v = u + at$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The term with $t$ is added to $u$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $u$ from both sides: $v - u = at$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v - u = at \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = \frac{v - u}{a}$$

Rearranging is the same skill as making $\cos A$ or $\sin A$ the subject of an
identity. In both cases, undo the operations one at a time.

</details>
</details>
</details>
