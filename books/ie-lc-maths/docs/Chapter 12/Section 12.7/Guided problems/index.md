# 12.7 Area of a triangle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Change $135^\circ$ into radians, giving the answer as a multiple of $\pi$.
2. In a triangle, $A = 45^\circ$, $B = 60^\circ$ and $a = 10$ cm. Find $b$, correct to two
   decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $135 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{3\pi}{4}$.
2. $\dfrac{b}{\sin 60^\circ} = \dfrac{10}{\sin 45^\circ}$, so
   $b = \dfrac{10 \times 0.8660}{0.7071} = 12.25$ cm.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A triangle has base $14$ cm and perpendicular height $6$ cm. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\tfrac12 \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A = \tfrac12 \times 14 \times 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \tfrac12 \times 14 \times 6 = 42 \text{ cm}^2$$

The height must be perpendicular to the base. A slanted side is not the height.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A triangle has $a = 9$ cm, $b = 12$ cm and $C = 40^\circ$. Find its area, correct to
two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $A = \tfrac12 ab\sin C$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A = \tfrac12 \times 9 \times 12 \times \sin 40^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 54 \times 0.6428 = 34.71 \text{ cm}^2$$

The angle $C$ is between the sides $a$ and $b$, which is what the formula needs.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A triangle has $b = 7$ cm, $c = 10$ cm and $A = 120^\circ$. Find its area, correct
to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is obtuse. The formula still works.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A = \tfrac12 \times 7 \times 10 \times \sin 120^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 35 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 30.31 \text{ cm}^2$$

$\sin 120^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is positive, so the area is positive. A sine is
never negative for an angle between $0^\circ$ and $180^\circ$, which is why the formula
works for an obtuse triangle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A triangle has sides of $6$ cm, $8$ cm and $10$ cm. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

All three sides are known and no angle is given, so use Heron's formula.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$s = \dfrac{6 + 8 + 10}{2} = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \sqrt{12 \times 6 \times 4 \times 2} = \sqrt{576} = 24 \text{ cm}^2$$

This is a right triangle, because $6^2 + 8^2 = 10^2$, so the area is also
$\tfrac12 \times 6 \times 8 = 24$. The two methods agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A parallelogram has adjacent sides of $8$ cm and $5$ cm, and the angle between them
is $65^\circ$. Find its area, correct to two decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A parallelogram is two triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A = ab\sin C = 8 \times 5 \times \sin 65^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 40 \times 0.9063 = 36.25 \text{ cm}^2$$

That is twice the triangle with the same two sides and angle:
$\tfrac12 \times 8 \times 5 \times \sin 65^\circ = 18.13$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A Coast Guard station and a lighthouse are $8$ km apart. The distance from the
lighthouse to a yacht is $5.47$ km, and the angle at the lighthouse is $60^\circ$. Find
the area of the triangle formed by the three, correct to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two known sides meet at the lighthouse, so the angle between them is the one at the
lighthouse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A = \tfrac12 \times 8 \times 5.47 \times \sin 60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 21.88 \times 0.8660 = 18.9 \text{ km}^2$$

This is the search area from the chapter problem. The rescue team searches about
$19$ km$^2$ of sea.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 12.6

**7.** In a triangle, $a = 7$ cm, $b = 9$ cm and $C = 80^\circ$. Find the side $c$, correct
to two decimal places, and then find the area of the triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two sides and the angle between them give $c$ by the cosine rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c^2 = 49 + 81 - 2(7)(9)\cos 80^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c^2 = 130 - 126 \times 0.1736 = 130 - 21.88 = 108.12 \qquad c = 10.40 \text{ cm}$$

The same two sides and angle give the area directly:

$$A = \tfrac12 \times 7 \times 9 \times \sin 80^\circ = 31.5 \times 0.9848 = 31.02 \text{ cm}^2$$

Both parts use the same three numbers, which is why one diagram answers both questions.

</details>
</details>
</details>
