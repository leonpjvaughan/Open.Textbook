# Chapter 12: Trigonometry

Trigonometry begins with one fact: in a right triangle, the ratio of two sides depends
only on the angle between them. That single fact lets you find a height you cannot
reach, a distance you cannot walk and a direction you cannot see. This chapter extends
the idea to angles of any size, to triangles with no right angle, and to the equations
that describe anything which repeats.

## The chapter problem

**The stranded yacht.** A yacht has gone aground on a sandbank off the west coast. The
rescue needs two answers: **where** the yacht is, and **when** the water will be deep
enough to float it.

**Where.** The Coast Guard station $C$ and the lighthouse $L$ are $8$ km apart on a
straight stretch of coast, with $L$ due east of $C$. The station stands on a cliff
$60$ m above the sea.

- From $C$, the bearing of the yacht $Y$ is $048^\circ$.
- From $L$, the bearing of $Y$ is $330^\circ$.

**When.** The depth of water over the sandbank, $t$ hours after low water, is

$$d = 1.4 + 1.2\sin(30^\circ t)$$

metres. The yacht floats once the depth reaches $2.0$ m.

**First contact — no working yet.** The yacht looks nearer to the lighthouse than to
the station. Write down which one you think is nearer, and estimate the angle at the
lighthouse between the two lines of sight. Keep both answers, and check them later.

By the end of the chapter you can find both distances, the area of sea the yacht could
be in, and the two times either side of high water when it floats.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- name the opposite, the adjacent and the hypotenuse, and choose the right ratio;
- find a side or an angle of a right triangle with sine, cosine or tangent;
- use Pythagoras' theorem, and test whether a triangle is right-angled;
- convert between degrees and radians, and find an arc length and a sector area;
- find the sine, cosine and tangent of any angle from the unit circle;
- sketch and read the graphs of the sine and cosine functions;
- solve a triangle with the sine rule or the cosine rule;
- find the area of a triangle from two sides and the angle between them;
- use the trigonometric identities, including the addition and double angle formulae;
- solve a trigonometric equation and give all the solutions in a range;
- work with bearings, and with a problem in three dimensions.

## Where it is used

Surveying and navigation, where a bearing and a distance fix a position. Engineering,
where a slope, a roof and a force are described by angles. Architecture, computer
graphics and animation, where every rotation is a pair of sine and cosine values.
Sound, light, tides and alternating current, which all repeat, and so are all modelled
by the sine function. Any time you need a distance you cannot measure directly, you
measure an angle instead.
