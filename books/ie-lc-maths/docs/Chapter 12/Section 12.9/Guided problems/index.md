# 12.9 Solving trigonometric equations — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. In which two quadrants is the cosine negative?
2. Write down the exact value of $\sin 30^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The second and third quadrants. The point is to the left of the $y$-axis there, so its
   $x$-coordinate, the cosine, is negative. If this was not immediate, look again at the
   sign table in Section 12.4.
2. $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$. This is one of the exact values from Section 12.4, and
   it is the reference angle for many of the equations below.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $\sin\theta = 0.5$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The calculator gives the reference angle. The sine is positive in two quadrants.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin^{-1}(0.5) = 30^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The reference angle is $30^\circ$. The sine is positive, so the solutions lie in the
first and second quadrants.

$$\theta = 30^\circ \quad\text{or}\quad \theta = 180^\circ - 30^\circ = 150^\circ$$

Check both: $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$ and $\sin 150^\circ = 0.5$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $\cos\theta = -0.5$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The negative cosine puts the solutions in the second and third quadrants.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The reference angle is $\cos^{-1}(0.5) = 60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = 180^\circ - 60^\circ = 120^\circ \quad\text{or}\quad
\theta = 180^\circ + 60^\circ = 240^\circ$$

Both angles have a cosine of $-0.5$, which is the check.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $\tan\theta = 1$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The tangent repeats every $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = 45^\circ \quad\text{or}\quad \theta = 45^\circ + 180^\circ = 225^\circ$$

The next solution would be $405^\circ$, which is outside the range.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $\sin 2\theta = 0$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Solve for $2\theta$ first, over the range $0^\circ \le 2\theta \le 720^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin x = 0$ gives $x = 0^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 360^\circ,\ 540^\circ,\ 720^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Divide each value of $x = 2\theta$ by $2$:

$$\theta = 0^\circ,\ 90^\circ,\ 180^\circ,\ 270^\circ,\ 360^\circ$$

There are five solutions, because $2\theta$ makes two full turns while $\theta$ makes
one.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $\cos 2\theta = 0.5$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The cosine is positive, so each full turn of $2\theta$ gives two solutions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2\theta = 60^\circ,\ 300^\circ,\ 420^\circ,\ 660^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Divide by $2$:

$$\theta = 30^\circ,\ 150^\circ,\ 210^\circ,\ 330^\circ$$

The values $420^\circ$ and $660^\circ$ are the second-turn copies of $60^\circ$ and
$300^\circ$. They exist because $2\theta$ runs to $720^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Solve $\sin\theta = 0.5$ for $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work in radians. $\pi$ radians is $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The reference angle is $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\theta = \frac{\pi}{6} \quad\text{or}\quad \theta = \pi - \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{5\pi}{6}$$

Both are in the range $0 \le \theta \le 2\pi$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Solve $2\cos^2\theta + \sin\theta = 1$ for $0^\circ \le \theta \le 360^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\cos^2\theta = 1 - \sin^2\theta$ to leave only the sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2\sin^2\theta - \sin\theta - 1 = 0$, which factorises as
$(2\sin\theta + 1)(\sin\theta - 1) = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The two possibilities are $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin\theta = 1$.

- $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$: the reference angle is $30^\circ$, and the sine is
  negative, so $\theta = 210^\circ$ or $330^\circ$.
- $\sin\theta = 1$: $\theta = 90^\circ$.

The solutions are $90^\circ$, $210^\circ$ and $330^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**8.** The depth over the sandbank is $d = 1.4 + 1.2\sin(30^\circ t)$ metres, $t$ hours
after low water. Solve $d = 2.0$ for $0 \le t \le 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange to $\sin(30^\circ t) = 0.5$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$30^\circ t = 30^\circ$ or $30^\circ t = 150^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1.4 + 1.2\sin(30^\circ t) = 2.0$$

$$1.2\sin(30^\circ t) = 0.6$$

$$\sin(30^\circ t) = 0.5$$

The reference angle is $30^\circ$, and the sine is positive, so
$30^\circ t = 30^\circ$ or $150^\circ$. Hence $t = 1$ or $t = 5$.

The yacht floats between $t = 1$ hour and $t = 5$ hours. High water is at $t = 3$ hours,
in the middle of that interval.

</details>
</details>
</details>
