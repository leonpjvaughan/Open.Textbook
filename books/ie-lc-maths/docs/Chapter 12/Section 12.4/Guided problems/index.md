# 12.4 The unit circle and trigonometric functions of any angle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the exact value of $\tan 45^\circ$.
2. Change $150^\circ$ into radians, giving the answer as a multiple of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $1$, because at $45^\circ$ the opposite and the adjacent sides are equal.
2. $150 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** State which quadrant the angle $200^\circ$ lies in, and the sign of its sine and
cosine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$180^\circ$ is the negative $x$-axis, and $270^\circ$ is the negative $y$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$200^\circ$ is between those two, so the point is to the left of the origin and below it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$200^\circ$ lies in the third quadrant. There both coordinates are negative, so
$\sin 200^\circ < 0$ and $\cos 200^\circ < 0$.

The tangent is positive there, because the two negatives cancel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the exact value of $\sin 120^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The reference angle is $180^\circ - 120^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$120^\circ$ is in the second quadrant, where sine is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin 120^\circ = \sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$$

The sine is positive, so the point is above the $x$-axis.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the exact value of $\cos 225^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The reference angle is $225^\circ - 180^\circ = 45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$225^\circ$ is in the third quadrant, where cosine is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\cos 225^\circ = -\cos 45^\circ = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the exact value of $\tan 300^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The reference angle is $360^\circ - 300^\circ = 60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$300^\circ$ is in the fourth quadrant, where sine is negative and cosine is positive, so
the tangent is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\tan 300^\circ = -\tan 60^\circ = -\sqrt{3}$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the exact value of $\sin 90^\circ$ and $\cos 90^\circ$, and explain why
$\tan 90^\circ$ has no value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Where is the point on the unit circle when the angle is $90^\circ$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The radius points straight up, so the point is $(0, 1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin 90^\circ = 1 \qquad \cos 90^\circ = 0$$

$$\tan 90^\circ = \frac{\sin 90^\circ}{\cos 90^\circ} = \frac{1}{0}$$

Division by zero has no value, so the tangent is undefined at $90^\circ$. On the graph
the tangent curve has a vertical asymptote there, which Section 12.5 shows.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A bearing of $330^\circ$ is measured clockwise from north. Write it as an angle in
standard position, and state the signs of its sine and cosine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A bearing of $330^\circ$ points $30^\circ$ west of north. North is $90^\circ$ in standard
position.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$90^\circ + 30^\circ = 120^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The standard angle is $120^\circ$, which lies in the second quadrant. So
$\sin 120^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is positive and
$\cos 120^\circ = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ is negative.

The negative cosine says the point is to the west of the origin. That matches the
direction: a bearing of $330^\circ$ points north-west.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 12.3

**7.** A sector has radius $12$ cm and angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ radians. Find the arc
length and the area, each as a multiple of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is in radians, so use $l = r\theta$ and $A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$l = 12 \times \dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 144 \times \dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$l = 2\pi \approx 6.28 \text{ cm} \qquad A = 12\pi \approx 37.70 \text{ cm}^2$$

The sector is one twelfth of the circle: one twelfth of $24\pi$ is $2\pi$, and one
twelfth of $144\pi$ is $12\pi$. Both agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>
