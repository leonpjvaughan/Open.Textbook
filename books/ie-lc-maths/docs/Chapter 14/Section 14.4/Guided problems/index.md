# 14.4 Enlargements and the scale factor — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A triangle has base 3 cm and height 2 cm. Find its area.
2. Work out $3^2$ and $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 2 = 3$ cm². The area of a triangle is half the base times
   the height.
2. $3^2 = 9$ and $\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{4}$. Squaring multiplies a
   number by itself, and the area factor of an enlargement is $k^2$.

If the area of a triangle felt uncertain, revisit Chapter 3 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Enlarge the point $(2,3)$ by scale factor $2$ about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply both coordinates by the scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $2 \times 2$ and $2 \times 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,3) \to (4,6)$$

The image is $(4,6)$, twice as far from the origin as the object.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Enlarge the point $(6,3)$ by scale factor $\frac{1}{2}$ about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A scale factor between 0 and 1 makes the image smaller.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $6 \times \frac{1}{2}$ and $3 \times \frac{1}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(6,3) \to (3,1.5)$$

The image is $(3,1.5)$, halfway from the origin to the object.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Enlarge the point $(5,4)$ by scale factor $2$ about the centre $C(1,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find how far the point is from the centre, multiply that by 2, then add the centre back.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The displacement from $C$ is $(5-1, 4-1) = (4,3)$. Doubling gives $(8,6)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(5,4) \to (1 + 2(5 - 1),\; 1 + 2(4 - 1)) = (1 + 8,\; 1 + 6) = (9,7)$$

The image is $(9,7)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A shape has area $20$ cm². It is enlarged by scale factor $2$. Find the area of the
image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area factor is $k^2$, not $k$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $2^2 = 4$, then multiply the area by 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = 20 \times 2^2 = 20 \times 4 = 80$$

The image has area $80$ cm².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A student enlarges a shape by scale factor $3$ and multiplies its area by $3$.
Explain what went wrong, and give the correct area factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How many dimensions does an area have?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

An area has two dimensions, so the scale factor applies twice.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The student used $k$ instead of $k^2$. An area has two dimensions, so the scale factor
applies to each of them.

$$\text{area factor} = 3^2 = 9$$

A shape of area $5$ would have an image of area $45$, not $15$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Enlarge the triangle with corners $(1,1)$, $(4,1)$ and $(1,3)$ by scale factor $3$
about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply each corner by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first corner $(1,1)$ becomes $(3,3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,1) \to (3,3) \qquad (4,1) \to (12,3) \qquad (1,3) \to (3,9)$$

The image triangle has corners $(3,3)$, $(12,3)$ and $(3,9)$. Its area is $3^2 = 9$ times
the object area.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A shape has area $45$ cm². After an enlargement its image has area $5$ cm². Find
the positive scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area factor is $\frac{5}{45}$. Take the square root to get $k$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac{5}{45} = \frac{1}{9}$, and the square root of $\frac{1}{9}$ is a simple fraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area factor} = \frac{5}{45} = \frac{1}{9}$$

$$k = \sqrt{\frac{1}{9}} = \frac{1}{3}$$

The scale factor is $\frac{1}{3}$, which is between 0 and 1, so the image is smaller.
That fits: the area went down.

</details>
</details>
</details>
