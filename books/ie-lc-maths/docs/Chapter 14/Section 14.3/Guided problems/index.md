# 14.3 Central symmetry and rotation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the midpoint of the points $(2,4)$ and $(6,8)$.
2. Work out $-(3)$ and $-(-7)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(4,6)$. The midpoint averages the two $x$-coordinates and the two $y$-coordinates.
2. $-(3) = -3$ and $-(-7) = 7$. A half-turn changes the sign of both coordinates, so
   this sign work matters here.

If the midpoint felt uncertain, revisit coordinates in Chapter 1 before you carry on.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Rotate $(4,1)$ by $90^\circ$ anticlockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rule for a quarter-turn anticlockwise about the origin is $(x,y) \to (-y,x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Put $x = 4$ and $y = 1$ into $(-y,x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(4,1) \to (-1,4)$$

The image is $(-1,4)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Rotate $(2,-3)$ by $90^\circ$ clockwise about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Clockwise is the other direction, so the rule is $(x,y) \to (y,-x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Put $x = 2$ and $y = -3$ into $(y,-x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,-3) \to (-3,-2)$$

The image is $(-3,-2)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Rotate $(3,1)$ by $180^\circ$ about the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A half-turn changes the sign of both coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $(x,y) \to (-x,-y)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,1) \to (-3,-1)$$

The image is $(-3,-1)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the image of $(5,1)$ under central symmetry about $C(2,3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Central symmetry about $C(c,d)$ sends $(x,y)$ to $(2c - x,\; 2d - y)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Here $c = 2$ and $d = 3$, so work out $2 \times 2 - 5$ and $2 \times 3 - 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(5,1) \to (4 - 5,\; 6 - 1) = (-1,5)$$

The image is $(-1,5)$. The centre $C(2,3)$ is the midpoint of $(5,1)$ and $(-1,5)$,
which confirms it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the order of rotational symmetry of a square.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the smallest turn that maps the square onto itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A quarter-turn, or $90^\circ$, fits. Divide $360^\circ$ by $90^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{order} = \frac{360^\circ}{90^\circ} = 4$$

The square has rotational symmetry of order 4.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student rotates $(4,1)$ by $90^\circ$ clockwise about the origin and writes down
$(-1,4)$. Explain what went wrong, and give the correct image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which rule did the student use, and which direction does it describe?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The rule $(-y,x)$ is for a quarter-turn anticlockwise.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The student used the anticlockwise rule $(-y,x)$. Clockwise is the opposite direction,
so the rule is $(x,y) \to (y,-x)$.

$$(4,1) \to (1,-4)$$

The correct image is $(1,-4)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 12

**7.** Rotate the triangle with corners $(1,1)$, $(4,1)$ and $(1,3)$ by $180^\circ$ about
the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rotate each corner with the same rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first corner $(1,1)$ becomes $(-1,-1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,1) \to (-1,-1) \qquad (4,1) \to (-4,-1) \qquad (1,3) \to (-1,-3)$$

The image triangle has corners $(-1,-1)$, $(-4,-1)$ and $(-1,-3)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
