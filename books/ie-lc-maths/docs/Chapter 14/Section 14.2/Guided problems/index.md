# 14.2 Axial symmetry and reflection — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the coordinates of the point that is 3 units left and 4 units up from the
   origin.
2. Work out $-(-5)$ and $-(3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(-3,4)$. Left is negative on the horizontal axis, and up is positive on the
   vertical axis.
2. $-(-5) = 5$ and $-(3) = -3$. A reflection changes the sign of one coordinate, so
   this sign work is the whole arithmetic.

If either answer felt uncertain, revisit negative numbers in Chapter 1 before you carry
on.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Reflect $(3,2)$ in the $y$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$-axis is vertical, so the point stays at the same height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Keep the $y$-coordinate and change the sign of the $x$-coordinate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,2) \to (-3,2)$$

The image is $(-3,2)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Reflect $(-4,5)$ in the $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$-axis is horizontal, so the point stays at the same horizontal position.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Keep the $x$-coordinate and change the sign of the $y$-coordinate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(-4,5) \to (-4,-5)$$

The image is $(-4,-5)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Reflect $(2,5)$ in the line $y = x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The line $y = x$ runs diagonally through the origin. The two coordinates swap.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the $y$-coordinate first and the $x$-coordinate second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2,5) \to (5,2)$$

The image is $(5,2)$. The point was above the line, and the image is below it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Reflect $(5,2)$ in the line $x = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The mirror line is vertical, so only the $x$-coordinate changes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The object is 2 units to the right of the line, so the image is 2 units to its left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(5,2) \to (2 \times 3 - 5,\; 2) = (1,2)$$

The image is $(1,2)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A student reflects $(2,5)$ in the line $y = x$ and writes down $(-2,5)$. Explain
what went wrong, and give the correct image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Did the student swap the coordinates, or change one sign?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The student used the rule for the $y$-axis instead of the rule for $y = x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The student changed the sign of the $x$-coordinate, which is the rule for reflection in
the $y$-axis. The line $y = x$ swaps the two coordinates instead.

$$(2,5) \to (5,2)$$

The correct image is $(5,2)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Reflect the triangle with corners $(1,1)$, $(4,1)$ and $(1,3)$ in the $y$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Reflect each corner with the same rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first corner $(1,1)$ becomes $(-1,1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(1,1) \to (-1,1) \qquad (4,1) \to (-4,1) \qquad (1,3) \to (-1,3)$$

The image triangle has corners $(-1,1)$, $(-4,1)$ and $(-1,3)$. It is congruent to the
object, but its orientation is reversed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 12

**7.** A shape is drawn on a coordinate grid and then reflected in the line $y = -x$.
A corner at $(3,-2)$ maps to an image corner. Find the image, and say what happens to
the shape's angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rule for $y = -x$ swaps the coordinates and changes both signs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Start from $(x,y) \to (-y,-x)$, then substitute $x = 3$ and $y = -2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3,-2) \to (2,-3)$$

The image corner is $(2,-3)$. A reflection leaves every angle unchanged, so the shape's
angles are the same after the reflection.

</details>
</details>
</details>
