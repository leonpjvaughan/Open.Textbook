# Chapter 14: Transformation geometry

A **transformation** moves a shape by a rule. Some rules slide a shape, some turn it,
some flip it, and some change its size. This chapter is about the rules themselves, and
about one question that matters in every one of them: what stays the same?

## The chapter problem: the tiled floor

A workshop makes a floor panel from one triangular tile. The tile has corners

$$A(1,1), \qquad B(4,1), \qquad C(1,3).$$

Four copies of the tile form a border around the panel:

- one copy is the tile **translated** by the vector $\binom{5}{0}$;
- one copy is the tile **reflected** in the $y$-axis;
- one copy is the tile under **central symmetry** about the origin, which is a half-turn;
- one copy is the tile **rotated** by $90^\circ$ anticlockwise about the origin.

The centre of the panel carries an **enlarged** copy, with scale factor $3$ about the
origin.

**First contact — no working yet.** The enlarged tile is three times as wide as the
original. Predict how many original tiles would cover it. Write your guess down, and keep
it. You will check it in Section 14.4.

By the end of the chapter you can write down the corners of all five copies. You can
also say which copies are exactly the same size and shape as the original, and prove how
many original tiles fit inside the enlarged one.

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- describe a translation by its vector and find the image of a point or a shape;
- reflect a point or a shape in the $x$-axis, the $y$-axis, the line $y = x$ and a line
  such as $x = 3$;
- find the image under a rotation, and under central symmetry about a point;
- enlarge a shape about a centre with a scale factor, and work out the effect on area;
- say what each transformation preserves, and use that to decide whether two shapes are
  congruent or only similar;
- compose two transformations in order, and describe the single transformation that has
  the same effect.

## Where it is used

Textiles, wallpaper and tiling, where one motif is repeated by sliding, turning and
reflecting. Architecture and engineering, where a design is built from one unit and its
images. Computer graphics and animation, where every sprite that moves, turns or grows
is running a transformation. Crystallography and the study of materials, where the
symmetry of a pattern reveals the structure of the solid.

## Your turn to connect it

Where have you seen a pattern that repeats? A tiled floor, a wallpaper, a fabric, the
pattern on a football, the layout of a car park, or a logo built from one shape.

Choose one. Write down how the pattern repeats — does it slide, turn, flip, or grow? You
will be able to name the transformation and describe it precisely by the end of the
chapter.
