# 14.5 Transformations and symmetry in the plane — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A translation takes the point $(1,1)$ to $(4,5)$. Find the translation vector.
2. Find the area factor of an enlargement with scale factor $4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(4-1,\; 5-1) = (3,4)$, so the vector is $\binom{3}{4}$.
2. The area factor is $4^2 = 16$. An area has two dimensions, so the scale factor
   applies twice.

If either answer felt uncertain, revisit Sections 14.1 and 14.4 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A single transformation takes $(3,4)$ to $(-3,4)$. Name the transformation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$-coordinate is unchanged, and the $x$-coordinate has changed sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Which mirror line leaves a point at the same height and swaps its side?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The $x$-coordinate changed sign and the $y$-coordinate did not, so the transformation is
a reflection in the $y$-axis.

$$(x,y) \to (-x,y)$$

Check: $(-1) \times 3 = -3$ and $4$ stays $4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A single transformation takes $(3,4)$ to $(4,3)$. Name the transformation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two coordinates have swapped places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Which diagonal mirror line swaps the coordinates?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The coordinates swapped, so the transformation is a reflection in the line $y = x$.

$$(x,y) \to (y,x)$$

Check: $4$ becomes the new $x$-coordinate and $3$ becomes the new $y$-coordinate.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A single transformation takes $(3,4)$ to $(-3,-4)$. Name the transformation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both coordinates changed sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Which transformation sends every point to the opposite side of the origin?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both signs changed, so the transformation is a rotation of $180^\circ$ about the origin.
It is also called central symmetry about the origin.

$$(x,y) \to (-x,-y)$$

Check: $-3$ and $-4$ are the two signs changed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the order of rotational symmetry of a regular octagon.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the smallest turn that maps the octagon onto itself, then divide $360^\circ$ by it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A regular octagon has 8 equal sides, so the smallest fitting turn is $360^\circ \div 8 =
45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{order} = \frac{360^\circ}{45^\circ} = 8$$

The regular octagon has rotational symmetry of order 8.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two triangles have side lengths $3$, $4$, $5$ cm and $6$, $8$, $10$ cm. Are they
congruent, similar, or neither? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare each matching pair of sides by dividing.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\frac{6}{3}$, $\frac{8}{4}$ and $\frac{10}{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{6}{3} = 2 \qquad \frac{8}{4} = 2 \qquad \frac{10}{5} = 2$$

Every matching side is in the same ratio, so the triangles are similar with scale factor
$2$. They are not congruent, because the sizes are different.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A parallelogram that is not a rectangle has rotational symmetry of order 2 and no
axis of symmetry. Explain why.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A half-turn fits. Does any reflection fit?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Try reflecting the parallelogram in a line through its centre and see whether it lands on
itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A half-turn about the centre sends each corner to the opposite corner, so the
parallelogram lands on itself. That gives rotational symmetry of order 2.

No reflection works. A reflection in either diagonal would swap two unequal sides, and a
reflection in a line through the midpoints of opposite sides would move the sloping sides
to the wrong place. So there is no axis of symmetry.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A rectangle is $6$ cm by $4$ cm. It is enlarged by scale factor $3$. Find the
lengths of the image rectangle and the area of the image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply each length by 3, and multiply the area by $3^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The object area is $6 \times 4 = 24$ cm².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The image is $18$ cm by $12$ cm, because each length is multiplied by 3.

$$\text{area} = 24 \times 3^2 = 24 \times 9 = 216 \text{ cm}^2$$

The image has area $216$ cm². Check by multiplying the image sides: $18 \times 12 = 216$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
