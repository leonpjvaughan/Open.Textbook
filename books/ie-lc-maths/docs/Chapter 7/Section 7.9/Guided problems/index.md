# 7.9 Curve sketching — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Factorise $x^2 - 4$.
2. Find the y-intercept of $y = x^3 - 3x + 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(x - 2)(x + 2)$. A factorised form shows the $x$-intercepts straight away.
2. Put $x = 0$: $y = 2$. The $y$-intercept is where the curve meets the vertical axis.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the $y$-intercept of $y = x^3 - 4x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $x = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y = 0 - 0 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The $y$-intercept is $0$, so the curve passes through the origin.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the $x$-intercepts of $y = x^3 - 4x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Factorise $x^3 - 4x$ by taking out $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x(x^2 - 4) = x(x - 2)(x + 2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The $x$-intercepts are $x = 0$, $x = 2$ and $x = -2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the stationary points of $y = x^3 - 3x$ and classify them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 3$ and $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 6x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = \pm 1$; the second derivative is $-6$ at $x = -1$ and $6$ at $x = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$(-1, 2)$ is a local maximum and $(1, -2)$ is a local minimum.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the point of inflection of $y = x^3 - 3x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set the second derivative $6x$ to zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = 0$, and $y = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The point of inflection is $(0, 0)$. The second derivative changes sign there, so the
concavity changes from down to up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** For the tank, $V(t) = 12t^2 - t^3$, find $V(4)$ and $V(8)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute both values into the rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$V(4) = 12(16) - 64$ and $V(8) = 12(64) - 512$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$V(4) = 192 - 64 = 128$ litres and $V(8) = 768 - 512 = 256$ litres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** For the tank, find the point of inflection and say what it means for the rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set $V''(t) = 24 - 6t$ to zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$t = 4$, and $V(4) = 128$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The point of inflection is $(4, 128)$. The rate $V'(t) = 24t - 3t^2$ is greatest there, so
the volume graph is steepest at its inflection point. Before $t = 4$ the rate is increasing;
after it, the rate is decreasing.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Sketch $y = x^3 - 3x$, marking the intercepts, the stationary points and the point of
inflection.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Collect the answers from questions 2, 3 and 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Intercepts at $-\sqrt{3}$, $0$ and $\sqrt{3}$; maximum at $(-1, 2)$; minimum at $(1, -2)$;
inflection at $(0, 0)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The curve falls from the bottom left, crosses the $x$-axis near $x = -1.73$, reaches a local
maximum at $(-1, 2)$, passes through the origin where it changes concavity, reaches a local
minimum at $(1, -2)$, crosses the axis near $x = 1.73$, and rises to the top right.

The $x$-intercepts are at $\pm\sqrt{3} \approx \pm 1.73$, which sit just outside the
stationary points at $x = \pm 1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 6

**8.** The graph of $y = (x - 1)^2$ is a parabola. State its vertex and its
$x$-intercept.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The completed-square form shows the vertex directly.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The smallest value of $(x - 1)^2$ is $0$, when $x = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The vertex is $(1, 0)$, which is also the only $x$-intercept. It is a minimum, because the
square cannot be negative.

Differentiating gives the same vertex: $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 2(x - 1) = 0$ at $x = 1$, and
$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 2 > 0$ confirms a minimum. Chapter 6 found the vertex by completing
the square, and this chapter finds it by differentiating.

</details>
</details>
</details>
