# 7.7 Rates of change in context — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Differentiate $y = 3t^2 - 12t + 9$.
2. Work out the value of $3t^2 - 12t + 9$ when $t = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = 6t - 12$.
2. $3(9) - 12(3) + 9 = 27 - 36 + 9 = 0$.

The first is a rate of change, and the second is a value. Keep the two ideas apart
throughout this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A car travels so that its distance after $t$ seconds is $s(t) = 4t^2$ metres. Find its
velocity $s'(t)$ and its value at $t = 3$ seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Velocity is the derivative of distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$s'(t) = 8t$, so $s'(3) = 24$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s'(t) = 8t \text{ metres per second} \qquad s'(3) = 24 \text{ metres per second}$$

The car is travelling at $24$ metres per second at $t = 3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the same car, work out the average velocity over the first three seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the total distance by the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$s(3) = 4(9) = 36$ metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{36 - 0}{3} = 12 \text{ metres per second}$$

The average is $12$ and the instantaneous velocity at $t = 3$ is $24$. The car is speeding
up, so the instantaneous value at the end is larger.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The tank volume is $V(t) = 12t^2 - t^3$ litres. Work out the instantaneous rate
$V'(4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Differentiate, then substitute $t = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$V'(t) = 24t - 3t^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V'(4) = 96 - 48 = 48 \text{ litres per minute}$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** For the tank, work out the average rate over the first four minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\dfrac{V(4) - V(0)}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$V(4) = 128$ and $V(0) = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{128 - 0}{4} = 32 \text{ litres per minute}$$

The average rate is $32$, which is less than the instantaneous rate of $48$ at $t = 4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A square has side $x$ cm and area $A = x^2$. Find $\dfrac{dA}{dx}$ and its value when
$x = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Differentiate the area with respect to the side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{dA}{dx} = 2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{dA}{dx} = 2x \qquad \frac{dA}{dx}\bigg|_{x=5} = 10 \text{ square centimetres per
centimetre}$$

The value $2x$ is the perimeter of the square. Increasing the side by a tiny amount adds a
thin L-shaped strip whose area is the perimeter times the increase.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A population after $t$ years is $P(t) = 500 + 40t - t^2$. Find the rate of change of
the population at $t = 5$ years.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Differentiate and substitute $t = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$P'(t) = 40 - 2t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P'(5) = 40 - 10 = 30 \text{ people per year}$$

The population is growing at $30$ people per year at that moment.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** For the tank, state the average rate over the first four minutes and the
instantaneous rate at $t = 4$, with units, and say which is larger.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the two answers from questions 3 and 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The average is $32$ litres per minute and the instantaneous rate is $48$ litres per minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The average rate is $32$ litres per minute, and the instantaneous rate at $t = 4$ is
$48$ litres per minute. The instantaneous rate is larger, so the tank is filling more
quickly at the end of the interval than on average.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 6

**8.** The cooling coffee has temperature $T(t) = 20 + 70(0.5)^{t/5}$. Work out the average
rate of cooling between $t = 5$ and $t = 10$ minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T(5) = 55$ and $T(10) = 37.5$ from Chapter 6.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The change is $37.5 - 55 = -17.5$ over $5$ minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{37.5 - 55}{5} = \frac{-17.5}{5} = -3.5^\circ C \text{ per minute}$$

The coffee cools at an average of $3.5^\circ C$ per minute over those five minutes. The rate
is not constant, and it is slower than the average over the first five minutes, because the
coffee cools quickly at first and then more slowly.

</details>
</details>
</details>
