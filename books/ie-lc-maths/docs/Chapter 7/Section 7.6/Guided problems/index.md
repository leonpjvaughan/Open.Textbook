# 7.6 The second derivative and concavity — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Differentiate $y = 6x^2 - 8x + 1$.
2. Differentiate $y = x^5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12x - 8$.
2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 5x^4$.

The second derivative is found by differentiating each of these answers once more.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ for $y = x^3 + 2x^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Differentiate once, then differentiate that answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 + 4x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 6x + 4$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ for $y = 2x^4 - x^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first derivative is $8x^3 - 2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Differentiate $8x^3 - 2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 24x^2 - 2$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find $\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2}$ for $y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first derivative is $3x^2 - 12x + 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Differentiate $3x^2 - 12x + 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 6x - 12$$

This is negative for $x < 2$ and positive for $x > 2$, so the curve changes concavity at
$x = 2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** For $y = x^3 - 3x$, find the second derivative and say where the curve is concave up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Concave up means the second derivative is positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 6x$, so solve $6x > 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{d^2y}{dx^2} = 6x > 0 \quad \text{when} \quad x > 0$$

The curve is concave up for $x > 0$ and concave down for $x < 0$. The origin is a point of
inflection.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** The tank rate is $V'(t) = 24t - 3t^2$. Find $V''(t)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Differentiate the rate with respect to $t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The derivative of $24t$ is $24$, and the derivative of $-3t^2$ is $-6t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V''(t) = 24 - 6t$$

This measures how fast the filling rate is changing.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** For the tank, work out $V''(4)$ and say what it means.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $t = 4$ into $V''(t) = 24 - 6t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$V''(4) = 24 - 24 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$V''(4) = 0$, so the rate is neither rising nor falling at $t = 4$. Just before $t = 4$ the
second derivative is positive, so the rate is still rising. Just after, it is negative, so
the rate is falling. The rate therefore reaches a maximum at $t = 4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** For $y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x$, find the point of inflection.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set the second derivative to zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6x - 12 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$x = 2$, and $y = 8 - 24 + 18 = 2$. The point of inflection is $(2, 2)$. The concavity
changes from down to up there, because the second derivative changes sign.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** Solve $6x - 12 = 0$, and say what the solution tells you about the curve
$y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange the linear equation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6x = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$x = 2$. Since $6x - 12$ is the second derivative of $y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 9x$, the solution
$x = 2$ is where the concavity can change. It is the point of inflection, provided the
second derivative really does change sign there. Checking the sign on each side is what
confirms it.

</details>
</details>
</details>
