# 7.3 The product rule — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Expand $(x + 4)(x - 3)$.
2. Differentiate $y = x^3 - 5x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x^2 + x - 12$. Multiplying the brackets gives four terms, and two of them combine.
2. $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 5$. Both of these are needed when a product is checked by
   expanding instead.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Differentiate $y = (x + 2)(x + 5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write down $u$, $v$, $u'$ and $v'$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$u = x + 2$, $u' = 1$, $v = x + 5$, $v' = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y' = 1(x + 5) + (x + 2)(1) = 2x + 7$$

Expanding first gives $x^2 + 7x + 10$, whose derivative is $2x + 7$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Differentiate $y = (3x + 1)(x - 4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The derivative of the first factor is $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y' = 3(x - 4) + (3x + 1)(1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y' = 3x - 12 + 3x + 1 = 6x - 11$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Differentiate $y = x^2(x + 6)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take $u = x^2$ and $v = x + 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y' = 2x(x + 6) + x^2(1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y' = 2x^2 + 12x + x^2 = 3x^2 + 12x$$

Expanding first gives $x^3 + 6x^2$, whose derivative is $3x^2 + 12x$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Differentiate $y = (2x - 1)(x^2 + 3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$u' = 2$ and $v' = 2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y' = 2(x^2 + 3) + (2x - 1)(2x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y' = 2x^2 + 6 + 4x^2 - 2x = 6x^2 - 2x + 6$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Differentiate $y = (x^2 + 2)(x - 1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take $u = x^2 + 2$ and $v = x - 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y' = 2x(x - 1) + (x^2 + 2)(1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y' = 2x^2 - 2x + x^2 + 2 = 3x^2 - 2x + 2$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Differentiate $y = (4x + 3)(2x - 5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both factors are linear, so both derivatives are constants.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y' = 4(2x - 5) + (4x + 3)(2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y' = 8x - 20 + 8x + 6 = 16x - 14$$

Expanding first gives $8x^2 - 14x - 15$, whose derivative is $16x - 14$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** The tank volume is $V(t) = t^2(12 - t)$ litres. Find $V'(t)$ and work out $V'(4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take $u = t^2$ and $v = 12 - t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$V'(t) = 2t(12 - t) + t^2(-1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V'(t) = 24t - 2t^2 - t^2 = 24t - 3t^2 \qquad V'(4) = 96 - 48 = 48$$

The rate is $48$ litres per minute at $t = 4$, matching Section 7.2.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** Expand $(2x + 1)(3x - 4)$ and use your expansion to find the gradient of the curve
$y = (2x + 1)(3x - 4)$ at $x = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply out first, then differentiate the quadratic.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(2x + 1)(3x - 4) = 6x^2 - 5x - 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The expansion is $6x^2 - 5x - 4$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx} = 12x - 5$. At $x = 1$ the gradient is
$7$.

The product rule gives the same value: $2(3x - 4) + (2x + 1)(3) = 12x - 5$. Expanding is
possible here because both factors are simple. The product rule is needed when they are not.

</details>
</details>
</details>
