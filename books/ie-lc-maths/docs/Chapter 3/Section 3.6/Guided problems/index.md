# 3.6 Similar figures: length, area and volume ratios — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the volume of a cylinder of radius $3$ cm and height $5$ cm.
2. Find the surface area of a cuboid $2$ cm by $4$ cm by $5$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $V = \pi \times 3^2 \times 5 = 45\pi \approx 141.37 \text{ cm}^3$.
2. $A = 2(8 + 10 + 20) = 76 \text{ cm}^2$.

This section predicts how those two answers change when every length is multiplied
by the same number.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Two similar rectangles have corresponding lengths in the ratio $3 : 1$. Find the
ratio of their areas.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Areas use the square of the scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$k = 3, \qquad k^2 = 9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The areas are in the ratio $9 : 1$.

A rectangle $1$ cm by $2$ cm has area $2 \text{ cm}^2$. The similar rectangle $3$ cm
by $6$ cm has area $18 \text{ cm}^2$, and $18 \div 2 = 9$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two similar solids have corresponding lengths in the ratio $2 : 1$. Find the ratio
of their volumes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Volumes use the cube of the scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$k = 2, \qquad k^3 = 8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The volumes are in the ratio $8 : 1$.

A cube of side $1$ cm has volume $1 \text{ cm}^3$. A cube of side $2$ cm has volume
$8 \text{ cm}^3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A model of a tank is built to a scale of $1 : 20$. Find the ratio of the volume of
the model to the volume of the real tank.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The scale factor from the real tank to the model is $\dfrac{1}{20}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\left(\frac{1}{20}\right)^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{1}{20^3} = \frac{1}{8000}$$

The model volume is $\dfrac{1}{8000}$ of the real volume. A model that looks small
holds almost nothing, because three lengths are scaled down.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two similar triangles have areas $25 \text{ cm}^2$ and $64 \text{ cm}^2$. Find the
ratio of their corresponding sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the square root of the area ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{\frac{64}{25}}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{\frac{64}{25}} = \frac{8}{5}$$

The sides are in the ratio $5 : 8$, and the scale factor from small to large is
$1.6$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two similar solids have volumes $8 \text{ cm}^3$ and $125 \text{ cm}^3$. Find the
ratio of their corresponding lengths.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the cube root of the volume ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt[3]{\frac{125}{8}}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt[3]{\frac{125}{8}} = \frac{5}{2}$$

The lengths are in the ratio $2 : 5$, and the scale factor from small to large is
$2.5$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Tank A has volume $0.432\pi \text{ m}^3$ and surface area $2.16\pi \text{ m}^2$. A
similar tank is twice as large in every length. Find its volume and its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Volume uses $k^3$ and surface area uses $k^2$, with $k = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Volume multiplier $= 2^3 = 8$. Surface multiplier $= 2^2 = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = 0.432\pi \times 8 = 3.456\pi \approx 10.86 \text{ m}^3$$

$$A = 2.16\pi \times 4 = 8.64\pi \approx 27.14 \text{ m}^2$$

The volume has grown eight times and the surface area only four times, so the larger
tank gives more water for each square metre of metal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**7.** The larger tank in Question 6 holds $8$ times as much water as Tank A. Express the
increase in capacity as a percentage.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An increase from $1$ to $8$ is an increase of $7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{7}{1} \times 100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The capacity increases by $700\%$.

The new value is $800\%$ of the old value, and the increase is $800\% - 100\% =
700\%$. Chapter 2 uses the same reasoning for any percentage change.

</details>
</details>
</details>
