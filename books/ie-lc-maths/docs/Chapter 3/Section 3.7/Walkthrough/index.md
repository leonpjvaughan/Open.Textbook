# 3.7 The volume and surface area of a sphere — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 3.2 — area of a circle; Section 3.3 — volume; Section 3.5 —
surface area.

**Objectives:** find the volume and the surface area of a sphere from its radius or
diameter; find the volume and the surface area of a hemisphere.

By the end of this section you can measure the roundest solid there is, and you can answer
the chapter problem.

## The two formulas

A sphere is the set of points the same distance from a centre. That distance is the radius
$r$.

Two formulas describe a sphere, and both are given in the Formulae and Tables booklet:

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3 \qquad A = 4\pi r^2$$

where $V$ is the volume and $A$ is the surface area.

The volume uses $r^3$, because a volume is three-dimensional. The surface area uses $r^2$,
because a surface is two-dimensional.

**Example 1.** Find the volume and the surface area of a sphere of radius $3$ cm.

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 3^3 = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 27 = 36\pi \approx 113.10 \text{ cm}^3$$

$$A = 4\pi \times 3^2 = 36\pi \approx 113.10 \text{ cm}^2$$

The two numbers are equal here, and that happens only when $r = 3$: $\tfrac{4}{3}r^3 = 4r^2$
gives $r = 3$.

**Example 2.** Find the volume of a sphere of diameter $12$ cm.

The radius is $6$ cm.

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 6^3 = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 216 = 288\pi \approx 904.78 \text{ cm}^3$$

## Hemisphere

A **hemisphere** is half of a sphere, cut through the centre.

- Its volume is half the volume of the sphere.
- Its **curved** surface is half the surface area of the sphere.
- Its **total** surface adds the flat circular face, which has area $\pi r^2$.

So for a hemisphere,

$$V = \tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3 \qquad A_{\text{curved}} = 2\pi r^2 \qquad A_{\text{total}} = 3\pi r^2$$

**Example 3.** Find the total surface area of a hemisphere of radius $5$ cm.

$$A = 3\pi \times 5^2 = 75\pi \approx 235.62 \text{ cm}^2$$

That is the curved surface $50\pi$ plus the flat circle $25\pi$.

> **Watch out.** A hemisphere has a flat face as well as a curved one, and a question that
> says **total surface area** wants both. For a hemisphere of radius $5$ cm the curved
> surface is $2\pi \times 25 = 50\pi \approx 157.08 \text{ cm}^2$, and the total is
> $50\pi + 25\pi = 75\pi \approx 235.62 \text{ cm}^2$. Read whether the question says
> curved or total, and check whether it wants the flat circle.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Tank B is a sphere of diameter $1.2$ m, so its radius is
$0.6$ m.

Work out the volume and the surface area of Tank B, each in exact form and then to
3 significant figures. Then work out the volume in litres.

Now put the two tanks side by side. Tank A holds $0.432\pi \text{ m}^3$ and needs
$2.16\pi \text{ m}^2$ of metal. Tank B holds $0.288\pi \text{ m}^3$ and needs
$1.44\pi \text{ m}^2$. Divide each tank's metal by its volume, and compare the two answers.
Then divide each volume by the other and do the same for the metal.

The chapter problem asks which tank gives more water for the money. The Review finishes the
comparison and gives the club its answer.

## You should now be able to

- find the volume of a sphere from $V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$;
- find the surface area of a sphere from $A = 4\pi r^2$;
- find the volume, the curved surface and the total surface of a hemisphere;
- halve a diameter before using either formula, and say why.
