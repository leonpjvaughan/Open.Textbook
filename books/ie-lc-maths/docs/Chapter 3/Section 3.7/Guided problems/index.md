# 3.7 The volume and surface area of a sphere — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a circle of radius $6$ cm.
2. Find the total surface area of a closed cylinder of radius $3$ cm and height $7$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $A = \pi \times 6^2 = 36\pi \approx 113.10 \text{ cm}^2$.
2. $A = 2\pi \times 9 + 2\pi \times 3 \times 7 = 18\pi + 42\pi = 60\pi \approx 188.50 \text{ cm}^2$.

A sphere formula uses a circle area inside it, so the warm-up is the first step of
both sphere formulas.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the volume of a sphere of radius $3$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi r^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 27$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 27 = 36\pi \approx 113.10 \text{ cm}^3$$

Cube the radius first: $3^3 = 27$. Then multiply by $\tfrac{4}{3}$ and by $\pi$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the surface area of a sphere of radius $3$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $A = 4\pi r^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A = 4\pi \times 9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 4\pi \times 9 = 36\pi \approx 113.10 \text{ cm}^2$$

The surface area formula squares the radius, and the volume formula cubes it. This
is the one radius where the two answers agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the volume of a sphere of diameter $12$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Halve the diameter first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$r = 6 \text{ cm}, \qquad V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 6^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 216 = 288\pi \approx 904.78 \text{ cm}^3$$

Using $12$ as the radius gives a volume eight times too big, because the radius is
cubed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the volume of a hemisphere of radius $6$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A hemisphere is half of a sphere.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Sphere volume $= \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 216 = 288\pi$. Halve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 288\pi = 144\pi \approx 452.39 \text{ cm}^3$$

That is the same as $\tfrac{2}{3}\pi r^3$ with $r = 6$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the total surface area of a hemisphere of radius $5$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total is the curved surface plus the flat circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Curved surface $= 2\pi \times 25 = 50\pi$. Flat circle $= \pi \times 25 = 25\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 50\pi + 25\pi = 75\pi \approx 235.62 \text{ cm}^2$$

The flat circle is the part that goes missing. A hemisphere is like a bowl with a
lid, and both surfaces are painted if the question says total.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Tank B is a sphere of diameter $1.2$ m. Find its volume and its surface area in
exact form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius is $0.6$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 0.6^3, \qquad A = 4\pi \times 0.6^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \tfrac{4}{3}\pi \times 0.216 = 0.288\pi \approx 0.905 \text{ m}^3$$

$$A = 4\pi \times 0.36 = 1.44\pi \approx 4.52 \text{ m}^2$$

Keep both exact values for the comparison in the next question.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Tank A needs $2.16\pi \text{ m}^2$ of metal and holds $0.432\pi \text{ m}^3$. Tank B
needs $1.44\pi \text{ m}^2$ and holds $0.288\pi \text{ m}^3$. Find the metal per cubic metre
for each tank, and compare.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the surface area by the volume. The $\pi$ cancels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Tank A: $\dfrac{2.16\pi}{0.432\pi}$. Tank B: $\dfrac{1.44\pi}{0.288\pi}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{Tank A: } \frac{2.16\pi}{0.432\pi} = 5 \text{ m}^2/\text{m}^3$$

$$\text{Tank B: } \frac{1.44\pi}{0.288\pi} = 5 \text{ m}^2/\text{m}^3$$

Both tanks need exactly $5$ square metres of metal for every cubic metre of water.
The two shapes cost the same per litre, so the club should choose on another
ground, such as the space the tank takes up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**8.** Tank B has surface area $1.44\pi \approx 4.524 \text{ m}^2$. Write this value to
3 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first three significant figures are $4$, $5$ and $2$. Look at the next digit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The fourth significant figure is $4$, so the $2$ stays as it is.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4.524 \text{ m}^2 \approx 4.52 \text{ m}^2$$

Round once, at the end. Keep the exact value $1.44\pi$ through the working and round
only the final decimal.

</details>
</details>
</details>
