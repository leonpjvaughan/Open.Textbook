# 3.4 Nets of prisms, cylinders and spheres — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the volume of a cylinder of radius $2$ cm and height $9$ cm.
2. Find the circumference of a circle of diameter $12$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $V = \pi \times 2^2 \times 9 = 36\pi \approx 113.10 \text{ cm}^3$.
2. $C = \pi \times 12 = 12\pi \approx 37.70 \text{ cm}$.

The circumference is the width of the rectangle in the net of a cylinder, so
Question 2 is the first step of the work in this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the area of the net of a cuboid $5$ cm by $3$ cm by $2$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The net holds three pairs of rectangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The pairs are $5 \times 3$, $5 \times 2$ and $3 \times 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 2(5 \times 3 + 5 \times 2 + 3 \times 2) = 2(15 + 10 + 6) = 2(31) = 62 \text{ cm}^2$$

The factor $2$ counts both copies of each rectangle, because the net has two of
every face.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A cube has edges of $4$ cm. Find the area of its net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A cube net is $6$ squares.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

One face has area $4 \times 4 = 16 \text{ cm}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 6 \times 16 = 96 \text{ cm}^2$$

Every face of a cube is the same square, so one face area is multiplied by $6$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A cylinder has radius $5$ cm and height $10$ cm. Find the width of the rectangle in
its net, and the area of that rectangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The width is the circumference of the base.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Width $= 2\pi \times 5 = 10\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{Width} = 10\pi \approx 31.42 \text{ cm}$$

$$\text{Area} = 10\pi \times 10 = 100\pi \approx 314.16 \text{ cm}^2$$

The height of the rectangle is the height of the cylinder, so the rectangle is
$10\pi$ cm by $10$ cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A cylinder has diameter $12$ cm and height $8$ cm. Find the width of the rectangle in
its net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the diameter directly: width $= \pi d$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\text{Width} = \pi \times 12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{Width} = 12\pi \approx 37.70 \text{ cm}$$

The width is the circumference, not the diameter. A rectangle $12$ cm wide would
roll into a tube too narrow for the $12$ cm circles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A triangular prism has a right-angled triangular cross-section with legs $3$ cm and
$4$ cm, and a hypotenuse of $5$ cm. Its length is $10$ cm. Find the total area of its net.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The net has two triangles and three rectangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Each triangle has area $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 4 = 6 \text{ cm}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 2(6) + (3 \times 10) + (4 \times 10) + (5 \times 10) = 12 + 30 + 40 + 50 = 132 \text{ cm}^2$$

Each rectangle is one side of the triangle multiplied by the length. The three
rectangles have different widths, because the three sides of the triangle are
different lengths.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A cylinder has diameter $14$ cm. What is the width of the rectangle in its net?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The width is the circumference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\text{Width} = \pi \times 14$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{Width} = 14\pi \approx 43.98 \text{ cm}$$

The diameter is $14$ cm and the circumference is about $44$ cm, so the rectangle
is more than three times as wide as the circles are across.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** The net of the cuboid in Question 1 has area $62 \text{ cm}^2$. Write $62 \text{ cm}^2$
in square metres, then write that value in scientific notation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide by $10\,000$ first. Then put one non-zero digit before the point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$62 \div 10\,000 = 0.0062$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$62 \text{ cm}^2 = 0.0062 \text{ m}^2 = 6.2 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m}^2$$

The point moves three places to the right, so the index is $-3$. The index is
negative because the number is smaller than $1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
