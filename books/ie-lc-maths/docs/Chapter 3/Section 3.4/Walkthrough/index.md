# 3.4 Nets of prisms, cylinders and spheres — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 3.1 — area of a rectangle and a triangle; Section 3.2 —
circumference and area of a circle.

**Objectives:** describe the net of a cube, a cuboid, a triangular prism and a cylinder;
find the dimensions of the rectangle in the net of a cylinder; explain why a sphere has no
flat net.

By the end of this section you can unfold a solid into a flat shape and see where its
surface area comes from.

## What a net is

A **net** is a flat shape that folds up to make a solid. Every face of the solid appears in
the net as a flat shape, and the edges that meet when the net folds are the same length.

A net is a plan for making the solid. A cardboard box is cut from a net, and a sheet-metal
tank is cut from a net too.

## Nets of prisms

A prism has two identical ends and rectangular sides.

- A **cube** has $6$ square faces, so its net is $6$ squares.
- A **cuboid** has $3$ pairs of identical rectangles, so its net is $6$ rectangles.
- A **triangular prism** has $2$ triangular ends and $3$ rectangular sides, so its net is
  $2$ triangles and $3$ rectangles.

The net of a cuboid with length $l$, width $w$ and height $h$ holds three pairs of
rectangles: two of $l \times w$, two of $l \times h$ and two of $w \times h$. So its
surface area $A$ is

$$A = 2(lw + lh + wh)$$

**Example 1.** Find the area of the net of a cuboid $5$ cm by $3$ cm by $2$ cm.

$$A = 2(5 \times 3 + 5 \times 2 + 3 \times 2) = 2(15 + 10 + 6) = 2(31) = 62 \text{ cm}^2$$

## The net of a cylinder

A cylinder has two circular ends and one curved surface. The curved surface unrolls into a
rectangle.

- The height of the rectangle is the height of the cylinder, $h$.
- The width of the rectangle is the distance around the circle, so it is the
  circumference, $\pi d$ or $2\pi r$.

So the net of a cylinder is a rectangle of width $2\pi r$ and height $h$, plus two circles
of radius $r$.

**Example 2.** Describe the net of a cylinder of radius $5$ cm and height $10$ cm, and
find the area of the rectangle.

$$A = 2\pi r \times h = 2\pi \times 5 \times 10 = 100\pi \approx 314.16 \text{ cm}^2$$

The two circles are not part of that rectangle. They are separate faces of the net, each
with area $\pi \times 5^2 = 25\pi \text{ cm}^2$.

## The net of a sphere

A sphere has no flat net. You cannot cut a sphere along its surface and press it flat
without stretching or tearing it. A flat map of the whole Earth always distorts something,
and for the same reason: a curved surface cannot be flattened without changing distances.

A football is the closest thing to a sphere made from flat pieces. Its panels are
pentagons and hexagons that are curved before they are stitched, and the stitching pulls
them into a ball. The panels are not flat when the ball is finished.

> **Watch out.** The rectangle in the net of a cylinder is as wide as the **circumference**
> of the base, not the diameter. For a cylinder of diameter $12$ cm, the width is
> $12\pi \approx 37.70$ cm, not $12$ cm. If you cut a rectangle $12$ cm wide and rolled it
> up, the tube would be far too narrow to fit the $12$ cm circles. Check the width against
> the circumference before you cut.

## Investigate: fold a net

Take a sheet of paper and a pair of scissors. You will make a cylinder and find its net
from the other direction.

**Predict.** A rectangle $31.4$ cm wide and $10$ cm high is rolled into a tube, and two
circles are cut for the ends. Write down the radius you expect the circles to need, and
write down why you expect that value. Use $3.14$ for $\pi$.

**Test.** Cut the rectangle and roll it into a tube, taping the long edges together. Cut
two circles with the radius you predicted, and try to fit them into the ends of the tube.

**Explain.** Write down the radius that fitted, and compare it with your prediction. The
width of the rectangle is the circumference, so the radius comes from
$r = \dfrac{C}{2\pi}$. Write one sentence saying why a circle of radius $5$ cm fits, and
one sentence saying what would happen with a circle of radius $10$ cm.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Tank A is a cylinder of diameter $1.2$ m and height $1.2$ m.
Its net is a rectangle plus two circles.

Describe the net in full: the width and height of the rectangle, and the radius of each
circle. Give the width of the rectangle exactly, in terms of $\pi$, and then as a decimal
to 2 decimal places.

The width is the circumference you found in Section 3.2, and the two circles have the area
you found there. Section 3.5 adds all three parts together to get the metal the tank needs.

## You should now be able to

- describe the net of a cube, a cuboid and a triangular prism;
- say what shape the curved surface of a cylinder becomes, and find the rectangle's
  dimensions;
- explain why a sphere has no flat net;
- use a net to list every face of a solid before you find its surface area.
