# 3.1 Perimeter and area of plane figures — Learn

**Prerequisites:** multiplication and division of decimals; the names of common plane
figures; the meaning of a right angle.

**Objectives:** find the perimeter of a polygon; find the area of a rectangle, a triangle,
a parallelogram and a trapezium; find the area of a composite shape; convert between square
units.

By the end of this section you can measure the distance around a flat shape and the amount
of surface inside it.

## Perimeter

The **perimeter** of a plane figure is the total distance around its edge. To find it, add
the lengths of all the sides.

For a rectangle with length $l$ and width $w$, the two pairs of sides are equal, so

$$P = 2l + 2w$$

**Example 1.** Find the perimeter of a rectangle $7$ m long and $4$ m wide.

$$P = 2(7) + 2(4) = 14 + 8 = 22 \text{ m}$$

Perimeter is a length, so the unit is metres, centimetres or millimetres — never a square
unit.

## Area of a rectangle

The **area** of a plane figure is the amount of surface it covers. Area is measured in
square units.

A rectangle $7$ m long and $4$ m wide can be covered by $7 \times 4 = 28$ unit squares of
side $1$ m. So

$$A = l \times w = 7 \times 4 = 28 \text{ m}^2$$

A square is a rectangle whose sides are all equal, so its area is $s^2$.

## Square units

A square metre is a square with sides of $1$ m. One metre is $100$ cm, so along one edge
there are $100$ centimetres. The square therefore holds $100 \times 100$ small squares of
side $1$ cm:

$$1 \text{ m}^2 = 100 \times 100 \text{ cm}^2 = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2$$

So an area in square centimetres becomes square metres when you divide by $10\,000$, and
square metres become square centimetres when you multiply by $10\,000$. This is different
from a length conversion, which divides or multiplies by $100$ once.

## Area of a triangle

A triangle is half of a parallelogram with the same base and the same perpendicular
height. So

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$$

**Example 2.** Find the area of a triangle with base $9$ cm and perpendicular height
$6$ cm.

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 6 = 27 \text{ cm}^2$$

The height must meet the base at a right angle. It is measured from the base to the
opposite vertex, not along a sloping side.

## Area of a parallelogram

Slide the triangle at one end of a parallelogram to the other end, and you get a
rectangle. The rectangle has the same base and the same perpendicular height, so

$$A = \text{base} \times \text{perpendicular height}$$

**Example 3.** Find the area of a parallelogram with base $8$ cm and perpendicular height
$5$ cm.

$$A = 8 \times 5 = 40 \text{ cm}^2$$

## Area of a trapezium

A trapezium has one pair of parallel sides. Turn two copies of it upside down and fit them
together, and you get a parallelogram of base $(a + b)$ and height $h$. One trapezium is
half of that, so

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(a + b)h$$

where $a$ and $b$ are the two parallel sides and $h$ is the perpendicular height between
them.

**Example 4.** Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides $6$ m and $10$ m, and
height $4$ m.

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(6 + 10)(4) = \tfrac{1}{2}(16)(4) = 32 \text{ m}^2$$

## Composite shapes

A composite shape is made from simpler shapes. There are two ways to find its area.

- **Split it** into rectangles, triangles or trapeziums, and add the areas.
- **Subtract it** from a larger rectangle or circle that contains it.

**Example 5.** A garden is a rectangle $10$ m by $6$ m with a triangular flower bed on one
long side. The bed has base $4$ m and perpendicular height $3$ m. Find the total area.

Split the shape into a rectangle and a triangle.

$$A = (10 \times 6) + \left(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3\right) = 60 + 6 = 66 \text{ m}^2$$

**Example 6.** Find the perimeter of the same garden.

Add every outer edge. The two ends are $6$ m each, and the long sides are $10$ m each. The
triangle adds two sloping edges, so you need their lengths to finish the perimeter. If a
question asks only for the area, you do not need them.

> **Watch out.** In a parallelogram or a trapezium the **height** is the perpendicular
> distance between the parallel sides. It is shorter than a sloping side. For a
> parallelogram with base $8$ cm, a perpendicular height of $5$ cm and a sloping side of
> $6$ cm, the area is $8 \times 5 = 40 \text{ cm}^2$. Using the sloping side gives
> $48 \text{ cm}^2$, which is too big. Check that the height meets the base at a right
> angle before you multiply.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The two rain tanks stand on a square concrete pad. The pad
measures $1.6$ m by $1.6$ m.

Work out the perimeter and the area of the pad. The club also wants to fence a rectangular
compound $4$ m by $3$ m around the pad, leaving the pad inside it. Work out the perimeter
and the area of that compound too.

The pad and the compound are rectangles, so this section is enough. The base of each tank
is a circle, and a circle needs its own two formulas. Section 3.2 finds them.

## You should now be able to

- find the perimeter of a rectangle and of any polygon by adding the side lengths;
- find the area of a rectangle, a triangle, a parallelogram and a trapezium;
- choose the perpendicular height, and say why a sloping side will not do;
- find the area of a composite shape by splitting it or subtracting from it;
- convert an area between square metres and square centimetres.
