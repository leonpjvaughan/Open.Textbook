# 3.1 Perimeter and area of plane figures — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $1.5^2$.
2. Round $3.476$ to 2 decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $1.5^2 = 1.5 \times 1.5 = 2.25$.
2. The third decimal place is $6$, so round the $7$ up: $3.48$.

Squaring a decimal and rounding a decimal both appear in every area question in
this section. Keep them exact until the final line.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the area $A$ and the perimeter $P$ of a rectangle $8$ m long and $5$ m
wide.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Area is length times width. Perimeter is the distance all the way around.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the perimeter, add the four sides: $8 + 5 + 8 + 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 8 \times 5 = 40 \text{ m}^2$$

$$P = 2(8) + 2(5) = 16 + 10 = 26 \text{ m}$$

The area is in square metres and the perimeter is in metres. The two units are
different, so they can never be added.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the area of a triangle with base $12$ cm and perpendicular height $7$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A triangle is half of a parallelogram with the same base and height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 7 = 42 \text{ cm}^2$$

Half of $84$ is $42$. The $12$ and the $7$ multiply to $84$ first, and the halving
comes at the end.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A parallelogram has base $9$ cm, perpendicular height $4$ cm and a sloping side
of $5$ cm. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sloping side is not the height. Look for the perpendicular distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the base and the perpendicular height only.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 9 \times 4 = 36 \text{ cm}^2$$

The sloping side of $5$ cm is a distractor. It belongs to the perimeter, not to the
area. The perpendicular height of $4$ cm is the shorter measurement, and that is
the one to use.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides $7$ m and $13$ m, and height
$5$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the two parallel sides first, then halve.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(7 + 13)(5)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2}(20)(5) = 50 \text{ m}^2$$

The order of the two parallel sides does not matter, because they are added. The
height is the perpendicular distance between them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** An L-shaped room is a rectangle $6$ m by $4$ m with a rectangle $2$ m by $1$ m
removed from one corner. Find the area of the room.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the large rectangle first, then take the small one away.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Large area $= 6 \times 4 = 24 \text{ m}^2$. Small area $= 2 \times 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = (6 \times 4) - (2 \times 1) = 24 - 2 = 22 \text{ m}^2$$

Subtracting is quicker than splitting here, because the missing piece is a
rectangle. Both methods give $22 \text{ m}^2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A garden is a rectangle $10$ m by $6$ m with a triangular flower bed of base
$4$ m and perpendicular height $3$ m attached to one long side. Find the total area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Split the shape into a rectangle and a triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rectangle area $= 10 \times 6 = 60 \text{ m}^2$. Triangle area
$= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = (10 \times 6) + \left(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 3\right) = 60 + 6 = 66 \text{ m}^2$$

The two parts are added, because the bed is attached to the rectangle rather than
cut out of it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A floor covers $2.4 \text{ m}^2$. How many square centimetres is that?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One square metre is $10\,000$ square centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply by $10\,000$, because the answer is wanted in the smaller unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2.4 \times 10\,000 = 24\,000 \text{ cm}^2$$

An area conversion uses $100^2 = 10\,000$, not $100$. A length of $2.4$ m would be
$240$ cm, but an area of $2.4 \text{ m}^2$ is $24\,000 \text{ cm}^2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**8.** A rectangular field is $120$ m by $85$ m. Write its area in scientific notation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the area first, then write the result with one non-zero digit before the point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$120 \times 85 = 10\,200 \text{ m}^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$120 \times 85 = 10\,200 = 1.02 \times 10^4 \text{ m}^2$$

The point moves four places to the left, so the index is $4$. Chapter 1 uses this
notation for very large and very small measurements, and a field area is one of
them.

</details>
</details>
</details>
