# 3.3 Volume of prisms and cylinders — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a circle of radius $4$ cm.
2. Find the area of a rectangle $9$ m long and $2$ m wide.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $A = \pi \times 4^2 = 16\pi \approx 50.27 \text{ cm}^2$.
2. $A = 9 \times 2 = 18 \text{ m}^2$.

Every volume in this section starts with an area. The cross-section is the shape
you find the area of first.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the volume of a cuboid $5$ m long, $3$ m wide and $2$ m high.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the three lengths together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$V = 5 \times 3 \times 2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = 5 \times 3 \times 2 = 30 \text{ m}^3$$

The unit is cubic metres, because three lengths are multiplied. A volume is never
in square metres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A triangular prism has a cross-section with base $8$ cm and perpendicular height
$5$ cm. Its length is $20$ cm. Find its volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the area of the triangular cross-section first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Cross-section area $= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 5 = 20 \text{ cm}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = 20 \times 20 = 400 \text{ cm}^3$$

The $20$ in the first line is the cross-section area, and the second $20$ is the
length of the prism. The two numbers happen to be equal here, so keep the labels
clear in your working.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the volume of a cylinder of radius $3$ cm and height $10$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $V = \pi r^2 h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$V = \pi \times 3^2 \times 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \pi \times 9 \times 10 = 90\pi \approx 282.74 \text{ cm}^3$$

Square the radius before multiplying by the height. $90\pi$ is the exact answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the volume of a cylinder of diameter $10$ cm and height $12$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Halve the diameter to get the radius.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$r = 5 \text{ cm}, \qquad V = \pi \times 5^2 \times 12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \pi \times 25 \times 12 = 300\pi \approx 942.48 \text{ cm}^3$$

Using $10$ as the radius gives $1200\pi$, which is four times too big. The halving
is the step that matters.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the volume of a cylinder of diameter $14$ cm and height $5$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius is $7$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$V = \pi \times 7^2 \times 5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \pi \times 49 \times 5 = 245\pi \approx 769.69 \text{ cm}^3$$

Leave the answer as $245\pi$ if the question wants exact form.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A tank holds $2.5 \text{ m}^3$ of water. How many litres is that?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One cubic metre is $1000$ litres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply by $1000$, because the answer is wanted in the smaller unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2.5 \times 1000 = 2500 \text{ L}$$

A cubic metre is a cube $1$ m on each side, and that cube holds $1000$ litres of
water.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Tank A is a cylinder of diameter $1.2$ m and height $1.2$ m. Find its volume in
cubic metres, exact and to 3 significant figures, and then in litres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius is $0.6$ m. Use $V = \pi r^2 h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$V = \pi \times 0.6^2 \times 1.2 = \pi \times 0.36 \times 1.2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = 0.432\pi \approx 1.36 \text{ m}^3$$

$$1.36 \times 1000 \approx 1360 \text{ L}$$

The exact volume is $0.432\pi \text{ m}^3$, and that is about $1.36 \text{ m}^3$ or
$1360$ litres to 3 significant figures.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**8.** A tank holds $500$ litres. A larger tank holds $12\%$ more. How many litres does
the larger tank hold?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $12\%$ of $500$ first, then add it on. Or multiply by $1.12$ in one step.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$500 \times 1.12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$500 \times 1.12 = 560 \text{ L}$$

The multiplier $1.12$ adds the $12\%$ in one step. Chapter 2 uses the same
multiplier for every percentage increase.

</details>
</details>
</details>
