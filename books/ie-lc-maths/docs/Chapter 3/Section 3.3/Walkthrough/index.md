# 3.3 Volume of prisms and cylinders — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 3.1 — area of a rectangle and a triangle; Section 3.2 — area of
a circle.

**Objectives:** find the volume of a cuboid, a prism and a cylinder; choose the correct
cross-section; convert between cubic metres, cubic centimetres and litres.

By the end of this section you can measure the space inside a solid, and say how much
liquid it holds.

## Volume of a cuboid

The **volume** of a solid is the amount of space it fills. Volume is measured in cubic
units.

A cuboid $5$ m long, $3$ m wide and $2$ m high holds $5 \times 3 \times 2 = 30$ cubes of
side $1$ m, so

$$V = l \times w \times h = 30 \text{ m}^3$$

The first two numbers, $l \times w$, are the area of the base. So the volume is the area of
the base multiplied by the height.

## Volume of a prism

A **prism** is a solid with the same cross-section all the way along. Its ends are
identical, and every slice between them is a copy of that shape.

- A cuboid is a prism with a rectangle for a cross-section.
- A triangular prism has a triangle for a cross-section.
- A cylinder is a prism with a circle for a cross-section.

Because every slice is the same, the volume is

$$V = \text{area of cross-section} \times \text{length}$$

**Example 1.** A triangular prism has a cross-section with base $8$ cm and height $5$ cm.
Its length is $20$ cm. Find its volume.

The cross-section is a triangle, so its area is $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 5 = 20
\text{ cm}^2$.

$$V = 20 \times 20 = 400 \text{ cm}^3$$

**Example 2.** A trapezium-shaped drain has a cross-section with parallel sides $0.6$ m and
$1.0$ m, and height $0.4$ m. The drain is $12$ m long. Find the volume.

The cross-section is a trapezium, so its area is $\tfrac{1}{2}(0.6 + 1.0)(0.4) = 0.32
\text{ m}^2$.

$$V = 0.32 \times 12 = 3.84 \text{ m}^3$$

## Volume of a cylinder

A cylinder is a prism, so the rule is the same. Its cross-section is a circle of area
$\pi r^2$, and its length is the height $h$:

$$V = \pi r^2 h$$

**Example 3.** Find the volume of a cylinder of radius $3$ cm and height $10$ cm.

$$V = \pi \times 3^2 \times 10 = 90\pi \approx 282.74 \text{ cm}^3$$

**Example 4.** Find the volume of a cylinder of diameter $10$ cm and height $12$ cm.

The radius is $5$ cm.

$$V = \pi \times 5^2 \times 12 = 300\pi \approx 942.48 \text{ cm}^3$$

## Cubic units and litres

A cube of side $1$ m holds $100 \times 100 \times 100 = 1\,000\,000$ cubes of side $1$ cm,
so

$$1 \text{ m}^3 = 1\,000\,000 \text{ cm}^3$$

A cubic metre is also $1000$ litres, because a litre is the volume of a cube $10$ cm on
each side:

$$1 \text{ m}^3 = 1000 \text{ L}, \qquad 1 \text{ cm}^3 = 1 \text{ mL}$$

To change cubic metres into litres, multiply by $1000$.

> **Watch out.** The volume formula for a cylinder needs the **radius**, and it squares it.
> For a cylinder of diameter $10$ cm and height $12$ cm, the correct volume is
> $300\pi \approx 942.48 \text{ cm}^3$. Two common errors give very different answers:
> using $10$ as the radius gives $1200\pi$, and forgetting to square gives
> $\pi \times 5 \times 12 = 60\pi$. Halve the diameter first, then square the radius, then
> multiply by the height.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Tank A is a cylinder. Its base is a circle of diameter
$1.2$ m, and its height is $1.2$ m.

Work out the volume of Tank A in cubic metres. Give the exact answer in terms of $\pi$,
then a decimal to 3 significant figures. Then change it to litres.

Keep the exact volume. Section 3.5 needs the surface area of the same tank, and Section 3.7
compares this volume with the sphere.

## You should now be able to

- find the volume of a cuboid from its three lengths;
- identify the cross-section of a prism and find the volume from its area and length;
- find the volume of a cylinder from its radius and height, halving the diameter first;
- convert between cubic metres, cubic centimetres and litres.
