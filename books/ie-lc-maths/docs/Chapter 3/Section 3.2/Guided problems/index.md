# 3.2 The circle: circumference and area — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a rectangle $8$ m long and $3$ m wide.
2. A triangle has base $10$ cm and perpendicular height $4$ cm. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $A = 8 \times 3 = 24 \text{ m}^2$.
2. $A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 4 = 20 \text{ cm}^2$.

Both are areas, so both answers carry square units. A circle area is the same idea,
with $\pi r^2$ in place of length times width.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the circumference of a circle of radius $7$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $C = 2\pi r$, because you are given the radius.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$C = 2\pi \times 7$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$C = 2\pi \times 7 = 14\pi \approx 43.98 \text{ cm}$$

Leave the answer as $14\pi$ if the question wants exact form. $43.98$ is the same
value rounded to 2 decimal places.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the circumference of a circle of diameter $9$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $C = \pi d$, because you are given the diameter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$C = \pi \times 9$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$C = 9\pi \approx 28.27 \text{ m}$$

You could also halve $9$ to get the radius and use $C = 2\pi r$. Both routes give
$9\pi$, which is a useful check.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the area of a circle of radius $5$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square the radius first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A = \pi \times 5^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \pi \times 25 = 25\pi \approx 78.54 \text{ cm}^2$$

$5^2 = 25$, and then the $25$ is multiplied by $\pi$. The order matters:
$\pi \times 5^2$ is not the same as $(\pi \times 5)^2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the area of a circle of diameter $12$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diameter is $12$ m, so what is the radius?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$r = 6 \text{ m}, \qquad A = \pi \times 6^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \pi \times 36 = 36\pi \approx 113.10 \text{ m}^2$$

The first step is always to halve the diameter. Using $12$ as the radius would give
$144\pi$, which is four times too big.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the area of a semicircle of radius $8$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A semicircle is half of a full circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Full circle area $= \pi \times 8^2 = 64\pi$. Halve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times \pi \times 8^2 = 32\pi \approx 100.53 \text{ cm}^2$$

The question asks for the area of the semicircle only, so the halving is the last
step.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A square concrete pad measures $1.6$ m by $1.6$ m. A circular tank base of
diameter $1.2$ m sits on it. Find the area of the pad that the base does not cover.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the circle from the square. Halve the diameter first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Pad area $= 1.6^2 = 2.56 \text{ m}^2$. Circle area $= \pi \times 0.6^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 1.6^2 - \pi \times 0.6^2 = 2.56 - 0.36\pi \approx 1.43 \text{ m}^2$$

The radius of the base is $0.6$ m. The uncovered area is the square minus the
circle, and it is a little more than half of the pad.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** The circle in Question 3 has area $78.5398\ldots \text{ cm}^2$. Write this to
3 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first three significant figures are $7$, $8$ and $5$. Look at the next digit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The fourth significant figure is $3$, so the $5$ stays as it is.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$78.5398\ldots \approx 78.5 \text{ cm}^2$$

Round once, at the end. Do not round the intermediate value $25\pi$ before
multiplying, because the error grows from there.

</details>
</details>
</details>
