# 3.5 Surface area and curved surface area — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the area of a circle of radius $3$ cm.
2. Find the area of a rectangle $10$ cm long and $4$ cm wide.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $A = \pi \times 3^2 = 9\pi \approx 28.27 \text{ cm}^2$.
2. $A = 10 \times 4 = 40 \text{ cm}^2$.

A cylinder surface area is made of circles and a rectangle, so these two areas are
the two ingredients.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the surface area of a cuboid $5$ cm by $3$ cm by $2$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are three pairs of rectangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The pairs have areas $15$, $10$ and $6 \text{ cm}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 2(15 + 10 + 6) = 62 \text{ cm}^2$$

Each pair is counted twice, once for each side of the cuboid.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A triangular prism has a right-angled cross-section with legs $3$ cm and $4$ cm, and
a hypotenuse of $5$ cm. Its length is $10$ cm. Find its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two triangles and three rectangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Each triangle has area $6 \text{ cm}^2$. The rectangles have areas $30$, $40$ and
$50 \text{ cm}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 2(6) + 30 + 40 + 50 = 132 \text{ cm}^2$$

The three rectangles are different, because the three sides of the triangle are
different lengths.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the curved surface area of a cylinder of radius $7$ cm and height $4$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $2\pi r h$. This is the tube only.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2\pi \times 7 \times 4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2\pi r h = 56\pi \approx 175.93 \text{ cm}^2$$

The word **curved** means the circles are left out.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the total surface area of a closed cylinder of radius $7$ cm and height $4$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the two circles to the curved surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two circles: $2 \times \pi \times 7^2 = 98\pi$. Curved surface: $56\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 98\pi + 56\pi = 154\pi \approx 483.81 \text{ cm}^2$$

The total is much bigger than the curved surface alone, because the two circles
add $98\pi$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A cylinder of radius $7$ cm and height $4$ cm has no lid. Find its surface area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How many circles does it have now?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

One circle: $49\pi$. Curved surface: $56\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 49\pi + 56\pi = 105\pi \approx 329.87 \text{ cm}^2$$

With no lid there is one circle instead of two. The curved surface is unchanged,
because the tube is still there.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Find the total surface area of a closed cylinder of diameter $10$ cm and height
$6$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius is $5$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A = 2\pi \times 5^2 + 2\pi \times 5 \times 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 50\pi + 60\pi = 110\pi \approx 345.58 \text{ cm}^2$$

Halve the diameter first. The two circles give $50\pi$ and the tube gives $60\pi$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Tank A is a closed cylinder of diameter $1.2$ m and height $1.2$ m. Find its total
surface area in square metres, exact and to 3 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius is $0.6$ m. Use $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$A = 2\pi \times 0.6^2 + 2\pi \times 0.6 \times 1.2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = 0.72\pi + 1.44\pi = 2.16\pi \approx 6.79 \text{ m}^2$$

The exact answer is $2.16\pi \text{ m}^2$. Keep it for the comparison with Tank B in
Section 3.7.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**8.** The sheet metal for the tanks costs €45 per square metre. The price rises by 15%.
Work out the new price per square metre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $1.15$ to add $15\%$ in one step.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$45 \times 1.15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$45 \times 1.15 = 51.75$$

The new price is €51.75 per square metre. Chapter 2 uses the multiplier $1.15$ for
every $15\%$ increase.

</details>
</details>
</details>
