# 3.5 Surface area and curved surface area — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 3.1 — area of a rectangle and a triangle; Section 3.2 — area of
a circle; Section 3.4 — nets.

**Objectives:** find the total surface area of a prism; find the curved surface area of a
cylinder; find the total surface area of a closed cylinder and of a cylinder with no lid.

By the end of this section you can work out how much card, metal or paint a solid needs.

## Surface area

The **surface area** of a solid is the total area of all its faces. It is the area of the
net, because the net is exactly the surface laid flat.

To find a surface area, list every face, find the area of each one, and add them. Nothing
is doubled by accident and nothing is left out.

**Example 1.** Find the surface area $A$ of a cuboid $5$ cm by $3$ cm by $2$ cm.

The faces come in three pairs.

$$A = 2(5 \times 3 + 5 \times 2 + 3 \times 2) = 2(15 + 10 + 6) = 62 \text{ cm}^2$$

**Example 2.** A triangular prism has a right-angled cross-section with legs $3$ cm and
$4$ cm, and a hypotenuse of $5$ cm. Its length is $10$ cm. Find its surface area.

Two triangular ends and three rectangles:

$$A = 2\left(\tfrac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 4\right) + (3 \times 10) + (4 \times 10) + (5 \times 10) = 12 + 30 + 40 + 50 = 132 \text{ cm}^2$$

## Curved surface area of a cylinder

The curved surface of a cylinder unrolls into a rectangle. Its height is the height of the
cylinder $h$, and its width is the circumference $2\pi r$. So the **curved surface area**
is

$$\text{curved surface area} = 2\pi r h$$

This is the area of the tube only. It does not include the circles.

**Example 3.** Find the curved surface area of a cylinder of radius $7$ cm and height
$4$ cm.

$$2\pi r h = 2\pi \times 7 \times 4 = 56\pi \approx 175.93 \text{ cm}^2$$

## Total surface area of a cylinder

A closed cylinder has the curved surface and two circular ends. Each end has area
$\pi r^2$, so

$$\text{total surface area} = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$$

The first term is the two circles and the second is the tube.

**Example 4.** Find the total surface area of a closed cylinder of radius $7$ cm and
height $4$ cm.

$$A = 2\pi \times 49 + 2\pi \times 7 \times 4 = 98\pi + 56\pi = 154\pi \approx 483.81 \text{ cm}^2$$

**Example 5.** A cylinder of radius $7$ cm and height $4$ cm has no lid. Find its surface
area.

The top circle is missing, so only one circle is counted.

$$A = 49\pi + 56\pi = 105\pi \approx 329.87 \text{ cm}^2$$

Read the question carefully. A tank with a lid and a tank without one need different
amounts of metal.

> **Watch out.** The curved surface area and the total surface area are different numbers.
> For a cylinder of radius $7$ cm and height $4$ cm, the curved surface area is
> $56\pi \approx 175.93 \text{ cm}^2$ and the total is $154\pi \approx 483.81 \text{ cm}^2$.
> The curved surface area leaves out both circles. Check whether the question says
> **curved** or **total**, and check whether the solid has a lid.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Tank A is a closed cylinder of diameter $1.2$ m and height
$1.2$ m. It has a circular lid, so both circles count.

Work out its total surface area in square metres. Give the exact answer in terms of $\pi$,
then a decimal to 3 significant figures. Then work out the curved surface area on its own,
so you can see how much of the metal is the tube.

The sheet metal costs €45 per square metre, and Section 3.7 uses that price to compare
Tank A with the sphere.

## You should now be able to

- find the surface area of a cuboid and of a triangular prism by listing the faces;
- find the curved surface area of a cylinder from $2\pi r h$;
- find the total surface area of a closed cylinder from $2\pi r^2 + 2\pi r h$;
- adjust the total when a cylinder has no lid, and say which faces you counted.
