# 13.1 Distance, midpoint and slope — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory before you look at the section.

1. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides $5$ cm and $12$ cm. Find the hypotenuse.
2. For the line $y = 3x - 2$, find the value of $y$ when $x = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $5^2 + 12^2 = 25 + 144 = 169$, and $\sqrt{169} = 13$ cm. The hypotenuse is the
   longest side, so it is the square root of the sum of the squares.
2. $y = 3 \times 4 - 2 = 10$. Substituting a value into a formula is the same move
   you use when you check that a point lies on a line.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the distance between $(2, 3)$ and $(5, 7)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the coordinates to get the run and the rise, then use Pythagoras.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The run is $5 - 2 = 3$ and the rise is $7 - 3 = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \sqrt{(5 - 2)^2 + (7 - 3)^2} = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$$

The distance is $5$ units.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the midpoint of the segment joining $(2, 3)$ and $(8, -1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average the $x$-coordinates, then average the $y$-coordinates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The $x$-coordinate is $\frac{2 + 8}{2}$ and the $y$-coordinate is
$\frac{3 + (-1)}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$M = \left(\frac{2 + 8}{2}, \frac{3 + (-1)}{2}\right) = \left(\frac{10}{2}, \frac{2}{2}\right) = (5, 1)$$

The midpoint is $(5, 1)$. It is the average of the endpoints, so it is halfway
along in both directions.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the slope of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 10)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Slope is the rise over the run.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the same order on the top and the bottom: $\frac{10 - 2}{5 - 1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$m = \frac{10 - 2}{5 - 1} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$$

The slope is $2$. The line rises $2$ units for every $1$ unit to the right, and
the answer is positive because the line rises.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the exact distance between $(0, 0)$ and $(4, 6)$. Give your answer as a
surd.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square the differences, add, then simplify the square root.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4^2 + 6^2 = 16 + 36 = 52$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \sqrt{4^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{52} = \sqrt{4 \times 13} = 2\sqrt{13}$$

The surd $2\sqrt{13}$ is exact. A decimal is only an approximation, so keep the
surd unless the question asks for a rounded value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** The main road runs from $A(-1, 1)$ to $B(7, 7)$. Find its length, its
midpoint and its slope.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the three formulas in turn. Keep the points in the same order in the slope.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The run is $7 - (-1) = 8$ and the rise is $7 - 1 = 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Length: $\sqrt{8^2 + 6^2} = 10$ units. Midpoint:
$\left(\frac{-1 + 7}{2}, \frac{1 + 7}{2}\right) = (3, 4)$. Slope:
$\frac{7 - 1}{7 - (-1)} = \frac{6}{8} = \frac{3}{4}$.

The length is $10$ units, which is $100$ metres on the design map.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The midpoint of a segment is $(4, 3)$ and one endpoint is $(1, 5)$. Find the
other endpoint.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The midpoint is the average, so work backwards from the average.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Twice the midpoint minus the known endpoint.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(2 \times 4 - 1, 2 \times 3 - 5) = (7, 1)$$

The other endpoint is $(7, 1)$. Check it: the midpoint of $(1, 5)$ and $(7, 1)$
is $(4, 3)$, which is the value you were given.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**7.** A line passes through $(0, 3)$ and $(4, 11)$. Find its slope and its
$y$-intercept.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $y$-intercept is the value of $y$ when $x = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The slope is $\frac{11 - 3}{4 - 0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The slope is $\frac{11 - 3}{4 - 0} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$. The line crosses the
$y$-axis at $(0, 3)$, so the $y$-intercept is $3$. In Section 13.2 you will
write this line as $y = 2x + 3$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
