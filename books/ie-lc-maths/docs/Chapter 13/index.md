# Chapter 13: Coordinate geometry

A line and a circle are shapes you have drawn since primary school. This chapter
puts them on the coordinate plane, where algebra can measure them. A straight
line becomes an equation, a circle becomes an equation, and questions about
crossing, touching and distance become questions about solving equations.

## The chapter problem

**The new roundabout.** A town is planning a new roundabout. On the design map,
one unit is 10 metres. Two junctions lie at $A(-1, 1)$ and $B(7, 7)$, and the
main road runs straight between them.

The roundabout will sit halfway between the two junctions, and both junctions
will lie on its edge. A side road will leave $B$ at a right angle to the main
road. A bypass will run along the line $x + y = 12$.

By the end of the chapter you can find the length of the main road, write the
equations of the roads and the roundabout, prove that the side road is a
tangent, find where the bypass meets the roundabout, and place a bus stop that
divides the main road in a given ratio. You will meet this problem again at the
end of every section.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- use the distance formula and the midpoint formula;
- find the slope of a line through two points;
- write the equation of a line, and convert it to the form $ax + by + c = 0$;
- decide whether two lines are parallel or perpendicular, and find a line
  through a point that is parallel or perpendicular to a given line;
- write the equation of a circle from its centre and radius, and read the centre
  and radius from an expanded equation;
- find the tangent to a circle at a point, and the length of a tangent from an
  outside point;
- find where a line meets a circle, and use the discriminant to count the points;
- divide a line segment in a given ratio.

## Where it is used

Satellite navigation, surveying and map making, computer graphics and games,
engineering design, and the path of a robot or a drone. Any time a machine needs
to know where two things are, how far apart they are, or whether they touch, it
is using coordinate geometry. The circle equation also turns up in physics,
where the range of a signal or the orbit of a body is a circle or a sphere.

## Your turn to connect it

Think of a road junction you know: a roundabout, a crossroads, a slip road, or
the curve of a path around a pond.

Sketch it roughly on squared paper and choose a unit. Write down where two
important points are, as coordinates. You will be able to measure the distance
between them and describe the road between them by the end of the chapter.
