# 13.4 The equation of a circle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory before you start.

1. Find the distance from $(3, 4)$ to the origin.
2. Expand $(x - 3)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$. This distance becomes the radius when the
   centre is at the origin.
2. $(x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9$. Expanding the bracket is how the centre-radius
   form turns into the expanded form.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write the equation of the circle with centre $(2, -3)$ and radius $4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2$ with $a = 2$ and $b = -3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$b = -3$, so the bracket is $(y + 3)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x - 2)^2 + (y + 3)^2 = 16$$

The right-hand side is $4^2 = 16$. The signs inside the brackets are the
opposite of the centre coordinates.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the centre and the radius of $x^2 + y^2 - 10x + 4y - 7 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Match the equation to $x^2 + y^2 + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2g = -10$ and $2f = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$g = -5$ and $f = 2$, so the centre is $(-g, -f) = (5, -2)$. Then
$r^2 = g^2 + f^2 - c = 25 + 4 - (-7) = 36$, so $r = 6$.

The centre is $(5, -2)$ and the radius is $6$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Is the point $(5, 4)$ inside, on, or outside the circle
$(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 25$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute the point and compare with $25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(5 - 1)^2 + (4 - 2)^2 = 16 + 4 = 20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$20 < 25$, so the point is inside the circle. Its distance from the centre is
$\sqrt{20} \approx 4.47$, which is less than the radius $5$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A circle has centre $(3, 4)$ and passes through $(7, 7)$. Find its
equation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius is the distance from the centre to the point. Work with $r^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$r^2 = (7 - 3)^2 + (7 - 4)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$r^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$, so $r = 5$. The equation is
$(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25$.

Working with $r^2$ avoids a square root. You only need the radius itself if the
question asks for it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** $A(-1, 1)$ and $B(7, 7)$ are the ends of a diameter of a circle. Find the
equation of the circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The centre is the midpoint of the diameter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The midpoint of $A$ and $B$ is $(3, 4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The centre is $(3, 4)$. The radius is the distance from the centre to $B$:
$r^2 = (7 - 3)^2 + (7 - 4)^2 = 25$. The equation is
$(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25$.

A diameter is a chord through the centre, so the midpoint of its endpoints is the
centre.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Find the centre and the radius of $x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The constant term is $0$ here.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2g = -6$ and $2f = -8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$g = -3$ and $f = -4$, so the centre is $(3, 4)$. Then
$r^2 = 9 + 16 - 0 = 25$, so $r = 5$.

The circle passes through the origin, which is why the constant term is zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A circle has radius $5$ cm. Find its circumference and its area, leaving
$\pi$ in your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Circumference is $2\pi r$ and area is $\pi r^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $r = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Circumference $= 2\pi \times 5 = 10\pi$ cm. Area $= \pi \times 25 = 25\pi$
cm$^2$.

Notice the $r^2$ in the area formula. The circle equation also carries $r^2$,
and for the same reason: the algebra of a circle is built on squared distances.

</details>
</details>
</details>
