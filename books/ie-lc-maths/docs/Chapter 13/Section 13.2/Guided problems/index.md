# 13.2 The equation of a line — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory before you start.

1. Find the slope of the line through $(2, 1)$ and $(6, 9)$.
2. Solve $3x + 4 = 19$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac{9 - 1}{6 - 2} = \frac{8}{4} = 2$. The slope is what you need before you
   can write an equation.
2. $3x = 15$, so $x = 5$. Rearranging an equation is the last step of almost
   every question on this page.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write down the slope and the $y$-intercept of $y = 2x - 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Match the equation to $y = mx + c$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The number multiplying $x$ is $m$; the constant is $c$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The slope is $2$ and the $y$-intercept is $-5$. The line crosses the $y$-axis at
$(0, -5)$, not at $(0, 5)$: the sign belongs to the constant.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the equation of the line with slope $3$ through $(1, 2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y - 2 = 3(x - 1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 2 = 3(x - 1) \quad\Rightarrow\quad y - 2 = 3x - 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = 3x - 1$$

Check the point: $3 \times 1 - 1 = 2$, which is correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the equation of the line through $(2, 1)$ and $(6, 9)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the slope first, then use either point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The slope is $\frac{9 - 1}{6 - 2} = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 1 = 2(x - 2) \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = 2x - 3$$

Both points satisfy the equation. Using the other point gives the same line, so
either choice is fine.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Write the main road through $A(-1, 1)$ and $B(7, 7)$ as $ax + by + c = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the slope $\frac{3}{4}$ from Section 13.1, then clear the fractions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y - 1 = \frac{3}{4}(x + 1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 1 = \frac{3}{4}(x + 1) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 4y - 4 = 3x + 3
\quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x - 4y + 7 = 0$$

Substitute $A$: $-3 - 4 + 7 = 0$. Substitute $B$: $21 - 28 + 7 = 0$. Both
junctions satisfy the equation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find where the line $3x + 4y = 12$ crosses the $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On the $x$-axis, $y = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $y = 0$ and solve for $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x + 0 = 12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 4$$

The line crosses the $x$-axis at $(4, 0)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A taxi charges a fixed €3.50 plus €2 for each kilometre. Write an equation
for the fare $y$ for $x$ kilometres, and find the fare for $7$ km.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which charge is the slope, and which is the intercept?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The fixed charge happens even for $0$ km, so it is the intercept.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = 2x + 3.5$$

For $7$ km, $y = 2 \times 7 + 3.5 = 17.5$, which is €17.50. The fixed charge is the intercept
because it does not change with distance.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**7.** Solve the simultaneous equations $y = 2x - 3$ and $y = x + 1$. What does
the solution mean on a graph?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both expressions equal $y$, so set them equal to each other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2x - 3 = x + 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2x - 3 = x + 1$, so $x = 4$. Then $y = 4 + 1 = 5$. The solution is $(4, 5)$.

On a graph, $(4, 5)$ is the point where the two lines cross. In Section 13.6 you
will use the same idea to find where a line crosses a circle.

</details>
</details>
</details>
