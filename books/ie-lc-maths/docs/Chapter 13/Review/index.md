# Chapter 13: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The new roundabout.** The junctions are $A(-1, 1)$ and $B(7, 7)$ on a map where
one unit is $10$ metres. The roundabout sits halfway between them, both junctions
lie on its edge, a side road leaves $B$ at a right angle to the main road, and a
bypass runs along $x + y = 12$.

**The main road.** The length is

$$AB = \sqrt{(7 - (-1))^2 + (7 - 1)^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = 10 \text{ units} = 100 \text{ m}$$

The midpoint is $\left(\frac{-1 + 7}{2}, \frac{1 + 7}{2}\right) = (3, 4)$, and the
slope is $\frac{7 - 1}{7 - (-1)} = \frac{3}{4}$.

**The equation of the main road.** Using $A$:
$y - 1 = \frac{3}{4}(x + 1)$, so $y = \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{7}{4}$, which is
$3x - 4y + 7 = 0$. Both junctions satisfy it.

**The roundabout.** The centre is the midpoint $(3, 4)$. The radius is the
distance from the centre to $B$:
$r^2 = (7 - 3)^2 + (7 - 4)^2 = 25$, so $r = 5$ units, which is $50$ m. The
equation is

$$(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25$$

Expanding gives $x^2 + y^2 - 6x - 8y = 0$. Since the main road is a diameter, it
passes through the centre.

**The side road.** The main road has slope $\frac{3}{4}$, so the side road has
slope $-\frac{4}{3}$. Through $B(7, 7)$:

$$y - 7 = -\frac{4}{3}(x - 7) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 4x + 3y = 49$$

**The side road is a tangent.** The distance from $C(3, 4)$ to $4x + 3y - 49 = 0$
is

$$\frac{|12 + 12 - 49|}{\sqrt{4^2 + 3^2}} = \frac{25}{5} = 5$$

The distance equals the radius, so the side road touches the roundabout once.

**The bypass.** Substitute $y = 12 - x$ into the roundabout:

$$(x - 3)^2 + (8 - x)^2 = 25 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x^2 - 11x + 24 = 0
\quad\Rightarrow\quad (x - 3)(x - 8) = 0$$

So $x = 3$ or $x = 8$, giving $(3, 9)$ and $(8, 4)$. The chord between them is
$\sqrt{5^2 + (-5)^2} = 5\sqrt{2}$ units, about $7.07$ units or $70.7$ m.

**The bus stop.** Dividing $AB$ in the ratio $2 : 3$:

$$P = \left(\frac{3 \times (-1) + 2 \times 7}{5}, \frac{3 \times 1 + 2 \times 7}{5}\right)
= \left(\frac{11}{5}, \frac{17}{5}\right)$$

That is $(2.2, 3.4)$, which is $2$ parts of $5$ along the road from $A$. It is
$40$ m from $A$ and $60$ m from $B$.

**One check.** The centre $(3, 4)$ is the midpoint of $AB$, so $A$ and $B$ are
opposite ends of a diameter. Both satisfy the circle equation:
$(-1 - 3)^2 + (1 - 4)^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$ and
$(7 - 3)^2 + (7 - 4)^2 = 16 + 9 = 25$.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work without notes. For each question, say **which method** you are using and
why, before you calculate. Three of them are deliberately questions where the
first method that comes to mind is the slow one.

1. Find the distance and the midpoint of $(-2, 5)$ and $(6, 1)$.
2. Find the slope and the equation of the line through $(1, -3)$ and $(5, 5)$.
3. Find the line through $(0, 1)$ perpendicular to $y = -2x + 5$.
4. Write the equation of the circle with centre $(-2, 3)$ and radius $6$, then
   expand it.
5. Find the centre and the radius of $x^2 + y^2 + 8x - 2y - 8 = 0$.
6. Find the tangent to $(x - 1)^2 + (y + 2)^2 = 25$ at $(4, 2)$.
7. Find the two points where $y = x + 1$ meets $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.
8. Divide the segment from $(1, 1)$ to $(9, 5)$ in the ratio $1 : 3$.
9. A ramp rises $3$ m over a horizontal run of $4$ m. Find its slope and the
   angle it makes with the ground.
10. The points $(2, 7)$ and $(10, 7)$ are joined. Decide what kind of line this
    is, and find its slope, its length and its midpoint.

**Two method questions.**

- A line and a circle are given. You only need to know whether they touch. Which
  is quicker: finding the distance from the centre to the line, or solving the
  simultaneous equations? Say why.
- You are given the two endpoints of a diameter. Do you need the distance formula
  to find the circle, or is there a shorter route? Explain.

## What you can now do

- measure a segment with the distance formula, and find its midpoint and slope;
- write the equation of a line from two points, or from a point and a slope;
- decide whether lines are parallel or perpendicular, and build a line through a
  point with either property;
- write the equation of a circle, and read the centre and radius from an expanded
  equation;
- find a tangent to a circle, and the length of a tangent from an outside point;
- find where a line meets a circle, and use the discriminant to count the points;
- divide a segment in a given ratio with the section formula.

## Thinking back

Return to the junction you sketched at the start of the chapter. You chose two
important points and wrote them as coordinates.

Now measure the road between them. Find its length, its midpoint and its slope,
and write its equation. If there is a roundabout or a bend, describe the circle
that would fit it, and say where a straight road would touch it.

Write a short paragraph about what the numbers tell you that the sketch did not.
A coordinate model turns a picture into measurements you can compare and check,
and that is the whole point of this chapter.
