# 13.5 Tangents to a circle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory before you start.

1. Find the slope of the line from the origin to $(3, 4)$.
2. Work out $\frac{4}{3} \times \left(-\frac{3}{4}\right)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The slope is $\frac{4}{3}$. This is the radius slope in the first example of
   the section.
2. The product is $-1$. A product of $-1$ is the test for a right angle, and the
   tangent uses it at every point.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** The radius to a point on a circle has slope $2$. Find the slope of the
tangent there.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the negative reciprocal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $2$ as $\frac{2}{1}$ and flip it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The tangent slope is $-\frac{1}{2}$, and
$2 \times \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$. The radius and the tangent meet at a
right angle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the tangent to $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ at $(3, 4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The centre is $(0, 0)$. Find the radius slope first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The radius slope is $\frac{4}{3}$, so the tangent slope is $-\frac{3}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 4 = -\frac{3}{4}(x - 3) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 4y - 16 = -3x + 9
\quad\Rightarrow\quad 3x + 4y = 25$$

Check the point: $3 \times 3 + 4 \times 4 = 25$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The circle has centre $C(3, 4)$ and radius $5$. Find the tangent at
$B(7, 7)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius slope is $\frac{3}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The tangent slope is $-\frac{4}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 7 = -\frac{4}{3}(x - 7) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3y - 21 = -4x + 28
\quad\Rightarrow\quad 4x + 3y = 49$$

This is the side road from the chapter problem. The side road is the tangent at
$B$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Show that $4x + 3y = 49$ is a tangent to the circle with centre $(3, 4)$
and radius $5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the distance from a point to a line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the line as $4x + 3y - 49 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \frac{|4 \times 3 + 3 \times 4 - 49|}{\sqrt{4^2 + 3^2}}
= \frac{|12 + 12 - 49|}{5} = \frac{25}{5} = 5$$

The distance $d$ equals the radius $5$, so the line touches the circle exactly once
and is a tangent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** From $Q(6, 9)$, a tangent is drawn to the circle with centre $C(3, 4)$ and
radius $5$. Find the length of the tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $QT^2 = CQ^2 - r^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$CQ^2 = (6 - 3)^2 + (9 - 4)^2 = 34$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$QT^2 = 34 - 25 = 9$, so $QT = 3$.

The right angle is at the point of contact, so Pythagoras links the centre, the
outside point and the tangent length.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The circle has centre $(3, 4)$ and radius $5$. Find the tangent at
$(3, 9)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What is the radius from $(3, 4)$ to $(3, 9)$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The radius is vertical, so the tangent is horizontal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The radius is vertical, so the tangent is horizontal and passes through $(3, 9)$:
$y = 9$.

Check it: the distance from the centre to $y = 9$ is $|9 - 4| = 5$, which is the
radius.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A tangent is drawn from a point $13$ cm from the centre of a circle of
radius $5$ cm. Find the length of the tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius meets the tangent at a right angle, so use Pythagoras.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The distance from the centre is the hypotenuse of the right triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the tangent length be $t$. Then $t^2 + 5^2 = 13^2$, so $t^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$
and $t = 12$ cm.

This is the same right triangle as in the section, with numbers chosen to give a
whole-number answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>
