# 13.6 Intersection of a line and a circle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory before you start.

1. Solve $x^2 + 9 = 25$.
2. Find the discriminant of $x^2 + 2x - 24$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x^2 = 16$, so $x = 4$ or $x = -4$. A quadratic has two roots, and both
   matter here.
2. $b^2 - 4ac = 4 + 96 = 100$. A positive discriminant means two real roots.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find where the line $y = 3$ meets the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $y = 3$ and solve for $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x^2 + 9 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$x^2 = 16$, so $x = 4$ or $x = -4$. The points are $(4, 3)$ and $(-4, 3)$.

The line is horizontal and cuts the circle twice, once on each side of the
$y$-axis.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find where the bypass $x + y = 12$ meets the roundabout
$(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange the line to $y = 12 - x$, then substitute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(x - 3)^2 + (8 - x)^2 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Expanding gives $2x^2 - 22x + 48 = 0$, so $x^2 - 11x + 24 = 0$ and
$(x - 3)(x - 8) = 0$. Then $x = 3$ or $x = 8$.

From $y = 12 - x$: when $x = 3$, $y = 9$; when $x = 8$, $y = 4$. The points are
$(3, 9)$ and $(8, 4)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find where the line $y = x$ meets the circle $(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $y = x$ into the circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(x - 3)^2 + (x - 4)^2 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$x^2 - 6x + 9 + x^2 - 8x + 16 = 25$, so $2x^2 - 14x = 0$. Factorise:
$2x(x - 7) = 0$, so $x = 0$ or $x = 7$. The points are $(0, 0)$ and $(7, 7)$.

One of the points is the origin. The circle passes through the origin, which is
why $x = 0$ is a root.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Use the discriminant to decide how many times $y = x + 1$ meets
$x^2 + y^2 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute, then simplify to a quadratic in $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2x^2 + 2x - 24 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The discriminant is $2^2 - 4 \times 2 \times (-24) = 4 + 192 = 196$. It is
positive, so there are two distinct points of intersection.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Show that $y = 2x + 5$ is a tangent to $(x - 1)^2 + (y - 2)^2 = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute and find the discriminant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(x - 1)^2 + (2x + 3)^2 = 5$ simplifies to $x^2 + 2x + 1 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The discriminant is $2^2 - 4 \times 1 \times 1 = 0$. A zero discriminant means
one repeated root, so the line touches the circle once and is a tangent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The line $x + y = 12$ cuts the circle $(x - 3)^2 + (y - 4)^2 = 25$ at
$(3, 9)$ and $(8, 4)$. Find the length of the chord.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The chord is the distance between the two points.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{(8 - 3)^2 + (4 - 9)^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{5^2 + (-5)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 25} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$$

The chord is $5\sqrt{2}$ units, about $7.07$ units. Keep the surd for an exact
answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**7.** Solve $x^2 - 11x + 24 = 0$ by factorising.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find two numbers that multiply to $24$ and add to $-11$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The numbers are $-3$ and $-8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x - 3)(x - 8) = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 3 \text{ or } x = 8$$

This is the quadratic you meet when the bypass crosses the roundabout. Being able
to factorise it quickly is what makes the intersection questions short.

</details>
</details>
</details>
