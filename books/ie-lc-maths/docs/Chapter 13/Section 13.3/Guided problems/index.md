# 13.3 Parallel and perpendicular lines — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory before you start.

1. Write down the slopes of $y = 2x + 1$ and $y = 2x - 5$.
2. Find the negative reciprocal of $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. Both slopes are $2$. Equal slopes mean the lines are parallel.
2. The negative reciprocal of $3$ is $-\frac{1}{3}$. Flip the fraction and change
   the sign: $3 \times \left(-\frac{1}{3}\right) = -1$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A line has slope $4$. Find the slope of a line perpendicular to it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Flip the fraction and change the sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $4$ as $\frac{4}{1}$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The perpendicular slope is $-\frac{1}{4}$. Check the product:
$4 \times \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = -1$, which is what a right angle requires.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the line through $(2, 3)$ parallel to $y = 5x - 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Parallel lines share a slope, so $m = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y - 3 = 5(x - 2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 3 = 5(x - 2) \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = 5x - 7$$

The new line has the same slope as the given line, so the two never meet.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the line through $(2, 3)$ perpendicular to $y = 5x - 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The perpendicular slope is $-\frac{1}{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y - 3 = -\frac{1}{5}(x - 2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 3 = -\frac{1}{5}(x - 2) \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = -\frac{1}{5}x + \frac{17}{5}$$

The product of the slopes is $5 \times \left(-\frac{1}{5}\right) = -1$, so the
lines meet at a right angle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The main road has slope $\frac{3}{4}$. A side road leaves $B(7, 7)$ at a
right angle to it. Find the equation of the side road.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the perpendicular slope first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The perpendicular slope is $-\frac{4}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 7 = -\frac{4}{3}(x - 7) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3y - 21 = -4x + 28
\quad\Rightarrow\quad 4x + 3y = 49$$

The side road is $4x + 3y = 49$. It passes through $B$, since
$4 \times 7 + 3 \times 7 = 49$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the perpendicular bisector of the segment from $A(-1, 1)$ to
$B(7, 7)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need the midpoint and the perpendicular slope.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The midpoint is $(3, 4)$ and the perpendicular slope is $-\frac{4}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y - 4 = -\frac{4}{3}(x - 3) \quad\Rightarrow\quad 3y - 12 = -4x + 12
\quad\Rightarrow\quad 4x + 3y = 24$$

The line passes through the midpoint $(3, 4)$, since $12 + 12 = 24$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The line through $(1, 2)$ and $(3, k)$ is perpendicular to a line of slope
$-\frac{1}{2}$. Find $k$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What slope is perpendicular to $-\frac{1}{2}$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The required slope is $2$, so $\frac{k - 2}{3 - 1} = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{k - 2}{2} = 2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k - 2 = 4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad k = 6$$

The line through $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 6)$ has slope $2$, and
$2 \times \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 12

**7.** A ramp rises $3$ m over a horizontal run of $4$ m. Find its slope, and find
the angle it makes with the ground.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The slope is the rise over the run. Trigonometry turns a slope into an angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The slope is $\frac{3}{4}$. Use $\tan\theta = \frac{3}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The slope is $\frac{3}{4} = 0.75$. Since $\tan\theta = 0.75$, the angle is
$\theta \approx 36.9^\circ$.

The slope and the tangent of the angle are the same number. That is why a slope
of $1$ means an angle of $45^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
