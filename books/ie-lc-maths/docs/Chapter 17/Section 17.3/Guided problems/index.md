# 17.3 Cyclic quadrilaterals — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Two angles on a straight line are $115^\circ$ and $x^\circ$. Find $x$.
2. In a circle, two angles stand on the same arc, and one of them is $48^\circ$. Find the
   other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x = 180 - 115 = 65$. Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$, by part 1 of the
   Protractor Axiom from Section 15.2.
2. $48^\circ$. Angles in the same segment are equal, by Corollary 2 from Section 17.2.

Both answers are the tools of this section. The straight line gives the exterior angle,
and the same segment gives the circle the four vertices share.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** $ABCD$ is a cyclic quadrilateral with $|\angle BAD| = 108^\circ$. Find
$|\angle BCD|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angles at $A$ and at $C$ are opposite.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle BCD| = 180^\circ - 108^\circ = 72^\circ.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A cyclic quadrilateral has two opposite angles of $2x$ and $3x$. Find $x$, then find
both angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the equation for the opposite pair.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2x + 3x = 180.$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5x = 180$$

$$x = 36.$$

The two angles are $2 \times 36 = 72^\circ$ and $3 \times 36 = 108^\circ$. Check:
$72 + 108 = 180$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** $ABCD$ is cyclic, and the side $AB$ is extended past $B$. The exterior angle at $B$
is $124^\circ$. Find $|\angle ABC|$ and $|\angle ADC|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The exterior angle and the interior angle at $B$ lie on a straight line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The exterior angle also equals the interior opposite angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The interior angle and the exterior angle add to $180^\circ$, so
$$|\angle ABC| = 180^\circ - 124^\circ = 56^\circ.$$

The exterior angle equals the interior opposite angle, so
$$|\angle ADC| = 124^\circ.$$

Check with Corollary 5: $56 + 124 = 180$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A convex quadrilateral has angles $88^\circ$, $92^\circ$, $75^\circ$ and $105^\circ$,
in order. Decide whether it is cyclic, and give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the first and third angles, then the second and fourth.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both opposite pairs must sum to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first and third angles are opposite:
$$88 + 75 = 163 \neq 180.$$

So the quadrilateral is not cyclic. The other pair gives $92 + 105 = 197$, which is also
not $180$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A cyclic quadrilateral $PQRS$ has $|\angle P| = 90^\circ$ and
$|\angle Q| = 85^\circ$. Find $|\angle R|$ and $|\angle S|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$P$ and $R$ are opposite, and $Q$ and $S$ are opposite.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract each given angle from $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle R| = 180^\circ - 90^\circ = 90^\circ$$

$$|\angle S| = 180^\circ - 85^\circ = 95^\circ.$$

Check the four angles: $90 + 85 + 90 + 95 = 360^\circ$, which is what the angles of any
quadrilateral add to.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two angles at the circle stand on the two arcs that make the whole circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two angles at the centre add to $360^\circ$, and each angle at the circle is half of
its own angle at the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $ABCD$ be cyclic with centre $O$. The angle $\angle ABC$ stands on one arc $AC$, and
$\angle ADC$ stands on the other arc $AC$. The two arcs make the whole circle, so the two
angles at the centre add to $360^\circ$.

By Theorem 19, each angle at the circle is half of its angle at the centre. So the two
angles at the circle add to half of $360^\circ$, which is $180^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 17.2

**7.** $BC$ is a diameter of a circle, and $A$ is another point of the circle. Find
$|\angle BAC|$. Then explain how this shows that $A$, $B$ and $C$ can be completed to a
cyclic quadrilateral.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A quadrilateral with two opposite right angles has a pair of opposite angles summing to
$180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle BAC| = 90^\circ,$$

by Corollary 3. Choose a fourth point $D$ on the major arc $BC$. Then $ABCD$ is cyclic,
because all four points lie on the circle. Its opposite angles at $A$ and at $C$ sum to
$180^\circ$, by Corollary 5. The angle at $A$ is $90^\circ$, so the angle at $C$ is also
$90^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
