# 17.8 Enlargements and similar triangles in the circle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Two angles stand on the same arc of a circle, in the same segment. What is true of
   them?
2. Two triangles are similar. The sides of the first are $3$ cm, $4$ cm and $5$ cm, and the
   matching sides of the second are $6$ cm, $8$ cm and $10$ cm. Find the scale factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. They are equal, by Corollary 2 from Section 17.2. The angles in the same segment are
   equal.
2. Each side of the second triangle is twice the matching side of the first, so the scale
   factor is $2$.

Both answers are used here. The same-segment result gives the equal angles, and the scale
factor links the two triangles.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Two chords $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $P$ inside a circle. Given $|PA| = 6$ cm,
$|PB| = 8$ cm and $|PC| = 4$ cm, find $|PD|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The product of the two parts of one chord equals the product of the two parts of the
other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$6 \times 8 = 4 \times |PD|.$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$48 = 4|PD|$$

$$|PD| = 12 \text{ cm}.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two chords cross at $P$, with $|PA| = 5$ cm, $|PB| = 12$ cm and $|PC| = 4$ cm. Find
$|PD|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the same rule as question 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5 \times 12 = 4 \times |PD|.$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$60 = 4|PD|$$

$$|PD| = 15 \text{ cm}.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A chord $AB$ of length $13$ cm is cut by another chord at $P$, with $|PA| = 4$ cm.
The other chord has one part $|PC| = 6$ cm. Find its other part $|PD|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $|PB|$ first, from the whole chord.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$|PB| = 13 - 4 = 9$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4 \times 9 = 6 \times |PD|$$

$$36 = 6|PD|$$

$$|PD| = 6 \text{ cm}.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two chords $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $P$ inside a circle. Name the two similar triangles,
and give the two pairs of equal angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One pair of angles is at the crossing point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The other pair stands on the same arc.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The similar triangles are $APC$ and $DPB$.

- $|\angle APC| = |\angle DPB|$, because the angles are vertically opposite.
- $|\angle PAC| = |\angle PDB|$, because both stand on the arc $BC$ and lie in the same
  segment.

Two pairs of equal angles make the triangles similar by AA.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** In question 1, find the scale factor of the enlargement centred at $P$ that takes
the triangle $APC$ to the triangle $DPB$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The matching vertex of $A$ is $D$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The scale factor is $|PD| \div |PA|$, with a minus sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$k = -\frac{|PD|}{|PA|} = -\frac{12}{6} = -2.$$

The image is twice as far from $P$ as the object, and on the other side of $P$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why $|PA| \times |PB| = |PC| \times |PD|$ for two chords that cross at $P$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start from the similar triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the ratio of corresponding sides from the matching order.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The triangles $APC$ and $DPB$ are similar, with $A$ matching $D$, $P$ matching $P$, and
$C$ matching $B$. So the ratio of corresponding sides is

$$\frac{|PA|}{|PD|} = \frac{|PC|}{|PB|}.$$

Multiply both sides by $|PD| \times |PB|$:

$$|PA| \times |PB| = |PC| \times |PD|.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 14.4

**7.** The two triangles made by two crossing chords lie on opposite sides of the crossing
point $P$. Explain why the scale factor of the enlargement centred at $P$ is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Where does a negative scale factor put the image?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Recall the enlargement rule from Section 14.4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A negative scale factor places the image on the opposite side of the centre from the
object, by Section 14.4.

The matching vertices $A$ and $D$ lie on opposite sides of $P$, because $P$ is between $A$
and $B$ on the chord $AB$, and $D$ is on the other chord. So the image is on the opposite
side of $P$ from the object, and the scale factor is negative.

</details>
</details>
</details>
