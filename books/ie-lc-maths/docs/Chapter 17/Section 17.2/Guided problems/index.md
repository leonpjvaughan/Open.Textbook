# 17.2 The angle in a semicircle and related theorems — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of $40^\circ$. Find each base angle.
2. In a triangle, the exterior angle at one vertex is $120^\circ$ and one interior
   opposite angle is $50^\circ$. Find the other interior opposite angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The two base angles are equal, and the three angles add to $180^\circ$. So each base
   angle is $(180^\circ - 40^\circ) \div 2 = 70^\circ$.
2. The exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles, so the other
   one is $120^\circ - 50^\circ = 70^\circ$.

Both answers are the tools used to prove the angle at the centre theorem in Section 17.7.
The isosceles triangle gives the two equal base angles, and the exterior angle adds them.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** $O$ is the centre of a circle. $A$ and $B$ are points of the circle, and
$|\angle AOB| = 110^\circ$. $C$ is a point on the major arc. Find $|\angle ACB|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circle on the same arc.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the angle at the centre by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle ACB| = 110^\circ \div 2 = 55^\circ.$$

The two angles stand on the same arc, so the angle at the circle is half the angle at the
centre.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** $BC$ is a diameter of a circle and $A$ is another point of the circle. Given
$|\angle ABC| = 62^\circ$, find $|\angle BAC|$ and $|\angle ACB|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The three angles of the triangle add to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle BAC| = 90^\circ,$$

because it is the angle in a semicircle.

$$|\angle ACB| = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 62^\circ = 28^\circ.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** $A$, $B$, $C$ and $D$ lie on a circle, and $A$ and $D$ are on the same side of the
chord $BC$. Given $|\angle BAC| = 37^\circ$, find $|\angle BDC|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two angles stand on the same chord and in the same segment.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Angles in the same segment are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle BDC| = 37^\circ,$$

by Corollary 2. Both angles stand on the arc $BC$ and lie on the same side of $BC$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** An arc of a circle stands at $25^\circ$ at a point of the circle. Find the angle it
stands at the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle at the centre is the larger one, so multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2 \times 25^\circ.$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle AOB| = 2 \times 25^\circ = 50^\circ.$$

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circle on the same arc.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** $O$ is the centre of a circle and $|\angle AOB| = 100^\circ$. The point $C$ lies on
the minor arc $AB$. Find $|\angle ACB|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle $\angle ACB$ stands on the minor arc, so the angle at the centre on that arc is
the reflex angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The reflex angle is $360^\circ - 100^\circ = 260^\circ$. Halve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$360^\circ - 100^\circ = 260^\circ$$

$$|\angle ACB| = 260^\circ \div 2 = 130^\circ.$$

The two possible answers on this circle are $50^\circ$ and $130^\circ$, and they add to
$180^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why the angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A diameter is a straight line through the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle at the centre standing on the diameter is a straight angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $BC$ be a diameter and let $A$ be another point of the circle. The angle at the centre
standing on the arc $BC$ is the straight angle along the diameter, which is $180^\circ$.

By Theorem 19, the angle at the circle is half the angle at the centre:
$$|\angle BAC| = 180^\circ \div 2 = 90^\circ.$$

So every angle in a semicircle is a right angle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 17.1

**7.** A chord of a circle is $16$ cm long and lies $6$ cm from the centre. Find the
radius, then write down the length of a diameter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use half the chord and Pythagoras, with $r$ the radius.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$r^2 = 6^2 + 8^2.$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$r^2 = 36 + 64 = 100$$

$$r = 10 \text{ cm}.$$

A diameter is twice a radius, so a diameter is $20$ cm. If a triangle is drawn on that
diameter, the angle in the semicircle is $90^\circ$, by Corollary 3.

</details>
</details>
</details>
