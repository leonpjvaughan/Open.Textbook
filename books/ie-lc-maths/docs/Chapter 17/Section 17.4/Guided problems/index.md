# 17.4 Tangents to a circle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of $26$ cm and one side of $10$ cm. Find the
   third side.
2. Two triangles each have a right angle, equal hypotenuses, and one pair of equal sides.
   Are the two triangles congruent?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $26^2 - 10^2 = 676 - 100 = 576$, and $\sqrt{576} = 24$ cm. Pythagoras' theorem gives
   the third side.
2. Yes. With the hypotenuse and one side equal, the third side is equal by Pythagoras, so
   the triangles are congruent by SSS. This test is the one used for the two tangents from
   a point.

Both answers come from Pythagoras, and both are used in this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A circle has radius $5$ cm. A point $T$ is $13$ cm from the centre. Find the length
of the tangent from $T$ to the circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The radius to the point of contact is perpendicular to the tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The tangent, the radius and the line to the centre make a right-angled triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|TU|^2 + 5^2 = 13^2$$

$$|TU|^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$$

$$|TU| = 12 \text{ cm}.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A tangent from a point $T$ to a circle has length $24$ cm. The radius is $7$ cm.
Find the distance from $T$ to the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The distance from $T$ to the centre is the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$|OT|^2 = 7^2 + 24^2.$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|OT|^2 = 49 + 576 = 625$$

$$|OT| = 25 \text{ cm}.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A circle has radius $9$ cm. A point $T$ is $41$ cm from the centre. Find the length
of the tangent from $T$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the right-angled triangle with the radius and the tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$|TU|^2 = 41^2 - 9^2.$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|TU|^2 = 1681 - 81 = 1600$$

$$|TU| = 40 \text{ cm}.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The two tangents from a point $T$ touch a circle at $U$ and at $V$. Given
$|\angle UTV| = 84^\circ$, find $|\angle OTV|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The line from $T$ to the centre bisects the angle between the two tangents.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Halve $84^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle OTV| = 84^\circ \div 2 = 42^\circ.$$

The two triangles $OUT$ and $OVT$ are congruent, so the two angles at $T$ are equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain why a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Suppose the angle is not $90^\circ$. Reflect the point of contact in the foot of the
perpendicular from the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The reflected point is the same distance from the centre, so it is also on the circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the tangent $l$ touch the circle with centre $O$ at $P$, and suppose $OP$ is not
perpendicular to $l$. Let $Q$ be the foot of the perpendicular from $O$ to $l$, and let
$R$ be the point of $l$ on the other side of $Q$ from $P$ with $|QR| = |QP|$.

The triangles $OQP$ and $OQR$ are congruent by SAS, so $|OR| = |OP|$. The point $R$ is
therefore on the circle as well as $P$, so $l$ meets the circle at two points. That
contradicts the definition of a tangent, so $OP$ must be perpendicular to $l$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Show that the two tangents from a point outside a circle are equal in length.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both tangents make a right angle with a radius, and both radii are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the common side $OT$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the tangents from $T$ touch the circle at $U$ and at $V$. By part 1 of Theorem 20,
$OU$ is perpendicular to $TU$ and $OV$ is perpendicular to $TV$.

The triangles $OUT$ and $OVT$ have a right angle at $U$ and at $V$, the radii
$|OU| = |OV|$, and the side $OT$ common. Two right-angled triangles with the hypotenuse and
one other side equal are congruent, so $|TU| = |TV|$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 17.1

**7.** A chord of a circle is $48$ cm long and lies $7$ cm from the centre. Find the
radius. A tangent is then drawn at one end of the chord. Explain why the radius to that
end is perpendicular to the tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use half the chord for the first part.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The tangent result is part 1 of Theorem 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The half-chord is $24$ cm, so $r^2 = 7^2 + 24^2 = 625$ and $r = 25$ cm.

The tangent touches the circle at the end of the chord, so that point is the point of
contact. By part 1 of Theorem 20, the tangent is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the
point of contact. So the radius to that end of the chord is perpendicular to the tangent.

</details>
</details>
</details>
