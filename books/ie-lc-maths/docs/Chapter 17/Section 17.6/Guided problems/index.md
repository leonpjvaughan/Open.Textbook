# 17.6 Constructions 16–22 — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Name the construction from Section 15.7 that bisects a given angle.
2. A point is the same distance from $A$ as it is from $B$. On which line does it lie?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. Construction 1. It draws an arc from the vertex, then two matching arcs from the two
   arms, and joins the vertex to the crossing point.
2. It lies on the perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$, by Property 2 of the perpendicular
   bisector from Section 15.6.

Both answers are used in this section. The angle bisector gives the incentre, and the
perpendicular bisector gives the circumcentre.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Describe how to construct the circumcircle of a triangle, and say why it works.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need two perpendicular bisectors.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Their crossing point is the same distance from all three vertices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Construct the perpendicular bisector of one side, then of a second side. The two cross at
the circumcentre $O$.

Draw the circle with centre $O$ through one vertex. It passes through the other two,
because $O$ is the same distance from all three vertices.

It works because the perpendicular bisectors of a triangle meet at one point, by
Proposition 17 from Section 17.5.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Describe how to construct the tangent to a circle at a point $P$ on it, and say why
the line is a tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw the radius to $P$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Construct the perpendicular to the radius at $P$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw the radius $OP$. Construct the line through $P$ perpendicular to $OP$, using
Construction 4.

The line is a tangent, by part 2 of Theorem 20: a line through a point of a circle that is
perpendicular to the radius at that point touches the circle at that point only.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Describe how to construct an angle of $60^\circ$ without a protractor, and say why
the angle is $60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use two arcs drawn with the same compass opening.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The three points make an equilateral triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw an arc from $A$ that cuts the given ray at $B$. With the same opening, draw an arc
from $B$ that cuts the first arc at $C$. Draw the ray $[AC$.

The three lengths $|AB|$, $|AC|$ and $|BC|$ are all the same compass opening, so the
triangle $ABC$ is equilateral. Its three angles are equal and add to $180^\circ$, so each
one is $60^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Describe how to construct the incircle of a triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need two angle bisectors and one perpendicular.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The perpendicular from the incentre to a side is the radius.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Construct the bisector of the angle at $A$, then of the angle at $B$. They cross at the
incentre $I$.

Drop the perpendicular from $I$ to one side, using Construction 3. With centre $I$ and
that perpendicular as radius, draw the circle.

It works because the angle bisectors meet at one point, by Proposition 18, and that point
is the same perpendicular distance from all three sides.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Describe how to construct the centroid of a triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need two medians.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A median joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Construct the midpoint of one side, and draw the median from the opposite vertex to it.
Construct the midpoint of a second side, and draw the median from the opposite vertex.

The two medians cross at the centroid $G$. The third median passes through the same point,
by Proposition 19.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A triangle has a right angle at $A$ and hypotenuse $BC$. Describe the shortest
construction for its circumcircle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The circumcentre of a right-angled triangle is the midpoint of the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

One perpendicular bisector is enough, on $BC$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Construct the perpendicular bisector of $BC$. It meets $BC$ at its midpoint, which is the
circumcentre.

Draw the circle with that centre through $B$. It passes through $A$ and $C$ as well,
because the midpoint of the hypotenuse is the same distance from all three vertices.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 15.7

**7.** Construction 22 needs the perpendicular from a vertex to the opposite side. The
vertex is not on that side. Which construction from Section 15.7 is used, and what does it
produce?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The point is off the line, not on it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The construction draws an arc from the point that cuts the line twice, then two matching
arcs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Construction 3 draws the perpendicular to a line through a point not on the line.

It draws an arc centred on the point that cuts the line at two points $A$ and $B$. Then it
draws two arcs of the same opening from $A$ and from $B$, crossing at $Q$. The line from
the given point through $Q$ is the perpendicular.

The perpendicular is the altitude of the triangle from that vertex.

</details>
</details>
</details>
