# Chapter 17: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The round-tower window.** A stonemason is cutting a circular window of radius $25$ cm,
with centre $O$. The design is set out from the circle, and every measurement the mason
needs comes from the theorems of this chapter.

### The predictions, checked

Two predictions were made on the chapter opener.

- The triangle $ABC$ has $BC$ along a diameter, with $|AB| = 30$ cm and $|AC| = 40$ cm.
  The prediction was the size of the angle at $A$. The angle is $90^\circ$, because every
  angle in a semicircle is a right angle.
- The two bars are $40$ cm and $48$ cm long. The prediction was which one lies closer to
  the centre. The $48$ cm bar lies $7$ cm from the centre, and the $40$ cm bar lies $15$ cm
  from the centre. The **$48$ cm bar** is closer.

### The bar across the window

The bar is a chord of length $48$ cm. The perpendicular from the centre bisects it, so the
half-chord is $24$ cm. The distance $d$ from the centre is one leg of a right-angled
triangle with hypotenuse $25$ cm:

$$d^2 + 24^2 = 25^2$$

$$d^2 = 625 - 576 = 49$$

$$d = 7 \text{ cm}.$$

The bar is $7$ cm from the centre. This is Theorem 21 part 1, from Section 17.1.

### The triangular pane

The pane is the triangle $ABC$ with $|AB| = 30$ cm, $|AC| = 40$ cm and $|BC| = 50$ cm.
Check the right angle with the converse of Pythagoras:

$$30^2 + 40^2 = 900 + 1600 = 2500 = 50^2.$$

So the angle at $A$ is a right angle. The chord $BC$ is a diameter of the window, by
Corollary 4, and the angle at $A$ is the angle in a semicircle. Either route gives

$$|\angle BAC| = 90^\circ.$$

The other two angles follow from the angle sum of a triangle:
$$|\angle ABC| + |\angle ACB| = 90^\circ.$$

The centre $O$ is the circumcentre of the triangle, because $BC$ is a diameter and the
midpoint of a diameter is the centre. The circumradius is $25$ cm, which is the radius of
the window. This is Section 17.5.

### The four-sided pane

The pane $PQRS$ has its four corners on the circle, so it is a cyclic quadrilateral. The
angle at $Q$ is $76^\circ$. The angles at $Q$ and at $S$ are opposite, so they sum to
$180^\circ$:

$$|\angle PSR| = 180^\circ - 76^\circ = 104^\circ.$$

The exterior angle at $Q$ is also $104^\circ$, because the exterior angle of a cyclic
quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle. This is Section 17.3.

### The support rod

The rod touches the rim at $U$ and is fixed at $T$, with $|OT| = 65$ cm. The radius $OU$
is perpendicular to the rod at the point of contact, by Theorem 20 part 1. So the triangle
$OUT$ has a right angle at $U$:

$$|TU|^2 + 25^2 = 65^2$$

$$|TU|^2 = 4225 - 625 = 3600$$

$$|TU| = 60 \text{ cm}.$$

The second rod from $T$ on the other side of the window is also $60$ cm long, because the
two tangents from an external point are equal. This is Section 17.4.

### The two crossing bars

Two lead bars $AB$ and $CD$ cross at $P$ inside the window, with $|PA| = 6$ cm,
$|PB| = 8$ cm and $|PC| = 4$ cm. The triangles $APC$ and $DPB$ are similar, so the product
of the parts of one chord equals the product of the parts of the other:

$$6 \times 8 = 4 \times |PD|$$

$$|PD| = 12 \text{ cm}.$$

The scale factor of the enlargement centred at $P$ that takes $APC$ to $DPB$ is
$-12 \div 6 = -2$. This is Section 17.8.

### The setting-out drawing

The mason draws the circle with a compass, constructs the circumcircle of the pane with
construction 16, and draws the tangent at a point of the rim with construction 19. The
first $60^\circ$ of the repeating design is construction 18. Every construction rests on a
result proved in this chapter or an earlier one, and Section 17.6 lists each one.

### The proof behind it all

The angle in a semicircle, which the whole design uses, is Corollary 3. It follows in two
lines from Theorem 19, the angle at the centre, and that proof is written out in full in
Section 17.7.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, say **which fact or theorem** you will use,
and why, before you calculate. Some need a chord, some an angle, some a tangent, and some
come from an earlier chapter.

1. A chord of a circle is $24$ cm long and the radius is $13$ cm. Find the distance from
   the centre to the chord.
2. $O$ is the centre of a circle and $|\angle AOB| = 128^\circ$. $C$ is a point on the
   major arc. Find $|\angle ACB|$.
3. A cyclic quadrilateral has two opposite angles of $4x$ and $5x$. Find $x$.
4. A tangent from $T$ to a circle has length $20$ cm, and the radius is $15$ cm. Find
   $|OT|$.
5. A triangle has a right angle at $A$ and a hypotenuse of $34$ cm. Find the circumradius.
6. Two chords cross at $P$ inside a circle, with $|PA| = 4$ cm, $|PB| = 9$ cm and
   $|PC| = 3$ cm. Find $|PD|$.
7. A circle has radius $25$ cm. A chord of length $30$ cm is drawn. Find its distance from
   the centre.
8. $BC$ is a diameter of a circle and $|\angle ABC| = 24^\circ$. Find $|\angle ACB|$.
9. From Chapter 15: an isosceles triangle has base angles of $65^\circ$. Find the apex
   angle.
10. From Chapter 16: a triangle has sides $7$ cm, $24$ cm and $25$ cm. Show that it is
    right-angled.
11. From Chapter 15: two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and one angle is
    $118^\circ$. Find the alternate angle.
12. From Chapter 12: find the circumference of a circle of radius $10$ cm, in terms of
    $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and reasons</summary>

1. $5$ cm. The half-chord is $12$ cm, and $\sqrt{13^2 - 12^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$, by
   Theorem 21.
2. $64^\circ$. The angle at the circle is half the angle at the centre on the same arc.
3. $x = 20$. The opposite angles sum to $180^\circ$, so $4x + 5x = 180$ and $9x = 180$.
4. $25$ cm. The radius is perpendicular to the tangent, so
   $|OT| = \sqrt{15^2 + 20^2} = \sqrt{625} = 25$.
5. $17$ cm. The circumcentre of a right-angled triangle is the midpoint of the hypotenuse,
   so the circumradius is half of $34$.
6. $12$ cm. The product of the parts of one chord equals the product of the parts of the
   other: $4 \times 9 = 3 \times |PD|$, so $|PD| = 12$.
7. $20$ cm. The half-chord is $15$ cm, and $\sqrt{25^2 - 15^2} = \sqrt{400} = 20$.
8. $66^\circ$. The angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$, so
   $|\angle ACB| = 180^\circ - 90^\circ - 24^\circ = 66^\circ$.
9. $50^\circ$. The two base angles are equal, so
   $180^\circ - 2 \times 65^\circ = 50^\circ$.
10. $7^2 + 24^2 = 49 + 576 = 625 = 25^2$, so the angle opposite the $25$ cm side is a
    right angle, by the converse of Pythagoras' theorem.
11. $118^\circ$. Alternate angles at parallel lines are equal, by Theorem 3 part 2.
12. $2\pi r = 20\pi$ cm.

</details>

## What you can now do

- name the parts of a circle, and use the radius, the chord and the tangent;
- use the perpendicular from the centre to a chord, and find the centre from a chord;
- use the angle at the centre, the angle in a semicircle and the angles in the same
  segment;
- use the opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral, and the converse as a test;
- use the tangent and the radius at the point of contact, and find a tangent length;
- find the circumcentre, the incentre, the centroid and the orthocentre of a triangle;
- carry out constructions 16 to 22 with a straight edge and compass;
- prove the angle at the centre theorem, and prove the tangent result by contradiction;
- use similar triangles formed by two chords that cross inside a circle.

## Thinking back

Return to the connection you wrote at the start of the chapter. You chose one round object
and one measurement the maker would need.

You can now say which theorem makes that measurement possible. Find your object in the
list below, or match it to the nearest one, and write the theorem beside it.

- If the measurement is **from the centre to a straight bar or a chord**, the theorem is
  Theorem 21. The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, and the right-angled
  triangle links the radius, the half-chord and the distance.
- If the measurement is **an angle at the rim**, the theorem is Theorem 19, with its
  corollaries. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circle, and the angle in a
  semicircle is a right angle.
- If the measurement is **where a straight edge just touches the rim**, the theorem is
  Theorem 20. The radius is perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact.
- If the measurement is **a length found from two bars that cross**, the theorem is the
  crossing-chords rule from Section 17.8.

Write the sentence in the same shape as the chapter problem: the measurement, then the
theorem that makes it possible. That sentence is what this chapter was for. Every round
object is set out from its centre, and you can now say why.
