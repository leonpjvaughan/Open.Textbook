# Chapter 6: Functions

A function is a rule that turns each input into exactly one output. You have met rules
like this all year: the area of a circle from its radius, the cost of a journey from its
length, the temperature of a drink from the time it has stood. This chapter is about what
makes a rule a function, how to describe it, how to combine two of them, and how to draw
and read its graph.

## The chapter problem

**The cooling coffee.** A cup of coffee is poured at $90^\circ C$ in a room at $20^\circ C$.
The gap between the coffee and the room halves every $5$ minutes, so the temperature after
$t$ minutes is

$$T(t) = 20 + 70(0.5)^{t/5}.$$

The coffee is comfortable to drink at $50^\circ C$. How long do you wait?

By the end of the chapter you can answer that question, explain why the rule gives exactly
one temperature for each time, and turn the rule around to give the time for a chosen
temperature. The rule is a function, and its graph is an exponential curve.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- tell a relation from a function, and use the vertical line test;
- write the domain and range of a function, and use function notation;
- form composite functions and find inverse functions;
- explain what injective, surjective and bijective mean;
- graph a function and describe the transformations that move it;
- work with exponential and logarithmic functions, and solve $a^x = b$.

## Where it is used

Functions describe how one quantity depends on another. A cooling drink, a charging phone,
a loan that gathers interest, the height of a ball, and the number of people who have heard
a rumour all follow a function of time. Graphs of functions are how scientists, engineers,
economists and doctors read a trend at a glance, and inverse functions are how they answer
"when?" as well as "how much?".

## Your turn to connect it

Think of a quantity that changes quickly at first and then settles — a hot drink cooling, a
cold room warming, a phone charging, the water level in a draining sink.

Choose one. Write down what the quantity is, what it starts at, and what it settles
towards. You will be able to describe it as a function by the end of the chapter, and say
how long it takes to reach a chosen value.
