# 6.5 Graphing functions and transformations — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out the value of $x^2$ when $x = 3$.
2. What shape is the graph of $y = x^2$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3^2 = 9$. The point $(3, 9)$ is on the curve.
2. A parabola, opening upwards, with its lowest point at the origin.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** The graph of $y = x^2$ is shifted up by $4$. Write the new rule and work out $y$ when
$x = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A shift up adds to the output.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The new rule is $y = x^2 + 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At $x = 1$: $y = 1^2 + 4 = 5$. Every point has risen by $4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** The graph of $y = x^2$ is shifted right by $2$. Write the new rule and work out $y$
when $x = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A shift right replaces $x$ by $x - 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The new rule is $y = (x - 2)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At $x = 4$: $y = (4 - 2)^2 = 4$. The old graph reached $4$ at $x = 2$, so the graph moved
$2$ to the right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The graph of $y = x^2$ is stretched vertically by a factor of $3$. Write the new rule
and work out $y$ when $x = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A vertical stretch multiplies the output.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The new rule is $y = 3x^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At $x = 2$: $y = 3(2^2) = 12$. Every output is three times its old value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The graph of $y = x^2$ is reflected in the $x$-axis. Write the new rule and work out
$y$ when $x = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A reflection in the $x$-axis changes the sign of every output.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The new rule is $y = -x^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At $x = 3$: $y = -(3^2) = -9$. The parabola now opens downwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** The graph of $y = (0.5)^t$ is stretched horizontally by a factor of $2$. Write the
new rule.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Replace $t$ by $t/2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$y = (0.5)^{t/2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The new rule is $y = (0.5)^{t/2}$. The curve now takes twice as long to fall to the same
height, because the input is halved before the power is taken.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** The coffee graph is $T(t) = 20 + 70(0.5)^{t/5}$. State its horizontal asymptote and
explain what it means.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

As $t$ grows, what happens to $(0.5)^{t/5}$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(0.5)^{t/5}$ shrinks towards $0$, so $T$ approaches $20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The horizontal asymptote is $T = 20$. The coffee cools towards the room temperature and
never falls below it. The value $20$ is the room temperature, not a temperature the coffee
reaches.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** For a function $f$, describe what the graph of $y = f(x + 2)$ looks like compared
with the graph of $y = f(x)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A change inside the bracket moves the graph the opposite way to the sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The value that used to happen at $x$ now happens at $x - 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The graph shifts left by $2$. Each point moves two places to the left. The rule adds $2$ to
the input, so the input must be smaller to produce the same output.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** Expand $(x - 3)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the two brackets, or use the square of a difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(x - 3)^2 = (x - 3)(x - 3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(x - 3)^2 = x^2 - 6x + 9$$

This is the rule of the graph in question 2, written in expanded form.

</details>
</details>
</details>
