## Practise with help

**1.** Find the length of $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square all three components, add, then take the square root.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{1 + 4 + 4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\left|\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 2 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}\right| = \sqrt{1^2 + 2^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{1 + 4 + 4} = \sqrt{9} = 3$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Write the displacement from $P(2, 1, 3)$ to $Q(5, 4, 1)$ as a column vector.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract each coordinate of $P$ from the matching coordinate of $Q$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The three components are $5 - 2$, $4 - 1$ and $1 - 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\vec{PQ} = \begin{pmatrix} 5 - 2 \\ 4 - 1 \\ 1 - 3 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$$

The negative third component means the displacement goes downwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ -1 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix} + \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add each of the three rows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The rows are $2 + 1$, $-1 + 4$ and $3 - 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\begin{pmatrix} 2 + 1 \\ -1 + 4 \\ 3 - 2 \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} \cdot \begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are three products to add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2 \times 3 + 1 \times (-2) + 4 \times 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6 - 2 + 4 = 8$$

The answer is positive, so the angle between the vectors is acute.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Show that $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$ and
$\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ -1 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$ are perpendicular.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the dot product.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2 \times 1 + 1 \times (-1) + 1 \times (-1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2 - 1 - 1 = 0$$

The dot product is zero, so the vectors are perpendicular.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Find the angle between $\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$ and
$\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The dot product and the two lengths give the cosine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The dot product is $1$, and the lengths are $\sqrt{2}$ and $1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\cos\theta = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2} \times 1} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}$$

So $\theta = 45^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 12

**7.** A cuboid has side lengths $2$ cm, $3$ cm and $6$ cm. Find the length of its space
diagonal, from one corner to the opposite corner.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diagonal is a three-dimensional vector with the three side lengths as its components.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{2^2 + 3^2 + 6^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The space diagonal is the length of the vector
$\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$ cm, so

$$\sqrt{2^2 + 3^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7 \text{ cm}$$

This is Pythagoras' theorem in three dimensions, and it is the same formula as the length
of a three-dimensional vector.

</details>
</details>
</details>
