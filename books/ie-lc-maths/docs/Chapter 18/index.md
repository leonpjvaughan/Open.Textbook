# Chapter 18: Vectors

A quantity such as a temperature needs one number. A quantity such as a journey needs two
numbers, because the direction matters as much as the distance. This chapter is about
those two-number quantities, how to add them, how to measure the angle between them, and
how to use them to prove facts about shapes.

## The chapter problem

**The drone's three legs.** A delivery drone leaves its depot at $O$ and flies two legs.

- The first leg has displacement $\mathbf{a} = \begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$ km.
- The second leg has displacement $\mathbf{b} = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 6 \end{pmatrix}$ km.

The drone then returns straight to the depot.

By the end of the chapter you can find the return leg, show that the route is
right-angled and isosceles, work out the total distance flown, and say what the angle at
the depot is.<!-- tier:h --> You can also find the area of the triangle the route
encloses.<!-- /tier:h --> The answer to the last question is a pleasant one, and it
follows from the right angle.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- describe a vector and write it as a column, or with $\mathbf{i}$ and $\mathbf{j}$;
- find the length of a vector and the unit vector in its direction;
- add and subtract vectors, and multiply a vector by a scalar;
- work out the dot product of two vectors and use its properties;
- test two vectors for perpendicularity and find the angle between them;
- use vectors to prove that a triangle has a right angle.

<!-- tier:h -->

- extend every one of these ideas to three dimensions;
- use vectors to prove that a shape is a parallelogram, and that points are collinear.

<!-- /tier:h -->

## Where it is used

Navigation and flight planning, where a heading and a speed are added as vectors.
Engineering, where forces combine. Computer graphics and games, where every position,
velocity and light direction is a vector. Physics, where displacement, velocity,
acceleration and force all carry a direction. Any time two quantities have to be combined
with their directions kept, vectors are the language.

## Your turn to connect it

Think of a journey you make often, or a force you have felt — a push, a current, a wind.
Write down the two parts of it: how far, and in which direction. Then write down what it
would mean to combine two of them.

You will be able to say something precise about that by the end of the chapter, and to
check it with the dot product.
