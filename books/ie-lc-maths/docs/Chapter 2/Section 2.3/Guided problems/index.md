# 2.3 Income tax, USC and net pay — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $30\%$ of €4,000.
2. Increase €500 by $6\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4\,000 \times 0.30 = 1\,200$. The answer is €1,200.
2. $500 \times 1.06 = 530$. The answer is €530.

Both are percentage calculations from Section 2.1, and every deduction in this
section is one of them.

</details>

## Practise with help

Throughout this page, use these rates: income tax at $20\%$ on the first €44,000
and $40\%$ on the rest; a tax credit of €3,000; USC at $2\%$ on the first €12,000
and $4\%$ on the rest.

**1.** A worker earns €24,000. Work out the income tax.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The whole income is below the €44,000 cut-off, so all of it is taxed at $20\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$24\,000 \times 0.20 - 3\,000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$24\,000 \times 0.20 = 4\,800$$

$$4\,800 - 3\,000 = 1\,800$$

The worker pays €1,800. The tax credit is taken off after the percentage is worked
out.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A worker earns €48,000. Work out the income tax.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Split the income at €44,000. The €4,000 above the cut-off is taxed at $40\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$44\,000 \times 0.20 + 4\,000 \times 0.40$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$44\,000 \times 0.20 = 8\,800$$

$$4\,000 \times 0.40 = 1\,600$$

$$8\,800 + 1\,600 = 10\,400$$

$$10\,400 - 3\,000 = 7\,400$$

The worker pays €7,400.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Work out the USC on an income of €20,000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first €12,000 is charged at $2\%$ and the €8,000 above it at $4\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12\,000 \times 0.02 + 8\,000 \times 0.04$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$12\,000 \times 0.02 = 240$$

$$8\,000 \times 0.04 = 320$$

$$240 + 320 = 560$$

The USC is €560. Charging $4\%$ on the whole €20,000 would give €800, which is too
much.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A worker earns €36,000. Work out the net pay.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the income tax and the USC separately, then subtract both from €36,000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The income tax is $36\,000 \times 0.20 - 3\,000$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$36\,000 \times 0.20 = 7\,200$$

$$7\,200 - 3\,000 = 4\,200$$

The USC is

$$12\,000 \times 0.02 + 24\,000 \times 0.04 = 240 + 960 = 1\,200$$

Then

$$36\,000 - 4\,200 - 1\,200 = 30\,600$$

The net pay is €30,600.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** The same worker also pays €1,200 a year into a pension. Work out the net
pay.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A pension is another deduction, so subtract it in the same way as the taxes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The income tax is €4,200 and the USC is €1,200, from the previous question.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$36\,000 - 4\,200 - 1\,200 - 1\,200 = 29\,400$$

The net pay is €29,400. A deduction does not have to be a tax to come off the
gross pay.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why a worker on €52,000 does not pay $40\%$ of €52,000 in income
tax.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Where is the income split?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The split is at the €44,000 cut-off, so the €8,000 above it is the top slice.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The €52,000 is split at the cut-off. The first €44,000 is taxed at $20\%$, giving
€8,800. Only the €8,000 above the cut-off is taxed at $40\%$, giving €3,200. The
tax before the credit is €12,000.

Charging $40\%$ on the whole €52,000 would give €20,800, and that is far too much.
The higher rate applies to the top slice only.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**7.** The oven adds €3,000 of extra profit in a year. The owner's income is below
the cut-off, so the extra profit is taxed at $20\%$, and the USC is $2\%$. Work out
the income tax, the USC and the profit that is left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both rates apply to the whole €3,000, because it is below both cut-offs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3\,000 \times 0.20 \quad \text{and} \quad 3\,000 \times 0.02$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3\,000 \times 0.20 = 600$$

$$3\,000 \times 0.02 = 60$$

$$3\,000 - 600 - 60 = 2\,340$$

The bakery keeps €2,340 of the €3,000. Section 2.6 uses this figure to work out
how long the oven takes to pay for itself.

</details>
</details>
</details>
