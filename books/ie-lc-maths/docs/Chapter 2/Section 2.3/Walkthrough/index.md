# 2.3 Income tax, USC and net pay — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 2.1 — percentages and multipliers.

**Objectives:** work out income tax from a standard rate, a higher rate and a tax
credit; work out USC; work out net pay from gross pay.

By the end of this section you can take a gross wage and work down to the amount
that reaches a bank account.

## Gross pay, deductions and net pay

- **Gross pay** is the pay before anything is taken off.
- A **deduction** is an amount taken off the pay.
- **Net pay** is what is left after every deduction.

$$\text{net pay} = \text{gross pay} - \text{income tax} - \text{USC} - \text{other deductions}$$

The two taxes in this section are **income tax** and the **Universal Social
Charge** (USC). Other deductions include a pension contribution or a union
subscription.

The rates change from year to year. A question always gives you the rates to use,
so read the table before you start. This section uses one set of rates throughout:

| Charge | Rate |
|---|---|
| Income tax, first €44,000 | 20% |
| Income tax, the balance above €44,000 | 40% |
| Tax credit | €3,000 |
| USC, first €12,000 | 2% |
| USC, the balance above €12,000 | 4% |

## Income tax

Income tax is charged in bands. The **standard rate** applies up to the cut-off,
and the **higher rate** applies to the income above it.

**Example 1.** A worker earns €30,000. Work out the income tax.

The whole €30,000 is below the €44,000 cut-off, so all of it is taxed at the
standard rate:

$$30\,000 \times 0.20 = 6\,000$$

The tax before the credit is €6,000. The **tax credit** is then taken off:

$$6\,000 - 3\,000 = 3\,000$$

The worker pays €3,000.

**Example 2.** A worker earns €52,000. Work out the income tax.

Split the income at the cut-off. The first €44,000 is taxed at $20\%$ and the
remaining €8,000 at $40\%$.

$$44\,000 \times 0.20 = 8\,800$$

$$8\,000 \times 0.40 = 3\,200$$

$$8\,800 + 3\,200 = 12\,000$$

The tax before the credit is €12,000. After the €3,000 credit:

$$12\,000 - 3\,000 = 9\,000$$

The worker pays €9,000.

## USC

USC is charged on gross income in bands. Each band has its own rate, and only the
income inside a band is charged at that band's rate.

**Example 3.** Work out the USC on an income of €30,000.

The first €12,000 is charged at $2\%$ and the remaining €18,000 at $4\%$:

$$12\,000 \times 0.02 = 240$$

$$18\,000 \times 0.04 = 720$$

$$240 + 720 = 960$$

The USC is €960.

## Net pay

Work out each deduction, then subtract all of them from the gross pay.

**Example 4.** A worker earns €52,000. Work out the net pay.

From Example 2, the income tax is €9,000. The USC is

$$12\,000 \times 0.02 = 240$$

$$40\,000 \times 0.04 = 1\,600$$

$$240 + 1\,600 = 1\,840$$

Then

$$52\,000 - 9\,000 - 1\,840 = 41\,160$$

The net pay is €41,160.

**Example 5.** The chapter problem. The bakery makes €3,000 of extra profit. The
owner's income is below the cut-off, so the extra profit is taxed at $20\%$, and
the USC is $2\%$.

$$\text{income tax} = 3\,000 \times 0.20 = 600$$

$$\text{USC} = 3\,000 \times 0.02 = 60$$

$$3\,000 - 600 - 60 = 2\,340$$

The bakery keeps €2,340 of the €3,000.

> **Watch out.** The higher rate does not apply to the whole income. It applies
> only to the part above the cut-off. A worker on €52,000 does not pay
> $40\%$ of €52,000, which would be €20,800; the correct figure is €12,000 before
> the credit. Read "the rest" or "the balance" as the top slice, and split the
> income at the cut-off before you multiply. The same rule holds for the USC
> bands.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The oven adds €3,000 of profit in a year. The owner
pays income tax at $20\%$ and USC at $2\%$ on it.

Work out the income tax, the USC and the profit that is left. Then work out how
many years of that net profit the oven costs, if the oven costs €11,070.

Keep the €2,340. Section 2.6 uses it to check the payback time.

## You should now be able to

- tell gross pay, deductions and net pay apart;
- work out income tax at the standard rate only;
- split an income at the cut-off and charge the higher rate on the top slice;
- take a tax credit off the tax due;
- work out USC in bands;
- subtract every deduction to find the net pay.
