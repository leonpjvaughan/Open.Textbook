# 2.4 Simple and compound interest — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $5\%$ of €300.
2. Increase €400 by $5\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $300 \times 0.05 = 15$. The answer is €15.
2. $400 \times 1.05 = 420$. The answer is €420.

The second answer adds the $5\%$ to the original. Compound interest does that
again and again.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** €1,500 is invested at $3\%$ simple interest for 2 years. Work out the
interest and the total value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Simple interest uses $I = P \times r \times t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1\,500 \times 0.03 \times 2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1\,500 \times 0.03 \times 2 = 90$$

$$1\,500 + 90 = 1\,590$$

The interest is €90 and the total is €1,590. Each year adds €45.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** €1,500 is invested at $3\%$ compound interest for 2 years. Work out the
total value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compound interest uses a power: $A = P(1 + i)^t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1\,500 \times 1.03^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1\,500 \times 1.03^2 = 1\,591.35$$

The total is €1,591.35. It is €1.35 more than the simple interest total, because
the second year's $3\%$ is taken from €1,545 and not from €1,500.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** €8,000 is invested at $5\%$ compound interest for 3 years. Work out the
total value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $1.05$ once for each of the three years.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8\,000 \times 1.05^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8\,000 \times 1.05^3 = 9\,261$$

The total is €9,261. The interest is €1,261.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out how much more €1,500 earns at $3\%$ compound interest than at
$3\%$ simple interest over 2 years.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You worked out both totals in questions 1 and 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1\,591.35 - 1\,590$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1\,591.35 - 1\,590 = 1.35$$

Compound interest earns €1.35 more. The gap is small over two years and grows
quickly over more.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** An investment is worth €9,261 after 3 years at $5\%$ compound interest.
Work out the amount invested.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the final value by $1.05^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$9\,261 \div 1.05^3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9\,261 \div 1.05^3 = 8\,000$$

The amount invested was €8,000. Dividing by the growth factor undoes compound
interest.

</details>
</details>
</details>

<!-- tier:o -->

**6.** A machine worth €20,000 loses $10\%$ of its value each year. Work out its
value after 2 years.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Losing $10\%$ leaves $90\%$, so the multiplier is $0.90$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$20\,000 \times 0.90^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$20\,000 \times 0.90^2 = 16\,200$$

The machine is worth €16,200 after two years. Depreciation works like compound
interest with a multiplier below $1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**7.** Niamh has €11,070 in savings that earn $3\%$ compound interest a year.
Work out the value after 2 years.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $1.03$ once for each year.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$11\,070 \times 1.03^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$11\,070 \times 1.03^2 = 11\,744.163$$

The savings are worth €11,744.16 to the nearest cent. That is more than the
€12,000 she would pay later, but the two figures are not measured at the same
time. Section 2.5 brings the €12,000 back to today.

</details>
</details>
</details>
