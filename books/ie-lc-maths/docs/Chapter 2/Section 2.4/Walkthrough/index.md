# 2.4 Simple and compound interest — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 2.1 — percentages and multipliers; Section 1.5 —
indices.

**Objectives:** work out simple interest; work out compound interest for up to
three years; compare the two; find the original amount from a final value.

By the end of this section you can work out what a loan or a saving grows to, and
say why compound interest grows faster than simple interest.

## Simple interest

**Simple interest** is paid on the original amount only. The original amount is
called the **principal**.

> **Simple interest.** $I = P \times r \times t$, where $P$ is the principal, $r$
> is the rate as a decimal, and $t$ is the number of years.

The total value is the principal plus the interest:

$$A = P(1 + rt)$$

**Example 1.** €2,000 is invested at $4\%$ simple interest for 3 years.

$$I = 2\,000 \times 0.04 \times 3 = 240$$

$$A = 2\,000 + 240 = 2\,240$$

The interest is €240, and the total is €2,240. Each year adds the same €80.

## Compound interest

**Compound interest** is paid on the amount at the start of each year, and that
amount already contains the earlier interest. So the interest earns interest.

> **Compound interest.** $A = P(1 + i)^t$, where $i$ is the rate as a decimal and
> $t$ is the number of years.

**Example 2.** €2,000 is invested at $4\%$ compound interest for 3 years.

$$A = 2\,000 \times 1.04^3 = 2\,249.728$$

The total is €2,249.73 to the nearest cent. The interest is €249.73.

**Example 3.** Work out how much more compound interest earns than simple
interest in Examples 1 and 2.

$$2\,249.728 - 2\,240 = 9.728$$

Compound interest earns €9.73 more over three years. The extra comes from the
interest that the interest earns in years two and three.

**Example 4.** €5,000 is invested at $6\%$ compound interest for 2 years.

$$A = 5\,000 \times 1.06^2 = 5\,618$$

The total is €5,618. Year one adds €300. Year two adds $6\%$ of €5,300, which is
€318.

## Working backwards

To find the principal, divide by the growth factor.

**Example 5.** An investment is worth €5,618 after 2 years at $6\%$ compound
interest. Work out the principal.

$$P = 5\,618 \div 1.06^2 = 5\,000$$

The principal was €5,000.

**Example 6.** The chapter problem. Niamh has €11,070 in savings that earn $3\%$
compound interest a year. Work out the value after 2 years.

$$11\,070 \times 1.03^2 = 11\,744.163$$

The savings are worth €11,744.16 to the nearest cent. Section 2.5 compares this
with paying €12,000 later.

> **Watch out.** Two traps sit in this section. The first is the rate: $4\%$ is
> the decimal $0.04$, so the multiplier is $1.04$ and not $1.4$ or $4$. The
> second is the difference between the two kinds of interest. Simple interest
> adds the same amount every year, while compound interest multiplies by the same
> factor every year. A question that says "compound" must use a power. Reading
> $5\%$ over two years as $10\%$ in total is also wrong, because the second year's
> interest is larger than the first.

## Depreciation

Some things lose value instead of gaining it. A machine that loses $15\%$ of its
value each year is **depreciating** by the reducing balance method. The value
multiplies by $0.85$ each year.

**Example 7.** A machine worth €12,000 loses $15\%$ of its value each year. Work
out its value after 2 years.

$$12\,000 \times 0.85^2 = 8\,670$$

The machine is worth €8,670 after two years.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Niamh's savings hold €11,070 and earn $3\%$ compound
interest a year.

Work out the value of the savings after 2 years. Then compare it with the €12,000
she would pay if she waited. Which figure is larger?

Keep both values. Section 2.5 brings the €12,000 back to today so that the two
offers can be compared fairly.

## You should now be able to

- work out simple interest and the total value;
- work out compound interest for up to three years;
- say why compound interest grows faster than simple interest;
- find the principal from a final value by dividing by the growth factor;
- work out compound growth over time.
