# 2.6 Checking whether a result is reasonable — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out €52,000 − €9,000 − €1,840.
2. Work out €11,070 ÷ 2,340 to 2 decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $52\,000 - 9\,000 - 1\,840 = 41\,160$. The answer is €41,160.
2. $11\,070 \div 2\,340 = 4.73$ to 2 decimal places.

The first answer is a net pay from Section 2.3. The second is a payback time,
which you will check in this section.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Estimate $6\,912 \times 31$ by rounding each number to one significant
figure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Round $6\,912$ to $7\,000$ and $31$ to $30$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$7\,000 \times 30$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7\,000 \times 30 = 210\,000$$

The estimate is $210\,000$. The exact answer is $214\,272$, which is close to the
estimate, so it is the right size.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A price after a $15\%$ increase is €92. Work backwards to find the original
price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The €92 is $115\%$ of the original, so divide by $1.15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$92 \div 1.15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$92 \div 1.15 = 80$$

The original price was €80. Check by working forwards: $15\%$ of €80 is €12, and
$80 + 12 = 92$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A machine costs €8,000 and saves €2,000 a year. Work out the payback time,
and check it by working forwards.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the cost by the saving each year.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$8\,000 \div 2\,000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$8\,000 \div 2\,000 = 4$$

The machine pays for itself in 4 years. Check: $4 \times 2\,000 = 8\,000$, which
is the cost of the machine. The answer is consistent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A payslip shows gross pay of €52,000 and net pay of €41,160. Is the net pay
possible? Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What do deductions do to the gross pay?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Deductions only take money off, so the net pay must be smaller.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The net pay is possible. It is €10,840 less than the gross pay, and every
deduction makes the pay smaller. The deductions are about $21\%$ of the gross pay,
which is a sensible size for income tax, USC and a pension together.

A net pay larger than the gross pay would be impossible.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** An estimate is $210\,000$ and the exact value is $214\,272$. Work out the
percentage error, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the size of the difference by the exact value, then multiply by $100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$|210\,000 - 214\,272| = 4\,272$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{4\,272}{214\,272} \times 100 = 1.99\ldots\%$$

The percentage error is $2.0\%$ to one decimal place. A percentage error of a few
per cent is small enough to trust the size of the exact answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Two lengths are $5.6$ m and $3.8$ m, each rounded to the nearest $0.1$ m.
Work out the largest possible error in their sum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A length rounded to the nearest $0.1$ m could be out by up to $0.05$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two errors add when the lengths are added.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.05 + 0.05 = 0.1 \text{ m}$$

The sum is $5.6 + 3.8 = 9.4$ m, and it could be out by up to $0.1$ m. Errors
accumulate when the measurements are added or subtracted.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A town's population grows from $8\,000$ to $8\,600$ in 4 years. Work out the
average rate of change per year.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the total change by the number of years.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{8\,600 - 8\,000}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{600}{4} = 150$$

The population grows by $150$ people a year on average. The average does not mean
that every year grew by exactly $150$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**8.** The oven costs €11,070 and adds €2,340 of net profit a year. Work out the
payback time to one decimal place, and check it by working forwards.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the cost by the yearly profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$11\,070 \div 2\,340 = 4.73\ldots$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$11\,070 \div 2\,340 = 4.73\ldots$$

The oven pays for itself in $4.7$ years to one decimal place.

Check by working forwards: $4.7 \times 2\,340 = 10\,998$, which is just below
€11,070. The answer is the right size. Five years would give $5 \times 2\,340 =
11\,700$, which is more than the cost, so the answer lies between $4$ and $5$
years.

</details>
</details>
</details>
