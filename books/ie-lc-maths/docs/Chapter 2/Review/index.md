# Chapter 2: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The second oven.** Niamh needs an oven that costs €9,000 plus VAT at $23\%$. She
can pay now from savings that earn $3\%$ a year, or pay €12,000 in two years. The
oven adds $2\,000$ loaves a year, each sold for €4 and costing €2.50 to make. Her
income is below the cut-off, so the extra profit pays income tax at $20\%$ and USC
at $2\%$.

**The price of the oven.** VAT adds $23\%$ to the price before VAT:

$$9\,000 \times 1.23 = 11\,070$$

The oven costs €11,070 now. The VAT is €2,070 of that.

**The profit on a loaf.** Each loaf sells for €4 and costs €2.50:

$$4 - 2.50 = 1.50$$

The profit is €1.50 on each loaf. As a percentage of the cost price:

$$\frac{1.50}{2.50} \times 100 = 60\%$$

and as a percentage of the selling price:

$$\frac{1.50}{4} \times 100 = 37.5\%$$

The mark-up is $60\%$ and the margin is $37.5\%$.

**The extra profit in a year.**

$$2\,000 \times 1.50 = 3\,000$$

The oven adds €3,000 of profit in a year, before tax.

**What survives tax.** The extra profit is below the cut-offs, so both rates apply
to the whole €3,000:

$$3\,000 \times 0.20 = 600$$

$$3\,000 \times 0.02 = 60$$

$$3\,000 - 600 - 60 = 2\,340$$

The bakery keeps €2,340 a year.

**The payback time.**

$$11\,070 \div 2\,340 = 4.73\ldots$$

The oven pays for itself in about $4.7$ years. Check by working forwards:
$4.7 \times 2\,340 = 10\,998$, which is just below €11,070.

**Which way of paying is cheaper.** The €11,070 is a present amount, and the
€12,000 is a future amount, so the €12,000 must be discounted:

$$12\,000 \div 1.03^2 = 11\,311.15$$

The present value of the later payment is €11,311.15 to the nearest cent. That is
more than €11,070, so

$$11\,311.15 - 11\,070 = 241.15$$

**Paying now is cheaper by about €241.**

**The same answer from the other side.** If Niamh keeps the €11,070 for two years,
it grows to

$$11\,070 \times 1.03^2 = 11\,744.16$$

She then pays €12,000, so she is short by

$$12\,000 - 11\,744.16 = 255.84$$

about €255.84 in two years' money. Both routes choose the same option, and the two
differences are the same amount measured at two different times.

**The answer.** Pay now, and the oven pays for itself in about $4.7$ years. If the
oven lasts longer than five years, it is worth buying.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, say **which method you will use
and why** before you calculate. The questions mix every section of the chapter
with two from earlier chapters, and two of them need a method that is not the
first one you might reach for.

1. Work out $18\%$ of €250.
2. A price falls from €80 to €68. Work out the percentage decrease.
3. A price after a $20\%$ increase is €96. Work out the price before the increase.
4. An item costs €30 and sells for €42. Work out the mark-up and the margin.
5. A worker earns €45,000. Income tax is $20\%$ on the first €44,000 and $40\%$ on
   the rest, with a tax credit of €3,000. Work out the income tax.
6. €3,000 is invested at $5\%$ compound interest for 2 years. Work out the total
   value.
7. €11,236 is due in 2 years. Money earns $6\%$ a year. Work out the present
   value.
8. Estimate $3\,891 \times 41$ by rounding each number to one significant figure.
9. Write $0.000\,456$ in scientific notation.
10. Simplify $\sqrt{48}$.
11. A quote is rounded to the nearest euro before a profit margin is added.
    Explain why the final figure may be out by more than €1, and say where the
    rounding should happen instead.

## What you can now do

- find a percentage of an amount, and increase or decrease by a percentage;
- work out a percentage change and find the original amount;
- work out profit and loss, and write each as a percentage of the cost price;
- tell mark-up from margin, and find a price from either one;
- work out income tax in bands, take a tax credit off, and work out USC;
- work from gross pay to net pay;
- work out simple and compound interest;
- find the present value of a future sum and compare two offers;
- check an answer by estimating its size, by working backwards, and by asking
  whether it is possible.

## Thinking back

Return to the price, bill or payslip you wrote down at the start of the chapter.

Now you can take it apart. Write down:

- the original figure and the figure you remember;
- whether a percentage was added, taken off, or charged over time;
- the multiplier that turns one figure into the other;
- whether the change was fair, and the calculation that shows it.

If the figure you remembered turns out to be the present value of something paid
later, say so. That is the difference between a price and an offer, and it is the
reason this chapter keeps bringing every amount back to today.
