# 2.1 Percentages and percentage change — Learn

**Prerequisites:** fractions and decimals; Section 1.7 — rounding.

**Objectives:** find a percentage of an amount; increase or decrease by a
percentage; work out a percentage change; find the original amount.

By the end of this section you can move a price up or down by any percentage, and
work out the percentage that a change represents.

## A percentage is a fraction of a hundred

The word **per cent** means "in every hundred". So $15\%$ means $15$ in every
$100$, which is the fraction $\dfrac{15}{100}$ and the decimal $0.15$.

To find a percentage of an amount, turn the percentage into a decimal and
multiply.

**Example 1.** Work out $15\%$ of €80.

$$15\% = 0.15$$

$$0.15 \times 80 = 12$$

So $15\%$ of €80 is €12.

## One multiplier for a change

You can increase or decrease an amount in one step.

- To **increase** by $23\%$, multiply by $1.23$. The original $100\%$ and the
  extra $23\%$ are both kept.
- To **decrease** by $23\%$, multiply by $0.77$. What is left is
  $100\% - 23\% = 77\%$.

The number you multiply by is the **multiplier**.

**Example 2.** Increase €250 by $12\%$.

$$250 \times 1.12 = 280$$

The new amount is €280. Adding $12\%$ of €250, which is €30, gives the same
answer: $250 + 30 = 280$.

**Example 3.** Decrease €180 by $35\%$.

$$180 \times 0.65 = 117$$

The new amount is €117. What is left after the reduction is $65\%$.

## Percentage change

A **percentage change** compares the size of a change with the original amount.

> **Percentage change.** $\text{percentage change} =
> \dfrac{\text{change}}{\text{original}} \times 100$.

The change is the new value minus the original value. A positive answer is an
increase, and a negative answer is a decrease.

**Example 4.** A price rises from €40 to €46. Work out the percentage increase.

$$46 - 40 = 6$$

$$\frac{6}{40} \times 100 = 15\%$$

The price rose by $15\%$. The denominator is the original €40, not the new €46.

## Finding the original amount

Sometimes you know the value after a change and want the value before it. Undo the
multiplier by dividing.

**Example 5.** A jacket is reduced by $20\%$ and now costs €64. Work out the price
before the reduction.

After a $20\%$ reduction, €64 is $80\%$ of the original price.

$$64 \div 0.8 = 80$$

The jacket cost €80 before the reduction.

Check: $20\%$ of €80 is €16, and $80 - 16 = 64$. The answer is correct.

**Example 6.** The chapter problem. The oven costs €9,000 before VAT, and VAT is
$23\%$.

$$9000 \times 1.23 = 11\,070$$

The price including VAT is €11,070. The VAT itself is $9000 \times 0.23 = 2070$,
so €2,070 of that price is VAT.

> **Watch out.** To undo a percentage change, divide by the multiplier. Do not
> apply the percentage in the other direction. A $20\%$ reduction divides by
> $0.8$; adding $20\%$ to €64 gives €76.80, and that is wrong. The $20\%$ was
> taken off the original price of €80, so it has to be undone from €80 and not
> from €64. Always check by working forwards from your answer.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The oven costs €9,000 and VAT is $23\%$.

Work out the price including VAT. Then the price before VAT rises by $2\%$ before
Niamh buys. Work out the new price including VAT, and the percentage increase on
the €9,000.

The €11,070 is the amount Niamh pays now, and Section 2.5 compares it with paying
€12,000 later.

## You should now be able to

- find a percentage of an amount;
- increase or decrease an amount in one step with a multiplier;
- work out a percentage change, dividing by the original amount;
- find the original amount after a percentage change by dividing by the
  multiplier;
- check a reverse percentage by working forwards from your answer.
