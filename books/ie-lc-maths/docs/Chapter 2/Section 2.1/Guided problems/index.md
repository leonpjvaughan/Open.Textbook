# 2.1 Percentages and percentage change — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $0.35$ as a fraction in its lowest terms.
2. Work out $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of €80.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.35 = \dfrac{35}{100} = \dfrac{7}{20}$. Divide the top and the bottom by $5$.
2. $\dfrac{3}{4} \times 80 = 60$. A quarter of €80 is €20, and three quarters is
   €60.

A percentage is a fraction of a hundred, so both of these warm-ups are the same
idea in a different form.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Work out $20\%$ of €150.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$20\%$ is the decimal $0.20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply $0.20$ by $150$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.20 \times 150 = 30$$

So $20\%$ of €150 is €30. A quick check: $10\%$ is €15, so $20\%$ is twice that,
which is €30.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Increase €90 by $30\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An increase of $30\%$ keeps the whole $100\%$ and adds $30\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The multiplier is $1.30$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$90 \times 1.30 = 117$$

The new amount is €117. Check: $30\%$ of €90 is €27, and $90 + 27 = 117$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Decrease €240 by $15\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A decrease of $15\%$ leaves $100\% - 15\% = 85\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The multiplier is $0.85$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$240 \times 0.85 = 204$$

The new amount is €204. The reduction is €36, because $240 \times 0.15 = 36$, and
$240 - 36 = 204$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A price rises from €80 to €92. Work out the percentage increase.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The change is $92 - 80$. Divide the change by the original €80.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{12}{80} \times 100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$92 - 80 = 12$$

$$\frac{12}{80} \times 100 = 15\%$$

The price rose by $15\%$. Dividing by €92 instead would give $13\%$, and that is
the wrong base.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A price after a $25\%$ increase is €150. Work out the price before the
increase.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The €150 is $125\%$ of the original, so the multiplier is $1.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide €150 by $1.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$150 \div 1.25 = 120$$

The original price was €120. Check: $25\%$ of €120 is €30, and $120 + 30 = 150$.
Adding $25\%$ to €150 gives €187.50, which is wrong.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A price rises by $10\%$ and then falls by $10\%$. Is the final price the
same as the starting price? Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the two multipliers together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$1.10 \times 0.90$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1.10 \times 0.90 = 0.99$$

The final price is $99\%$ of the starting price, so it is $1\%$ lower, not the
same. The $10\%$ fall is taken off the risen price, which is larger than the
starting price.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 1.7

**7.** Write the price €11,070 to 2 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first two significant figures are $1$ and $1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Look at the digit after the second $1$, which is $0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$11\,070 \approx 11\,000$$

The first two significant figures are $1$ and $1$, and the next digit is $0$, so
the second $1$ does not change. Section 2.6 uses estimates like this one to check
the size of an answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**8.** The oven costs €9,000 before VAT. VAT is $23\%$. Work out the price
including VAT, and the VAT charged.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the €9,000 by $1.23$ for the price including VAT.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The VAT on its own is $0.23$ of €9,000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9\,000 \times 1.23 = 11\,070$$

$$9\,000 \times 0.23 = 2\,070$$

The price including VAT is €11,070, and €2,070 of it is VAT. Check that the two
parts add up: $9\,000 + 2\,070 = 11\,070$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
