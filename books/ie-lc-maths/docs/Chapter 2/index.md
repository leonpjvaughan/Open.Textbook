# Chapter 2: Arithmetic in context

Most of the arithmetic you meet after school is about money. A price rises by a
percentage, a shop adds a mark-up, a payslip loses tax before it reaches a bank
account, and a loan grows by interest every year. This chapter works through that
arithmetic in the order an adult meets it.

## The chapter problem: The second oven

Niamh runs a bakery as a sole trader. She needs a second oven to bake more bread.
The oven costs €9,000, and she must pay VAT at 23% on top of that.

She has two ways to pay:

- **Pay now.** She takes the full price including VAT from her savings. The
  savings earn 3% a year compound interest.
- **Pay later.** She pays nothing now, and pays €12,000 in two years.

The oven lets her bake and sell 2,000 extra loaves a year. Each loaf sells for €4
and costs €2.50 to make. Her income is below the tax cut-off, so she pays income
tax at 20% and USC at 2% on the extra profit.

**First contact — no working yet.** The oven costs €9,000, but the price
including VAT is higher, and the two ways of paying are not the same. Which way
of paying do you expect to be cheaper? Write down your prediction before you read
on.

By the end of the chapter you can work out the price including VAT, the profit on
a loaf, the profit that survives tax, and the present value of the €12,000. Then
you can say which way of paying is cheaper, and how long the oven takes to pay
for itself.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- work out a percentage of an amount, and increase or decrease by a percentage;
- work out a percentage change, and find the original value when you know the new
  one;
- work out profit and loss, as an amount and as a percentage of the cost price;
- tell mark-up from margin, and use either one to find a price;
- work out income tax, USC and net pay from a set of rates;
- work out simple and compound interest;
- find the present value of a future sum, and use it to compare two offers;
- check whether a result is of the right size.

## Where it is used

Every payslip, every bill and every loan uses this arithmetic. A shopkeeper sets a
price by marking up a cost. An employer deducts tax before paying wages. A bank
grows a loan by compound interest, and a supplier offers a discount for early
payment. The same skills let you tell a fair offer from an expensive one.

## Your turn to connect it

Think of a price, a bill or a payslip you have seen. Maybe a phone contract, a
sale price, or a first payslip. Write down the figure you remember and what
happened to it.

Was a percentage added, taken off, or charged over time? You will be able to work
out the number behind it, and say whether it was fair, by the end of the chapter.
