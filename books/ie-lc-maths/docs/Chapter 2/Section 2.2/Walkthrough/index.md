# 2.2 Profit, loss and mark-up — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 2.1 — percentages and multipliers.

**Objectives:** work out profit and loss; write profit as a percentage of the cost
price; tell mark-up from margin; find a selling price or a cost price from a
mark-up.

By the end of this section you can price an item, and say what the profit is as a
percentage of either the cost or the selling price.

## Cost price, selling price, profit and loss

A trader buys an item for one price and sells it for another.

- The **cost price** is what the trader paid for the item.
- The **selling price** is what the customer pays.
- The **profit** is the selling price minus the cost price.
- A **loss** is what happens when the selling price is below the cost price.

**Example 1.** A loaf costs €2.50 to make and sells for €4. Work out the profit.

$$4 - 2.50 = 1.50$$

The profit is €1.50 on each loaf.

**Example 2.** A trader buys stock for €80 and sells it for €68. Work out the
loss.

$$80 - 68 = 12$$

The loss is €12.

## Profit as a percentage

A profit of €1.50 means little on its own. It is large on a cheap item and small
on an expensive one. So profit is usually written as a percentage, and the base
matters.

> **Profit on the cost price.** $\text{percentage profit} =
> \dfrac{\text{profit}}{\text{cost price}} \times 100$.

That percentage is also called the **mark-up**, because it is the amount the
trader marks the cost price up by.

The same profit can be written as a percentage of the selling price. That
percentage is the **margin**.

> **Margin.** $\text{margin} =
> \dfrac{\text{profit}}{\text{selling price}} \times 100$.

**Example 3.** For the loaf that costs €2.50 and sells for €4, work out the
mark-up and the margin.

The profit is €1.50.

$$\text{mark-up} = \frac{1.50}{2.50} \times 100 = 60\%$$

$$\text{margin} = \frac{1.50}{4} \times 100 = 37.5\%$$

The same €1.50 is $60\%$ of the cost and $37.5\%$ of the selling price. The
selling price is larger, so the margin is the smaller of the two.

**Example 4.** A trader buys stock for €80 and sells it for €68. Work out the loss
as a percentage of the cost price.

$$\frac{12}{80} \times 100 = 15\%$$

The loss is $15\%$ of the cost price.

## From a mark-up to a price

A mark-up of $40\%$ means the selling price is $140\%$ of the cost price, so the
multiplier is $1.4$.

**Example 5.** A shop buys an item for €45 and marks it up by $40\%$. Work out the
selling price.

$$45 \times 1.4 = 63$$

The selling price is €63.

**Example 6.** An item sells for €63 after a mark-up of $40\%$ on the cost price.
Work out the cost price.

$$63 \div 1.4 = 45$$

The cost price is €45. Dividing undoes the mark-up.

## Discount

A **discount** is a reduction on the selling price. It works like any other
percentage decrease.

**Example 7.** A coat priced at €250 is reduced by $15\%$. Work out the reduced
price.

$$250 \times 0.85 = 212.50$$

The reduced price is €212.50.

> **Watch out.** Mark-up and margin are not the same, and swapping them changes
> the answer. Mark-up divides the profit by the cost price; margin divides it by
> the selling price. A $60\%$ mark-up is a $37.5\%$ margin. When a question says
> "mark-up", use the cost price; when it says "margin", use the selling price.
> Read the word before you choose the base.

## Costing a job

Some jobs need more than one cost. A **costing** adds the materials, the labour
and an allowance for wastage, and then applies the mark-up.

**Example 8.** A job needs €100 of materials and 3 hours of labour at €20 an hour.
The trader adds $5\%$ for wastage, and then marks the cost up by $25\%$. Work out
the selling price.

Materials: €100.

Labour: $3 \times 20 = 60$.

$$100 + 60 = 160$$

Wastage adds $5\%$:

$$160 \times 1.05 = 168$$

The cost is €168. The mark-up of $25\%$ gives

$$168 \times 1.25 = 210$$

The selling price is €210.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Each extra loaf sells for €4 and costs €2.50 to make.

Work out the profit on one loaf, the mark-up and the margin. Then work out the
extra profit from $2\,000$ extra loaves in a year.

The mark-up and the margin describe the same €1.50 in two ways. Section 2.3 takes
the €3,000 of extra profit and works out how much of it survives tax.

## You should now be able to

- work out the profit or the loss from the cost price and the selling price;
- write a profit or a loss as a percentage of the cost price;
- tell mark-up from margin, and choose the right base;
- find a selling price from a cost price and a mark-up;
- find a cost price from a selling price and a mark-up;
- work out a discounted price.
