# 2.2 Profit, loss and mark-up — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Increase €60 by $25\%$.
2. Decrease €80 by $10\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $60 \times 1.25 = 75$. The new amount is €75.
2. $80 \times 0.90 = 72$. The new amount is €72.

Both questions use a multiplier from Section 2.1, and both multipliers return in
this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** An item costs €6 and sells for €9. Work out the profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Profit is the selling price minus the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$9 - 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9 - 6 = 3$$

The profit is €3 on each item.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the same item, costing €6 and selling for €9, work out the profit as a
percentage of the cost price and as a percentage of the selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The profit is €3. Divide it by €6 for the first answer and by €9 for the second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{3}{6} \times 100 \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{3}{9} \times 100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{3}{6} \times 100 = 50\%$$

$$\frac{3}{9} \times 100 = 33.3\ldots\%$$

The mark-up is $50\%$ and the margin is about $33.3\%$. The same €3 gives two
different percentages, because the bases are different.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A shop buys an item for €24 and marks it up by $25\%$. Work out the selling
price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A mark-up of $25\%$ gives the multiplier $1.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$24 \times 1.25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$24 \times 1.25 = 30$$

The selling price is €30. The mark-up itself is €6.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** An item sells for €84 after a mark-up of $40\%$ on the cost price. Work out
the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The €84 is $140\%$ of the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide €84 by $1.4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$84 \div 1.4 = 60$$

The cost price is €60. Check: $40\%$ of €60 is €24, and $60 + 24 = 84$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A trader buys stock for €150 and sells it for €120. Work out the loss and
the loss as a percentage of the cost price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The loss is $150 - 120$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{30}{150} \times 100$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$150 - 120 = 30$$

$$\frac{30}{150} \times 100 = 20\%$$

The trader loses €30, which is $20\%$ of the cost price. The loss percentage uses
the cost price as its base.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A coat priced at €80 is reduced by $20\%$. Work out the reduced price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A reduction of $20\%$ leaves $80\%$ of the price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$80 \times 0.80$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$80 \times 0.80 = 64$$

The reduced price is €64. The discount is €16.

</details>
</details>
</details>

<!-- tier:o -->

**7.** A job needs €100 of materials and 3 hours of labour at €20 an hour. The
trader adds $5\%$ for wastage and then marks the cost up by $25\%$. Work out the
selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the materials and the labour first, then apply the wastage, then the mark-up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The materials and labour come to $100 + 3 \times 20 = 160$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$100 + 60 = 160$$

$$160 \times 1.05 = 168$$

$$168 \times 1.25 = 210$$

The selling price is €210. The wastage is added to the cost before the mark-up,
because the trader has to buy the wasted material.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**8.** Each extra loaf sells for €4 and costs €2.50 to make. Work out the profit
on one loaf, the mark-up and the margin. Then work out the extra profit from
$2\,000$ extra loaves.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The profit is $4 - 2.50$. The mark-up divides by the cost price and the margin
divides by the selling price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The profit is €1.50. For the mark-up use $\dfrac{1.50}{2.50}$, and for the margin
use $\dfrac{1.50}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4 - 2.50 = 1.50$$

$$\frac{1.50}{2.50} \times 100 = 60\%$$

$$\frac{1.50}{4} \times 100 = 37.5\%$$

The mark-up is $60\%$ and the margin is $37.5\%$. The extra profit in a year is

$$2\,000 \times 1.50 = 3\,000$$

so the oven adds €3,000 of profit a year before tax. Section 2.3 works out what
is left after tax.

</details>
</details>
</details>
