# 15.6 Perpendicular lines and right angles — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of $70^\circ$. Find each base angle.
2. Work out $90 - 35$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $(180 - 70) \div 2 = 55^\circ$ for each base angle. The two base angles are equal, by
   Theorem 2 part 1.
2. $90 - 35 = 55$. The two acute angles of a right-angled triangle add to $90^\circ$.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Section 15.5 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A right-angled triangle has one acute angle of $35^\circ$. Find the other acute
angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two acute angles add to $90^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $35^\circ$ from $90^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$$

The other acute angle is $55^\circ$. Check: $35 + 55 = 90$, and the right angle makes
$90 + 90 = 180$ in total.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A line $l$ is perpendicular to a line $m$, and a line $n$ is also perpendicular to
$m$. What can you say about $l$ and $n$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both lines make a right angle with the same transversal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two alternate angles are both $90^\circ$, so they are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

By Proposition 1, the lines $l$ and $n$ are parallel.

The line $m$ is a transversal, and it makes a right angle with each of them. The alternate
angles are equal, so Theorem 3 part 1 gives the parallel lines.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** $[AB]$ has length $18$ cm, and the perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$ meets $AB$ at
$M$. Find $|AM|$ and the angle between the perpendicular bisector and $AB$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The perpendicular bisector passes through the midpoint.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Halve $18$ cm, and remember the meaning of perpendicular.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|AM| = \frac{18}{2} = 9 \text{ cm}$$

The angle between the perpendicular bisector and $AB$ is $90^\circ$, because the two
lines are perpendicular.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A point $P$ is $12$ cm from $A$ and $12$ cm from $B$. What can you say about $P$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the two distances.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The distances are equal, so use Property 2 of the perpendicular bisector.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The two distances are equal, so $P$ is the same distance from $A$ as from $B$.

By Property 2, $P$ lies on the perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** In a right-angled isosceles triangle, the two acute angles are equal. Find each
acute angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two acute angles add to $90^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide $90^\circ$ into two equal parts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$90^\circ \div 2 = 45^\circ$$

Each acute angle is $45^\circ$. Check: $45 + 45 + 90 = 180$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Prove that every point on the perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$ is the same distance
from $A$ as from $B$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Let $M$ be the midpoint, and compare the two triangles $PMA$ and $PMB$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find three matching parts, including the right angle at $M$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $M$ be the midpoint of $[AB]$, and let $P$ be any point on the perpendicular bisector,
so $PM \perp AB$.

- $|MA| = |MB|$, because $M$ is the midpoint.
- $|\angle PMA| = |\angle PMB| = 90^\circ$, because $PM \perp AB$.
- $|PM|$ is common.

So $\triangle PMA \cong \triangle PMB$ by SAS, and $|PA| = |PB|$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 13

**7.** Two lines have slopes $2$ and $-\dfrac{1}{2}$. Explain whether the lines are
perpendicular, and say what this has to do with right angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the two slopes together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For perpendicular lines the product of the slopes is $-1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2 \times \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$$

The product is $-1$, so the two lines are perpendicular.

In coordinate geometry, the slopes multiply to $-1$ exactly when the lines meet at a right
angle. That is the coordinate version of the angle being $90^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
