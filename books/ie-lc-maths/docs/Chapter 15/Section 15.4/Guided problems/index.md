# 15.4 Parallel lines — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. In two triangles, $|AB| = |A'B'| = 6$ cm, $|AC| = |A'C'| = 8$ cm and
   $|\angle A| = |\angle A'|$. Name the congruence test.
2. Work out $180 - 72$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. SAS. The angle $\angle A$ lies between the two given sides, so it is the included
   angle.
2. $180 - 72 = 108$. Two angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$, so the second
   angle is what is left.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Sections 15.2 and 15.3 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is $72^\circ$. Find its
alternate angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lines are parallel, so Theorem 3 part 2 applies.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Alternate angles at parallel lines are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The alternate angle is $72^\circ$.

The two lines are parallel, so the transversal makes equal alternate angles, by Theorem 3
part 2. Check for the parallel marks before you use the rule.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is $72^\circ$. Find the
angle inside the parallel lines on the same side of the transversal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

That angle and the alternate angle sit on a straight line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $72^\circ$ from $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The alternate angle is $72^\circ$, and the angle inside on the same side is supplementary
to it:

$$180^\circ - 72^\circ = 108^\circ$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** In $\triangle ABC$, $|\angle A| = 65^\circ$ and $|\angle B| = 48^\circ$. Find
$|\angle C|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $65$ and $48$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$65 + 48 = 113 \qquad |\angle C| = 180 - 113 = 67^\circ$$

This is the glazier's pane from the chapter problem. The third angle is $67^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** In $\triangle ABC$, the exterior angle at $A$ is $113^\circ$ and
$|\angle B| = 65^\circ$. Find $|\angle C|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $113 = 65 + |\angle C|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|\angle C| = 113 - 65 = 48^\circ$$

Check with the angle sum: $|\angle A| = 180 - 113 = 67^\circ$, and
$67 + 65 + 48 = 180$. Both rules agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. A pair of corresponding angles are
$3x$ and $2x + 30$. Find $x$, then find the size of each angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Corresponding angles at parallel lines are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $3x = 2x + 30$, and subtract $2x$ from both sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 30$$

Both angles are $3 \times 30 = 90^\circ$. The transversal is perpendicular to the two
parallel lines.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Prove that the angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$. Write the proof in your own
words, and name each fact you use.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw the line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the parallel line as a transversal, and find the alternate angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $\triangle ABC$ be any triangle. Draw the line through $A$ parallel to $BC$, and name
its two ends $D$ and $E$.

The line $AB$ is a transversal of the parallel lines, so the alternate angles are equal:

$$|\angle ABC| = |\angle DAB|$$

The line $AC$ is also a transversal, so

$$|\angle ACB| = |\angle CAE|$$

The three angles at $A$ make the straight angle $\angle DAE$:

$$|\angle DAB| + |\angle BAC| + |\angle CAE| = 180^\circ$$

Putting the equal angles in place:

$$|\angle ABC| + |\angle BAC| + |\angle ACB| = 180^\circ$$

The facts used are the Axiom of Parallels, the alternate angles theorem, and the
Protractor Axiom.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 13

**7.** Two lines have slopes $3$ and $3$. A student says the lines are parallel. Explain
whether the student is right, and what this has to do with alternate angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Equal slopes mean the lines rise at the same rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A transversal crossing two lines of equal slope makes equal corresponding angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The student is right. In coordinate geometry, two lines are parallel exactly when their
slopes are equal.

The connection is the corresponding angles theorem. Draw any transversal. The angle each
line makes with the transversal depends on its slope, so equal slopes give equal
corresponding angles. By Theorem 5, equal corresponding angles mean the lines are
parallel.

</details>
</details>
</details>
