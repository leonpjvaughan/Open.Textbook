# 15.2 The first theorems on angles — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 15.1 — the Protractor Axiom, and the words theorem and proof.

**Objectives:** find angles on a straight line and at a point; prove that vertically
opposite angles are equal; state the angle sum of a triangle.

By the end of this section you can find every angle at a crossing, and you can prove why
your answers are right.

## Angles on a straight line

A straight angle has $180^\circ$, by part 1 of the Protractor Axiom. When two angles sit
side by side on a straight line, part 3 lets us add their measures. So the two angles add
to $180^\circ$.

> **Angles on a straight line.** Two angles that together make a straight angle add to
> $180^\circ$.

Two angles that add to $180^\circ$ are called **supplementary**. The same rule works for
more than two angles: every angle on one side of a straight line adds to $180^\circ$.

## Angles at a point

A full angle has $360^\circ$. The angles that go all the way around a point fill a full
angle, so they add to $360^\circ$.

> **Angles at a point.** The angles around a point add to $360^\circ$.

## Theorem 1: vertically opposite angles

When two lines cross, they make four angles. The two angles directly opposite each other
at the crossing are called **vertically opposite angles**.

> **Theorem 1.** Vertically opposite angles are equal in measure.

**Proof.** Let two lines cross at the point $O$. Name the four angles around $O$ as $a$,
$b$, $c$ and $d$, in order.

The angles $a$ and $b$ sit on one straight line, so
$$a + b = 180^\circ.$$

The angles $c$ and $b$ sit on the other straight line, so
$$c + b = 180^\circ.$$

The two right-hand sides are equal, so
$$a + b = c + b.$$

Subtract $b$ from both sides:
$$a = c.$$

So the angle $a$ and the angle opposite it, $c$, are equal. The same argument with $b$
and $d$ gives $b = d$. Every pair of vertically opposite angles is equal. $\blacksquare$

The proof uses three things: angles on a straight line, the fact that two things equal to
the same thing are equal to each other, and subtraction. Nothing else.

## Worked examples

**Example 1.** Two straight lines cross. One of the four angles is $65^\circ$. Find the
other three.

The angle vertically opposite the $65^\circ$ angle is also $65^\circ$.

The other two angles are supplementary to $65^\circ$:
$$180^\circ - 65^\circ = 115^\circ.$$

So the four angles are $65^\circ$, $115^\circ$, $65^\circ$ and $115^\circ$, in order
around the crossing. The two pairs of vertically opposite angles are equal, as Theorem 1
requires.

**Example 2.** Four angles around a point are $120^\circ$, $95^\circ$, $70^\circ$ and
$x^\circ$. Find $x$.

The angles around a point add to $360^\circ$.
$$120 + 95 + 70 + x = 360$$
$$285 + x = 360$$
$$x = 75^\circ$$

**Example 3.** Two angles on a straight line are $3x$ and $2x$. Find $x$, then find the
two angles.

The angles add to $180^\circ$.
$$3x + 2x = 180$$
$$5x = 180$$
$$x = 36$$

The two angles are $3 \times 36 = 108^\circ$ and $2 \times 36 = 72^\circ$. Check:
$108 + 72 = 180$, which agrees with the straight line.

**Example 4.** An ordinary angle at a point measures $65^\circ$. Find the reflex angle at
the same point.

The ordinary angle and the reflex angle together fill a full angle.
$$360^\circ - 65^\circ = 295^\circ$$

The reflex angle is $295^\circ$, which is more than $180^\circ$, as a reflex angle must
be.

> **Watch out.** At a crossing, the angles next to each other are supplementary, and the
> angles opposite each other are equal. Only the opposite pair is equal. A student who
> writes $65^\circ$ for all four angles has read every angle as vertically opposite, and
> the two angles that share an arm must add to $180^\circ$ instead.
>
> **Re-check:** mark the two angles that share an arm. Add them. If the total is not
> $180^\circ$, one of the four angles is wrong.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The glazier's cutting guide is a straight edge, and it
crosses a second straight edge. The angle marked between them is $65^\circ$, which is one
of the angles of the pane.

Find the other three angles at that crossing. Say which one is vertically opposite the
$65^\circ$ angle, and say which two are supplementary to it. Keep the four angles. Section
15.4 uses the $65^\circ$ angle again in the proof.

## You should now be able to

- find the angles on a straight line, using the fact that they add to $180^\circ$;
- find the angles around a point, using the fact that they add to $360^\circ$;
- state and prove that vertically opposite angles are equal;
- find the other three angles at a crossing when one is given;
- state the angle sum of a triangle, and say that the proof comes in Section 15.4.
