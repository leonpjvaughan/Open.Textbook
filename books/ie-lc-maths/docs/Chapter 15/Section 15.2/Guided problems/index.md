# 15.2 The first theorems on angles — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $360 - 65$.
2. Write the converse of "if a shape is a square, then it has four right angles".

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $360 - 65 = 295$. The angles around a point add to $360^\circ$, so the part left over
   is $360^\circ$ take away the part you know.
2. "If a shape has four right angles, then it is a square." The converse swaps the two
   parts of the statement, and this one is false: a rectangle is a counterexample.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Section 15.1 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Two straight lines cross. One of the angles is $65^\circ$. Find the angle
vertically opposite it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Vertically opposite angles are equal. Use Theorem 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two lines cross, so the angle opposite the $65^\circ$ angle is the same size.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angle vertically opposite the $65^\circ$ angle is $65^\circ$.

The proof is Theorem 1. The two angles are each supplementary to the same angle between
them, so they must be equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two angles on a straight line are $65^\circ$ and $x^\circ$. Find $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $65 + x = 180$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 180 - 65 = 115^\circ$$

Check: $65 + 115 = 180$, so the two angles do make a straight line.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Three angles around a point are $120^\circ$, $95^\circ$ and $x^\circ$. Find $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angles around a point add to $360^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the two known angles first: $120 + 95$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$120 + 95 = 215 \qquad x = 360 - 215 = 145^\circ$$

Check: $120 + 95 + 145 = 360$, so the three angles fill the full turn.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two angles on a straight line are $4x$ and $5x$. Find $x$, then find the two
angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two angles add to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $4x + 5x = 180$, and collect the terms on the left.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9x = 180 \qquad x = 20$$

The angles are $4 \times 20 = 80^\circ$ and $5 \times 20 = 100^\circ$. Check:
$80 + 100 = 180$, which agrees.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Prove that vertically opposite angles are equal. Write the proof in your own
words, and name each fact you use.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Name the four angles around the crossing, and look at each straight line in turn.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write down two equations, one for each straight line through the crossing.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the four angles around the crossing be $a$, $b$, $c$ and $d$ in order.

The angles $a$ and $b$ sit on one straight line, so $a + b = 180^\circ$.

The angles $c$ and $b$ sit on the other straight line, so $c + b = 180^\circ$.

The two totals are equal, so $a + b = c + b$. Subtract $b$ from both sides to get
$a = c$.

The same argument gives $b = d$. So each pair of vertically opposite angles is equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student writes that all four angles at a crossing are $65^\circ$. Explain what
is wrong, and give the correct four angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two of the four angles are supplementary, not equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the angles that share an arm with the $65^\circ$ angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The student has treated every angle as vertically opposite. Only the opposite pair is
equal.

The angle opposite the $65^\circ$ angle is $65^\circ$. The other two angles are each
supplementary to $65^\circ$:

$$180 - 65 = 115^\circ$$

The four angles are $65^\circ$, $115^\circ$, $65^\circ$ and $115^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 12

**7.** A roof truss has two straight beams that cross. One angle at the crossing is
$128^\circ$. A carpenter needs the angle on the other side of the same beam. Find it, and
say which rule you used.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two angles lie on the same straight beam.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$180^\circ - 128^\circ = 52^\circ$$

The rule is that angles on a straight line add to $180^\circ$. The two angles are
supplementary.

</details>
</details>
</details>
