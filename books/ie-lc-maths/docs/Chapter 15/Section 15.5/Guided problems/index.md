# 15.5 The isosceles triangle and its converse — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. In $\triangle ABC$, $|\angle A| = 40^\circ$ and $|\angle B| = 75^\circ$. Find
   $|\angle C|$.
2. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal, and one angle is $130^\circ$. Find its
   alternate angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $180 - 40 - 75 = 65^\circ$. The three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$, by
   Theorem 4.
2. $130^\circ$. Alternate angles at parallel lines are equal, by Theorem 3 part 2.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Section 15.4 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of $100^\circ$. Find each base angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two base angles are equal. Call each one $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $100 + x + x = 180$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x = 80 \qquad x = 40^\circ$$

Each base angle is $40^\circ$, and $40 + 40 + 100 = 180$, which checks.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** An isosceles triangle has a base angle of $55^\circ$. Find the apex angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both base angles are $55^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract the two base angles from $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$55 + 55 = 110 \qquad 180 - 110 = 70^\circ$$

The apex angle is $70^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** In $\triangle ABC$, $|\angle B| = |\angle C| = 68^\circ$. Show that the triangle is
isosceles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two angles are equal. Which theorem turns that into two equal sides?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the converse, Theorem 2 part 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angles $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ are equal. By Theorem 2 part 2, the sides opposite
them are equal.

The side opposite $\angle B$ is $AC$, and the side opposite $\angle C$ is $AB$. So
$|AC| = |AB|$, and the triangle is isosceles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** In a triangle, $|\angle A| = 80^\circ$, $|\angle B| = 60^\circ$ and
$|\angle C| = 40^\circ$. Which side is longest, and which is shortest?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The longest side is opposite the largest angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the largest and smallest angles first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The largest angle is $\angle A = 80^\circ$, so the longest side is $BC$, which is
opposite $A$.

The smallest angle is $\angle C = 40^\circ$, so the shortest side is $AB$, which is
opposite $C$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** An equilateral triangle has all three sides equal. Show that each angle is
$60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the isosceles theorem twice.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two equal sides give two equal angles. Repeat for another pair of sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Since $|AB| = |AC|$, the angles opposite them are equal: $|\angle B| = |\angle C|$.

Since $|BA| = |BC|$, the angles opposite them are equal: $|\angle A| = |\angle C|$.

So all three angles are equal. Let each be $x$:

$$3x = 180 \qquad x = 60^\circ$$

Each angle is $60^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Prove that the two base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the triangle with itself, swapping the labels at the two ends of the base.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Compare $\triangle ABC$ with $\triangle ACB$, and find three matching parts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $|AB| = |AC|$.

Compare $\triangle ABC$ with $\triangle ACB$. The matching parts are:

- $|AB| = |AC|$, given;
- $|AC| = |AB|$, the same sides the other way round;
- $|\angle A| = |\angle A|$, the same angle.

So $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle ACB$ by SAS, and the congruence matches $B$ with $C$.
Therefore $|\angle B| = |\angle C|$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A triangle has sides $7$ cm, $4$ cm and $2$ cm. Explain whether these three lengths
can form a triangle, and state the rule you used.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the two shorter sides and compare with the longest.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two sides together must be greater than the third.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Add the two shorter sides: $4 + 2 = 6$ cm. The longest side is $7$ cm.

Since $6 < 7$, the two shorter sides do not reach across the longest side. No triangle
can have these three side lengths.

The rule is the triangle inequality: two sides of a triangle are together greater than
the third.

</details>
</details>
</details>
