# 15.3 Congruent triangles — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Two straight lines cross. One angle is $47^\circ$. Find the angle vertically opposite
   it.
2. Work out $180 - 55 - 65$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $47^\circ$. Vertically opposite angles are equal, by Theorem 1 from Section 15.2.
2. $180 - 55 - 65 = 60$. The three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$, so the third
   angle is what is left.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Sections 15.1 and 15.2 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** In two triangles, $|AB| = |A'B'| = 7$ cm, $|AC| = |A'C'| = 9$ cm, and
$|\angle A| = |\angle A'| = 40^\circ$. Name the congruence test.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Check whether the given angle lies between the two given sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle $\angle A$ is the angle between the sides $AB$ and $AC$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angle is between the two sides, so the test is SAS: two sides and the included angle.

$$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** In two triangles, $|\angle B| = |\angle B'| = 55^\circ$,
$|\angle C| = |\angle C'| = 65^\circ$, and $|BC| = |B'C'| = 8$ cm. Name the test, and say
which side lies between the two angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The side between the angles $\angle B$ and $\angle C$ is $BC$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two angles and the side between them is ASA.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The side $BC$ lies between the angles at $B$ and at $C$, so the test is ASA.

$$\triangle ABC \cong \triangle A'B'C'$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Two triangles have sides $5$ cm, $7$ cm and $9$ cm, and sides $5$ cm, $7$ cm and
$9$ cm. Name the test, and say whether the angles need to be measured.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

All three sides match.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Three matching sides is SSS.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The test is SSS. Once the three sides match, the triangles are congruent, so the angles
do not need to be measured at all.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$, and $|AB| = 12$ cm. Find $|PQ|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the order of the letters.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A$ matches $P$, and $B$ matches $Q$, so the side $AB$ matches the side $PQ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|PQ| = |AB| = 12 \text{ cm}$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** $\triangle ABC \cong \triangle PQR$, with $|BC| = 15$ cm, $|AC| = 11$ cm and
$|\angle A| = 70^\circ$. Find $|QR|$ and $|\angle P|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Match the letters: $A$ with $P$, $B$ with $Q$, $C$ with $R$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$BC$ matches $QR$, and $\angle A$ matches $\angle P$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|QR| = |BC| = 15 \text{ cm} \qquad |\angle P| = |\angle A| = 70^\circ$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why two sides and an angle that is not between them do not prove that two
triangles are congruent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

In the two-answer case from Chapter 12, two different triangles share the same data.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $|AB| = 6$ cm, $|BC| = 4$ cm and $|\angle A| = 30^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angle $\angle A$ is opposite the side $BC$, so it is not between the two given sides.

By the sine rule, $\sin C = 0.75$, so $C = 48.6^\circ$ or $C = 131.4^\circ$. Both give a
triangle with $|AB| = 6$ cm, $|BC| = 4$ cm and $|\angle A| = 30^\circ$, but the two
triangles have different shapes.

So the same three measurements can describe two different triangles. That is why SSA is
not a congruence test.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 14

**7.** A translation moves a triangle to a new position. Explain why the image triangle
is congruent to the object triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What does a translation do to every length and every angle?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Every point moves by the same vector, so every side and every angle is unchanged.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A translation moves every point of the triangle by the same vector. So each side of the
image has the same length as the matching side of the object, and each angle is unchanged.

All three sides match, so the two triangles are congruent by SSS.

</details>
</details>
</details>
