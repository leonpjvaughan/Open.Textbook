# 15.7 Constructions 1–6 — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A right-angled triangle has one acute angle of $38^\circ$. Find the other acute angle.
2. A point $P$ is the same distance from $A$ as from $B$. Which line does $P$ lie on?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $90 - 38 = 52^\circ$. The two acute angles of a right-angled triangle add to
   $90^\circ$.
2. The perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$. Every point that is the same distance from $A$
   as from $B$ lies on it, by Property 2 from Section 15.6.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Section 15.6 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** An angle of $90^\circ$ is bisected. What is each part?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A bisector cuts the angle into two equal parts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide $90^\circ$ by $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$90^\circ \div 2 = 45^\circ$$

Each part is $45^\circ$, and $45 + 45 = 90$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A segment has length $21$ cm. It is divided into three equal parts. Find the length
of each part.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Three equal parts fill the whole segment.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide $21$ by $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$21 \div 3 = 7 \text{ cm}$$

Each part is $7$ cm, and $7 + 7 + 7 = 21$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Describe the steps for constructing the perpendicular bisector of a segment $[AB]$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need two points that are each the same distance from $A$ as from $B$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Draw arcs from $A$ and from $B$ with the same compass opening.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

1. With centre $A$, open the compass to more than half of $|AB|$ and draw an arc on each
   side of $AB$.
2. With centre $B$, and the same compass opening, draw arcs that cut the first two at $P$
   and $Q$.
3. Draw the line $PQ$. It is the perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Describe the steps for constructing the perpendicular to a line $l$ at a point $P$
on $l$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start by marking two points on $l$ that are the same distance from $P$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Then find a second point $Q$ that is the same distance from both marked points.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

1. With centre $P$, draw an arc that cuts $l$ at $A$ and at $B$, one on each side of $P$.
2. With centres $A$ and $B$, and a compass opening greater than half of $|AB|$, draw two
   arcs that cross at $Q$.
3. Draw the line $PQ$. It is perpendicular to $l$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Describe the steps for drawing a line through a point $P$ parallel to a line $l$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use a transversal, then copy an angle to make the alternate angles equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Draw a line through $P$ meeting $l$ at $Q$, and copy the angle at $Q$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

1. Draw any line through $P$ that meets $l$ at $Q$.
2. At $P$, copy the angle $\angle PQA$ so that the copy is on the opposite side of $PQ$
   from $A$. Call the new ray $PT$.
3. The line through $P$ and $T$ is parallel to $l$, because the alternate angles
   $\angle PQA$ and $\angle QPT$ are equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student constructs a perpendicular bisector but changes the compass opening
between the arc from $A$ and the arc from $B$. Explain what goes wrong.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The proof needs the two distances from $A$ to match the two distances from $B$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

With different openings, $|PA|$ and $|PB|$ are no longer equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The proof of Construction 2 uses $|PA| = |PB|$ and $|QA| = |QB|$, and both equalities come
from the same compass opening.

If the opening changes, those equalities fail. The points $P$ and $Q$ are no longer the
same distance from $A$ as from $B$, so the line $PQ$ is not the perpendicular bisector. It
is some other line.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A rectangular sheet is $24$ cm by $18$ cm. It is divided into three equal strips
along its length. Find the width of each strip, and say which construction divides a
segment without measuring.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The strips run along the $24$ cm length.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide $24$ by $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$24 \div 3 = 8 \text{ cm}$$

Each strip is $8$ cm wide.

The construction is Construction 6: divide a segment into equal parts without measuring.
It marks off equal segments on a ray, then draws parallel lines through the marks.

</details>
</details>
</details>
