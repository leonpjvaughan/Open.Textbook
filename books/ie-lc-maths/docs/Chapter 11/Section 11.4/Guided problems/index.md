# 11.4 Measures of centre and spread — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the sum of $8$, $12$, $15$, $20$ and $25$.
2. Divide your answer by $5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $8 + 12 + 15 + 20 + 25 = 80$.
2. $80 \div 5 = 16$.

Those two steps are the whole mean: add the values, then divide by how many there are.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the mean of $4$, $7$, $9$, $12$, $13$ and $15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the values, then divide by $6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4 + 7 + 9 + 12 + 13 + 15 = 60$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\bar{x} = \frac{60}{6} = 10$$

The mean is $10$. Every value took part in the sum, which is why the mean can be pulled
by an extreme value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the median of $3$, $5$, $8$, $10$, $12$ and $14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The values are already in order, and there are $6$ of them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Take the mean of the $3$rd and $4$th values.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{8 + 10}{2} = 9$$

The median is $9$. With an even number of values there is no single middle value, so the
median is the mean of the two in the middle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the mode of $2$, $3$, $3$, $5$, $7$, $7$, $7$ and $9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count how often each value appears.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The value $7$ appears three times.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The mode is $7$, because it appears more often than any other value. The mode is the only
average that also works for categorical data, such as the most common favourite colour.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the range of $12$, $15$, $11$, $19$ and $14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the largest and the smallest value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The largest is $19$ and the smallest is $11$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$19 - 11 = 8$$

The range is $8$. The range uses only two values, so one outlier can make it very large.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the mean of the data in this table.

| Value $x$ | Frequency $f$ |
|---|---|
| $0$ | $4$ |
| $1$ | $7$ |
| $2$ | $6$ |
| $3$ | $3$ |

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out $\sum fx$ and $\sum f$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sum fx = 0 \times 4 + 1 \times 7 + 2 \times 6 + 3 \times 3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sum fx = 0 + 7 + 12 + 9 = 28, \qquad \sum f = 4 + 7 + 6 + 3 = 20$$

$$\bar{x} = \frac{28}{20} = 1.4$$

The mean is $1.4$, which is not one of the values in the table. A mean need not be a value
that actually occurs.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Back to the chapter problem. The polling company recorded the ages of $10$ voters:
$18$, $22$, $25$, $31$, $34$, $41$, $47$, $53$, $61$ and $78$. Find the mean.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the ten ages, then divide by $10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$18 + 22 + 25 + 31 + 34 + 41 + 47 + 53 + 61 + 78$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The sum is $410$, so

$$\bar{x} = \frac{410}{10} = 41$$

The mean age is $41$. The value $78$ is much larger than the rest, and Section 11.5 shows
how much it increases the spread.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**7.** Solve $3x + 5 = 20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract $5$ from both sides first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3x = 15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x = 20 - 5 = 15, \qquad x = 5$$

Check: $3 \times 5 + 5 = 20$. Rearranging is the same skill as working backwards from a
mean to find a missing value.

</details>
</details>
</details>
