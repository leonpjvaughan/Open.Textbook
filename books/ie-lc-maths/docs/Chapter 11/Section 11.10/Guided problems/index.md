# 11.10 Correlation and regression — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the mean of $1$, $2$, $3$, $4$ and $5$.
2. Substitute $x = 4$ into $y = 10 - 2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The sum is $15$ over $5$ values, so the mean is $3$.
2. $y = 10 - 2(4) = 2$.

Substituting into a line is exactly what a prediction does: the line gives a value of $y$
for a chosen $x$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** The pairs $(1, 2)$, $(2, 3)$, $(3, 5)$, $(4, 4)$ and $(5, 6)$ are plotted. The
correlation coefficient is $r = 0.9$. Describe the correlation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Is $r$ positive or negative, and is it close to $1$ or close to $0$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The sign gives the direction and the size gives the strength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$r = 0.9$ is positive and close to $1$, so the correlation is strong and positive. As $x$
increases, $y$ tends to increase, and the points lie close to a straight line.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the same pairs, the least-squares line is $y = 1.3 + 0.9x$. State the gradient
and the intercept.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The line is written $y = a + bx$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Match $a$ and $b$ to the numbers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The intercept is $a = 1.3$ and the gradient is $b = 0.9$. The gradient says that each
extra unit of $x$ is associated with about $0.9$ more of $y$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Use the line $y = 1.3 + 0.9x$ to predict $y$ when $x = 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $x = 6$ into the line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$y = 1.3 + 0.9(6)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = 1.3 + 5.4 = 6.7$$

The prediction is $6.7$. The value $x = 6$ is just outside the data range $1$ to $5$, so
the prediction is a short extrapolation and should be treated with care.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The pairs $(1, 10)$, $(2, 9)$, $(3, 7)$, $(4, 5)$ and $(5, 4)$ are plotted. The
correlation coefficient is about $r = -0.99$. Describe the correlation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sign is negative and the size is close to $1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Negative means one variable falls as the other rises.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$r = -0.99$ is close to $-1$, so the correlation is very strong and negative. As $x$
increases, $y$ decreases, and the points lie very close to a straight line.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A study finds $r = -0.92$ between the number of hours of exercise each week and the
resting heart rate. Explain what this says, and why it does not prove that exercise lowers
the heart rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Describe the direction and strength first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Then think of another variable that could explain both.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The correlation is strong and negative: people who exercise more tend to have a lower
resting heart rate. It does not prove that exercise causes the lower rate. People who
exercise may also eat better or sleep more, and any of those could be the cause. Only a
designed experiment with random allocation can support a causal claim.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Back to the chapter problem. The scatterplot of poll result against actual result
for eight referendums has $r = 0.97$ and a line close to $y = 3.9 + 0.91x$. For the
referendum in the chapter problem, the poll was $52\%$ and the result was $48\%$. Use the
line to predict the result when the poll is $52\%$, and say whether the actual result is
above or below the line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $x = 52$ into the line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$y = 3.9 + 0.91(52)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y = 3.9 + 47.3 = 51.2$$

The line predicts about $51.2\%$. The actual result was $48\%$, which is about three
points below the line. This is the poll that got it wrong, and it is one point among
eight.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 13

**7.** Find the gradient of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(3, 8)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Gradient is the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{8 - 2}{3 - 1}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{8 - 2}{3 - 1} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$

The gradient of a line is the same idea as the gradient of a regression line: how much $y$
changes when $x$ increases by $1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
