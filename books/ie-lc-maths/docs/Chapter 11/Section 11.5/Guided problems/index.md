# 11.5 The standard deviation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the mean of $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ and $10$.
2. Work out $(-2)^2$ and $3^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The sum is $30$ over $5$ values, so the mean is $6$.
2. $(-2)^2 = 4$ and $3^2 = 9$.

The standard deviation squares each deviation, so a negative deviation becomes positive.
That is what stops the deviations cancelling out.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the standard deviation of $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ and $10$, dividing by $n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the mean first, then the squared deviations.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The mean is $6$, and the squared deviations are $16$, $4$, $0$, $4$ and $16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The squared deviations add to $40$, so the variance is $\dfrac{40}{5} = 8$ and

$$\sigma = \sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.83$$

The standard deviation $\sigma$ is about $2.83$, in the same units as the data.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the variance of $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ and $10$, dividing by $n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The variance is the square of the standard deviation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

You already found $\sigma = \sqrt{8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The variance is $\sigma^2 = 8$. It is the mean of the squared deviations, so its units are
the square of the data's units. The standard deviation is reported more often because its
units match the data.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the interquartile range of $1$, $3$, $4$, $6$, $8$, $9$, $11$ and $14$, using
the $(n + 1)$ position method.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are $8$ values, so the position of the lower quartile $Q_1$ is $\dfrac{9}{4} = 2.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The position of the upper quartile $Q_3$ is $\dfrac{27}{4} = 6.75$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$Q_1 = 3 + 0.25(4 - 3) = 3.25, \qquad Q_3 = 9 + 0.75(11 - 9) = 10.5$$

$$\text{IQR} = 10.5 - 3.25 = 7.25 = \frac{29}{4}$$

The IQR is the spread of the middle half of the data.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the standard deviation of the data in this table, dividing by $\sum f$.

| Value $x$ | Frequency $f$ |
|---|---|
| $1$ | $2$ |
| $2$ | $4$ |
| $3$ | $6$ |
| $4$ | $4$ |
| $5$ | $2$ |

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the mean $\mu$ first. It is $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Weight each squared deviation by its frequency: $2(4) + 4(1) + 6(0) + 4(1) + 2(4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sum f(x - \mu)^2 = 8 + 4 + 0 + 4 + 8 = 24, \qquad \sum f = 18$$

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{24}{18}} = \sqrt{\frac{4}{3}} = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 1.15$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the sample standard deviation of $2$, $4$, $6$, $8$ and $10$, dividing by
$n - 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The squared deviations still add to $40$, and $n - 1 = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$s = \sqrt{\frac{40}{4}}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = \sqrt{10} \approx 3.16$$

The sample standard deviation $s$ is larger than the value $2.83$ found by dividing by
$n$. The sample estimate is used when the data are a sample from a larger population.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Back to the chapter problem. The $10$ ages recorded by the polling company are
$18$, $22$, $25$, $31$, $34$, $41$, $47$, $53$, $61$ and $78$, with mean $41$. The
squared deviations add to $3244$. Find the standard deviation, dividing by $n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the sum of squared deviations by $n = 10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{3244}{10}}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sigma = \sqrt{324.4} \approx 18.0$$

The ages are spread about $18$ years either side of the mean. A sample of $10$ people has
a wide spread, which is one reason it cannot pin down the country's opinion.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** Write $\sqrt{50}$ in its simplest surd form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look for a square factor of $50$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$50 = 25 \times 2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = \sqrt{25}\sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2}$$

A standard deviation is often a surd, so simplifying surds keeps the answer exact.

</details>
</details>
</details>
