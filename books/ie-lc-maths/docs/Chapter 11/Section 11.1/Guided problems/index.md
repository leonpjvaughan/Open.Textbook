# 11.1 Populations and samples — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $15\%$ of $240$.
2. Write $\dfrac{40}{1200}$ in its simplest form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $15\%$ of $240 = 0.15 \times 240 = 36$.
2. Divide top and bottom by $40$: $\dfrac{40}{1200} = \dfrac{1}{30}$.

Both skills return in this section: a sample is a fraction of a population, and a
percentage is often used to describe the part chosen.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A golf club has $24$ members and takes a census of them. How many members are
surveyed?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A census measures the whole population.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The population is the $24$ members.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A census measures every member of the population, so all $24$ members are surveyed.

A sample would have been some smaller number of members, such as $10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A year group has $250$ students. A researcher chooses a simple random sample of one
fifth of them. How many students are in the sample?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One fifth of the population is chosen.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide $250$ by $5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$250 \div 5 = 50 \text{ students}$$

The sample is $50$ students, which is $20\%$ of the year group.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A company tests $12\%$ of the $250$ components in a batch. How many components are
tested?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $12\%$ of $250$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Change $12\%$ to a decimal and multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.12 \times 250 = 30 \text{ components}$$

The remaining $220$ components are not tested. That is the trade-off a sample makes:
less work, but only part of the batch is known.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A sample of $40$ students is taken from a school of $1200$ students. What fraction
of the population is sampled? Give the fraction in its simplest form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the sample size over the population size.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The fraction is $\dfrac{40}{1200}$. Divide top and bottom by a common factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{40}{1200} = \frac{1}{30}$$

So one student in every $30$ is in the sample. A fraction near $\dfrac{1}{30}$ is a
small sample, and Section 11.7 shows how much that limits the conclusions.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A school surveys $50$ of its $800$ students about the canteen. Name the population
and the sample.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The population is the whole group the question is about.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The sample is the part that was actually asked.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The population is all $800$ students in the school. The sample is the $50$ students who
were asked.

The answer from the $50$ is a statistic. It is used to estimate a parameter, the opinion
of all $800$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Back to the chapter problem. The polling company asked $1000$ voters and reported
that $52\%$ would vote Yes. How many of the $1000$ voters said Yes?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $52\%$ of $1000$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.52 \times 1000$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.52 \times 1000 = 520 \text{ voters}$$

So $520$ of the $1000$ said Yes. The number $0.52$ is the sample proportion, and it is
the statistic the company reports. Section 11.7 asks how far that $0.52$ could be from
the population proportion.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**7.** A jacket costs €$80$. Its price rises by $15\%$. Find the new price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $15\%$ of $80$, then add it to the original price.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.15 \times 80 = 12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.15 \times 80 = 12, \qquad 80 + 12 = 92$$

The new price is €$92$. Percentage change returns in Section 11.11, where a percentage
is used to make a claim look larger than it is.

</details>
</details>
</details>
