# 4.5 Evaluating and simplifying expressions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $4 \times 40$.
2. Simplify $3n + 2n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $4 \times 40 = 160$.
2. $3n + 2n = 5n$. The letter part does not change, so the coefficients add.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Evaluate $C = 240 + 4n$ at $n = 50$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply before you add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4 \times 50 = 200$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$C = 240 + 200 = 440$$

The cost is €440.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Simplify $6a + 4a - 2a$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

All three terms are like terms, so add the coefficients.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6 + 4 - 2 = 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6a + 4a - 2a = 8a$$

The letter stays $a$, and the index stays $1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Simplify $5x + 3 - 2x + 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Gather the $x$ terms and the numbers separately.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$5x - 2x = 3x$ and $3 + 4 = 7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5x + 3 - 2x + 4 = 3x + 7$$

Terms with different letters cannot be combined, but a letter term and a number
can sit in the same expression.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Expand $3(2x + 5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply each term inside the bracket by $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3(2x) = 6x$ and $3(5) = 15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3(2x + 5) = 6x + 15$$

Both terms inside are multiplied, not only the first.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Expand and simplify $4(x + 2) - 2(x - 3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Expand each bracket first, and watch the minus sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4(x + 2) = 4x + 8$ and $-2(x - 3) = -2x + 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4x + 8 - 2x + 6 = 2x + 14$$

The $-2$ multiplies both terms in the second bracket, so $-2 \times (-3) = +6$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Evaluate $5x - 2y$ at $x = 3$ and $y = -4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute both values, and keep the brackets around the negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$5(3) - 2(-4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$15 - (-8) = 15 + 8 = 23$$

Subtracting a negative adds. Writing $2(-4)$ in brackets is what keeps the sign
straight.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** Simplify $2^3 \times 2^4$ by writing it as a single power of $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bases are the same, so add the indices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2^{3+4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^3 \times 2^4 = 2^7 = 128$$

The laws of indices are another kind of simplification, and they follow the same
idea: replace several things with one.

</details>
</details>
</details>
