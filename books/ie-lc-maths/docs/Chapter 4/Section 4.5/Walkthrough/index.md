# 4.5 Evaluating and simplifying expressions — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 4.1 — expressions; arithmetic with negative numbers.

**Objectives:** substitute a value into an expression; collect like terms; expand
a single bracket; combine brackets with a sign in front.

By the end of this section you can tidy an expression into its shortest form and
then substitute a value into it.

## Evaluating: substituting a value

To **evaluate** an expression, replace each letter with its value and work out the
arithmetic. Multiplication happens before addition.

**Example 1.** Evaluate $C = 240 + 4n$ at $n = 40$ and at $n = 50$.

$$n = 40:\quad C = 240 + 4(40) = 240 + 160 = 400$$

$$n = 50:\quad C = 240 + 4(50) = 240 + 200 = 440$$

At $40$ candles the cost is €400. At $50$ candles it is €440.

**Example 2.** Evaluate $5x - 2y$ at $x = 3$ and $y = -4$.

$$5(3) - 2(-4) = 15 + 8 = 23$$

The product $2(-4)$ is $-8$, and subtracting it adds $8$. A negative substituted
value is the most common place a sign goes wrong.

## Collecting like terms

**Like terms** have exactly the same letter part. They can be added or
subtracted, because they count the same thing.

$$3a + 5a = 8a \qquad 7b - 2b = 5b$$

The letters do not change, and the index does not change. $3a + 5a$ is $8a$, not
$8a^2$, because it counts eight lots of $a$.

Terms with different letters stay separate.

$$4x + 3y - x + 2y = 3x + 5y$$

**Example 3.** Simplify $8p + 3 - 2p + 7$.

$$8p - 2p = 6p \qquad 3 + 7 = 10 \qquad 6p + 10$$

Gather the $p$ terms together and the numbers together. The order does not matter,
because addition is commutative.

## Expanding a bracket

To **expand** a bracket, multiply every term inside by the number outside.

$$a(bx + c) = abx + ac$$

where $a$, $b$ and $c$ are numbers.

**Example 4.** Expand $4(2x + 3)$.

$$4(2x) + 4(3) = 8x + 12$$

**Example 5.** Expand and simplify $5(2x - 1) - 3(x + 4)$.

Expand each bracket first:

$$10x - 5 - 3x - 12$$

The $-3$ multiplies both terms in the second bracket, so $-3 \times 4 = -12$.
Now collect like terms:

$$10x - 3x - 5 - 12 = 7x - 17$$

## Expanding two brackets

Two brackets are expanded by multiplying every term in the first by every term in
the second, then collecting.

$$(x + y)(w + z) = xw + xz + yw + yz$$

where $x$, $y$, $w$ and $z$ stand for any numbers.

**Example 6.** Expand $(x + 3)(x + 4)$.

$$x^2 + 4x + 3x + 12 = x^2 + 7x + 12$$

The four products give $x^2$, two $x$ terms and a number. Collecting the middle
two gives $7x$.

<!-- /tier:h -->

> **Watch out.** $3a + 5a = 8a$, not $8a^2$. Adding like terms counts them; it
> does not multiply the letters. A second trap is a minus in front of a bracket:
> the sign multiplies **every** term inside, so $-3(x + 4) = -3x - 12$.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The profit is the income minus the cost:

$$\text{profit} = 10n - (240 + 4n)$$

Expand the bracket and collect like terms to simplify the profit expression. Then
evaluate it at $n = 40$ and at $n = 50$, and say what each value means for the
business.

## You should now be able to

- evaluate an expression by substitution, including a negative value;
- collect like terms without changing the letter part;
- expand a single bracket, and a bracket with a minus in front;
- simplify an expression into its shortest form.
