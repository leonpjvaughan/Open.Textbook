# 4.7 Simultaneous equations in two variables — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find $y$ when $x = 3$ and $y = 2x + 1$.
2. Solve $2x + 1 = 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $y = 2(3) + 1 = 7$.
2. $2x = 8$, so $x = 4$. Substituting and solving are the two moves a
   simultaneous system uses.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $y = 2x + 1$ and $y = 5x - 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both expressions equal $y$, so set them equal to each other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2x + 1 = 5x - 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x + 1 = 5x - 8 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 9 = 3x \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 3$$

Then $y = 2(3) + 1 = 7$. Check in the second: $y = 5(3) - 8 = 7$. The solution is
$(3, 7)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $x + y = 10$ and $x - y = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Adding the equations removes $y$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2x = 14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = 7$$

Then $7 + y = 10$, so $y = 3$. Check in the second equation: $7 - 3 = 4$. The
solution is $(7, 3)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $2x + 3y = 12$ and $x - y = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange the second equation to make $x$ the subject.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = y + 1$, so $2(y + 1) + 3y = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2y + 2 + 3y = 12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 5y = 10 \quad\Rightarrow\quad y = 2$$

Then $x = 2 + 1 = 3$. Check: $2(3) + 3(2) = 12$. The solution is $(3, 2)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The cost of $n$ candles is $C = 240 + 4n$ and the income is $R = 10n$. Solve
the system to find the break-even point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At break-even $C = R$, so substitute one expression into the other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$240 + 4n = 10n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$240 = 6n \quad\Rightarrow\quad n = 40$$

Then $R = 10(40) = 400$ and $C = 240 + 160 = 400$.

The solution is $n = 40$, with $C = R = 400$, which is €400. The two lines cross at $(40, 400)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $y = 3x - 2$ and $y = x + 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set the two expressions for $y$ equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3x - 2 = x + 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2x = 6 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 3$$

Then $y = 3(3) - 2 = 7$. Check in the other equation: $y = 3 + 4 = 7$. The
solution is $(3, 7)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why one equation in two unknowns is not enough to find both values.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How many pairs of numbers satisfy $x + y = 10$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Many pairs satisfy it, so the equation on its own does not pin the values down.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The equation $x + y = 10$ is true for $x = 1$ with $y = 9$, for $x = 4$ with
$y = 6$, and for every other pair on that line. One equation describes a whole
line of points, so it cannot single out one pair.

A second equation describes a second line. Two different lines meet at one point
at most, and that point is the simultaneous solution.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**7.** An item costs €40 and is sold for €50. Work out the percentage profit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Percentage profit is the profit divided by the cost price, times $100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The profit is $50 - 40 = 10$, and the cost price is $40$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{10}{40} \times 100 = 25\%$$

The profit is $25\%$ of the cost price. The candle problem uses the same
difference, but compares cost with income over a range of values rather than at
one point.

</details>
</details>
</details>
