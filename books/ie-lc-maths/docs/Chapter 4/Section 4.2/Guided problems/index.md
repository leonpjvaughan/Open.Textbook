# 4.2 Representing relations in tables and graphs — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Substitute $n = 4$ into $3n + 1$.
2. Find the gap in the sequence $3, 8, 13, \dots$ and the next term.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3(4) + 1 = 13$.
2. The gap is $5$, so the next term is $18$. The gap is the coefficient of $n$
   in the expression for this sequence.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A rule is $C = 240 + 4n$. Work out $C$ when $n = 15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $n = 15$ and multiply before you add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4 \times 15 = 60$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$C = 240 + 4(15) = 240 + 60 = 300$$

So the cost is €300 when $15$ candles are made.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A rule is $R = 10n$. Complete the table.

| $n$ | $5$ | $10$ | $15$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $R$ | | | |

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply each input by $10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10 \times 5 = 50$, and the pattern continues.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The values are $50$, $100$ and $150$.

Each step of $5$ in the input adds $50$ to the output, because the coefficient is
$10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Which of the points $(3, 7)$ and $(3, 8)$ lies on the line $y = 2x + 1$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $x = 3$ and see which $y$ comes out.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2(3) + 1 = 7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The point $(3, 7)$ lies on the line. The point $(3, 8)$ does not, because the
line passes through $y = 7$ when $x = 3$.

A point lies on a line exactly when its coordinates satisfy the equation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The income rule is $R = 10n$. Use it to find the income from $12$ candles,
then say whether the business makes a profit at that point if the cost is €300.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the income, then compare it with the cost.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = 10(12) = 120$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The income is €120 and the cost is €300, so the business makes a loss of

$$300 - 120 = 180$$

The loss is €180.

The income is below the cost, which is the loss region of the graph.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain why the cost line starts at $(0, 240)$ but the income line starts at
$(0, 0)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What does each formula give when $n = 0$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

At $n = 0$, the cost formula gives $240 + 0$ and the income formula gives $0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At $n = 0$ the cost is $240 + 4(0) = 240$, so the cost line starts at
$(0, 240)$. The €240 is paid before any candle is made.

At $n = 0$ the income is $10(0) = 0$, so the income line starts at $(0, 0)$. No
candles means no money in.

The starting height of each line is the constant in its formula.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A table shows a cost of €320 at $n = 20$ and €360 at $n = 30$. What is the
cost per candle, and what is the fixed cost?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The cost per candle is the change in cost divided by the change in $n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The cost rose by €40 while $n$ rose by $10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{cost per candle} = \frac{360 - 320}{30 - 20} = \frac{40}{10} = 4$$

So each candle costs €4.

The fixed cost is what is left after the candles: $320 - 4(20) = 320 - 80 = 240$, which is €240.

The formula is $C = 240 + 4n$, as before.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A rectangle is $5$ m long and $3$ m wide. Work out its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Area of a rectangle is length times width.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$5 \times 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$5 \times 3 = 15 \text{ m}^2$$

The same multiplication appears in an algebra question when the sides are
letters: a rectangle $x$ m long and $3$ m wide has area $3x$ m$^2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
