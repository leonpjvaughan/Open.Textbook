# 4.8 Solving quadratic equations — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Expand $(x - 2)(x - 3)$.
2. Solve $x - 2 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x^2 - 3x - 2x + 6 = x^2 - 5x + 6$.
2. $x = 2$. The zero product rule turns one quadratic equation into two short
   equations like this one.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ by factorising.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find two numbers that multiply to $6$ and add to $-5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The numbers are $-2$ and $-3$, so the factorisation is $(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

If a product is zero, at least one factor is zero:

$$x - 2 = 0 \quad\text{or}\quad x - 3 = 0$$

So $x = 2$ or $x = 3$. Check both in the original equation: each gives $0$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $x^2 + x - 12 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two numbers multiply to $-12$ and add to $1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The numbers are $4$ and $-3$, so $(x + 4)(x - 3) = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = -4 \quad\text{or}\quad x = 3$$

The two numbers have opposite signs, because the constant is negative.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $2x^2 - 7x + 3 = 0$ with the quadratic formula.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Identify $a = 2$, $b = -7$ and $c = 3$, then substitute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{49 - 24}}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = \frac{7 \pm \sqrt{25}}{4} = \frac{7 \pm 5}{4}$$

So $x = 3$ or $x = \frac{1}{2}$.

Check $x = \frac{1}{2}$: $2\left(\frac{1}{4}\right) - \frac{7}{2} + 3 = 0.5 - 3.5 + 3 = 0$.
Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $x^2 - 4x + 1 = 0$, leaving the answer in surd form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the formula with $a = 1$, $b = -4$ and $c = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 - 4}}{2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{12}}{2} = \frac{4 \pm 2\sqrt{3}}{2} = 2 \pm \sqrt{3}$$

So $x = 2 + \sqrt{3}$ or $x = 2 - \sqrt{3}$.

Simplify $\sqrt{12}$ to $2\sqrt{3}$ before dividing, and the $2$ cancels.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $n^2 - 100n + 2400 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two numbers multiply to $2400$ and add to $-100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The numbers are $-40$ and $-60$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(n - 40)(n - 60) = 0 \qquad n = 40 \quad\text{or}\quad n = 60$$

This is the break-even equation for the candle business with the discount, so the
business breaks even at $40$ candles and again at $60$ candles.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why a quadratic equation can have two solutions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What shape is the graph of a quadratic?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The graph is a parabola, which can cross a horizontal line twice.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The graph of $y = ax^2 + bx + c$ is a parabola. Solving
$ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ asks where the parabola crosses the horizontal axis, and a
parabola can cross it at two points, at one point, or at no point.

The algebra says the same thing: the formula contains $\pm$, which gives two
values whenever the discriminant is positive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A cylinder has radius $3$ cm and height $10$ cm. Work out its volume,
leaving $\pi$ in your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Volume of a cylinder is $\pi r^2 h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\pi \times 3^2 \times 10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = \pi(3)^2(10) = 90\pi \text{ cm}^3$$

The formula has a squared term, just as a quadratic does. The difference is that
here the squared quantity is known, and in this section it is the unknown to be
found.

</details>
</details>
</details>
