# 4.9 Linear inequalities — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 4.6 — solving linear equations.

**Objectives:** solve a linear inequality; reverse the sign when multiplying or
dividing by a negative; show the solution set on a number line.

By the end of this section you can describe a whole range of values, not only one,
and you can say where the candle business is profitable.

## Four symbols

An **inequality** compares two expressions with one of four symbols.

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| $<$ | is less than |
| $\leq$ | is less than or equal to |
| $>$ | is greater than |
| $\geq$ | is greater than or equal to |

The solution of an inequality is not one value but a whole **range** of values.
Solving works in the same way as solving an equation, with one extra rule.

## The extra rule

Do the same to both sides, just as before. But when you multiply or divide both
sides by a **negative** number, the inequality sign reverses.

$$2 < 5 \qquad \text{multiply by } -1: \qquad -2 > -5$$

The numbers $-2$ and $-5$ are on the other side of zero, so their order swaps.
The sign must swap with them.

**Example 1.** Solve $2x + 3 < 11$.

$$2x < 8 \qquad x < 4$$

Divide by $2$, which is positive, so the sign does not change.

**Example 2.** Solve $5x - 2 \geq 13$.

$$5x \geq 15 \qquad x \geq 3$$

The solution is every number from $3$ upward, including $3$ itself.

**Example 3.** Solve $-2x > 8$.

$$x < -4$$

Dividing by $-2$ reverses the sign, so the answer is $x < -4$, not $x > -4$.
Check with $x = -5$: $-2(-5) = 10$, and $10 > 8$. Correct.

## On a number line

A number line shows the solution at a glance.

- an **open** circle means the endpoint is not included, for $<$ and $>$;
- a **filled** circle means the endpoint is included, for $\leq$ and $\geq$;
- the arrow points in the direction of the solution.

So $x < 4$ is an open circle at $4$ with an arrow to the left, and $x \geq 3$ is a
filled circle at $3$ with an arrow to the right.

**Example 4.** Solve $3(x - 1) \leq 12$ and show the solution on a number line.

$$x - 1 \leq 4 \qquad x \leq 5$$

The circle at $5$ is filled, because $5$ is included, and the arrow points left.

## The profitable region

An inequality is how a business describes a range of good outcomes.

The candle business makes a profit when the income is greater than the cost:

$$10n > 240 + 4n$$

$$6n > 240 \qquad n > 40$$

So any number of candles above $40$ gives a profit. To break even or do better,
the business needs $n \geq 40$.

> **Watch out.** Multiply or divide by a negative, and the sign reverses. Leave
> it as it was and every value in the answer is wrong. A quick check with one
> number from the solution catches it: substitute a value and see whether the
> original inequality is true.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Write the inequality for the business breaking even
or doing better, with the income at least as large as the cost.

Solve it, then show the solution on a number line. Finally, use a filled or open
circle to say whether $40$ candles itself counts as breaking even, and explain
your choice.

## You should now be able to

- solve a linear inequality by the same steps as an equation;
- reverse the inequality sign when multiplying or dividing by a negative;
- show a solution set on a number line with the correct circle;
- turn a sentence about a range into an inequality and solve it.
