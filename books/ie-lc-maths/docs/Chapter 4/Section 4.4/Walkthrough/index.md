# 4.4 Features of linear relationships — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 4.2 — tables and graphs; Section 4.3 — formulae.

**Objectives:** find the gradient and the $y$-intercept of a line; interpret them
in context; recognise direct proportion; find the common value of two linear
relationships.

By the end of this section you can look at a formula or a graph and say what the
rate of change is and where the line starts.

## The form y = mx + c

A **linear relationship** has a constant rate of change, so its graph is a
straight line. Every linear relationship can be written in the form

$$y = mx + c$$

- $m$ is the **gradient**, the amount $y$ changes when $x$ increases by $1$;
- $c$ is the **$y$-intercept**, the value of $y$ when $x = 0$.

The gradient is the **rate of change**. It is found from two points:

$$m = \frac{\text{change in } y}{\text{change in } x}$$

**Example 1.** A line passes through $(2, 11)$ and $(5, 20)$. Find its gradient.

$$m = \frac{20 - 11}{5 - 2} = \frac{9}{3} = 3$$

The gradient is $3$. The line rises $3$ units for every $1$ unit across.

## Gradient and intercept in context

The candle formulae have a meaning attached to each number.

$$C = 240 + 4n \qquad R = 10n$$

For the cost $C = 240 + 4n$:

- the gradient is $4$, the cost of one more candle;
- the $y$-intercept is $240$, the fixed cost paid before any candle is made.

For the income $R = 10n$:

- the gradient is $10$, the price of one more candle;
- the $y$-intercept is $0$, because no candles means no money in.

The income gradient is larger than the cost gradient. That is what makes the
business profitable in the end: each extra candle brings in €10 and costs €4, so
it adds €6 towards the fixed cost.

**Example 2.** Compare $y = 2x + 7$ and $y = 5x + 1$. Which one grows faster, and
which one starts higher?

- $y = 2x + 7$ starts at $7$ and grows by $2$ each step;
- $y = 5x + 1$ starts at $1$ and grows by $5$ each step.

The second line grows faster because its gradient is larger. The first line
starts higher because its intercept is larger. At small $x$ the first is above
the second; as $x$ grows, the faster line overtakes it.

## Direct proportion

A relationship is in **direct proportion** when $y = kx$ for some constant $k$,
with no constant added. Its graph is a straight line through the origin, and the
ratio $y/x$ is the same for every point.

The income $R = 10n$ is directly proportional to the number of candles, with
$k = 10$: twice the candles give twice the income.

The cost $C = 240 + 4n$ is **not** directly proportional, because of the €240. At
$n = 10$ the ratio $C/n = 280/10 = 28$, and at $n = 20$ it is $320/20 = 16$. The
ratio changes, so the relationship is linear but not proportional.

> **Watch out.** The gradient is not $y$ divided by $x$. For the cost at
> $n = 10$, $C/n = 28$, but the gradient is $4$. The gradient compares two
> *changes*, not two values, so the fixed cost must be removed before you divide.

## Finding a common value

Two linear relationships often have one point in common, and the point is what
the question is really about.

**Example 3.** Find the value of $x$ where $y = 2x + 1$ and $y = 4x - 3$ are
equal.

At the common point both expressions give the same $y$:

$$2x + 1 = 4x - 3 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 4 = 2x \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 2$$

Then $y = 2(2) + 1 = 5$. The two lines meet at $(2, 5)$.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The cost is $C = 240 + 4n$ and the income is
$R = 10n$.

State the gradient and the $y$-intercept of each line, and say what each number
means in the story. Then say which gradient is larger and what that tells you
about the business. Finally, describe what the point where the two lines meet
means. Section 4.7 finds that point with a method for two equations.

## You should now be able to

- read the gradient and the $y$-intercept from a formula or a graph;
- find a gradient from two points using the change in $y$ over the change in $x$;
- explain a gradient and an intercept in the context they came from;
- decide whether a relationship is directly proportional;
- find the common value of two linear relationships.
