# 4.4 Features of linear relationships — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the gap in $4, 9, 14, \dots$
2. Work out $10 \times 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The gap is $5$, which is the gradient of the line through these points.
2. $10 \times 8 = 80$. A gradient of $10$ multiplies the input in the same way.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** State the gradient and the $y$-intercept of $y = 3x + 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare it with $y = mx + c$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The number multiplying $x$ is the gradient, and the number on its own is the
intercept.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The gradient is $3$ and the $y$-intercept is $5$.

The line crosses the vertical axis at $5$ and rises $3$ for every $1$ across.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the gradient of the line through $(1, 4)$ and $(3, 10)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the change in $y$ over the change in $x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The change in $y$ is $10 - 4 = 6$ and the change in $x$ is $3 - 1 = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$m = \frac{10 - 4}{3 - 1} = \frac{6}{2} = 3$$

The gradient is $3$. Subtracting the coordinates in the same order top and
bottom is what keeps the sign right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A cost formula is $C = 240 + 4n$. State the gradient and the $y$-intercept,
and say what each means.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which number is paid once, and which is paid for each candle?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The gradient is the coefficient of $n$, and the intercept is the constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The gradient is $4$ and the $y$-intercept is $240$.

The gradient is the cost of making one more candle. The intercept is the fixed
cost, paid before any candle is made.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Is $R = 10n$ directly proportional? Explain.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Does the graph pass through the origin?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There is no constant added, so at $n = 0$ the income is $0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Yes. The relationship has the form $y = kx$ with $k = 10$ and no constant, so
the graph is a straight line through the origin.

Twice the candles give twice the income, and the ratio $R/n$ is always $10$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Find the value of $x$ where $y = 3x - 2$ and $y = x + 4$ are equal, and the
common value of $y$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the common point both expressions give the same $y$, so set them equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3x - 2 = x + 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x - 2 = x + 4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad 2x = 6 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 3$$

Then $y = 3(3) - 2 = 7$. Check with the other formula: $y = 3 + 4 = 7$. Correct.

The two lines meet at $(3, 7)$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Compare $y = 4x + 1$ and $y = 2x + 6$. Which grows faster, and which starts
higher?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the gradients first, then the intercepts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first has gradient $4$ and intercept $1$; the second has gradient $2$ and
intercept $6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first line grows faster, because its gradient of $4$ is larger than $2$. The
second line starts higher, because its intercept of $6$ is larger than $1$.

The first line overtakes the second as $x$ grows. Setting $4x + 1 = 2x + 6$ gives
$x = 2.5$, so they meet at $x = 2.5$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** Work out $2^5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply five $2$s together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^5 = 32$$

A power grows by multiplying, so its graph curves upward. That is the difference
between a linear relationship and a power, and Section 4.8 meets the curve a
quadratic makes.

</details>
</details>
</details>
