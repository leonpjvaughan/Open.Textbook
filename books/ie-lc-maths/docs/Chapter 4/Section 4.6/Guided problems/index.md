# 4.6 Solving linear equations — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Solve $x + 5 = 12$.
2. Simplify $3x + 2x$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $x = 7$, by subtracting $5$ from both sides.
2. $3x + 2x = 5x$. Collecting like terms is often the last step of solving an
   equation.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $4x + 5 = 17$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Undo the $+5$ first, then the $\times 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $5$ from both sides: $4x = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4x = 12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 3$$

Check: $4(3) + 5 = 17$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Solve $3x - 7 = 11$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add $7$ to both sides first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3x = 18$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x = 18 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 6$$

Check: $3(6) - 7 = 11$. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Solve $5x + 2 = 2x + 14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Collect the $x$ terms on one side and the numbers on the other.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract $2x$: $3x + 2 = 14$. Then subtract $2$: $3x = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3x = 12 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 4$$

Check both sides: $5(4) + 2 = 22$ and $2(4) + 14 = 22$. Both sides agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Solve $240 + 4n = 10n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract $4n$ from both sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$240 = 6n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$n = 40$$

At $40$ candles the cost is $240 + 4(40) = 400$, which is €400, and the income is
$10(40) = 400$, which is €400. They are equal, so this is the break-even point.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Solve $3(x - 2) = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Expand the bracket, or divide both sides by $3$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Dividing by $3$ gives $x - 2 = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x - 2 = 4 \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = 6$$

Check: $3(6 - 2) = 3(4) = 12$. Correct.

Dividing first is quicker here, because both sides divide exactly.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student claims that $x = 5$ solves $2x + 3 = 14$. Check the claim.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $x = 5$ into the left side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2(5) + 3 = 13$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The left side is $13$, but the right side is $14$, so $x = 5$ does not solve the
equation.

The correct solution is $2x = 11$, so $x = 5.5$. Substituting the answer back
into the original equation is what caught the error.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** Solve $2^x = 32$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write $32$ as a power of $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$32 = 2^5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2^x = 2^5$, so $x = 5$.

Here the unknown is the index rather than a coefficient, but the aim is the same:
find the value that makes the two sides equal.

</details>
</details>
</details>
