# 10.7 Independent events — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. $P(A \cap B) = 0.2$ and $P(B) = 0.5$. Find $P(A \mid B)$.
2. Work out $0.5 \times 0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $P(A \mid B) = \dfrac{0.2}{0.5} = 0.4$.
2. $0.5 \times 0.5 = 0.25$.

This section compares $P(A \cap B)$ with $P(A) \times P(B)$, so both ideas are needed.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A fair coin is tossed twice. Find the probability of two heads.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two tosses are independent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{1}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{HH}) = \frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{1}{4}$$

There are four equally likely outcomes, and HH is one of them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A fair die is rolled twice. Find the probability of two sixes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each roll has probability $\dfrac{1}{6}$ of a six.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{1}{6} \times \dfrac{1}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(6 \text{ and } 6) = \frac{1}{6} \times \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{36}$$

The rolls are independent, so the probabilities multiply.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** $A$ and $B$ are independent with $P(A) = 0.5$ and $P(B) = 0.4$. Find $P(A \cap B)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the two probabilities.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.5 \times 0.4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(A \cap B) = 0.5 \times 0.4 = 0.2$$

Independence is what lets you multiply without more information.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** $P(A) = 0.5$, $P(B) = 0.4$ and $P(A \cap B) = 0.25$. Are $A$ and $B$ independent?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out $P(A) \times P(B)$ and compare.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.5 \times 0.4 = 0.2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(A) \times P(B) = 0.2 \ne 0.25 = P(A \cap B)$$

The two values differ, so $A$ and $B$ are not independent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Three independent events each have probability $0.5$. Find the probability that all
three happen.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply all three probabilities.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.5 \times 0.5 \times 0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.5 \times 0.5 \times 0.5 = \frac{1}{8}$$

Each event leaves the chance of the others unchanged.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Back to the chapter problem. The lucky dip bag has $5$ red and $3$ blue counters. Find
the probability of two red counters when the counter is replaced, and compare it with the
probability without replacement, $\dfrac{5}{14}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

With replacement the probability of red is $\dfrac{5}{8}$ both times.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{5}{8} \times \dfrac{5}{8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{both red, with replacement}) = \frac{5}{8} \times \frac{5}{8} = \frac{25}{64} \approx 0.391$$

This is greater than $\dfrac{5}{14} \approx 0.357$, so replacing the counter gives the
better chance of the small prize. The draws without replacement are not independent.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 10.6

**7.** A bag holds $3$ red and $2$ green counters. Two are drawn without replacement. Find
the probability that both are red.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The draws are not independent, so use a conditional probability.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{3}{5} \times \dfrac{2}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P(\text{both red}) = \frac{3}{5} \times \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{10}$$

Without replacement, the second probability changes, so this is not a product of
independent events.

</details>
</details>
</details>
