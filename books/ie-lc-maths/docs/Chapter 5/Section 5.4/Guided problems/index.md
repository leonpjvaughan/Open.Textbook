# 5.4 The Argand diagram and modulus — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the distance between $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 6)$.
2. Write down the conjugate of $-3 + 2i$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$.
2. $-3 - 2i$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find $|6 + 8i|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{36 + 64}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|6 + 8i| = \sqrt{36 + 64} = \sqrt{100} = 10$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find $|1 - i|$, leaving your answer as a surd.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\sqrt{1^2 + (-1)^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|1 - i| = \sqrt{1 + 1} = \sqrt{2}$$

The sign of the imaginary part does not matter: $(-1)^2 = 1^2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the distance between $2 + i$ and $-1 + 5i$ on the Argand diagram.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract, then take the modulus.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(-1 + 5i) - (2 + i) = -3 + 4i$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|(-1 + 5i) - (2 + i)| = |-3 + 4i| = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$$

The horizontal gap is $3$ and the vertical gap is $4$, whichever order you subtract in,
because the modulus squares both.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** For $z = 1 + i$ and $w = 3 + 4i$, work out $|z|$, $|w|$ and $|zw|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$|zw| = |z||w|$, or multiply first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$|z| = \sqrt{2}$, $|w| = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|z| = \sqrt{2} \qquad |w| = 5 \qquad |zw| = 5\sqrt{2}$$

Check by multiplying: $zw = (1 + i)(3 + 4i) = 3 + 4i + 3i + 4i^2 = -1 + 7i$, and
$|-1 + 7i| = \sqrt{1 + 49} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$. Both routes agree.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Describe the set of points on the Argand diagram for which $|z| = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The modulus is a distance from the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

All points at distance 3 from the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

It is a **circle** of radius $3$ centred at the origin.

Writing $z = x + iy$, $|z| = 3$ becomes $x^2 + y^2 = 9$, which is the ordinary circle
equation. The Argand diagram turns a modulus equation into a geometrical shape.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A point $z$ lies on the imaginary axis. What can you say about $\operatorname{Re}(z)$
and about $\bar z$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The imaginary axis is the vertical axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Points on it have the form $bi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Points on the imaginary axis have $\operatorname{Re}(z) = 0$, so $z = bi$ for some real
$b$.

Then $\bar z = -bi = -z$. So a purely imaginary number's conjugate is its negative. That
is the one case where the conjugate and the negative coincide — and it happens only
because the real part is zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 5.3

**7.** For $z = 3 + 4i$, plot $z$, $\bar z$ and $|z|$ on one Argand diagram, and say what
the three points tell you.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$z$ is at $(3, 4)$, and $|z|$ is a number, not a point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$|z| = 5$, which is a real number, so it lies on the real axis at $(5, 0)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

- $z = 3 + 4i$ is the point $(3, 4)$.
- $\bar z = 3 - 4i$ is the point $(3, -4)$, the reflection of $z$ in the real axis.
- $|z| = 5$ is the point $(5, 0)$ on the real axis.

The three together show the two ideas of the section. The conjugate is a reflection in the
real axis, and the modulus is a **distance** — here it is the length of the line from the
origin to $(3, 4)$, and it happens to land on a whole number, which is why the $3$-$4$-$5$
triangle is worth remembering.

</details>
</details>
</details>
