# 1.7 Scientific notation and accuracy — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $10^3$.
2. Round $3.7$ to the nearest whole number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $10^3 = 1000$.
2. $3.7 \approx 4$, because the digit after the point is $7$.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Write $62\,000$ in scientific notation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put one non-zero digit before the decimal point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Count how many places the point moves: $62\,000 = 6.2 \times 10^?$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$62\,000 = 6.2 \times 10^4$$

The point moved four places to the left, so the index is $4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Write $0.0009$ in scientific notation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The number is smaller than $1$, so the index is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Move the point to sit after the $9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.0009 = 9 \times 10^{-4}$$

The point moved four places to the right, so the index is $-4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Write $7.1 \times 10^4$ as an ordinary number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A positive index moves the point to the right.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Move the point four places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7.1 \times 10^4 = 71\,000$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $(4 \times 10^2)(2 \times 10^3)$ and write the answer in
scientific notation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the front numbers, add the indices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4 \times 2 = 8$ and $2 + 3 = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(4 \times 10^2)(2 \times 10^3) = 8 \times 10^5$$

The front number is between $1$ and $10$, so the answer is already in scientific
notation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Round $0.006\,789$ to 2 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The leading zeros do not count. The first significant figure is $6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first two significant figures are $6$ and $7$. Look at the next digit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$0.006\,789 \approx 0.0068$$

The next digit is $8$, so the $7$ rounds up to $8$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Round $25.371$ to 3 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first three significant figures are $2$, $5$ and $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The next digit is $7$, so round the $3$ up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$25.371 \approx 25.4$$

The first three significant figures become $2$, $5$ and $4$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 1.6

**7.** Solve $2^x = 64$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write $64$ as a power of $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$64 = 2^6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^x = 2^6 \qquad x = 6$$

Scientific notation uses the same index arithmetic, with $10$ as the base.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**8.** The exact quote is €533.937096. Round it to 3 significant figures, then
write your answer as an ordinary number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first three significant figures are $5$, $3$ and $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The next digit is $9$, so round the last $3$ up.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$533.937096 \approx 534$$

to 3 significant figures. As an ordinary number that is $534$.

To the nearest cent the quote is €533.94. Rounding to 3 significant figures is
rougher, and it is useful for a quick check rather than for the invoice.

</details>
</details>
</details>
