# 1.5 Indices and the rules of indices — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $3^2$ and $3^3$.
2. Work out $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $3^2 = 9$ and $3^3 = 27$.
2. $2 \times 2 \times 2 \times 2 = 16 = 2^4$. Counting the copies is what an
   index does.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Simplify $2^5 \times 2^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bases are the same, so use the multiplying law.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the indices: $5 + 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2^5 \times 2^3 = 2^{5+3} = 2^8$$

$2^8 = 256$ if you need the number. The single power is the simplified answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Simplify $7^6 \div 7^4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bases are the same, so use the dividing law.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract the indices: $6 - 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$7^6 \div 7^4 = 7^{6-4} = 7^2 = 49$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Simplify $(3^2)^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A power of a power multiplies the indices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2 \times 3 = 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(3^2)^3 = 3^{2 \times 3} = 3^6 = 729$$

The bracket tells you to multiply. Without it, $3^2 \times 3^3$ would add to
$3^5$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Work out $4^0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the quotient law with the same power on top and bottom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4^2 \div 4^2 = 4^{2-2} = 4^0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4^0 = 1$$

Any non-zero number raised to the power $0$ is $1$, because the number divided by
itself is $1$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Write $3^{-2}$ as a fraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A negative index means a reciprocal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3^{-2} = \dfrac{1}{3^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3^{-2} = \frac{1}{3^2} = \frac{1}{9}$$

The minus sign on the index flips the base to the other side of the fraction bar.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Simplify $y^4 \times y^7$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Same base, so add the indices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4 + 7 = 11$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$y^4 \times y^7 = y^{11}$$

The law is the same for a letter as for a number.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 1.2

**7.** Simplify $\sqrt{50}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the largest square factor of $50$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$50 = 25 \times 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$$

A square root is a power of $\frac{1}{2}$, so surds and indices are two views of
the same idea.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**8.** The room is $4.35$ m by $3.28$ m. If both measurements were doubled, by
what factor would the area grow?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area is a length times a length, so each length is multiplied by $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $(2x)^2 = 2^2 x^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The area is $4.35 \times 3.28$. Doubling both measurements gives
$(2 \times 4.35) \times (2 \times 3.28) = 2^2 \times (4.35 \times 3.28)$.

So the area grows by a factor of $4$. A doubling of each length quadruples the
area, because the area depends on the square of a length.

</details>
</details>
</details>
