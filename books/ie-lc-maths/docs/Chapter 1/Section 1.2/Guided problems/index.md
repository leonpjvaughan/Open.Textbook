# 1.2 Irrational numbers and surds — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $0.3$ as a fraction in its lowest terms.
2. Work out $7^2$ and $2^2 \times 5^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.3 = \dfrac{3}{10}$.
2. $7^2 = 49$, and $2^2 \times 5^2 = 4 \times 25 = 100$. Square factors are what
   make a surd simplify.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Simplify $\sqrt{20}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look for the largest square factor of $20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$20 = 4 \times 5$, and $\sqrt{4} = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{20} = \sqrt{4 \times 5} = \sqrt{4}\,\sqrt{5} = 2\sqrt{5}$$

The largest square factor of $20$ is $4$. Check: $20 = 4 \times 5$ and no larger
square divides it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Simplify $\sqrt{27}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which square divides $27$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$27 = 9 \times 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{27} = \sqrt{9 \times 3} = 3\sqrt{3}$$

$9$ is the largest square factor of $27$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Simplify $\sqrt{32} + \sqrt{18}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Simplify each surd on its own first, then add like surds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{32} = 4\sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{18} = 3\sqrt{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{32} = \sqrt{16 \times 2} = 4\sqrt{2} \qquad \sqrt{18} = \sqrt{9 \times 2} = 3\sqrt{2}$$

$$4\sqrt{2} + 3\sqrt{2} = 7\sqrt{2}$$

Both are like surds once simplified, because both carry $\sqrt{2}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Write $\dfrac{4}{\sqrt{2}}$ with a whole-number denominator.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply top and bottom by $\sqrt{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} = 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} \times \frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{4\sqrt{2}}{2} = 2\sqrt{2}$$

The bottom is now a whole number, so the answer is rationalised.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Write $\dfrac{3}{\sqrt{7} - 2}$ in the form $a + b\sqrt{7}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply top and bottom by the conjugate $\sqrt{7} + 2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(\sqrt{7} - 2)(\sqrt{7} + 2) = 7 - 4 = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{3}{\sqrt{7} - 2} \times \frac{\sqrt{7} + 2}{\sqrt{7} + 2} = \frac{3(\sqrt{7} + 2)}{3} = \sqrt{7} + 2$$

The $3$ on the bottom cancels the $3$ on the top. The answer is
$\sqrt{7} + 2$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Simplify $(\sqrt{5} + 2)(\sqrt{5} - 2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is the difference of two squares, $(a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(\sqrt{5})^2 - 2^2 = 5 - 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(\sqrt{5})^2 - 2^2 = 5 - 4 = 1$$

The surd disappears. That is why multiplying by a conjugate clears the bottom of
a fraction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 1.1

**7.** Is $\sqrt{49}$ rational or irrational? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the square root before you decide.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Which whole number squares to $49$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$\sqrt{49} = 7$, which is a whole number. So $\sqrt{49}$ is rational.

It is not a surd, because it simplifies to a rational number. Only a root that
stays under the radical sign is a surd.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**8.** The diagonal of the room is $\sqrt{4.35^2 + 3.28^2}$. Work out the value
under the root, then write the diagonal to 2 decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square each measurement, then add.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4.35^2 = 18.9225$ and $3.28^2 = 10.7584$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$18.9225 + 10.7584 = 29.6809$$

$$d = \sqrt{29.6809} = 5.448\dots \approx 5.45 \text{ m}$$

The exact length is $\sqrt{29.6809}$, which is irrational. $5.45$ m is a rounded
value, so it is not the exact length.

</details>
</details>
</details>
