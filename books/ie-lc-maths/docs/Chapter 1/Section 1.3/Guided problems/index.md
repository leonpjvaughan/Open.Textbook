# 1.3 Geometric construction of √2 and √3 — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Simplify $\sqrt{8}$.
2. Find the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $3$ and $4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sqrt{8} = 2\sqrt{2}$.
2. $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$. Pythagoras turns two legs into the
   hypotenuse.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the length of the diagonal of a unit square.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $1$ and $1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$$

The length is irrational, but it is a real length that you can draw.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Describe how to construct $\sqrt{2}$ on the number line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with a unit square standing on the line at $0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the diagonal as the compass opening.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw a square of side $1$ on the number line with one corner at $0$. Its diagonal
is $\sqrt{2}$ by Pythagoras.

Open the compass to the diagonal, put the point at $0$, and draw an arc across the
number line. The crossing point is at $\sqrt{2}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Describe how to construct $\sqrt{3}$ on the number line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the triangle with legs $1$ and $\sqrt{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Its hypotenuse is $\sqrt{1 + 2} = \sqrt{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw a right triangle with legs $1$ and $\sqrt{2}$. Its hypotenuse is

$$\sqrt{1^2 + (\sqrt{2})^2} = \sqrt{3}$$

Open the compass to the hypotenuse and mark the distance from $0$ on the number
line. The point marked is $\sqrt{3}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Find the length of the diagonal of a rectangle measuring $2$ by $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The diagonal is the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs $2$ and $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sqrt{2^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \sqrt{2^2 + 3^2} = \sqrt{13}$$

$13$ has no square factor, so $\sqrt{13}$ is in simplest form.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Show that $\sqrt{2}$ lies between $1.4$ and $1.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square all three numbers and compare.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1.4^2 = 1.96$ and $1.5^2 = 2.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$1.4^2 = 1.96$, $(\sqrt{2})^2 = 2$ and $1.5^2 = 2.25$. Since
$1.96 < 2 < 2.25$, the square roots are in the same order:

$$1.4 < \sqrt{2} < 1.5$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Describe how to construct $\sqrt{5}$ on the number line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write $5$ as a sum of two squares.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$5 = 1 + 4 = 1^2 + 2^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw a right triangle with legs $1$ and $2$. Its hypotenuse is
$\sqrt{1^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{5}$.

Open the compass to the hypotenuse and mark the distance from $0$ on the number
line. The point marked is $\sqrt{5}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 1.2

**7.** Simplify $(\sqrt{7} + \sqrt{3})(\sqrt{7} - \sqrt{3})$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the difference of two squares.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$(\sqrt{7})^2 - (\sqrt{3})^2 = 7 - 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(\sqrt{7})^2 - (\sqrt{3})^2 = 7 - 3 = 4$$

The surds cancel, so the product is a whole number. This is the same identity that
makes a conjugate clear a denominator.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem

**8.** The room is $4.35$ m by $3.28$ m. A square of side $4.35$ m would have a
diagonal of $4.35\sqrt{2}$ m. Explain why that diagonal is irrational.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A rational number times an irrational number is irrational.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Suppose $4.35\sqrt{2}$ were a fraction. Divide by $4.35$ and see what follows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Suppose $4.35\sqrt{2} = \dfrac{a}{b}$ for whole numbers $a$ and $b$. Then

$$\sqrt{2} = \frac{a}{4.35b}$$

The right-hand side is a fraction, because $4.35$ is a fraction and the product
and quotient of fractions are fractions. That would make $\sqrt{2}$ rational,
which Section 1.2 proved false. So $4.35\sqrt{2}$ is irrational.

A non-zero rational number times an irrational number is always irrational.

</details>
</details>
</details>
