# 16.6 Area and the area of a triangle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 6$.
2. A rectangle is $8$ cm by $5$ cm. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 6 = 30$. Half of $10$ is $5$, and $5 \times 6 = 30$.
2. The area is $40$ cm$^2$. The area of a rectangle is length times width.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Chapter 3 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A triangle has base $10$ cm and height $6$ cm. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area is half the base by the height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 6 = 30 \text{ cm}^2.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A parallelogram has base $9$ m and height $4$ m. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area of a parallelogram is base times height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$9 \times 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = 9 \times 4 = 36 \text{ m}^2.$$

The height is perpendicular to the base, so the slanted side is not used.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A trapezium has parallel sides of $8$ cm and $12$ cm, and the distance between them
is $5$ cm. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average the two parallel sides, then multiply by the distance between them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\frac{8 + 12}{2} \times 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = \frac{8 + 12}{2} \times 5 = 10 \times 5 = 50 \text{ cm}^2.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A triangle has sides of $8$ cm and $11$ cm, and the angle between them is
$30^\circ$. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the formula $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 8 \times 11 \times \sin 30^\circ = 44 \times 0.5 = 22 \text{ cm}^2.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A rectangle is $60$ m long and $25$ m wide. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The area of a rectangle is length times width.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$60 \times 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = 60 \times 25 = 1500 \text{ m}^2.$$

This is the field from the chapter problem.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why base times height does not depend on which side of a triangle is chosen
as the base.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare two altitudes, and look for similar triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two right-angled triangles that contain the altitudes share an angle at a vertex.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let the altitudes be $AD$ from $A$ to $BC$, and $BE$ from $B$ to $AC$.

The triangles $BCE$ and $ACD$ are right-angled and share the angle at $C$, so they are
similar by the AA rule. Therefore

$$\frac{|AD|}{|BE|} = \frac{|AC|}{|BC|},$$

and multiplying across gives

$$|AD| \times |BC| = |BE| \times |AC|.$$

The left side is the base $BC$ times its height, and the right side is the base $AC$
times its height. The two products are equal, so the choice of base does not matter.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A rectangular garden is $12$ m by $9$ m. A triangular flower bed in the garden has
base $6$ m and height $4$ m. Find the area of the garden that is left as grass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the two areas separately, then subtract.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The garden area is $12 \times 9$, and the bed area is $\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{garden} = 12 \times 9 = 108 \text{ m}^2$$

$$\text{flower bed} = \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 4 = 12 \text{ m}^2$$

$$\text{grass} = 108 - 12 = 96 \text{ m}^2.$$

</details>
</details>
</details>
