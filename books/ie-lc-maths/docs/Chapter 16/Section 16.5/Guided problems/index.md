# 16.5 Pythagoras' theorem proved — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A right-angled triangle has one acute angle of $35^\circ$. Find the other acute angle.
2. Work out $9^2 + 12^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $90 - 35 = 55^\circ$. The two acute angles of a right-angled triangle add to
   $90^\circ$.
2. $9^2 + 12^2 = 81 + 144 = 225$. This is the square of the hypotenuse in the $9$, $12$,
   $15$ triangle.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Section 15.6 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A right-angled triangle has legs of $9$ cm and $12$ cm. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The hypotenuse $h$ is opposite the right angle, so add the two squares.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h^2 = 9^2 + 12^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h^2 = 81 + 144 = 225$$

$$h = \sqrt{225} = 15 \text{ cm}$$

Check: $9^2 + 12^2 = 15^2$, so the triangle is right-angled.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of $13$ cm and one leg of $5$ cm. Find the
other leg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The unknown side is the leg $b$, so subtract.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$b^2 = 13^2 - 5^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$b^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$$

$$b = \sqrt{144} = 12 \text{ cm}$$

The other leg is $12$ cm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A triangle has sides of $6$ cm, $8$ cm and $10$ cm. Is it right-angled?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The longest side would be the hypotenuse. Test the converse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Compare $6^2 + 8^2$ with $10^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6^2 + 8^2 = 36 + 64 = 100 = 10^2$$

The sum of the squares of the two shorter sides equals the square of the longest side, so
the triangle is right-angled. The right angle is opposite the $10$ cm side.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A ladder $5$ m long leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is $3$ m from the base
of the wall. How far up the wall does the ladder reach?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The ladder is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle, and the height up the wall is
$h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h^2 = 5^2 - 3^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h^2 = 25 - 9 = 16$$

$$h = 4 \text{ m}$$

The ladder reaches $4$ m up the wall.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A rectangle is $60$ m long and $25$ m wide. Find the length of a diagonal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the diagonal as $d$. A diagonal of a rectangle makes a right-angled triangle with
two sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$d^2 = 60^2 + 25^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d^2 = 3600 + 625 = 4225$$

$$d = \sqrt{4225} = 65 \text{ m}$$

The diagonal is $65$ m. This is the diagonal of the field from the chapter problem.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Prove Pythagoras' theorem using similar triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Drop the perpendicular from the right angle to the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two pairs of similar triangles appear. Write the proportional sides for each pair.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $\triangle ABC$ have a right angle at $B$. Draw the perpendicular from $B$ to $AC$,
meeting $AC$ at $D$.

$\triangle ABC \sim \triangle ADB$, because both have a right angle and they share the
angle at $A$. So

$$\frac{|AC|}{|AB|} = \frac{|AB|}{|AD|}, \qquad |AB|^2 = |AC| \times |AD|.$$

In the same way $\triangle ABC \sim \triangle BDC$, so

$$|BC|^2 = |AC| \times |DC|.$$

Add the two equations:

$$|AB|^2 + |BC|^2 = |AC| \times (|AD| + |DC|) = |AC| \times |AC| = |AC|^2.$$

The theorem follows.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 13

**7.** Find the distance between the points $A(1,2)$ and $B(4,6)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The distance formula comes from Pythagoras' theorem.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The horizontal difference is $3$, and the vertical difference is $4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|AB|^2 = (4 - 1)^2 + (6 - 2)^2 = 3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25$$

$$|AB| = \sqrt{25} = 5.$$

The distance is $5$ units.

</details>
</details>
</details>
