# 16.1 Quadrilaterals and parallelograms — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. In a triangle, two of the angles are $70^\circ$ and $55^\circ$. Find the third angle.
2. Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle is $68^\circ$. Find its
   alternate angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $180 - 70 - 55 = 55^\circ$. The three angles of a triangle add to $180^\circ$.
2. $68^\circ$. The two lines are parallel, so the alternate angles are equal, by
   Theorem 3 part 2.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Sections 15.2 and 15.4 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A quadrilateral has both pairs of opposite sides parallel. Name the shape.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The definition names the shape directly.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Which named quadrilateral is defined by two pairs of parallel opposite sides?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

It is a parallelogram. A parallelogram is a quadrilateral in which both pairs of opposite
sides are parallel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** In a parallelogram $ABCD$, $|AB| = 14$ cm. Find $|DC|$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$AB$ and $DC$ are opposite sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Theorem 9 says opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$|DC| = |AB| = 14 \text{ cm}$$

The two sides are opposite each other, so they are equal by Theorem 9.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** In a parallelogram $ABCD$, $|\angle A| = 68^\circ$. Find the other three angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One of the three is opposite $\angle A$, and the other two are consecutive to it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Opposite angles are equal. Consecutive angles are supplementary.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$|\angle C| = 68^\circ$, because it is opposite $\angle A$.

$|\angle B| = |\angle D| = 180^\circ - 68^\circ = 112^\circ$, because each is consecutive
to $\angle A$.

Check: $68 + 112 + 68 + 112 = 360$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A parallelogram has adjacent sides of $9$ cm and $7$ cm. Find its perimeter.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Opposite sides are equal, so the four sides come in two matching pairs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The four sides are $9$, $7$, $9$ and $7$ cm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9 + 7 + 9 + 7 = 32 \text{ cm}$$

The perimeter is $2 \times (9 + 7) = 32$ cm, which agrees.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Prove that the opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal. Write the proof in your
own words, and name each fact you use.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw a diagonal, and look for two congruent triangles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the alternate angles at the diagonal, and the ASA test.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $ABCD$ be a parallelogram, with $AB \parallel DC$ and $AD \parallel BC$. Draw the
diagonal $BD$.

- $|\angle ABD| = |\angle BDC|$, by alternate angles at the parallel lines $AB$ and $DC$.
- $|\angle ADB| = |\angle DBC|$, by alternate angles at the parallel lines $AD$ and $BC$.
- $|BD| = |DB|$, the same side.

So $\triangle DAB \cong \triangle BCD$ by ASA. Therefore $|AB| = |DC|$ and
$|AD| = |BC|$.

The facts used are the alternate angles theorem and the ASA congruence test.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A rectangle has a right angle at each of its four vertices. Explain why every
rectangle is a parallelogram.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need to show that each pair of opposite sides is parallel.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

At one vertex, the angle on the other side of a side is also $90^\circ$. Compare it with
the opposite angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $ABCD$ be a rectangle. Extend $AB$ past $A$ to $E$. The angles $\angle EAD$ and
$\angle ADC$ are alternate angles at the transversal $AD$.

$\angle EAD$ is supplementary to the right angle $\angle DAB$, so
$|\angle EAD| = 90^\circ$. Also $|\angle ADC| = 90^\circ$.

The alternate angles are equal, so $AB \parallel DC$ by Theorem 3 part 1. The same
argument gives $AD \parallel BC$. Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, so $ABCD$ is
a parallelogram.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 13

**7.** A quadrilateral has vertices $A(0,0)$, $B(4,1)$, $C(6,5)$ and $D(2,4)$. Show that
it is a parallelogram.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A quadrilateral is a parallelogram when both pairs of opposite sides are parallel.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the slope of each side, and compare opposite sides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Slope of $AB$:
$$\frac{1 - 0}{4 - 0} = \frac{1}{4}.$$

Slope of $DC$:
$$\frac{4 - 5}{2 - 6} = \frac{-1}{-4} = \frac{1}{4}.$$

The slopes are equal, so $AB \parallel DC$.

Slope of $AD$:
$$\frac{4 - 0}{2 - 0} = 2.$$

Slope of $BC$:
$$\frac{5 - 1}{6 - 4} = \frac{4}{2} = 2.$$

The slopes are equal, so $AD \parallel BC$. Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, so
the quadrilateral is a parallelogram.

</details>
</details>
</details>
