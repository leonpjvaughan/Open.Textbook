# 16.7 Constructions 7–15 — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Two sides and the angle between them are given. Which congruence test fixes the
   triangle?
2. A segment is $18$ cm long. It is divided into three equal parts. Find the length of
   each part.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. SAS. The angle lies between the two given sides, so the triangle is fixed.
2. $18 \div 3 = 6$ cm. Three equal parts fill the whole segment.

If either answer felt uncertain, look again at Sections 15.3 and 15.7 before you carry on.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A segment is $24$ cm long. Divide it into four equal parts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Mark four equal segments along a ray, then draw parallels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Join the last mark to the end of the segment, then draw parallels through the other marks.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw a ray from one end at any angle. Mark four equal segments along it with the compass.
Join the fourth mark to the other end of the segment. Draw lines parallel to that join
through the first three marks.

Each part is $24 \div 4 = 6$ cm. The parallel lines cut off equal segments on the
original segment by Theorem 11.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Construct a triangle with sides of $5$ cm, $6$ cm and $7$ cm. Which congruence
test does the construction use?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw one side, then find the third vertex from the other two.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two arcs, one from each end of the first side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw the $7$ cm side. With centre at one end, draw an arc of radius $5$ cm. With centre at
the other end, draw an arc of radius $6$ cm. The arcs meet at the third vertex.

The construction uses SSS, because the three sides are fixed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A right-angled triangle has a hypotenuse of $13$ cm and one leg of $5$ cm. Construct
it, and find the third side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw the leg, then construct a perpendicular at one end.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The arc from the other end has radius equal to the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw the $5$ cm leg $AB$. Construct the perpendicular to $AB$ at $B$. With centre $A$ and
radius $13$ cm, draw an arc cutting the perpendicular at $C$. Draw $AC$.

The third side is found by Pythagoras:

$$\sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = \sqrt{169 - 25} = \sqrt{144} = 12 \text{ cm}.$$

The construction is justified by RHS.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Construct a rectangle with sides of $60$ mm and $25$ mm.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with one side, then make the right angles at its ends.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Mark the second side on one perpendicular, then complete the rectangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw the $60$ mm side $AB$. Construct the perpendicular to $AB$ at $A$, and at $B$. On the
perpendicular at $B$, mark $C$ with $|BC| = 25$ mm. Construct the perpendicular to $BC$ at
$C$, meeting the perpendicular at $A$ at $D$.

The four angles are right angles, so $ABCD$ is a rectangle. The opposite sides are equal by
Theorem 9, so $|CD| = 60$ mm and $|AD| = 25$ mm.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Construct a triangle with two angles of $40^\circ$ and $65^\circ$, and the side
between them $8$ cm long. Find the third angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw the side first, then put one angle at each end.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The three angles add to $180^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Draw the $8$ cm side $AB$. At $A$, construct an angle of $40^\circ$. At $B$, construct an
angle of $65^\circ$ on the same side of $AB$. The two rays meet at $C$.

The third angle is

$$180^\circ - 40^\circ - 65^\circ = 75^\circ.$$

The construction uses ASA.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why the two arcs in Construction 10 fix the triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each arc keeps the third vertex at a fixed distance from one end of the first side.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Two distances from two fixed points determine one point.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first arc is the set of points at the second side's distance from $A$. The second arc
is the set of points at the third side's distance from $B$. A point on both arcs is at
both distances, so it is the third vertex.

Any triangle built from the same three sides has the same three side lengths. By SSS, it
is congruent to the triangle drawn. So the arcs fix the triangle completely, up to
reflection.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 15

**7.** Describe how to construct the perpendicular bisector of a segment $[AB]$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need two points that are each the same distance from $A$ as from $B$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Draw arcs from $A$ and from $B$ with the same compass opening.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

1. With centre $A$, open the compass to more than half of $|AB|$ and draw an arc on each
   side of $AB$.
2. With centre $B$, and the same compass opening, draw arcs cutting the first two at $P$
   and $Q$.
3. Draw the line $PQ$. It is the perpendicular bisector of $[AB]$.

The construction is Construction 2 from Section 15.7. It works because $P$ and $Q$ are
each the same distance from $A$ as from $B$, so both lie on the perpendicular bisector.

</details>
</details>
</details>
