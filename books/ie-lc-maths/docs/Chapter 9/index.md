# Chapter 9: Sequences and series

A sequence is a list of numbers in order. A series is what you get when you add a
sequence up. Two kinds of sequence turn up everywhere — one that adds the same amount each
step, and one that multiplies by the same amount — and this chapter is about both, and
about how to add up a great many terms without adding them one at a time.

## The chapter problem

**The two savings plans.** You can save for two years in one of two ways.

- **Plan A.** Save €50 in the first month, then €20 more each month: €50, €70, €90, …
- **Plan B.** Save €50 in the first month, then increase the amount by 10% each month:
  €50, €55, €60.50, …

Both plans start with the same €50. Which one leaves you with more after 24 months?

The answer is not the one that looks more exciting. By the end of the chapter you can work
out the total for each plan, say which wins and by how much, and — because the arithmetic
one has a formula — prove that formula rather than trusting it.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- recognise an arithmetic sequence and find any term from $a$ and $d$;
- add up an arithmetic series with $S_n = \dfrac{n}{2}(a + l)$;
- recognise a geometric sequence and use $T_n = ar^{n-1}$ and the sum formula;
- find the sum to infinity of a geometric series, and say when it exists;
- use a recurrence relation and find its fixed point;
- prove a formula for a sum by induction.

## Where it is used

Savings and loans, population growth and decay, depreciation, the spread of a disease, the
time a bouncing ball takes to stop, and any process that repeats with a fixed step or a
fixed multiplier. Every time you see "compound interest" you are looking at a geometric
sequence, and the sum formula is what turns it into a total.

## Your turn to connect it

Where have you seen something grow or shrink by a fixed rule — a phone battery that loses
the same percentage each hour, a rumour spreading, a loan that gathers interest, a price
that rises by a fixed amount each year?

Choose one. Write down whether the change is **adding** or **multiplying**, and what the
starting value is. You will be able to say something quantitative about it by the end of
the chapter.
