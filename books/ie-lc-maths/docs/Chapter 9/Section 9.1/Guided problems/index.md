# 9.1 Arithmetic sequences — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Solve $2x - 6 = 14$.
2. Simplify $4 + 3(n - 1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2x = 20$, so $x = 10$.
2. $4 + 3n - 3 = 3n + 1$. This rearrangement is what turns $T_n = a + (n-1)d$ into a
   simpler formula.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the common difference of $6, 10, 14, 18, \dots$ and the next two terms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract each term from the next.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10 - 6 = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$d = 4$. The next two terms are $22$ and $26$.

Check: every difference is $4$, so the sequence is arithmetic.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Find the fifteenth term of $4, 9, 14, \dots$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$T_n = a + (n-1)d$ with $a = 4$, $d = 5$, $n = 15$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4 + 14(5)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T_{15} = 4 + 14(5) = 74$$

The number of steps is $14$, not $15$. $T_n$ always has $n - 1$ steps from the start.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The sequence $11, 15, 19, \dots$ has a term equal to $99$. Which term is it?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set $T_n = 99$ and solve for $n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$11 + (n-1)4 = 99$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$(n-1)4 = 88 \quad\Rightarrow\quad n - 1 = 22 \quad\Rightarrow\quad n = 23$$

It is the $23$rd term.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The fifth term of an arithmetic sequence is $20$ and the twelfth is $55$. Find $d$
and $a$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

How many steps between the fifth and twelfth terms?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$12 - 5 = 7$ steps, and the value rose by $35$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \frac{55 - 20}{7} = \frac{35}{7} = 5$$

Then $T_5 = a + 4d = 20$, so $a = 20 - 20 = 0$.

The first term is $0$, which is allowed. The sequence is $0, 5, 10, 15, 20, \dots$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A sequence has $a = 40$ and $d = -6$. Find the first negative term.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write $T_n = 40 - 6(n-1)$ and find the first $n$ that makes it negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$40 - 6(n-1) < 0$ means $6(n-1) > 40$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$n - 1 > \dfrac{40}{6} = 6.67$, so $n - 1 = 7$ and $n = 8$.

$$T_8 = 40 - 6(7) = 40 - 42 = -2$$

The first negative term is $-2$, the $8$th term. Note $n$ had to be rounded **up** — the
$7$th term is $T_7 = 40 - 36 = 4$, still positive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why $2, 6, 18, 54, \dots$ is not arithmetic.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the differences.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$6 - 2 = 4$, but $18 - 6 = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The differences are $4, 12, 36, \dots$, which are not constant. A sequence is arithmetic
only when **every** difference is the same, so this one is not.

It is geometric instead: each term is $3$ times the one before. Section 9.3 is about that
kind of sequence.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**7.** Solve $3n + 1 = 46$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract 1, then divide by 3.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$3n = 45$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$n = 15$.

This is exactly the last step of "which term equals 46 in the sequence $4, 7, 10,
\dots$?", whose formula is $T_n = 3n + 1$. Solving the formula for $n$ is how a term
number is found.

</details>
</details>
</details>
