# 6.2 Conservation of momentum — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 6.1, momentum and impulse; Section 4.5, connected
bodies and the forces between them; Section 4.1, Newton's third law.

**Objectives:** state the law of conservation of momentum; explain when it
applies; use it for bodies that lock together, for head-on collisions, and for
explosions.

In a collision the two bodies push on each other. Newton's third law says the
pushes are equal and opposite, so one body loses exactly the momentum the other
gains. The total momentum does not change. That single idea answers most
collision problems.

## The law of conservation of momentum

> **The law of conservation of momentum.** When no external resultant force acts
> on a system, the total momentum of the system stays constant.
>
>
>
> $$\text{total momentum before} = \text{total momentum after}$$

For two bodies moving along a line, with velocities $u_1$ and $u_2$ before and
$v_1$ and $v_2$ after,

$$m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$$

The forces the bodies exert on each other are **internal** to the system. They
are equal and opposite, so they cancel in the total. Only a force from outside
the system, such as friction or a rope, can change the total momentum.

## Bodies that lock together

If the two bodies stick together, they share one final velocity $v$.

**Example 1.** A test car of mass $1200$ kg moving at $15$ m/s collides with a
stationary car of mass $800$ kg. The cars lock together. Find their common
velocity.

The momentum before is

$$1200 \times 15 = 18000\,\text{kg m/s}$$

After the collision the total mass is $1200 + 800 = 2000$ kg, moving at $v$.

$$2000v = 18000$$

$$v = \frac{18000}{2000} = 9\,\text{m/s}$$

The cars move off together at $9$ m/s in the original direction.

**Example 2.** A trolley of mass $3$ kg moving at $4$ m/s catches a trolley of
mass $1$ kg moving at $2$ m/s in the same direction. They lock together. Find
their common velocity.

$$3 \times 4 + 1 \times 2 = (3 + 1)v$$

$$12 + 2 = 4v$$

$$v = \frac{14}{4} = 3.5\,\text{m/s}$$

The common velocity lies between the two original velocities, closer to the
heavier trolley's.

## Head-on collisions

When the bodies move towards each other, choose a positive direction and give
one velocity a negative sign.

**Example 3.** A body of mass $3$ kg moves at $4$ m/s to the right. It meets a
body of mass $2$ kg moving at $3$ m/s to the left. They lock together. Find
their common velocity, taking the right as positive.

The momentum before is

$$3 \times 4 + 2 \times (-3) = 12 - 6 = 6\,\text{kg m/s}$$

The total mass is $5$ kg, so

$$5v = 6 \qquad v = 1.2\,\text{m/s}$$

The common velocity is $1.2$ m/s to the right, the direction of the heavier,
faster body.

## Explosions and recoil

An explosion is a collision in reverse. The total momentum before is zero, so
the total momentum after must also be zero. The parts fly apart with equal and
opposite momenta.

**Example 4.** A body of mass $5$ kg is at rest and splits into two parts. One
part of mass $2$ kg moves at $6$ m/s. Find the speed of the other part.

The total momentum before is zero. The $2$ kg part carries $2 \times 6 = 12$
kg m/s, so the $3$ kg part must carry $12$ kg m/s the other way.

$$3v = 12 \qquad v = 4\,\text{m/s}$$

The other part moves at $4$ m/s in the opposite direction.

**Example 5.** A rifle of mass $4$ kg fires a bullet of mass $0.02$ kg at
$500$ m/s. Find the recoil speed of the rifle.

The bullet carries $0.02 \times 500 = 10$ kg m/s forwards, so the rifle carries
$10$ kg m/s backwards.

$$4v = 10 \qquad v = 2.5\,\text{m/s}$$

The rifle recoils at $2.5$ m/s. The momentum of the rifle equals the momentum of
the bullet, but its larger mass gives it a smaller speed.

> **Watch out.** Conservation of momentum needs no external resultant force. In
> a real collision the ground may be rough, and friction is an external force.
> During a short collision, however, the forces between the bodies are far
> larger than friction, so the total momentum barely changes. Treat it as
> conserved. A second error is to use speeds without signs: two bodies moving
> towards each other must have opposite signs, or the total is wrong.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. In test A the cars lock together.

The momentum before the impact is $18000$ kg m/s. After the impact the total
mass is $2000$ kg, moving at a common velocity $v$. Use conservation of
momentum:

$$2000v = 18000 \qquad v = \underline{\hspace{2cm}}\,\text{m/s}$$

Keep that number. Section 6.3 uses it to find the impulse on each car, and
Section 6.5 uses it to find the kinetic energy lost.

## You should now be able to

- state the law of conservation of momentum and the condition for it;
- explain why the forces inside a collision cancel;
- use $m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$;
- find a common velocity for two bodies that lock together;
- handle a head-on collision by giving one velocity a negative sign;
- use conservation of momentum for an explosion or a recoil, where the total
  momentum starts at zero.
