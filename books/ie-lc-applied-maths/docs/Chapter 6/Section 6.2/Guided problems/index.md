# 6.2 Conservation of momentum — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $3 \times 4 + 1 \times 2$.
2. Solve $4v = 14$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $14$. That is the total momentum of a $3$ kg trolley at $4$ m/s and a $1$ kg
   trolley at $2$ m/s moving the same way.
2. $v = 3.5$. Dividing the total momentum by the total mass gives the common
   velocity.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A body of mass $2$ kg moves at $5$ m/s and hits a stationary body of mass
$3$ kg. They lock together. Find their common velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The stationary body has zero momentum before the collision.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$2 \times 5 + 3 \times 0 = (2 + 3)v$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10 = 5v \qquad v = 2\,\text{m/s}$$

The common velocity is $2$ m/s, in the direction the $2$ kg body was moving.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A body of mass $4$ kg moves at $3$ m/s and catches a body of mass $2$ kg
moving at $1.5$ m/s in the same direction. They lock together. Find their common
velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the two momenta, then divide by the total mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4 \times 3 + 2 \times 1.5 = (4 + 2)v$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$12 + 3 = 6v \qquad v = \frac{15}{6} = 2.5\,\text{m/s}$$

The common velocity is $2.5$ m/s. It lies between $1.5$ m/s and $3$ m/s, as it
must.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A body of mass $3$ kg moves at $5$ m/s to the right. It meets a body of
mass $2$ kg moving at $5$ m/s to the left. They lock together. Find their common
velocity, taking the right as positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Give the leftward velocity a negative sign.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3 \times 5 + 2 \times (-5) = (3 + 2)v$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$15 - 10 = 5v \qquad v = \frac{5}{5} = 1\,\text{m/s}$$

The common velocity is $1$ m/s to the right. The total momentum before was
$5$ kg m/s to the right, and it is $5$ kg m/s after.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A body of mass $6$ kg is at rest and splits into two parts. One part of
mass $2$ kg moves at $9$ m/s. Find the speed of the other part.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total momentum before the split is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The $2$ kg part carries $2 \times 9 = 18$ kg m/s. The other part has mass
$4$ kg and must carry $18$ kg m/s the other way.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4v = 18 \qquad v = 4.5\,\text{m/s}$$

The other part moves at $4.5$ m/s in the opposite direction. The two momenta are
equal and opposite, so the total stays zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A rifle of mass $5$ kg fires a bullet of mass $0.01$ kg at $400$ m/s.
Find the recoil speed of the rifle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The momentum of the rifle equals the momentum of the bullet, in the opposite
direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The bullet carries $0.01 \times 400 = 4$ kg m/s.

$$5v = 4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \frac{4}{5} = 0.8\,\text{m/s}$$

The rifle recoils at $0.8$ m/s. The bullet and the rifle carry the same
momentum, but the rifle's larger mass gives it a much smaller speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A body of mass $5$ kg moving at $4$ m/s hits a stationary body of mass
$3$ kg. After the collision the $5$ kg body continues at $1$ m/s. Find the
velocity of the $3$ kg body.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the momentum before equal to the momentum after, with one unknown.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5 \times 4 = 5 \times 1 + 3v$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$20 = 5 + 3v$$

$$3v = 15 \qquad v = 5\,\text{m/s}$$

The $3$ kg body moves off at $5$ m/s in the original direction. Check: the
momentum after is $5 + 15 = 20$ kg m/s, which matches the momentum before.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A bullet of mass $0.03$ kg moving at $200$ m/s embeds itself in a block
of mass $2.97$ kg at rest. Find the common speed after the impact.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The bullet and the block move together after the impact.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$0.03 \times 200 = (0.03 + 2.97)v$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6 = 3v \qquad v = 2\,\text{m/s}$$

The common speed is $2$ m/s. Almost all of the momentum came from the bullet,
because the block was at rest.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** Two bodies of masses $3$ kg and $5$ kg hang from a light string over a
smooth pulley. Find their acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is a connected-particles question. Find the resultant force and divide by
the total mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The difference in weight is the resultant:

$$5g - 3g = 2g$$

The total mass is $3 + 5 = 8$ kg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{2 \times 9.8}{8} = 2.45\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The heavier body falls and the lighter rises, both with acceleration
$2.45$ m/s². Conservation of momentum and Newton's laws describe the same
motion. This chapter adds the momentum view, which is most useful when the
forces act for a short time.

</details>
</details>
</details>
