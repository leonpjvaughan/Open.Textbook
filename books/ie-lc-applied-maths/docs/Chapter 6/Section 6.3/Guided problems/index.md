# 6.3 Collisions in one dimension — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $0.15 \times (30 + 40)$.
2. A ball is brought to rest from $8$ m/s. By how much does its velocity change?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $10.5$. That is the impulse on a $0.15$ kg ball whose velocity changes by
   $70$ m/s.
2. $8$ m/s. The change in velocity is the final velocity minus the initial
   velocity, so here it is $0 - 8 = -8$ m/s. The change in momentum keeps the
   sign.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A ball of mass $2$ kg is at rest and is struck so that it moves at
$6$ m/s. Find the impulse on it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The impulse equals the change in momentum, and the ball starts at rest.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$J = m(v - u) = 2 \times (6 - 0)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$J = 2 \times 6 = 12\,\text{N s}$$

The impulse points in the direction the ball moves.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A ball of mass $0.5$ kg moving at $8$ m/s is brought to rest. Find the
impulse on it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The final velocity is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$J = 0.5 \times (0 - 8)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$J = 0.5 \times (-8) = -4\,\text{N s}$$

The impulse is $4$ N s against the motion. The negative sign shows its
direction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A ball of mass $0.1$ kg hits a wall at $12$ m/s and rebounds at $8$ m/s.
Find the magnitude of the impulse on the ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the direction away from the wall as positive, so the velocities are $-12$
and $+8$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$J = 0.1 \times (8 - (-12))$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$J = 0.1 \times 20 = 2\,\text{N s}$$

The change in velocity is $20$ m/s, not $4$ m/s, because the ball reverses
direction. That is why a rebound needs a larger impulse than a stop.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** An impulse of $12$ N s acts for $0.03$ s. Find the average force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Force is impulse divided by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F = \frac{12}{0.03}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{12}{0.03} = 400\,\text{N}$$

A short contact time turns a modest impulse into a large force.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A body of mass $3$ kg moves at $5$ m/s to the right and meets a body of
mass $2$ kg moving at $5$ m/s to the left. They lock together. Find their common
velocity, and the impulse on the $2$ kg body.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the common velocity from conservation of momentum, then the impulse from
the change in momentum of the $2$ kg body.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3 \times 5 + 2 \times (-5) = 5v \qquad v = 1\,\text{m/s}$$

The $2$ kg body changes from $-5$ m/s to $+1$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The common velocity is $1$ m/s to the right. The impulse on the $2$ kg body is

$$J = 2 \times (1 - (-5)) = 2 \times 6 = 12\,\text{N s}$$

The impulse points to the right. The impulse on the $3$ kg body is
$3 \times (1 - 5) = -12$ N s, equal and opposite.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A body of mass $5$ kg moving at $4$ m/s hits a stationary body of mass
$3$ kg. After the collision the $5$ kg body continues at $1$ m/s. Find the
velocity of the $3$ kg body, and the impulse on each body.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the unknown velocity from conservation of momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$5 \times 4 = 5 \times 1 + 3v \qquad v = 5\,\text{m/s}$$

The impulse on each body is its change in momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The $3$ kg body moves at $5$ m/s. Its impulse is $3 \times (5 - 0) = 15$ N s.
The $5$ kg body loses momentum $5 \times (1 - 4) = -15$ N s. The two impulses are
equal and opposite, which is Newton's third law.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A car of mass $1000$ kg strikes a stationary car of mass $800$ kg. The
two lock together. The impulse on the $800$ kg car is $+3600$ N s. State the
impulse on the $1000$ kg car, and find the common velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The impulses are equal and opposite. Use the impulse on the lighter car to find
its velocity change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The impulse on the $1000$ kg car is $-3600$ N s.

The $800$ kg car starts at rest, so $800v = 3600$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \frac{3600}{800} = 4.5\,\text{m/s}$$

The common velocity is $4.5$ m/s. Check the heavier car: it slows to $4.5$ m/s,
so its change in momentum is $1000 \times (4.5 - u)$, which must equal
$-3600$ N s. Solving gives $u = 8.1$ m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**8.** A car decelerates uniformly from $20$ m/s to rest in $4$ s. Find its
acceleration and the distance it travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the equations of motion for constant acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{0 - 20}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = -5\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The distance is the average velocity times the time:

$$s = \frac{u + v}{2} \times t = \frac{20 + 0}{2} \times 4 = 40\,\text{m}$$

This is a kinematics question, and it belongs to Chapter 3. The momentum view
would use the same information: the change in momentum is $m \times 20$, and the
impulse that causes it is the braking force times $4$ s.

</details>
</details>
</details>
