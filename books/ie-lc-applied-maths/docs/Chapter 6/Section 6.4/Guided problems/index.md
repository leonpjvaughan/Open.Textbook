# 6.4 Newton's experimental law of restitution — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\sqrt{0.64}$.
2. Solve $v_2 - v_1 = 7.5$ and $1200v_1 + 800v_2 = 18000$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.8$. That is a coefficient of restitution: the square root of the ratio of
   the rebound height to the drop height.
2. $v_1 = 6$ and $v_2 = 13.5$. These are the velocities in test B of the chapter
   problem. Substitute $v_2 = v_1 + 7.5$ into the momentum equation.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A ball hits the floor at $6$ m/s and rebounds at $4.5$ m/s. Find the
coefficient of restitution.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The coefficient is the rebound speed divided by the approach speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$e = \frac{4.5}{6}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$e = \frac{4.5}{6} = 0.75$$

The value lies between $0$ and $1$, as it must.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A ball hits the floor at $10$ m/s. The coefficient of restitution is
$0.8$. Find its rebound speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $e = \dfrac{v}{u}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v = eu = 0.8 \times 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = 8\,\text{m/s}$$

The rebound speed is less than the approach speed, because some kinetic energy
is lost.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A ball of mass $2$ kg moving at $6$ m/s hits a stationary ball of mass
$2$ kg. The coefficient of restitution is $0.5$. Find the velocity of each ball
after the collision.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the momentum equation and the restitution equation, then solve them
together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Momentum: $2v_1 + 2v_2 = 12$, so $v_1 + v_2 = 6$.

Restitution: $v_2 - v_1 = 0.5 \times 6 = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Add the two equations:

$$2v_2 = 9 \qquad v_2 = 4.5\,\text{m/s}$$

Then $v_1 = 6 - 4.5 = 1.5$ m/s. Both balls move forwards, the struck ball
faster. Check the momentum: $2 \times 1.5 + 2 \times 4.5 = 3 + 9 = 12$ kg m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A body of mass $4$ kg moving at $5$ m/s hits a stationary body of mass
$1$ kg. The coefficient of restitution is $0.6$. Find the velocity of each body
after the collision.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v_1 = \dfrac{(m_1 - em_2)u_1}{m_1 + m_2}$ and
$v_2 = \dfrac{(1 + e)m_1u_1}{m_1 + m_2}$, or solve the two equations directly.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_1 = \frac{(4 - 0.6 \times 1) \times 5}{4 + 1} = \frac{3.4 \times 5}{5}$$

$$v_2 = \frac{(1 + 0.6) \times 4 \times 5}{5}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_1 = 3.4\,\text{m/s} \qquad v_2 = 6.4\,\text{m/s}$$

Check the momentum: $4 \times 3.4 + 1 \times 6.4 = 13.6 + 6.4 = 20$ kg m/s,
which matches the momentum before, $4 \times 5 = 20$ kg m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball is dropped from a height of $1.8$ m and rebounds to a height of
$0.45$ m. Find the coefficient of restitution.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The coefficient is the square root of the ratio of the heights.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$e = \sqrt{\frac{0.45}{1.8}} = \sqrt{0.25}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$e = 0.5$$

The rebound height is one quarter of the drop height, and the square root of one
quarter is one half.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ball rebounds to a height of $0.8$ m. The coefficient of restitution is
$0.4$. Find the height from which it was dropped.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rebound height is $e^2$ times the drop height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$h_1 = \frac{h_2}{e^2} = \frac{0.8}{0.4^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h_1 = \frac{0.8}{0.16} = 5\,\text{m}$$

The ball was dropped from $5$ m. A small coefficient of restitution means a
large drop for a given rebound.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** State the possible values of the coefficient of restitution, and explain
what $e = 1$ and $e = 0$ mean.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the speeds of approach and separation in each case.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The coefficient lies between $0$ and $1$. At $e = 1$ the bodies separate as fast
as they approached; at $e = 0$ they do not separate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The coefficient of restitution lies between $0$ and $1$ inclusive.

- $e = 1$ describes a perfectly elastic collision. The separation speed equals
  the approach speed, and no kinetic energy is lost.
- $e = 0$ describes a perfectly inelastic collision. The bodies move off
  together, and as much kinetic energy is lost as the collision allows.

A value above $1$ is impossible, because it would mean the collision created
energy.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 5

**8.** A block of mass $5$ kg slides on a rough horizontal floor. The
coefficient of friction is $0.3$. Find the friction force. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On a horizontal floor the normal reaction equals the weight, and friction is
limiting while the block slides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg = 5 \times 9.8 = 49\,\text{N}$$

$$F = \mu R = 0.3 \times 49$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = 0.3 \times 49 = 14.7\,\text{N}$$

The friction acts against the sliding. This is a Chapter 5 question, and it is
here to show that friction is an external force. During a short collision,
friction has little effect, so momentum is conserved. Over a long slide, friction
changes the momentum, and the impulse–momentum theorem is the right tool.

</details>
</details>
</details>
