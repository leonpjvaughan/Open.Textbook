# 6.5 Elastic and inelastic collisions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 6^2$.
2. Work out $\frac{1}{2} \times 1200 \times 15^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $36$ J. That is the kinetic energy of a $2$ kg body moving at $6$ m/s.
2. $135000$ J. That is the kinetic energy of the test car before the crash, and
   the starting point for every energy comparison in this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A body of mass $4$ kg moves at $5$ m/s. Find its kinetic energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$E_k = 0.5 \times 4 \times 25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E_k = 0.5 \times 4 \times 25 = 50\,\text{J}$$

Kinetic energy is a scalar, so the direction of motion does not matter.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A body of mass $3$ kg moving at $8$ m/s hits a stationary body of mass
$1$ kg. They lock together. Find the common velocity, and the kinetic energy
lost.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the common velocity first, then the kinetic energy before and after.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$3 \times 8 = 4v \qquad v = 6\,\text{m/s}$$

Kinetic energy before: $0.5 \times 3 \times 64 = 96$ J.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Kinetic energy after: $0.5 \times 4 \times 36 = 72$ J. The energy lost is
$96 - 72 = 24$ J. Momentum is conserved, but a quarter of the kinetic energy has
gone into heat, sound and deformation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A ball of mass $2$ kg moving at $6$ m/s collides elastically with a
stationary ball of the same mass. Find the velocity of each ball, and the
kinetic energy before and after.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Equal masses in an elastic collision exchange velocities.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first ball stops and the second moves off at $6$ m/s.

$$E_k = 0.5 \times 2 \times 36$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The kinetic energy is $36$ J before and $36$ J after. The collision is elastic,
so no kinetic energy is lost, even though the motion has changed completely.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A ball of mass $2$ kg moving at $5$ m/s collides elastically with a
stationary ball of mass $3$ kg. Find the velocity of each ball, and check that
the kinetic energy is conserved.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the elastic formulas
$v_1 = \dfrac{(m_1 - m_2)u_1}{m_1 + m_2}$ and
$v_2 = \dfrac{2m_1u_1}{m_1 + m_2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_1 = \frac{(2 - 3) \times 5}{5} = -1\,\text{m/s}$$

$$v_2 = \frac{2 \times 2 \times 5}{5} = 4\,\text{m/s}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first ball rebounds at $1$ m/s and the second moves forwards at $4$ m/s.

Kinetic energy before: $0.5 \times 2 \times 25 = 25$ J.

Kinetic energy after: $0.5 \times 2 \times 1 + 0.5 \times 3 \times 16
= 1 + 24 = 25$ J. The energy is conserved, so the collision is elastic.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two bodies of masses $4$ kg and $2$ kg collide with a relative approach
speed of $6$ m/s. The coefficient of restitution is $0.5$. Use
$E_{\text{lost}} = \frac{1}{2}\dfrac{m_1m_2}{m_1 + m_2}(1 - e^2)u^2$ to find the
kinetic energy lost.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute the masses, the coefficient and the relative speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$E_{\text{lost}} = 0.5 \times \frac{4 \times 2}{6} \times (1 - 0.25) \times 36$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E_{\text{lost}} = 0.5 \times \frac{8}{6} \times 0.75 \times 36
= 0.5 \times 1.3333 \times 0.75 \times 36 = 18\,\text{J}$$

The energy lost grows with the relative speed and with the amount of
inelasticity. A perfectly elastic collision, $e = 1$, would lose nothing.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ball is dropped and rebounds to $0.81$ of its drop height. Find the
coefficient of restitution, and say whether the collision with the floor is
nearly elastic or strongly inelastic.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The coefficient is the square root of the height ratio.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$e = \sqrt{0.81}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$e = 0.9$$

The collision is nearly elastic: it keeps $81\%$ of the height, and the
coefficient is close to $1$. A coefficient of $0.1$ would describe a strongly
inelastic collision, where the ball barely bounces.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain how momentum can be conserved in an inelastic collision when
kinetic energy is lost.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the difference between a vector and a scalar, and where the lost
energy goes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Momentum is conserved because the internal forces are equal and opposite.
Kinetic energy changes form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The forces the bodies exert on each other are equal and opposite, so they change
the two momenta by equal and opposite amounts. The total momentum does not
change. Kinetic energy is a scalar, and some of it is changed into heat, sound
and the energy of deformation. The total energy is still conserved, but the
kinetic part is not. Momentum and kinetic energy measure different things, so
conserving one does not conserve the other.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**8.** A ball is dropped from rest from a height of $20$ m. Taking
$g = 9.8$ m/s², find its speed when it reaches the ground.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ with $u = 0$ and $a = g$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v^2 = 2 \times 9.8 \times 20 = 392$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{392} = 19.8\,\text{m/s}$$

This is a Chapter 3 question. It finds the speed just before a bounce, which is
the approach speed that the coefficient of restitution uses. The next section
follows the ball through a whole series of bounces.

</details>
</details>
</details>
