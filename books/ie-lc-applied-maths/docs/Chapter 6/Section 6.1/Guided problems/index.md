# 6.1 Momentum and impulse — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $0.25 \times 8$.
2. A car travels $90$ m in $6$ s. Find its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2$. That is the momentum of a $0.25$ kg ball moving at $8$ m/s.
2. $15$ m/s. Speed is distance divided by time, and a velocity is the speed
   with a direction. Momentum needs the velocity, not only the speed.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A body of mass $4$ kg moves at $3$ m/s. Find its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Momentum is mass times velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$p = mv = 4 \times 3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$p = mv = 4 \times 3 = 12\,\text{kg m/s}$$

The momentum points in the direction of motion.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A car of mass $1500$ kg moves at $20$ m/s. Find its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $p = mv$ with the mass in kilograms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$p = 1500 \times 20$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$p = 1500 \times 20 = 30000\,\text{kg m/s}$$

A large mass moving quickly carries a large momentum, which is why a car takes
a long distance to stop.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A force of $40$ N acts for $0.25$ s. Find the impulse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Impulse is force times time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$J = F\Delta t = 40 \times 0.25$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$J = F\Delta t = 40 \times 0.25 = 10\,\text{N s}$$

The unit N s is the same as kg m/s, so an impulse is a change in momentum.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A ball of mass $2$ kg moves at $8$ m/s and is brought to rest in $0.05$ s.
Find the impulse on the ball and the average force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the impulse from the change in momentum, then divide by the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$J = mv = 2 \times 8 = 16\,\text{N s}$$

$$F = \frac{J}{\Delta t} = \frac{16}{0.05}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The momentum falls from $16$ kg m/s to zero, so $J = 16$ N s against the
motion. The average force is

$$F = \frac{16}{0.05} = 320\,\text{N}$$

A short stopping time needs a large force for the same impulse.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball of mass $0.2$ kg hits a wall at $5$ m/s and rebounds at $3$ m/s.
Taking the direction away from the wall as positive, find the impulse on the
ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The velocity reverses, so give it a sign before and after.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The velocity before, $u$, is $-5$ m/s and the velocity after, $v$, is $+3$ m/s.

$$J = m(v - u) = 0.2 \times (3 - (-5))$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$J = 0.2 \times 8 = 1.6\,\text{N s}$$

The impulse is $1.6$ N s away from the wall. Note that the change in velocity is
$8$ m/s, not $2$ m/s, because the ball reverses direction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A car of mass $1000$ kg speeds up from $15$ m/s to $30$ m/s in $6$ s.
Find the average resultant force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\Delta p = 1000 \times (30 - 15) = 15000\,\text{kg m/s}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{15000}{6} = 2500\,\text{N}$$

The force acts in the direction of travel. The same answer comes from
$F = ma$, with $a = 2.5$ m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why a longer collision time reduces the force on an object, even
though the change in momentum is the same.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start from the impulse–momentum theorem and rearrange it for the force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$J = F\Delta t, \qquad \text{so} \qquad F = \frac{J}{\Delta t}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The impulse needed to change the momentum is fixed by the mass and the change in
velocity. If the same impulse is spread over a longer time, the average force is
smaller, because force is impulse divided by time.

This is why a crumple zone, a crash mat or a pair of gloves works. The change in
momentum is the same, but the force is smaller and the object survives.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** A body of mass $6$ kg rests on a smooth horizontal floor. A horizontal
force of $18$ N acts on it. Find its acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is a forces question, so use Newton's second law.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F = ma, \qquad 18 = 6a$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{18}{6} = 3\,\text{m/s}^2$$

Momentum and forces are two views of the same motion. The force of $18$ N acting
for a time would change the body's momentum at a rate of $18$ kg m/s each
second, which is the same statement as $a = 3$ m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>
