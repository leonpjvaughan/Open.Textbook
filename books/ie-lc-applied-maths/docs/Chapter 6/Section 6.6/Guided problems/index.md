# 6.6 Collisions in two dimensions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Resolve a velocity of $10$ m/s at $60^\circ$ to the $x$-axis into components.
2. Work out $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $10\cos 60^\circ = 5$ m/s along the $x$-axis and
   $10\sin 60^\circ = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.66$ m/s along the $y$-axis.
2. $5$. When two momenta are at right angles, their magnitudes combine like the
   sides of a right-angled triangle.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A ball of mass $3$ kg moves at $4$ m/s along the $x$-axis and hits a
stationary ball of mass $2$ kg. After the collision the $3$ kg ball moves at
$2$ m/s at $60^\circ$ to the $x$-axis. Find the $x$- and $y$-components of the
momentum of the $2$ kg ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Resolve the final velocity of the $3$ kg ball, then subtract from the total.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Total momentum: $3 \times 4 = 12$ kg m/s along the $x$-axis.

The $3$ kg ball carries $x$-momentum $3 \times 2\cos 60^\circ = 3$ and
$y$-momentum $3 \times 2\sin 60^\circ = 3\sqrt{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The $2$ kg ball carries the rest:

$$p_x = 12 - 3 = 9\,\text{kg m/s}$$

$$p_y = -3\sqrt{3}\,\text{kg m/s}$$

The $y$-momentum is negative because the total $y$-momentum must stay zero, and
the $3$ kg ball moved upwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** The $2$ kg ball in the previous question has momentum components
$9$ kg m/s and $3\sqrt{3}$ kg m/s at right angles. Find its speed and the angle
of its motion to the $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide each momentum component by the mass, then combine them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Velocity components: $\dfrac{9}{2} = 4.5$ m/s and
$\dfrac{3\sqrt{3}}{2} = 1.5\sqrt{3}$ m/s.

$$v = \sqrt{4.5^2 + (1.5\sqrt{3})^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{20.25 + 6.75} = \sqrt{27} = 3\sqrt{3} \approx 5.20\,\text{m/s}$$

The angle satisfies
$\tan\theta = \dfrac{1.5\sqrt{3}}{4.5} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, so
$\theta = 30^\circ$ below the $x$-axis.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A ball of mass $2$ kg moves at $6$ m/s and hits a stationary ball of mass
$2$ kg. After the collision the two balls move off at right angles. The first
ball moves at $4.8$ m/s. Find the speed of the second ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total momentum is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$p_1^2 + p_2^2 = (2 \times 6)^2$$

$$p_1 = 2 \times 4.8 = 9.6\,\text{kg m/s}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$p_2 = \sqrt{12^2 - 9.6^2} = \sqrt{144 - 92.16} = \sqrt{51.84}
= 7.2\,\text{kg m/s}$$

The second ball has mass $2$ kg, so its speed is $\dfrac{7.2}{2} = 3.6$ m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A car of mass $1000$ kg moves at $12$ m/s along the $x$-axis and hits a
stationary car of mass $500$ kg. After the collision the $1000$ kg car moves at
$6$ m/s at $60^\circ$ to the $x$-axis. Find the speed and direction of the
$500$ kg car.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the momentum components of the $500$ kg car, then divide by its mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Total $x$-momentum: $1000 \times 12 = 12000$ kg m/s.

The $1000$ kg car carries $x$-momentum $1000 \times 6\cos 60^\circ = 3000$ and
$y$-momentum $1000 \times 6\sin 60^\circ = 3000\sqrt{3}$ kg m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The $500$ kg car carries $12000 - 3000 = 9000$ kg m/s in the $x$-direction and
$-3000\sqrt{3}$ kg m/s in the $y$-direction. Its velocity components are

$$\frac{9000}{500} = 18\,\text{m/s}, \qquad
\frac{3000\sqrt{3}}{500} = 6\sqrt{3}\,\text{m/s}$$

$$v = \sqrt{18^2 + (6\sqrt{3})^2} = \sqrt{324 + 108} = \sqrt{432}
= 12\sqrt{3} \approx 20.8\,\text{m/s}$$

The angle satisfies
$\tan\theta = \dfrac{6\sqrt{3}}{18} = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$, so
$\theta = 30^\circ$ below the $x$-axis.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball of mass $2$ kg moves at $10$ m/s at $30^\circ$ above the
$x$-axis. Find the $x$- and $y$-components of its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Resolve the velocity, then multiply each component by the mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_x = 10\cos 30^\circ = 5\sqrt{3}, \qquad
v_y = 10\sin 30^\circ = 5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$p_x = 2 \times 5\sqrt{3} = 10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.3\,\text{kg m/s}$$

$$p_y = 2 \times 5 = 10\,\text{kg m/s}$$

Resolving before you conserve is the whole method. The $x$- and $y$-components
are conserved separately.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Two balls of equal mass collide. One moves at $8$ m/s and the other is at
rest. After the collision they move off at right angles. The first ball moves at
$4.8$ m/s. Find the speed of the second ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the right-angle property, with the total momentum as the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Let the mass be $m$. Then $p_{\text{total}} = 8m$ and $p_1 = 4.8m$.

$$p_2 = \sqrt{(8m)^2 - (4.8m)^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$p_2 = m\sqrt{64 - 23.04} = m\sqrt{40.96} = 6.4m$$

The second ball has mass $m$, so its speed is $6.4$ m/s. Notice that the mass
cancels, so the answer does not depend on it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Describe how conservation of momentum is used in a two-dimensional
collision.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the two directions and what you resolve first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Resolve every velocity, then conserve the $x$-direction and the $y$-direction
separately.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Momentum is a vector, so conservation applies to each direction on its own.
Resolve every velocity into an $x$-component and a $y$-component, before and
after the collision. Write the total $x$-momentum before equal to the total
$x$-momentum after, and do the same for the $y$-direction. That gives two
equations, one for each direction, which is enough for the two unknown
quantities in most problems. Finally, combine the components of any unknown
velocity to find its speed and direction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**8.** A ball is thrown at $20$ m/s at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Find
the horizontal and vertical components of its velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the cosine for the horizontal component and the sine for the vertical one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$u_x = 20\cos 30^\circ, \qquad u_y = 20\sin 30^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$u_x = 20 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 10\sqrt{3} \approx 17.3\,\text{m/s}$$

$$u_y = 20 \times 0.5 = 10\,\text{m/s}$$

This is a Chapter 3 question, and the skill is the same one this section uses.
A velocity at an angle is split into two components, and each direction is
treated on its own. For a projectile the horizontal component is constant and
the vertical one changes; for a collision both components are conserved.

</details>
</details>
</details>
