# 3.1 Displacement, velocity and acceleration — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. How many metres are in $2.5$ km?
2. Make $s$ the subject of $v = \dfrac{s}{t}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2500$ m. One kilometre is $1000$ m, so $2.5 \times 1000 = 2500$.
2. $s = vt$. Multiply both sides by $t$. You will use this rearrangement when a
   question gives you a velocity and a time and asks for a displacement.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A walker walks 300 m north, then 100 m south. Find the distance travelled
and the displacement. Take north as positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Distance adds both legs of the walk. Displacement subtracts the leg that points
the other way.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Distance is $300 + 100$. Displacement is $300 - 100$, and it points north.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = 300 + 100 = 400\,\text{m}$$

$$s = 300 - 100 = 200\,\text{m}$$

The distance is 400 m. The displacement is 200 m north, because the answer is
positive and north was chosen as positive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A cyclist travels 1500 m in 100 s. Find the average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average speed is distance divided by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v = \frac{1500}{100}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \frac{d}{t} = \frac{1500}{100} = 15\,\text{m/s}$$

The average speed is 15 m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A bus drives 200 m around a roundabout in 40 s and returns to the point
where it started. Find its average speed and its average velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For the velocity you need the displacement, not the distance. Where does the bus
finish?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The bus finishes where it started, so its displacement is 0 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Average speed uses the distance:

$$v = \frac{200}{40} = 5\,\text{m/s}$$

Average velocity uses the displacement:

$$v = \frac{0}{40} = 0\,\text{m/s}$$

The average speed is 5 m/s and the average velocity is 0 m/s. The bus moved, but
its position did not change overall.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A motorcyclist speeds up from 12 m/s to 26 m/s in 4 s. Find the
acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$, with $u = 12$ m/s and $v = 26$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{26 - 12}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{26 - 12}{4} = \frac{14}{4} = 3.5\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 3.5 m/s² in the direction of travel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A van travelling at 15 m/s brakes and stops in 5 s. Find the acceleration,
taking forward as positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The van stops, so its final velocity is 0 m/s. The initial velocity is 15 m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{0 - 15}{5}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{0 - 15}{5} = -3\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is $-3$ m/s². The negative sign means it points backwards, so the
van decelerates at 3 m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A train travels at 108 km/h. Convert this speed to metres per second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by 1000 to turn kilometres into metres, then divide by 3600 to turn
hours into seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$108 \times 1000 = 108\,000$, so the train travels $108\,000$ m each hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$108\,\text{km/h} = \frac{108 \times 1000}{3600} = 30\,\text{m/s}$$

Dividing a speed in km/h by 3.6 gives the same answer, because
$1000 \div 3600 = \dfrac{1}{3.6}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A student writes: "A car that returns to its starting point has zero
average velocity, so the car was not moving." Explain what is wrong with this
statement.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average velocity describes the whole journey. What does a zero displacement tell
you, and what does it not tell you?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The car can travel quickly along a path and still finish where it began.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Average velocity is displacement divided by time. If the car finishes where it
started, the displacement is 0 m, so the average velocity is 0 m/s.

That does not mean the car was still. The car covered the whole path at some
speed, so its average speed is greater than zero. Zero average velocity says the
position did not change overall. It says nothing about how fast the car moved
along the way.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**8.** In Chapter 1 a model was judged by what it leaves out. Example 4 uses one
model: the car has a constant acceleration of 2 m/s². State one thing this model
leaves out about a real car, and one question it can still answer well.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the driver, the road and the engine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A real car's acceleration changes as the driver presses the pedal and the engine
responds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The model leaves out the way a real acceleration changes. The driver presses the
pedal gradually, the road may slope, and the engine responds differently at
different speeds. None of that appears in a constant 2 m/s².

The model still answers a useful question: how fast the car is going after a few
seconds of steady acceleration from a known starting speed. Over a short interval
where the acceleration is nearly constant, the answer is close to the real one.

</details>
</details>
</details>
