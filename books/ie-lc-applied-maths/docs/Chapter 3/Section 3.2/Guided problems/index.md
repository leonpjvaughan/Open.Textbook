# 3.2 Motion with constant acceleration — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A car speeds up from $9$ m/s to $21$ m/s in $4$ s. Find its acceleration.
2. Make $a$ the subject of $v = u + at$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t} = \dfrac{21 - 9}{4} = \dfrac{12}{4} = 3$ m/s².
2. $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$. Subtract $u$ from both sides, then divide by $t$.
   This section uses the same equation, rearranged to suit the question.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A car has an initial velocity of $5$ m/s and accelerates at $3$ m/s²
for $4$ s. Find its final velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The quantities are $u$, $a$ and $t$, and $v$ is wanted. Use the equation without
$s$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v = u + at = 5 + 3 \times 4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = u + at = 5 + 3 \times 4 = 5 + 12 = 17\,\text{m/s}$$

Here $u$ is the initial velocity, $v$ the final velocity, $a$ the acceleration,
$s$ the displacement and $t$ the time.

The car ends at 17 m/s. Its velocity grows by 3 m/s every second, so after
4 s it has grown by 12 m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A trolley starts from rest and accelerates at $2$ m/s² for $5$ s. Find
the distance it travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Starting from rest means $u = 0$. Use the equation without $v$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 5^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 25 = 25\,\text{m}$$

The first term vanishes because $u = 0$. The trolley travels 25 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A cyclist speeds up from $8$ m/s to $20$ m/s with an acceleration of
$2$ m/s². Find the distance travelled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The time is not mentioned, so use the equation without $t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$s = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} = \frac{400 - 64}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} = \frac{400 - 64}{2 \times 2} = \frac{336}{4} = 84\,\text{m}$$

The cyclist covers 84 m while speeding up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A van travelling at $30$ m/s brakes uniformly and stops in $6$ s.
Taking forward as positive, find its acceleration and the distance it travels
while braking.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The van stops, so its final velocity is $0$ m/s. Find the acceleration first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{0 - 30}{6}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{0 - 30}{6} = -5\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The negative sign means the acceleration points backwards. For the distance,
use the average velocity:

$$s = \frac{1}{2}(u + v)t = \frac{1}{2}(30 + 0) \times 6 = 90\,\text{m}$$

The van travels 90 m while braking.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A runner accelerates uniformly from $4$ m/s to $16$ m/s in $6$ s. Find
the distance covered.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The acceleration is not mentioned, so use the equation without $a$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$s = \frac{1}{2}(u + v)t = \frac{1}{2}(4 + 16) \times 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = \frac{1}{2}(u + v)t = \frac{1}{2} \times 20 \times 6 = 60\,\text{m}$$

The average velocity is 10 m/s, and the runner holds it for 6 s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A particle starts at $10$ m/s and accelerates uniformly at $2$ m/s².
It travels $96$ m. Find the time taken.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$. It gives a quadratic in $t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$96 = 10t + t^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Rearrange to $t^2 + 10t - 96 = 0$, which factorises as
$(t + 16)(t - 6) = 0$. The roots are $t = -16$ and $t = 6$.

A negative time is outside the model, so the time taken is 6 s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A train travels at $108$ km/h and brakes uniformly at $1.5$ m/s² until
it stops. Find the time taken to stop and the distance it travels while
braking.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Convert the speed to m/s first. Divide $108$ by $3.6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$108$ km/h $= 30$ m/s. The time to stop is $t = \dfrac{u}{a} = \dfrac{30}{1.5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = \frac{u}{a} = \frac{30}{1.5} = 20\,\text{s}$$

$$s = \frac{1}{2}ut = \frac{1}{2} \times 30 \times 20 = 300\,\text{m}$$

The train stops in 20 s and travels 300 m while braking.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**8.** In Chapter 1 a model was judged by what it leaves out. The equations in
this section use one model: the acceleration is constant. State one thing this
model leaves out about a real car, and one question it can still answer well.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the driver, the road and the engine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A real car's acceleration changes as the driver presses the pedal and the road
slopes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The model leaves out the way a real acceleration changes. The driver presses the
pedal gradually, the road may slope, and the engine responds differently at
different speeds. None of that appears in a single constant value.

The model still answers a useful question: how fast the car is going after a few
seconds of steady acceleration from a known starting speed. Over a short
interval, where the acceleration is nearly constant, the answer is close to the
real one.

</details>
</details>
</details>
