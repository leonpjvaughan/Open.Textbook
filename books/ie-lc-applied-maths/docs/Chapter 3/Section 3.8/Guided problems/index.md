# 3.8 Time of flight and range — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find $\dfrac{2 \times 10}{9.8}$, to $3$ significant figures.
2. Find $\dfrac{400 \times 0.8660}{9.8}$, to $3$ significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\dfrac{20}{9.8} = 2.04$. This is a time of flight for a vertical component
   of 10 m/s.
2. $\dfrac{346.4}{9.8} = 35.3$. Here $0.8660 = \sin 60^\circ$, which appears in
   the range formula when $2\theta = 60^\circ$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A ball is kicked at $20$ m/s at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Its
vertical component is $10$ m/s. Taking $g = 9.8$ m/s², find the time of flight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T = \dfrac{2u_y}{g}$. The factor $2$ is there because the rise and the fall
take the same time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = \frac{2 \times 10}{9.8}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{20}{9.8} = 2.04\,\text{s}$$

The ball is in the air for about 2.04 s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** The same ball has a horizontal component of $17.32$ m/s. Find its range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The range is the horizontal velocity times the time of flight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 17.32 \times 2.04$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = 17.32 \times 2.04 = 35.35\,\text{m}$$

The range is about 35.35 m, to four significant figures. The formula
$R = \dfrac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$ gives the same value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The same ball has a vertical component of $10$ m/s. Find its greatest
height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the highest point the vertical velocity is zero. Use
$H = \dfrac{u_y^2}{2g}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$H = \frac{10^2}{2 \times 9.8}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$H = \frac{100}{19.6} = 5.10\,\text{m}$$

The ball rises about 5.10 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A ball is kicked at $25$ m/s at $45^\circ$ above the horizontal. Taking
$g = 9.8$ m/s², find its range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At $45^\circ$, $\sin 2\theta = \sin 90^\circ = 1$, so $R = \dfrac{u^2}{g}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = \frac{25^2}{9.8} = \frac{625}{9.8}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = \frac{625}{9.8} = 63.78\,\text{m}$$

This angle gives the greatest range for a launch speed of 25 m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball is kicked at $25$ m/s at $45^\circ$. Its vertical component is
$17.68$ m/s. Find the time of flight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T = \dfrac{2u_y}{g}$ with the vertical component.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = \frac{2 \times 17.68}{9.8}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{35.36}{9.8} = 3.61\,\text{s}$$

The ball is in the air for about 3.61 s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ball is kicked at $30$ m/s at $20^\circ$. Its components are
$28.19$ m/s horizontally and $10.26$ m/s vertically. Find its range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the time of flight first, then multiply by the horizontal component.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = \frac{2 \times 10.26}{9.8} = 2.09\,\text{s}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = u_x T = 28.19 \times 2.09 = 59.03\,\text{m}$$

The range formula gives
$\dfrac{900 \times \sin 40^\circ}{9.8} = 59.03$ m, which agrees.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A projectile has a time of flight of $3$ s. Taking $g = 9.8$ m/s², find
its greatest height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The time to the highest point is half the time of flight. Find $u_y$ first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$t = 1.5$ s and $u_y = gt = 9.8 \times 1.5 = 14.7$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$H = \frac{u_y^2}{2g} = \frac{14.7^2}{19.6} = \frac{216.09}{19.6}
= 11.025\,\text{m}$$

The greatest height is about 11.0 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**8.** In Chapter 1 a model was judged by what it leaves out. The range formula
$R = \dfrac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$ is a model of a kick. State one thing it leaves
out, and one question it can still answer well.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about where the ball lands, and about the air.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The formula assumes the ball lands at the same height it started from, and that
only gravity acts on it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The formula leaves out the height of the landing point. It also leaves out air
resistance, the spin of the ball and the wind. On a sloping pitch, or with a
light ball, the real range differs.

The formula still answers a useful question well: how far a heavy ball travels
on level ground at a given speed and angle. That is close to the real range for
a football or a cricket ball over a short flight.

</details>
</details>
</details>
