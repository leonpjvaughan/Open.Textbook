# 3.9 Projectiles launched at an angle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find $\sin 60^\circ$ and $\sin 120^\circ$, each to $3$ significant figures.
2. Find $90 - 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. Both are $0.866$. Two angles have the same sine when they add to
   $180^\circ$, and that is why two launch angles share a range.
2. $65$. Angles that add to $90^\circ$ are complementary, and they give the
   same range.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A ball is kicked at $20$ m/s at $60^\circ$ above the horizontal. Its
components are $10$ m/s horizontally and $17.32$ m/s vertically. Taking
$g = 9.8$ m/s², find its range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $R = \dfrac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$. Here $2\theta = 120^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sin 120^\circ = \sin 60^\circ = 0.8660$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = \frac{400 \times 0.8660}{9.8} = 35.35\,\text{m}$$

The range is about 35.35 m. This is the same as the range at $30^\circ$, because
the two angles are complementary.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A ball is kicked at $20$ m/s. Taking $g = 9.8$ m/s², find the greatest
range it can have on level ground, and state the angle that gives it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The greatest range comes from $\sin 2\theta = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2\theta = 90^\circ$, so $\theta = 45^\circ$, and $R_{\max} = \dfrac{u^2}{g}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R_{\max} = \frac{400}{9.8} = 40.82\,\text{m}$$

The greatest range is about 40.82 m, and it comes from a launch at $45^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A projectile is launched at $25^\circ$ to the horizontal. At what other
angle, with the same speed, does it have the same range?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Complementary angles give the same range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$90^\circ - 25^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$90^\circ - 25^\circ = 65^\circ$$

The angles $25^\circ$ and $65^\circ$ are complementary, so they give the same
range. The steeper launch stays in the air longer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A ball is kicked at $15$ m/s and lands $20$ m away on level ground. Find
the two possible launch angles, each to $2$ decimal places.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $R = \dfrac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$ to find $\sin 2\theta$, then find two
angles with that sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sin 2\theta = \frac{20 \times 9.8}{225} = 0.8711$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$2\theta = 60.57^\circ$ or $2\theta = 180^\circ - 60.57^\circ = 119.43^\circ$.
Halving gives $\theta = 30.29^\circ$ or $\theta = 59.71^\circ$.

Both angles give a range of 20 m. They are complementary, which is why they
share a range.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball is thrown at $15$ m/s at $30^\circ$ from the top of a cliff $10$ m
high. Its components are $12.99$ m/s horizontally and $7.5$ m/s vertically.
Find the time until it lands, and the horizontal distance it travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the top of the cliff as $y = 0$. The ball lands at $y = -10$. Solve the
quadratic for the positive time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4.9t^2 - 7.5t - 10 = 0$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = \frac{7.5 + \sqrt{7.5^2 + 4 \times 4.9 \times 10}}{9.8}
= \frac{7.5 + 15.88}{9.8} = 2.386\,\text{s}$$

$$R = u_x t = 12.99 \times 2.386 = 30.99\,\text{m}$$

The ball is in the air for about 2.39 s and travels about 31 m horizontally.
The range formula does not apply here, because the ball lands below the point
it started from.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ball is kicked at $20$ m/s at $45^\circ$. Taking $g = 9.8$ m/s², find
its range and its greatest height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At $45^\circ$, $u_x = u_y = 20\sin 45^\circ = 14.14$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = \frac{u^2}{g} \qquad H = \frac{u_y^2}{2g}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = \frac{400}{9.8} = 40.82\,\text{m}$$

$$H = \frac{200}{19.6} = 10.20\,\text{m}$$

At $45^\circ$ the greatest height is exactly one quarter of the range.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A ball is kicked at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal and lands $25$ m
away on level ground. Taking $g = 9.8$ m/s², find the speed at which it was
kicked.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $R = \dfrac{u^2\sin 2\theta}{g}$ with $2\theta = 60^\circ$ and make $u$ the
subject.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$25 = \frac{u^2 \times 0.8660}{9.8}, \qquad u^2 = \frac{25 \times 9.8}{0.8660}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$u^2 = \frac{245}{0.8660} = 282.9$$

$$u = \sqrt{282.9} = 16.82\,\text{m/s}$$

The ball was kicked at about 16.82 m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**8.** In Chapter 1 a model was judged by what it leaves out. The range formula
assumes level ground and no air resistance. State one thing this leaves out
about a real kick, and one question the formula can still answer well.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about a sloping pitch and a spinning ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A real kick often starts and lands at different heights, and the ball spins.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The formula leaves out the slope of the ground, the spin of the ball and the
air. On a hillside, or with a curling free kick, the real range is different.

The formula still answers a useful question well: how far a heavy ball travels
on level ground at a given speed and angle. A coach can use that to choose an
angle before allowing for the wind.

</details>
</details>
</details>
