# 4.3 Motion on a smooth horizontal plane — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $12 \times 9.8$.
2. Solve $5a = 20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $117.6$. This is a weight, and you will use it for the normal reaction.
2. $a = 4$. Dividing both sides by 5 gives the acceleration when the force is 20
   N and the mass is 5 kg.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A block of mass $9$ kg rests on a smooth horizontal floor. A horizontal
force of $36$ N pulls it. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The horizontal resultant is the 36 N pull. Use $F = ma$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$36 = 9a$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{36}{9} = 4\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 4 m/s² in the direction of the pull.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the same block, find the normal reaction from the floor. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The block does not move vertically, so the vertical forces balance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg = 9 \times 9.8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg = 9 \times 9.8 = 88.2\,\text{N}$$

The normal reaction is 88.2 N upwards. It is vertical, so it does not appear in
the horizontal equation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A crate of mass $4$ kg is pulled with $25$ N to the right and $9$ N to
the left on a smooth floor. Find the resultant force and the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the smaller force from the larger, then use the resultant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 25 - 9 = 16\,\text{N}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = 25 - 9 = 16\,\text{N} \text{ to the right}$$

$$a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{16}{4} = 4\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 4 m/s² to the right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A box of mass $20$ kg rests on a smooth horizontal floor. Find the
normal reaction. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $R = mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 20 \times 9.8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg = 20 \times 9.8 = 196\,\text{N}$$

The normal reaction is 196 N upwards, equal to the weight.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A puck of mass $8$ kg slides on smooth ice. A force of $20$ N acts
against its motion. Find the acceleration, taking the direction of motion as
positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The opposing force is negative in the chosen direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$-20 = 8a$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{-20}{8} = -2.5\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is $-2.5$ m/s². The negative sign means the puck is slowing
down, and its speed falls by 2.5 m/s each second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A trolley of mass $3$ kg is pulled with $15$ N one way and $6$ N the
other way on a smooth floor. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the resultant first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 15 - 6 = 9\,\text{N}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = 15 - 6 = 9\,\text{N}$$

$$a = \frac{9}{3} = 3\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 3 m/s² in the direction of the 15 N force.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A student writes: "The normal reaction on a block is 100 N, so the block
accelerates at 100 N divided by its mass." Explain what is wrong.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which direction does the normal reaction act in?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The normal reaction is vertical, and the acceleration on a horizontal floor is
horizontal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The normal reaction acts at right angles to the floor, so it is vertical. A
block on a horizontal floor does not accelerate vertically, because the normal
reaction balances the weight.

The acceleration of the block is horizontal, so it comes from the horizontal
resultant. The normal reaction does not appear in that equation at all. Only the
forces in the direction of the motion affect the acceleration.

</details>
</details>
</details>
