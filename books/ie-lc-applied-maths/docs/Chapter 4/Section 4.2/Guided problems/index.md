# 4.2 Resultant force and equilibrium — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A right-angled triangle has two shorter sides of $9$ cm and $12$ cm. Find the
   hypotenuse.
2. Make $m$ the subject of $F = ma$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{81 + 144} = \sqrt{225} = 15$ cm. Forces at right
   angles combine in exactly this way.
2. $m = \dfrac{F}{a}$. You will use this rearrangement when a question gives a
   force and an acceleration.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Forces of $7$ N and $2$ N act on a particle in the same direction. A
third force of $4$ N acts in the opposite direction. Find the resultant force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the forces that share a direction, then subtract the one that opposes them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = (7 + 2) - 4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = 9 - 4 = 5\,\text{N}$$

The resultant is 5 N in the direction of the 7 N and 2 N forces.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Two forces of $5$ N and $12$ N act at right angles. Find the size of the
resultant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $R = \sqrt{F_1^2 + F_2^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13\,\text{N}$$

The resultant is 13 N. The two given forces and the resultant form a 5-12-13
right-angled triangle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Two forces of $8$ N and $15$ N act at right angles. Find the angle
between the resultant and the 15 N force, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$, with the 8 N force
opposite the angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\tan\theta = \frac{8}{15}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\tan\theta = \frac{8}{15} = 0.5333\ldots$$

$$\theta = 28.1^\circ$$

The angle is 28.1° to the 15 N force. The resultant itself has size
$\sqrt{8^2 + 15^2} = 17$ N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A force of $50$ N acts at $40^\circ$ above the horizontal. Find its
horizontal and vertical components, to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The horizontal component is along the direction of the angle, so it uses
cosine. The vertical component uses sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F_x = 50\cos 40^\circ \qquad F_y = 50\sin 40^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F_x = 50\cos 40^\circ = 50 \times 0.7660 = 38.3\,\text{N}$$

$$F_y = 50\sin 40^\circ = 50 \times 0.6428 = 32.1\,\text{N}$$

Check with Pythagoras:
$\sqrt{38.3^2 + 32.1^2} = \sqrt{1467 + 1030} = 50$ N, to the accuracy of the
data. The resolution is correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two forces of $10$ N and $24$ N act at right angles on a particle. A
third force keeps the particle in equilibrium. Find the size of the third force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the resultant of the two given forces. The third force must balance
it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = \sqrt{10^2 + 24^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = \sqrt{100 + 576} = \sqrt{676} = 26\,\text{N}$$

The third force must be equal and opposite to this resultant, so its size is
26 N. It acts along the same line as the resultant, but the opposite way.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A particle is in equilibrium under three forces. Two of them are $9$ N
and $12$ N, acting at right angles. Find the size of the third force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

In equilibrium the three forces must form a closed triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The third force balances the resultant of the first two.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The resultant of the 9 N and 12 N forces is
$\sqrt{9^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{225} = 15$ N. For equilibrium the third force must be
15 N, opposite to that resultant.

The three forces 9 N, 12 N and 15 N form a right-angled triangle, which is a
useful picture to keep: three forces in equilibrium always close up into a
triangle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A student resolves a $30$ N force at $25^\circ$ to the horizontal and
writes "horizontal component $= 30\sin 25^\circ = 12.7$ N". Explain the mistake
and give the correct horizontal component, to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which component lies along the direction of the angle?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The horizontal direction is the one the angle is measured from, so it uses
cosine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angle is measured from the horizontal, so the component along the horizontal
uses cosine, not sine. The student used sine, which gives the component at right
angles to the horizontal.

$$F_x = 30\cos 25^\circ = 30 \times 0.9063 = 27.2\,\text{N}$$

The correct horizontal component is 27.2 N. A quick check: at a small angle most
of the force points along the horizontal, so the horizontal component should be
close to 30 N. The value 12.7 N is far too small.

</details>
</details>
</details>
