# 4.5 Tension and connected particles — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Sections 4.1 to 4.3, including $F = ma$ and free-body
diagrams.

**Objectives:** explain tension and what a smooth pulley does to it; write
Newton's second law for each of two connected bodies; find the acceleration and
the tension.

Two objects joined by a string move together. The string pulls both of them, and
the pull is the **tension**. This section shows how to write one equation for
each object and then combine them.

## Tension

**Tension** is the pull in a string, rope or cable. It is measured in newtons.

For a **light** string (one whose mass we ignore) over a **smooth** pulley (one
with no friction), the tension is the same at every point. If the string were
heavier or the pulley rough, the tension would change along the string. In this
course the string is light and the pulley smooth, so one value of $T$ serves
both ends.

## Connected particles move together

Two particles joined by a taut string have the same **magnitude** of
acceleration. One may go up while the other goes down, but the size of the
acceleration is the same for both.

The method is always the same.

1. Draw a free-body diagram for each particle.
2. Choose a positive direction for each particle, in the direction it moves.
3. Write $F = ma$ for each particle.
4. Add the two equations. The tension cancels, and you can find $a$.
5. Substitute back into either equation to find $T$.

**Example 1.** Masses of $3$ kg and $5$ kg hang from a light string over a
smooth pulley. Find the acceleration and the tension. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

The 5 kg mass falls and the 3 kg mass rises. For the 5 kg mass, taking downwards
as positive:

$$5g - T = 5a$$

For the 3 kg mass, taking upwards as positive:

$$T - 3g = 3a$$

Add the two equations. The $T$ terms cancel:

$$(5g - T) + (T - 3g) = 5a + 3a$$

$$2g = 8a$$

$$a = \frac{2 \times 9.8}{8}$$

$$a = 2.45\,\text{m/s}^2$$

Substitute back into the equation for the 3 kg mass:

$$T = 3g + 3a$$

$$T = 3(9.8 + 2.45)$$

$$T = 3 \times 12.25$$

$$T = 36.75\,\text{N}$$

The acceleration is 2.45 m/s², and the tension is 36.75 N.

**Example 2.** A car of mass $1200$ kg tows a trailer of mass $800$ kg on a
level road. The driving force is $4000$ N. Find the acceleration and the tension
in the tow bar.

The two move together, so the total mass is $1200 + 800 = 2000$ kg. The
resultant force on the whole system is the 4000 N driving force.

$$F = ma$$

$$4000 = 2000a$$

$$a = 2\,\text{m/s}^2$$

For the trailer, the only horizontal force is the pull of the tow bar:

$$T = m_{\text{trailer}} a$$

$$T = 800 \times 2$$

$$T = 1600\,\text{N}$$

The acceleration is 2 m/s², and the tension in the tow bar is 1600 N.

**Example 3.** Two masses, $3$ kg and $2$ kg, sit on a smooth horizontal table
joined by a light string. A force of $10$ N pulls the $2$ kg mass away from the
$3$ kg mass. Find the acceleration and the tension.

The total mass is $3 + 2 = 5$ kg, and the driving force is 10 N.

$$a = \frac{10}{5} = 2\,\text{m/s}^2$$

For the 3 kg mass, the tension is the only horizontal force:

$$T = 3 \times 2 = 6\,\text{N}$$

The acceleration is 2 m/s², and the tension is 6 N. Check with the 2 kg mass:
$10 - T = 2 \times 2 = 4$ N, so $T = 6$ N, as before.

> **Watch out.** Do not add the two weights and divide by the total mass unless
> the question really is a pulley problem. For Example 1 the resultant on the
> whole system is $5g - 3g$, not $5g + 3g$, because the two weights pull in
> opposite directions. A second error is to treat the tension as an external
> force on the system: inside the system the tension pulls one way on one body
> and the other way on the other, so it cancels when you add the equations. That
> cancellation is what makes the method work.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The crate of mass $20$ kg on the $30^\circ$ ramp is
joined by a rope over a smooth pulley to the hanging mass of $15$ kg.

The rope pulls the crate up the ramp with tension $T$, and it pulls the hanging
mass upwards with the same tension $T$. The hanging mass falls.

Write the two equations, taking the crate's motion up the ramp as positive and
the hanging mass's motion down as positive.

- For the crate: $T - 20g\sin 30^\circ = 20a$, which is
  $T - 98 = 20a$.
- For the hanging mass: $15g - T = 15a$, which is $147 - T = 15a$.

Add them and the tension cancels:

$$(T - 98) + (147 - T) = 20a + 15a$$

$$49 = 35a$$

Find $a$ from that equation, and then find $T$. Keep both answers; Section 4.6
checks them against the full method.

## You should now be able to

- explain tension, and say why it is the same throughout a light string over a
  smooth pulley;
- explain why connected particles have the same magnitude of acceleration;
- write $F = ma$ for each of two connected particles;
- add the equations to eliminate the tension and find the acceleration;
- substitute back to find the tension.
