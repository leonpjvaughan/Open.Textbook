# 4.5 Tension and connected particles — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Make $T$ the subject of $T - 3g = 3a$.
2. Work out $5 + 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $T = 3g + 3a$. Add $3g$ to both sides. You will use this rearrangement to
   find the tension once the acceleration is known.
2. $8$. The total mass is the sum of the two masses, and that sum divides the
   driving force.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Masses of $2$ kg and $6$ kg hang from a light string over a smooth
pulley. Find the acceleration. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The resultant on the system is the difference of the weights, and the mass moved
is the sum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{(6 - 2) \times 9.8}{2 + 6}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{4 \times 9.8}{8} = \frac{39.2}{8} = 4.9\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The 6 kg mass falls and the 2 kg mass rises, both with acceleration 4.9 m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the same two masses, find the tension in the string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the 2 kg mass, which accelerates upwards. Then $T - 2g = 2a$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = 2(9.8 + 4.9)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = 2g + 2a = 2(9.8 + 4.9) = 2 \times 14.7 = 29.4\,\text{N}$$

Check with the 6 kg mass: $6g - T = 58.8 - 29.4 = 29.4$ N, and $6a = 29.4$ N.
The check passes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A car of mass $1000$ kg tows a trailer of mass $500$ kg on a level road.
The driving force is $3000$ N. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total mass moves together.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{3000}{1000 + 500}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{3000}{1500} = 2\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The car and trailer accelerate at 2 m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** For the same car and trailer, find the tension in the tow bar.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For the trailer, the tow bar is the only horizontal force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = 500 \times 2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = m_{\text{trailer}} a = 500 \times 2 = 1000\,\text{N}$$

The tension in the tow bar is 1000 N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Masses of $1$ kg and $3$ kg hang from a light string over a smooth
pulley. Find the acceleration and the tension. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find $a$ from the difference of the weights over the total mass, then find
$T$ from the 1 kg mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{(3 - 1) \times 9.8}{1 + 3} \qquad T = 1(9.8 + a)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{2 \times 9.8}{4} = \frac{19.6}{4} = 4.9\,\text{m/s}^2$$

$$T = 1(9.8 + 4.9) = 14.7\,\text{N}$$

The acceleration is 4.9 m/s², and the tension is 14.7 N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Two masses, $2$ kg and $3$ kg, sit on a smooth horizontal table joined by
a light string. A force of $15$ N pulls the $3$ kg mass away from the $2$ kg
mass. Find the acceleration and the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total mass is $2 + 3 = 5$ kg. The tension pulls the 2 kg mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{15}{5} \qquad T = 2a$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{15}{5} = 3\,\text{m/s}^2$$

$$T = 2 \times 3 = 6\,\text{N}$$

Check with the 3 kg mass: $15 - T = 15 - 6 = 9$ N, and $3a = 9$ N. The check
passes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why the tension is the same at both ends of a light string that
passes over a smooth pulley.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Apply Newton's second law to the string itself.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A light string has negligible mass, so the resultant force on it must be
negligible.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Take the string as the body. Its mass is negligible, so Newton's second law for
the string is $F = ma$ with $m$ almost zero. For the acceleration to stay
finite, the resultant force on the string must be almost zero.

The two pulls on the string are the tensions at its two ends. If the resultant
is zero, those two pulls are equal. So the tension has the same size at both
ends. A rough pulley would add friction and break the equality.

</details>
</details>
</details>
