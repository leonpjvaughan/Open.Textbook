# 4.4 Motion on an inclined plane — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down $\sin 30^\circ$ and $\cos 30^\circ$ to three significant figures.
2. Work out $8 \times 9.8 \times 0.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$ and $\cos 30^\circ = 0.866$, to three significant
   figures. These two values appear in almost every slope question.
2. $39.2$. This is the component down a $30^\circ$ plane of the weight of an
   8 kg mass.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A block of mass $6$ kg rests on a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$.
Find the component of its weight down the plane. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The component down the plane is $mg\sin\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$mg\sin 30^\circ = 6 \times 9.8 \times 0.5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$6 \times 9.8 \times 0.5 = 29.4\,\text{N}$$

The component of the weight down the plane is 29.4 N. The full weight is
$6 \times 9.8 = 58.8$ N, so the slope takes about half of it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the same block, find the normal reaction, to three significant
figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The normal reaction $R$ on a slope is $mg\cos\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 6 \times 9.8 \times \cos 30^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = 6 \times 9.8 \times 0.8660 = 50.9\,\text{N}$$

The normal reaction is 50.9 N. It is smaller than the weight of 58.8 N, because
the plane is tilted.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A block slides down a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$. Find its
acceleration. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On a smooth slope the acceleration is $g\sin\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = 9.8 \times \sin 30^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = 9.8 \times 0.5 = 4.9\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 4.9 m/s² down the plane. It does not depend on the mass of
the block.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A block of mass $10$ kg is held at rest on a smooth plane inclined at
$30^\circ$ by a force acting up the plane. Find the force. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

In equilibrium the force $F$ up the plane balances the component of the weight
down the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F = mg\sin 30^\circ = 10 \times 9.8 \times 0.5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = 49\,\text{N}$$

The force needed is 49 N up the plane. The full weight is 98 N, so the ramp
halves the force needed to hold the block.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A block slides down a smooth plane inclined at $45^\circ$. Find its
acceleration, to three significant figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = g\sin 45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = 9.8 \times 0.7071$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = 9.8 \times \sin 45^\circ = 9.8 \times 0.7071 = 6.93\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 6.93 m/s², to three significant figures. A steeper plane
gives a larger acceleration.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A block slides down a smooth plane with an acceleration of $4.9$ m/s².
Taking $g = 9.8$ m/s², find the angle of the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = g\sin\theta$ and make $\sin\theta$ the subject.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sin\theta = \frac{4.9}{9.8} = 0.5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin\theta = 0.5$$

$$\theta = 30^\circ$$

The plane is inclined at 30°, because $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A student writes: "The component of the weight down a $40^\circ$ plane is
$mg\cos 40^\circ$." Explain the mistake and give the correct component.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which component is small when the plane is nearly flat?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

On a nearly flat plane the block hardly slides, so the down-slope component must
be small. That is the sine component.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The component down the plane is the one along the slope, and it is
$mg\sin\theta$. The student used cosine, which gives the component
perpendicular to the plane.

On a nearly flat plane, $\sin\theta$ is close to zero, so the down-slope
component is small. That matches what happens: a block on a gentle slope barely
slides. The component down the plane is

$$mg\sin 40^\circ = mg \times 0.6428$$

and the component into the plane is $mg\cos 40^\circ = mg \times 0.7660$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
