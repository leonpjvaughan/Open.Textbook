# 4.7 Newton's laws in vector form — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Add the vectors $\begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$ and
   $\begin{pmatrix} 4 \\ -1 \end{pmatrix}$.
2. Multiply the vector $\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix}$ by $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 2 \end{pmatrix}$. Add the matching components: $2 + 4 =
   6$ and $3 + (-1) = 2$.
2. $\begin{pmatrix} 6 \\ 8 \end{pmatrix}$. Multiply each component by 2. Dividing
   by a mass works the same way.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the resultant of $(2\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j})$ N and
$(3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j})$ N.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the $\mathbf{i}$ components, then add the $\mathbf{j}$ components.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$(2 + 3)\mathbf{i} + (5 - 2)\mathbf{j}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\mathbf{R} = 5\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}\,\text{N}$$

The resultant $\mathbf{R}$ is $5\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$ N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A force of $(8\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ N acts on a particle of mass
$4$ kg. Find the acceleration as a vector.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide each component of the force by the mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\mathbf{a} = \frac{1}{4}(8\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\mathbf{a} = 2\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}\,\text{m/s}^2$$

Check each component: $4 \times 2 = 8$ and $4 \times 1 = 4$, which match the
force.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Find the magnitude of the vector $3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Pythagoras on the two components.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$$

The magnitude is 5. The components and the vector form a 3-4-5 right-angled
triangle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A force of $20$ N acts at $30^\circ$ above the positive $x$-axis. Write
it in $\mathbf{i}$, $\mathbf{j}$ form, to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$-component uses cosine, and the $y$-component uses sine.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F_x = 20\cos 30^\circ \qquad F_y = 20\sin 30^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F_x = 20 \times 0.8660 = 17.32\,\text{N}$$

$$F_y = 20 \times 0.5 = 10\,\text{N}$$

So $\mathbf{F} = 17.32\mathbf{i} + 10\mathbf{j}$ N, to three significant
figures.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Two forces act on a particle: $(5\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j})$ N and
$(-2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j})$ N. Find the third force that keeps the particle
in equilibrium.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the resultant first, then take its negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\mathbf{R} = (5 - 2)\mathbf{i} + (2 + 3)\mathbf{j} = 3\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The resultant is $3\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$ N. For equilibrium the third force
must be the negative of the resultant:

$$\mathbf{F}_3 = -3\mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j}\,\text{N}$$

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A force of $(10\mathbf{i} + 15\mathbf{j})$ N gives a particle an
acceleration of $(2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j})$ m/s². Find the mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use one component at a time. The mass must come out the same from both.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From the $\mathbf{i}$ components, $m = \dfrac{10}{2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

From the $\mathbf{i}$ components:

$$m = \frac{10}{2} = 5\,\text{kg}$$

Check with the $\mathbf{j}$ components: $\dfrac{15}{3} = 5$ kg. Both give 5 kg,
so the mass is 5 kg.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why the vector equation $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$ gives two
separate equations, and state them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two vectors are equal only when every matching component is equal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\mathbf{F} = F_x\mathbf{i} + F_y\mathbf{j}$ and
$\mathbf{a} = a_x\mathbf{i} + a_y\mathbf{j}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Write both sides in components:

$$F_x\mathbf{i} + F_y\mathbf{j} = m a_x\mathbf{i} + m a_y\mathbf{j}$$

Two vectors are equal only when their $\mathbf{i}$ components are equal and
their $\mathbf{j}$ components are equal. So the single vector equation is really

$$F_x = m a_x \qquad F_y = m a_y$$

The two directions are independent. You can solve for the $x$ motion from the
$x$ forces alone, and then do the same for $y$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
