# 4.6 Pulleys — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $20 \times 9.8 \times 0.5$.
2. Solve $8a = 29.4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $98$. This is the component down a $30^\circ$ ramp of the weight of a 20 kg
   crate, and it is half the full weight.
2. $a = 3.675$. Dividing both sides by 8 gives the acceleration when the driving
   force is 29.4 N and the total mass is 8 kg.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A mass of $3$ kg rests on a smooth table. A string from it passes over a
smooth pulley and carries a hanging mass of $2$ kg. Find the acceleration and
the tension. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The driving force is the hanging weight. The total mass is $3 + 2 = 5$ kg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{2 \times 9.8}{5} \qquad T = 3a$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{19.6}{5} = 3.92\,\text{m/s}^2$$

$$T = m_1 a = 3 \times 3.92 = 11.76\,\text{N}$$

The acceleration is 3.92 m/s², and the tension is 11.76 N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A mass of $6$ kg rests on a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$. A string
from it runs up the plane, over a smooth pulley, and carries a hanging mass of
$4$ kg. Find the acceleration and the tension. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The resultant is the hanging weight minus the component of the slope mass's
weight down the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{4 \times 9.8 - 6 \times 9.8 \times 0.5}{6 + 4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{39.2 - 29.4}{10} = \frac{9.8}{10} = 0.98\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The hanging mass falls, because its weight of 39.2 N is larger than the 29.4 N
component down the plane.

$$T = 4(9.8 - 0.98) = 4 \times 8.82 = 35.28\,\text{N}$$

The acceleration is 0.98 m/s², and the tension is 35.28 N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A mass of $8$ kg rests on a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$. A string
runs from it, up the plane and over a smooth pulley, and carries a hanging mass
that keeps the system in equilibrium. Find the hanging mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

In equilibrium $m_2 = m_1\sin\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$m_2 = 8 \times \sin 30^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$m_2 = 8 \times 0.5 = 4\,\text{kg}$$

With 4 kg on the end, the hanging weight of 39.2 N equals the component of the
8 kg mass's weight down the plane, so nothing moves.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A mass of $5$ kg rests on a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$. A string
from it runs up the plane, over a smooth pulley, and carries a hanging mass of
$5$ kg. Find the acceleration. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two masses are equal, so the hanging weight is larger than the component
down the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{5 \times 9.8 - 5 \times 9.8 \times 0.5}{5 + 5}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{49 - 24.5}{10} = \frac{24.5}{10} = 2.45\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The hanging mass falls and the slope mass moves up the plane at 2.45 m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A mass of $2$ kg rests on a smooth table. A string from it passes over a
smooth pulley and carries a hanging mass of $5$ kg. Find the acceleration and
the tension. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The driving force is the hanging weight, and the total mass is $2 + 5 = 7$ kg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{5 \times 9.8}{7} \qquad T = 2a$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{49}{7} = 7\,\text{m/s}^2$$

$$T = m_1 a = 2 \times 7 = 14\,\text{N}$$

The acceleration is 7 m/s², and the tension is 14 N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A mass of $6$ kg rests on a smooth plane inclined at $30^\circ$. A string
from it runs up the plane, over a smooth pulley, and carries a hanging mass of
$4$ kg. State which way the system moves, and give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the hanging weight with the component of the slope mass's weight down
the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The hanging weight is $4 \times 9.8 = 39.2$ N. The component down the plane is
$6 \times 9.8 \times 0.5 = 29.4$ N.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The hanging weight, 39.2 N, is larger than the component of the slope mass's
weight down the plane, 29.4 N. The larger force wins, so the hanging mass falls
and the slope mass moves up the plane.

The resultant is $39.2 - 29.4 = 9.8$ N, and the total mass is 10 kg, giving the
acceleration $9.8 \div 10 = 0.98$ m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A student solves a pulley problem by writing "total mass $= m_1$" and
using the full weight of the slope mass instead of its component. Explain both
mistakes, and what effect they have on the answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which bodies actually move, and how much of the slope mass's weight pulls down
the plane?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both bodies accelerate, and only $mg\sin\theta$ pulls down the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first mistake is using only $m_1$ as the mass. Both bodies move, so the total
mass is $m_1 + m_2$. Using $m_1$ alone makes the denominator too small, so the
acceleration comes out too large.

The second mistake is using $mg$ instead of $mg\sin\theta$ for the pull down the
plane. The full weight does not act along the slope. Using the full weight makes
the numerator too small, so the acceleration comes out too small.

The two mistakes push the answer in opposite directions, so they can partly hide
each other. That is why a final check matters: the acceleration of a system like
this must be less than $g$, and both equations should balance once you substitute
the tension.

</details>
</details>
</details>
