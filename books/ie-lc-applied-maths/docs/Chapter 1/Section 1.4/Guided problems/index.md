# 1.4 Translating a situation into mathematics — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A car travels at 12 m/s for 5 seconds. Find the distance it travels, in
   metres.
2. A queue holds 42 students and the till serves 7 students each minute. Find
   the wait for a student at the back, in minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $12 \times 5 = 60$ m. Distance is speed multiplied by time.
2. $42 \div 7 = 6$ minutes.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A till serves 12 students each minute and 4 students join each minute.
Find the net rate at which the queue shrinks, in students per minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The queue drains at the service rate minus the arrival rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$12 - 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s - a = 12 - 4 = 8$$

The queue shrinks by 8 students each minute. The rate is positive, so the queue
drains rather than grows.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A queue of 45 students clears in 9 minutes. Find the net rate
$s - a$, in students per minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $T = \dfrac{Q}{s - a}$ to make $s - a$ the subject.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$s - a = \dfrac{Q}{T}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s - a = \frac{Q}{T} = \frac{45}{9} = 5$$

The net rate is 5 students each minute. If 4 students joined each minute, the
till served $5 + 4 = 9$ each minute.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A queue of 40 students must be gone in 5 minutes. Students join at 4 each
minute. Find the service rate the till needs, in students per minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $s = a + \dfrac{Q}{T}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$s = 4 + \dfrac{40}{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = a + \frac{Q}{T} = 4 + \frac{40}{5} = 4 + 8 = 12$$

The till must serve 12 students each minute. That is one student every five
seconds.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A queue of 40 students clears in 5 minutes and the till serves 12 each
minute. Find the arrival rate, in students per minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange to make $a$ the subject: $a = s - \dfrac{Q}{T}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = 12 - \dfrac{40}{5}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = s - \frac{Q}{T} = 12 - \frac{40}{5} = 12 - 8 = 4$$

The arrival rate is 4 students each minute.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A cyclist travels at a steady 15 m/s for 8 seconds. Use $d = vt$ to find
the distance, in metres, and check the units.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the speed by the time, and watch what happens to the units.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$15 \times 8$, and m/s multiplied by s leaves m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = vt = 15 \times 8 = 120$$

The distance is 120 m. The units confirm it: metres per second multiplied by
seconds cancels the seconds and leaves metres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student writes $T = \dfrac{Q}{a - s}$ for the clear time. Explain what
goes wrong, and write the correct relationship.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Try the chapter problem numbers: $Q = 40$, $a = 5$, $s = 10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

With $a - s = 5 - 10 = -5$, the time comes out as $40 \div (-5) = -8$ minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

With $a - s$ the denominator is negative whenever the service rate is larger
than the arrival rate, which is the usual case. The time then comes out
negative, and a negative time is impossible. The correct relationship is

$$T = \frac{Q}{s - a}$$

because the queue drains at the service rate minus the arrival rate. Check it
with the chapter problem: $40 \div (10 - 5) = 8$ minutes, which is positive and
sensible.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Write the relationship for the clear time of a queue, and define every
letter with its unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are four letters: the queue, the two rates and the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$Q$ is the queue, $a$ and $s$ are the rates, and $T$ is the clear time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $Q$ be the number of students in the queue, in students. Let $a$ be the rate
at which students join, and $s$ the rate at which the till serves them, both in
students per minute. Let $T$ be the clear time, in minutes. Then

$$T = \frac{Q}{s - a}$$

The units check: students divided by students per minute gives minutes, which is
the unit of $T$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
