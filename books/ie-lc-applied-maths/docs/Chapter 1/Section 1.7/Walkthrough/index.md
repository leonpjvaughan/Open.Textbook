# 1.7 How to write a modelling report — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Sections 1.1 to 1.6.

**Objectives:** write a report with its six parts; state assumptions; present
the model and the answer; evaluate honestly.

By the end of this section you can write a modelling report that another person
can follow, check and criticise.

## Why the report matters

The modelling project, worth 20% of your mark, is marked on the report. A
correct answer with no report cannot be marked, because the reader cannot see
where the answer came from. The report is part of the mathematics, not a wrapper
around it.

A report is also how a model is checked. A reader who can see the question and
the assumptions can test the answer, and can say where they disagree.

## The six parts

A modelling report has six parts. They follow the cycle.

1. **The question.** One sentence, with the unit.
2. **The assumptions.** A list, each one stating what it leaves out.
3. **The model.** The letters, their units, and the relationships between them.
4. **The solution.** The substitution, the arithmetic and the units.
5. **The evaluation.** The comparison with data, and the refinement.
6. **The conclusion.** The answer in one sentence, with its limits.

## What each part looks like

**The question.** "How many minutes does a student who joins the canteen queue
at 12:30 wait before being served?"

**The assumptions.** "Students arrive at 5 each minute. The till serves 10 each
minute. Nobody leaves the queue. The queue holds 40 students at 12:30."

**The model.** "$Q$ is the queue at 12:30 in students, $s$ is the service rate
in students per minute, and $W$ is the wait in minutes. Then $W = Q/s$."

**The solution.** "$W = 40 \div 10 = 4$. The wait is 4 minutes."

**The evaluation.** "A count of the real queue gave a wait of 6 minutes, so the
model is too low. The queue at 12:30 was nearer 60 students. With $Q = 60$ the
model gives 6 minutes, which matches the count. The assumption about the
starting queue was wrong."

**The conclusion.** "A student joining at 12:30 waits about 6 minutes. The
answer depends on the starting queue, and a heavier lunch rush would make it
longer."

## A short report

Here is the canteen report, written out as a reader would see it.

> **Question.** How many minutes does a student who joins the canteen queue at
> 12:30 wait before being served?
>
> **Assumptions.** Students arrive at 5 each minute. One till serves 10 students
> each minute. Nobody leaves the queue. The queue holds 40 students at 12:30.
>
> **Model.** Let $Q$ be the number of students in the queue at 12:30, and let
> $s$ be the number served each minute. The wait is $W = Q/s$, in minutes.
>
> **Solution.** $W = 40 \div 10 = 4$ minutes.
>
> **Evaluation.** A count of the real queue gave a wait of about 6 minutes, so
> the model is too low. The starting queue was nearer 60 students. With
> $Q = 60$, the model gives $W = 60 \div 10 = 6$ minutes, which matches the
> count.
>
> **Conclusion.** A student joining at 12:30 waits about 6 minutes. The answer
> depends most on the starting queue, and a heavier lunch rush would make it
> longer.

The report is short, and every part is present. A reader can follow it, check
the arithmetic, and disagree with one assumption by name.

> **Watch out.** The most common report error is to give the answer without the
> assumptions. A reader who cannot see the assumptions cannot tell whether the
> answer applies to their own canteen. Write the assumptions first, even when
> they seem plain.
>
> A second error is to claim more than the model supports. "The wait is 6
> minutes" is a claim about one queue on one day. "The wait is about 6 minutes
> at 12:30, and longer if the rush is heavier" is honest about the limits.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. Write the conclusion of the report in three
sentences.

1. The answer to the question, with its unit.
2. The assumption the answer depends on most.
3. One thing the model cannot tell the reader.

## You should now be able to

- name the six parts of a modelling report, in order;
- write a precise question with a unit;
- list the assumptions before the mathematics, and say what each one leaves out;
- write a conclusion that gives the answer, its main assumption and its limits.
