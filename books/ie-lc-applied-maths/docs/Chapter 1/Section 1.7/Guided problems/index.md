# 1.7 How to write a modelling report — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A report models a queue of 40 students served at 10 each minute. Find the
   wait for a student at the back, in minutes.
2. Name the stage of the cycle at which you compare a model with data.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $40 \div 10 = 4$ minutes.
2. Evaluate. It comes after computing, and it feeds the refine stage.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Name the six parts of a modelling report, in order.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

They follow the cycle: decide, assume, write, calculate, compare, conclude.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first three are the question, the assumptions and the model.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The question, the assumptions, the model, the solution, the evaluation and the
conclusion. The question and the assumptions are formulating. The model is
translating. The solution is computing. The evaluation is evaluating and
refining. The conclusion states the answer and its limits.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A report models a canteen queue of 40 students served at 10 students each
minute. Find the wait, in minutes, for a student who joins at the back.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wait is the queue divided by the service rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$40 \div 10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$W = \frac{Q}{s} = \frac{40}{10} = 4$$

The report states the answer as "about 4 minutes", because the model is an
estimate. A report that claims exactly 4 minutes claims more than the model can
support.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A count shows that 60 students, not 40, were queuing. The till serves
10 students each minute. Find the wait, in minutes, and say what the refinement
changed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the corrected queue length in the same relationship.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$60 \div 10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$W = \frac{60}{10} = 6$$

The wait is 6 minutes. The refinement changed one assumption, the number already
in the queue, from 40 to 60. The answer rose from 4 minutes to 6 minutes, which
matches the measured wait.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A report gives an answer of 6 minutes but lists no assumptions. Explain
why the report is incomplete.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What does a reader need in order to check the answer, or to use it?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A reader cannot check a number without knowing what the model treated as true.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A reader cannot check the answer without knowing what the model treated as true.
A reader also cannot tell whether the answer applies to their own canteen, which
may have different arrival and service rates. The assumptions are what make the
answer usable, so a report without them is incomplete.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain why every letter in a report's model needs a unit beside it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about a reader who has only the report, not the original question.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The unit says what the letter measures, and it lets the reader check the
relationship.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The unit says what the letter measures, so another person can read the model and
know what each symbol means. The units also let a reader check that the
relationship balances. A model with missing units cannot be checked, and a
reader may read $Q$ as a rate when it is a total.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A report concludes that a student waits about 6 minutes at 12:30. State
one thing the model cannot tell the reader, and explain why it matters.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The model works with an average rate. What does that leave out?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

It cannot say how long one particular student waits.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The model gives an average, so it cannot say how long one particular student
waits. A real student could be served sooner or later. This matters because a
reader who treats the average as a promise will be surprised by the real queue.
Saying so is part of an honest report.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Write the conclusion of the canteen report. It must give the answer with
its unit, the assumption the answer depends on most, and one limit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Three sentences: the answer, the assumption, the limit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The answer is about 6 minutes, and the starting queue matters most.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A student who joins the canteen queue at 12:30 waits about 6 minutes. The answer
depends most on how many students are already queuing, so a heavier lunch rush
would make the wait longer. The model gives an average, so it cannot say how
long one particular student waits.

Each sentence does one job. The first gives the answer, the second names the
assumption, and the third states the limit.

</details>
</details>
</details>
