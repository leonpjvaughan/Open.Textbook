# 1.1 The problem-solving cycle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. How many minutes are in $2\frac{1}{2}$ hours?
2. A car travels 90 km in 1.5 hours. Find its average speed in kilometres per
   hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2\frac{1}{2} \times 60 = 150$ minutes.
2. $90 \div 1.5 = 60$ km/h. A rate is a total divided by a time, and every model
   in this chapter works with a rate.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A tank holds 500 litres. A tap puts water in at 20 litres each minute,
and there is no leak. Find the time to fill the tank, in minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The time is the volume divided by the rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$500 \div 20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{V}{r} = \frac{500}{20} = 25$$

The tank takes 25 minutes to fill. Check it: $20 \times 25 = 500$, which is the
volume of the tank.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A tank holds 480 litres. A tap puts water in at 20 litres each minute and
a leak takes water out at 4 litres each minute. Find the time to fill the tank,
in minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out the net rate first. The tap and the leak act in opposite directions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The net rate is $20 - 4 = 16$ litres each minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{480}{20 - 4} = \frac{480}{16} = 30$$

The tank takes 30 minutes to fill. Compare this with question 1: the leak costs
you 5 minutes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Name the five stages of the modelling cycle, in order, and say what
happens at the compute stage.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The stages follow the order of the work: decide, write, calculate, compare,
change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first three are formulate, translate and compute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Formulate, translate, compute, evaluate, refine. At the compute stage you put
the numbers into the relationship and work out the answer. You keep the unit
beside every number, so the answer says what it measures.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A canteen queue holds 30 students. The till serves 6 students each minute,
and no new students join. Find the time until the queue is gone, in minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The queue falls by 6 students each minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$30 \div 6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{30}{6} = 5$$

The queue is gone after 5 minutes. Check it: $6 \times 5 = 30$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A canteen queue holds 30 students. Students join at 6 each minute, and the
till serves 12 each minute. Find the time until the queue is gone, in minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The queue drains at the service rate minus the arrival rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$12 - 6 = 6$ students each minute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{Q}{s - a} = \frac{30}{12 - 6} = \frac{30}{6} = 5$$

The queue is gone after 5 minutes. The same time as question 4, because the net
rate is the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student answers a question about a queue of 40 students served at
10 each minute by writing $40 \times 10 = 400$ minutes. Say what is wrong, and
give the correct answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What does the service rate measure, and how does the total change each minute?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A rate of 10 students each minute means the queue falls by 10 every minute, so
you divide rather than multiply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Multiplying by the rate gives the number of students served in 400 minutes,
which is far more than the queue holds. The queue falls by 10 each minute, so
the time is

$$T = \frac{40}{10} = 4$$

minutes. Check it by reversing the arithmetic: $10 \times 4 = 40$, which is the
queue. The check passes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A model predicts that a queue clears in 8 minutes. The real queue cleared
in 11 minutes. Name the stage of the cycle that deals with the difference, and
say what happens at that stage.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The cycle has a stage for comparing the answer with reality, and a stage for
changing something.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The comparison is evaluating, and the change is refining.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The difference is dealt with at the evaluate stage, which compares the model's
answer of 8 minutes with the measured 11 minutes. The next stage is refine,
which changes one assumption and works the model again. Here the arrival rate
may have been higher than the model assumed, so the queue took longer to drain.

</details>
</details>
</details>
