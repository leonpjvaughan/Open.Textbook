# 7.1 Angular velocity and period — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wheel turns $120$ times each minute. How many times does it turn each
   second?
2. Work out $\dfrac{2\pi}{4}$, leaving your answer in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $2$ times each second. Divide the rate each minute by $60$, because a minute
   has $60$ seconds.
2. $\dfrac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.57$. A full turn is $2\pi$ radians, so a quarter of
   a turn is $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ radians.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A point moves round a circle with period $4$ s. Find its angular velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle in one full turn is $2\pi$ radians.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T} = \frac{2\pi}{4}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\omega = \frac{2\pi}{4} = \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.57\ \text{rad/s}$$

The angular velocity is the angle turned each second. It does not depend on the
radius of the circle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A wheel has period $0.25$ s. Find its frequency and its angular velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Frequency is the reciprocal of the period.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{0.25} = 4\ \text{Hz}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$f = 4\ \text{Hz}, \qquad \omega = 2\pi f = 8\pi \approx 25.1\ \text{rad/s}$$

Four turns each second, and each turn is $2\pi$ radians, so the wheel turns
$8\pi$ radians each second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A disc turns at $600$ revolutions each minute. Find its angular velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Convert the rate to revolutions each second first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$600 \div 60 = 10\ \text{revolutions each second}$$

$$\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 10$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\omega = 20\pi \approx 62.8\ \text{rad/s}$$

Ten turns each second, each of $2\pi$ radians. Leaving the answer as $20\pi$
keeps it exact.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A point on the rim of a wheel of radius $0.3$ m has angular velocity
$20$ rad/s. Find its linear speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v = \omega r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v = \omega r = 20 \times 0.3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = 6\ \text{m/s}$$

The angular velocity describes the turn. Multiplying by the radius turns it into
the distance travelled along the circle each second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A car goes round a roundabout of radius $25$ m at $15$ m/s. Find its
angular velocity and its period.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the angular velocity from $v = \omega r$, then the period from
$\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\omega = \frac{v}{r} = \frac{15}{25} = 0.6\ \text{rad/s}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{0.6} = \frac{10\pi}{3} \approx 10.5\ \text{s}$$

The car turns through $0.6$ radians each second, so one full turn of $2\pi$
radians takes about $10.5$ s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A wheel of radius $0.35$ m turns with period $0.2$ s. Find its angular
velocity and the speed of a point on its rim.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the angular velocity from the period, then use $v = \omega r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\omega = \frac{2\pi}{0.2} = 10\pi\ \text{rad/s}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \omega r = 10\pi \times 0.35 = 3.5\pi \approx 11.0\ \text{m/s}$$

Two points on the same wheel share the angular velocity but not the linear
speed. A point nearer the centre travels more slowly.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why every point on a rigid turning wheel has the same angular
velocity, but points further from the centre have a greater linear speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the angle each point turns through in one second, and then about the
distance each point travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

In one second every point turns through the same angle $\omega$. A point at
distance $r$ travels the arc $\omega r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The wheel is rigid, so all its points stay in the same positions relative to one
another. In a given time they all turn through the same angle, so they share the
same angular velocity. The distance each point travels along its circle is the
angle times its radius, $v = \omega r$. A point further from the centre has a
larger $r$, so it travels further in the same time and has a greater linear
speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**8.** A cyclist travels $240$ m in $20$ s along a straight road. Find the
average speed, and say why this question is about linear speed rather than
angular velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Speed is distance divided by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v = \frac{240}{20}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = 12\ \text{m/s}$$

The road is straight, so the cyclist does not turn through an angle and the
angular velocity is zero. This is a Chapter 3 question about motion in a
straight line. When the path is a circle, the same linear speed $v$ goes with an
angular velocity $\omega = \dfrac{v}{r}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
