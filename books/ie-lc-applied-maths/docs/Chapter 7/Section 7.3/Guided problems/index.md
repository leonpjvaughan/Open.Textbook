# 7.3 Circular motion in context — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $0.6 \times 9.8 \times 10$.
2. A surface has coefficient of friction $0.5$ and normal reaction $400$ N. Find
   the greatest friction force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $58.8$. This is the shape of the expression inside the square root for the
   greatest speed on a bend.
2. $F_{\max} = \mu R = 0.5 \times 400 = 200$ N. Limiting friction is the
   coefficient times the normal reaction.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A car goes round a flat bend of radius $30$ m. The coefficient of friction
is $0.6$. Find the greatest speed at which the car can take the bend.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v_{\max} = \sqrt{\mu g r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_{\max} = \sqrt{0.6 \times 9.8 \times 30}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_{\max} = \sqrt{176.4} \approx 13.3\ \text{m/s}$$

The mass cancelled out, so a heavier car takes the bend at the same greatest
speed. What matters is the friction available and the radius.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A car of mass $1200$ kg goes round a flat bend of radius $40$ m with
coefficient of friction $0.7$. Find the greatest speed, and say whether the mass
affected your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The mass cancels in $v_{\max} = \sqrt{\mu g r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_{\max} = \sqrt{0.7 \times 9.8 \times 40}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_{\max} = \sqrt{274.4} \approx 16.6\ \text{m/s}$$

The mass did not affect the answer. The force needed is $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ and
the friction available is $\mu mg$, and $m$ appears on both sides, so it
cancels.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A car of mass $1500$ kg goes round a flat bend of radius $60$ m at
$15$ m/s. Find the friction force needed towards the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F = \frac{1500 \times 15^2}{60}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{1500 \times 225}{60} = 5625\ \text{N}$$

Friction is not at its limiting value here. It takes whatever value is needed to
hold the car on the bend, up to the limit $\mu R$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A car goes round a flat bend of radius $50$ m with coefficient of friction
$0.4$. The car's speed is $18$ m/s. Decide whether the car stays on the bend, and
justify your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the greatest speed and compare it with $18$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_{\max} = \sqrt{0.4 \times 9.8 \times 50} = \sqrt{196}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_{\max} = 14\ \text{m/s}$$

The car's speed of $18$ m/s is greater than $14$ m/s, so the friction available
is not enough. The car slides outwards, and the driver must slow down. The exact
value $196$ has a whole-number square root, so no rounding is needed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A car of mass $1000$ kg crosses a hump-back bridge whose top is an arc of
radius $50$ m. The speed is $12$ m/s. Find the normal reaction on the car at the
top.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the top, the resultant towards the centre is $mg - R$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg - \frac{mv^2}{r} = 1000 \times 9.8 - \frac{1000 \times 12^2}{50}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = 9800 - 2880 = 6920\ \text{N}$$

The reaction is less than the weight of $9800$ N, because the car is accelerating
downwards towards the centre of the arc.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ball moves in a vertical circle of radius $1.6$ m. Find the least speed
it can have at the top and still keep the string taut.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set the tension to zero in $T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} - mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{mv^2}{r} = mg, \qquad v^2 = gr$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{gr} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 1.6} = \sqrt{15.68} \approx 3.96\ \text{m/s}$$

At this speed the tension is exactly zero. Below it the string would go slack,
because the weight alone is more than the force needed for the circular path.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A ball of mass $0.2$ kg on a string moves in a vertical circle of radius
$1.0$ m. At the top its speed is $5$ m/s. Find the tension in the string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the top, the weight and the tension both point towards the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T + mg = \frac{mv^2}{r}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{0.2 \times 5^2}{1.0} - 0.2 \times 9.8 = 5 - 1.96 = 3.04\ \text{N}$$

The tension is positive, so the string is taut. Its speed is above the least
speed $\sqrt{9.8 \times 1.0} \approx 3.13$ m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 5

**8.** A box of mass $10$ kg rests on a rough horizontal floor with coefficient
of friction $0.3$. Find the greatest friction force the floor can give.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The normal reaction on a horizontal floor balances the weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg = 10 \times 9.8 = 98\ \text{N}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F_{\max} = \mu R = 0.3 \times 98 = 29.4\ \text{N}$$

This is a Chapter 5 question about friction. This section uses the same result:
on a flat bend, the greatest friction $\mu mg$ is what sets the greatest speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>
