# 7.5 Hooke's law and elastic strings — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $0.8 - 0.5$.
2. A spring of natural length $1.2$ m is stretched to a length of $1.5$ m. Find
   its extension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.3$. This is the shape of an extension: the stretched length minus the
   natural length.
2. $x = 1.5 - 1.2 = 0.3$ m. Hooke's law uses the extension, never the stretched
   length.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A spring of stiffness $250$ N/m is extended by $0.2$ m. Find the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T = kx$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = 250 \times 0.2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = 50\ \text{N}$$

The tension is proportional to the extension, so doubling the extension would
double the tension.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A spring of natural length $0.4$ m and stiffness $150$ N/m is stretched to
a length of $0.6$ m. Find the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the extension first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x = 0.6 - 0.4 = 0.2\ \text{m}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = kx = 150 \times 0.2 = 30\ \text{N}$$

Using the length $0.6$ m in place of the extension would give $90$ N, which is
three times too large.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A mass of $3$ kg hangs at rest from a spring of stiffness $600$ N/m. Find
the extension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At rest the tension balances the weight, so $kx = mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x = \frac{mg}{k} = \frac{3 \times 9.8}{600}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = \frac{29.4}{600} = 0.049\ \text{m}$$

The spring is $4.9$ cm longer than its natural length. The mass does not affect
the stiffness; it sets the tension through its weight.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** An elastic string has natural length $0.8$ m and modulus $80$ N. It is
stretched to a length of $1.1$ m. Find the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T = \dfrac{\lambda x}{L}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$x = 1.1 - 0.8 = 0.3\ \text{m}$$

$$T = \frac{80 \times 0.3}{0.8}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{24}{0.8} = 30\ \text{N}$$

The modulus $\lambda$ is measured in newtons, and it describes the string itself.
A thicker string of the same length has a larger modulus.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A mass of $1.5$ kg hangs from a spring of stiffness $300$ N/m in a lift
that accelerates upwards at $1.5$ m/s². Find the tension and the extension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The tension must support the weight and accelerate the mass, so $T = m(g + a)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = 1.5 \times (9.8 + 1.5)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = 1.5 \times 11.3 = 16.95\ \text{N}$$

$$x = \frac{T}{k} = \frac{16.95}{300} = 0.0565\ \text{m}$$

The spring is longer than it would be at rest, because the tension also has to
accelerate the mass upwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A spring is extended by $0.08$ m by a tension of $24$ N. Find its
stiffness.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $T = kx$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$k = \frac{T}{x} = \frac{24}{0.08}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$k = 300\ \text{N/m}$$

The stiffness is the tension for each metre of extension. A larger stiffness
means a spring that is harder to stretch.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain what the elastic limit is, and why Hooke's law stops working
beyond it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what happens to the natural length after the force is removed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Hooke's law says the tension is proportional to the extension. Beyond the
elastic limit that proportion fails.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The elastic limit is the extension beyond which the spring does not return to its
natural length when the force is removed. Up to that point the material behaves
elastically, and the tension is proportional to the extension, so $T = kx$
holds. Beyond the limit the material is permanently stretched, the natural length
has changed, and the same extension no longer gives the same tension. The
proportionality has broken down, so Hooke's law no longer describes the spring.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** A mass of $4$ kg hangs at rest from a light vertical string. Find the
tension in the string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At rest the resultant force is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$T = mg = 4 \times 9.8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = 39.2\ \text{N}$$

This is a Chapter 4 question about equilibrium, and it is the same balance this
section uses. For a mass on a spring, the tension $mg$ is also equal to $kx$, and
that is what fixes the extension.

</details>
</details>
</details>
