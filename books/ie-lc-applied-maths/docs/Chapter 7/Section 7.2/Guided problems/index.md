# 7.2 Centripetal acceleration and force — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\dfrac{6^2}{2}$.
2. A circle has diameter $0.8$ m. What is its radius?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $18$. Square the speed first, then divide by the radius.
2. $0.4$ m. The radius is half the diameter, and the centripetal formulas use
   the radius.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A body moves in a circle of radius $2$ m at a steady $6$ m/s. Find its
centripetal acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{6^2}{2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{36}{2} = 18\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration points towards the centre of the circle, in the direction the
body keeps turning.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A body of mass $0.5$ kg moves in a circle of radius $1.5$ m at $3$ m/s.
Find its centripetal acceleration and the force towards the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the acceleration first, then use $F = ma$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{3^2}{1.5} = 6\ \text{m/s}^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = 6\ \text{m/s}^2, \qquad F = ma = 0.5 \times 6 = 3\ \text{N}$$

The force is $3$ N towards the centre. Something must supply it, such as the
tension in a string.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A body moves in a circle of radius $0.5$ m with angular velocity
$4$ rad/s. Find its centripetal acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = \omega^2 r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = 4^2 \times 0.5$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = 16 \times 0.5 = 8\ \text{m/s}^2$$

The form $a = \omega^2 r$ is quicker when you are given the angular velocity
rather than the speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A body of mass $2$ kg moves in a circle of radius $0.4$ m with angular
velocity $5$ rad/s. Find the force towards the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $F = m\omega^2 r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F = 2 \times 5^2 \times 0.4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = 2 \times 25 \times 0.4 = 20\ \text{N}$$

The force is $20$ N towards the centre. In this form the linear speed never
appears, because $v = \omega r$ was used to build it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A car of mass $1500$ kg goes round a bend of radius $50$ m at $20$ m/s.
Find the centripetal acceleration and the force towards the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$, then $F = ma$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = \frac{20^2}{50} = 8\ \text{m/s}^2$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = 1500 \times 8 = 12000\ \text{N}$$

The friction between the tyres and the road supplies this force. Section 7.3
asks how much friction the road can give before the car slides.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A ball of mass $0.15$ kg on a string moves in a horizontal circle of
radius $0.6$ m and makes $2$ revolutions each second. Find the tension in the
string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the angular velocity from the frequency, then use $F = m\omega^2 r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 2 = 4\pi\ \text{rad/s}$$

$$F = 0.15 \times (4\pi)^2 \times 0.6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = 0.15 \times 16\pi^2 \times 0.6 = 1.44\pi^2 \approx 14.2\ \text{N}$$

The tension points along the string towards the centre of the circle. The string
is the only thing pulling the ball inwards, so the tension is the centripetal
force.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why the centripetal force does not change the speed of a body
moving in a circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the direction of the force compared with the direction of motion.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The force points towards the centre, and the velocity points along the tangent.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The velocity of the body points along the tangent to the circle, at right angles
to the radius. The centripetal force points along the radius, towards the
centre. So the force is at right angles to the motion at every instant. A force
at right angles to the motion changes the direction of the velocity but not its
size, so the speed stays the same and only the direction changes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** A body of mass $4$ kg is acted on by a resultant force of $20$ N. Find
its acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is Newton's second law.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$F = ma, \qquad 20 = 4a$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{20}{4} = 5\ \text{m/s}^2$$

This is a Chapter 4 question, and it is the same law this section uses. The only
new idea is that the resultant force points towards the centre of the circle and
has the size $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>
