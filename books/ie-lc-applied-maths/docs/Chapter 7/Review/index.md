# Chapter 7: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The car on the roundabout and the banked bend.** A car of mass $1200$ kg takes a
roundabout of radius $20$ m at $10$ m/s, then a motorway bend of radius $150$ m banked at
$15^\circ$. Its four springs have combined stiffness $120\,000$ N/m. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

**The predictions.** The car needs a force pushing it **inwards**, towards the centre —
the road surface provides it, through friction. On a wet day the road is more slippery,
so the greatest speed is **lower**. A banked bend allows a **higher** speed at the same
radius, because part of the inward force comes from the slope rather than from friction.

**Angular velocity and period.** With $r = 20$ m and $v = 10$ m/s:

$$\omega = \frac{v}{r} = \frac{10}{20} = 0.5\ \text{rad/s}
\qquad
T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = \frac{2\pi}{0.5} = 4\pi \approx 12.6\ \text{s}$$

**Centripetal acceleration and force.**

$$a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{10^2}{20} = 5\ \text{m/s}^2
\qquad
F = ma = 1200 \times 5 = 6000\ \text{N}$$

The force is $6000$ N directed towards the centre of the roundabout. Friction between the
tyres and the road provides it.

**Greatest speed on the roundabout.** With $\mu = 0.8$, friction can supply at most
$\mu mg$, so

$$\frac{mv^2}{r} = \mu mg \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{\mu g r} = \sqrt{0.8 \times 9.8 \times 20} \approx 12.5\ \text{m/s}$$

The car can take the roundabout at up to about $12.5$ m/s, which is $45$ km/h. Above that
it slides outwards, because friction can no longer supply the inward force needed.

**Design speed of the banked bend.** On a banked track the slope itself provides the
inward force, and at the design speed no friction is needed:

$$v = \sqrt{gr\tan\theta} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 150 \times \tan 15^\circ} \approx 19.8\ \text{m/s}$$

That is about $71$ km/h. At that speed the car needs no friction at all, which is why a
banked bend is safer in the wet: the design speed does not depend on the road being dry.

**Compression of the springs.** The weight of the car is

$$mg = 1200 \times 9.8 = 11\,760\ \text{N}$$

By Hooke's law, $F = kx$, so

$$x = \frac{F}{k} = \frac{11\,760}{120\,000} = 0.098\ \text{m}$$

The springs compress by about $9.8$ cm when the car is at rest on them.

**Period of the bounce.** A mass on a spring moves with simple harmonic motion, and

$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1200}{120\,000}} = 2\pi\sqrt{0.01} = 0.2\pi \approx 0.63\ \text{s}$$

The car bounces about once every $0.63$ s. Notice that the period does not depend on how
far the car is pushed down — that is the defining property of simple harmonic motion, and
it is why a car's suspension has one natural bounce period whatever the load.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **what is supplying the centripetal
force** before you calculate: friction, tension, gravity, the normal force, or a
combination.

1. A stone on a string of length $0.80$ m is whirled at $4.0$ m/s. Find its angular
   velocity, its period and its centripetal acceleration.
2. A $0.50$ kg mass on a string moves in a horizontal circle of radius $1.2$ m at
   $3.0$ m/s. Find the tension in the string.
3. A satellite orbits at a height where $g = 8.0$ m/s² and its orbital radius is
   $7.0 \times 10^6$ m. Find its speed.
4. A car of mass $1400$ kg takes a flat bend of radius $80$ m. If $\mu = 0.7$, find the
   greatest speed.
5. A bend of radius $200$ m is banked at $10^\circ$. Find its design speed.
6. A bucket of water is swung in a vertical circle of radius $0.90$ m. Find the minimum
   speed at the top for the water to stay in.
7. A spring stretches by $0.15$ m when a $3.0$ kg mass hangs on it. Find its spring
   constant and the period of the resulting oscillation.
8. A simple harmonic motion has amplitude $0.040$ m and period $0.50$ s. Find its
   maximum speed and its maximum acceleration.
9. Explain why a car crossing a hump-back bridge feels lighter at the top.
10. A student says a body moving in a circle at constant speed has no acceleration.
    Explain what is wrong.

## What you can now do

- define angular velocity and period, and convert between them and a linear speed;
- explain why motion in a circle at constant speed is still an acceleration;
- use $a = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$ and $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$;
- find the greatest speed on a flat bend from the coefficient of friction;
- resolve the forces on a conical pendulum and on a car on a banked track, and find the
  design speed;
- apply Hooke's law to a spring or an elastic string;
- state the conditions for simple harmonic motion and use $a = -\omega^2 x$;
- find the period, maximum speed and maximum acceleration of a simple harmonic motion.

## Thinking back

Look at the rotation you chose at the start of the chapter — a washing machine on its spin
cycle, a satellite, a bucket of water, a fairground ride.

You can now say something quantitative about it. Write down the radius and the speed, then
work out the acceleration and the force. Then answer the question that matters: **what is
supplying that force?** If nothing can supply it, the object does not stay in the circle —
which is exactly what happens when a car slides on a wet bend or when a bucket is swung
too slowly at the top.

If your estimate is rough, say how rough. Judging whether the force is available is part
of the applied mathematics, and it is the step that turns the formula into an answer.
