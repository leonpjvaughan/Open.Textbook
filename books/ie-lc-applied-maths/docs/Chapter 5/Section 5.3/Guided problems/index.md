# 5.3 Motion on rough planes — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $9.8 \times 0.8660$, to four significant figures.
2. Solve $5a = 16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $8.487$. That is $g\cos 30^\circ$, which turns a mass into the normal
   reaction on a $30^\circ$ slope.
2. $a = 3.2$. Dividing both sides by 5 gives an acceleration when the
   resultant force is 16 N and the mass is 5 kg.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A block of mass $6$ kg slides down a rough plane inclined at
$30^\circ$. The coefficient of friction is $0.25$. Find its acceleration, to
three significant figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = g(\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = 9.8(0.5 - 0.25 \times 0.8660)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = g(\sin 30^\circ - \mu\cos 30^\circ)$$

$$a = 9.8(0.5 - 0.25 \times 0.8660)$$

$$a = 9.8 \times 0.2835 = 2.78\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 2.78 m/s² down the plane. The mass of the block does not
appear, so it does not matter.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A block slides down a rough plane inclined at $45^\circ$. The
coefficient of friction is $0.2$. Find its acceleration, to three significant
figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On a $45^\circ$ plane, $\sin 45^\circ = \cos 45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = 9.8 \times 0.7071 \times (1 - 0.2)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

On a $45^\circ$ plane the two components are equal, so

$$a = g\sin 45^\circ(1 - \mu)$$

$$a = 9.8 \times 0.7071 \times 0.8 = 5.54\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The acceleration is 5.54 m/s² down the plane.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A crate of mass $20$ kg slides down a rough plane inclined at
$30^\circ$. The coefficient of friction is $0.25$. Find the normal reaction and
the friction force, each to four significant figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $R$ perpendicular to the plane first, then use $F = \mu R$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 20 \times 9.8 \times 0.8660 = 169.74\,\text{N}$$

$$F = 0.25 \times 169.74$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg\cos 30^\circ = 20 \times 9.8 \times 0.8660 = 169.74\,\text{N}$$

The crate is sliding, so friction is limiting:

$$F = \mu R = 0.25 \times 169.74 = 42.44\,\text{N}$$

Friction acts up the plane. The weight component down the plane is $98$ N, so
the crate accelerates down the plane.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A block rests on a rough plane inclined at $20^\circ$. The coefficient of
friction is $0.5$. Decide whether the block slides.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare $\tan\theta$ with $\mu$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\tan 20^\circ = 0.364$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The block slides only when $\tan\theta > \mu$. Here

$$\tan 20^\circ = 0.364 \quad\text{and}\quad \mu = 0.5$$

Since $0.364 < 0.5$, the block does not slide. It stays at rest, and friction
takes the value $mg\sin 20^\circ$ needed to hold it. That is less than the
maximum $\mu mg\cos 20^\circ$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A block is projected up a rough plane inclined at $30^\circ$. The
coefficient of friction is $0.3$. Find its deceleration, to three significant
figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On the way up, the weight component and friction both act down the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$a = 9.8(0.5 + 0.3 \times 0.8660)$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = g(\sin 30^\circ + \mu\cos 30^\circ)$$

$$a = 9.8(0.5 + 0.3 \times 0.8660)$$

$$a = 9.8 \times 0.7598 = 7.45\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The block slows down at 7.45 m/s² on the way up. On the way down, friction
reverses, and the acceleration is $g(\sin 30^\circ - 0.3\cos 30^\circ) =
2.35$ m/s².

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A block of mass $12$ kg slides on a rough horizontal floor. The
coefficient of friction is $0.4$ and no other horizontal force acts. Find the
acceleration, taking the direction of motion as positive. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On a horizontal floor, $R = mg$ and friction is $\mu mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$ma = -\mu mg$$

$$a = -\mu g = -0.4 \times 9.8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg = 12 \times 9.8 = 117.6\,\text{N}$$

$$F = \mu R = 0.4 \times 117.6 = 47.04\,\text{N}$$

Friction acts against the motion, so

$$a = -\frac{47.04}{12} = -3.92\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The mass cancels: $a = -\mu g = -3.92$ m/s². The negative sign means the block
is slowing down.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why the acceleration of a block sliding down a rough plane does
not depend on the mass of the block.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both the driving force and the friction contain the mass as a factor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$ma = mg\sin\theta - \mu mg\cos\theta$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The driving force down the plane is $mg\sin\theta$, and the friction is
$\mu mg\cos\theta$. Both are proportional to the mass. Newton's second law gives

$$ma = mg\sin\theta - \mu mg\cos\theta$$

Divide both sides by $m$. Every term loses the mass:

$$a = g(\sin\theta - \mu\cos\theta)$$

The acceleration depends on $g$, on the angle and on the coefficient, and not
on the mass.

</details>
</details>
</details>
