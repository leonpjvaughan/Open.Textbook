# 5.1 Friction and the normal reaction — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $6 \times 9.8$.
2. Write down $\sin 30^\circ$ and $\cos 30^\circ$ as decimals, each to four
   significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $58.8$. That is the weight of a 6 kg mass, and you will meet it again as a
   normal reaction.
2. $\sin 30^\circ = 0.5$ and $\cos 30^\circ = 0.8660$. On a slope, the weight
   component down the plane uses the sine, and the normal reaction uses the
   cosine.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A box of mass $9$ kg rests on a horizontal floor. Find the normal
reaction. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The box does not move up or down, so the vertical forces balance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg = 9 \times 9.8$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg = 9 \times 9.8 = 88.2\,\text{N}$$

The normal reaction is 88.2 N upwards, equal to the weight. It acts
perpendicular to the floor.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A block of mass $4$ kg rests on a plane inclined at $30^\circ$ to the
horizontal. Find the normal reaction, to four significant figures. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The block does not move perpendicular to the plane, so those forces balance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg\cos 30^\circ = 4 \times 9.8 \times 0.8660$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg\cos 30^\circ = 4 \times 9.8 \times 0.8660 = 33.95\,\text{N}$$

The normal reaction is 33.95 N, to four significant figures. The weight is
$39.2$ N, so the normal reaction is smaller, because the plane is tilted.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A crate of mass $20$ kg rests on a plane inclined at $45^\circ$ to the
horizontal. Find the normal reaction, to five significant figures. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $R = mg\cos\theta$ with $\theta = 45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 20 \times 9.8 \times 0.70711$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg\cos 45^\circ = 20 \times 9.8 \times 0.70711 = 138.59\,\text{N}$$

The weight is $196$ N, so the normal reaction is smaller again. A steeper plane
pushes less of the weight into the surface.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A block of mass $6$ kg rests on a rough plane inclined at $30^\circ$ to
the horizontal. A force of $20$ N acts on it up the plane, parallel to the
surface, and the block stays at rest. Find the friction force. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the component of the weight down the plane.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$mg\sin 30^\circ = 6 \times 9.8 \times 0.5 = 29.4\,\text{N}$$

The block is at rest, so friction makes up the difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The weight component down the plane is $29.4$ N. The 20 N force acts up the
plane. The block is at rest, so the forces along the plane balance:

$$F = 29.4 - 20 = 9.4\,\text{N}$$

Friction is 9.4 N up the plane. It is not at its maximum, because the block is
not sliding.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A block of mass $5$ kg rests on a rough horizontal floor. A force of
$12$ N acts on it vertically downwards. Find the normal reaction. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two forces press the block into the floor: its weight and the extra 12 N.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg + 12 = 49 + 12$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg + 12 = 5 \times 9.8 + 12 = 49 + 12 = 61\,\text{N}$$

The normal reaction is 61 N upwards. Pressing the block into the surface
increases the normal reaction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A packing case of mass $40$ kg rests on a horizontal floor. A rope pulls
it with a force of $50$ N at $30^\circ$ above the horizontal. Find the normal
reaction. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Only the upward part of the rope matters vertically.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$50\sin 30^\circ = 25\,\text{N} \text{ upwards}$$

The normal reaction and this part together balance the weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The upward part of the rope is $50\sin 30^\circ = 25$ N. The case does not move
vertically, so

$$R = mg - 25 = 40 \times 9.8 - 25 = 392 - 25 = 367\,\text{N}$$

The normal reaction is 367 N. It is less than the weight, because the rope
carries part of it. A smaller normal reaction means a smaller maximum friction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A block sits at rest on a rough slope and does not slide. Explain why the
friction force is smaller than its maximum value here.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Friction is a response to the other forces along the surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Friction grows only until the forces along the plane balance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The block is at rest, so the forces along the plane must balance. Friction
takes exactly the value needed to balance the component of the weight down the
plane. That value is smaller than the maximum the surface can provide.

Friction would only reach its maximum if the block were on the point of
sliding. Because the slope is gentle enough, the block never needs that much
grip. The extra grip is there, but it is not used.

</details>
</details>
</details>
