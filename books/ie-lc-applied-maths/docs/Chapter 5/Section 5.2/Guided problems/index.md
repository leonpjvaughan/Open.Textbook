# 5.2 The coefficient of friction — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $0.3 \times 147$.
2. Write down $\tan 45^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $44.1$. This is a friction force: a coefficient of $0.3$ on a normal
   reaction of 147 N.
2. $\tan 45^\circ = 1$. At $45^\circ$ a block is on the point of sliding when
   the coefficient is 1, so friction equals the normal reaction.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A block of mass $25$ kg on a rough horizontal floor just begins to slide
when pushed with a horizontal force of $73.5$ N. Find the coefficient of
friction. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the normal reaction first, then use $\mu = \dfrac{F}{R}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = mg = 25 \times 9.8 = 245\,\text{N}$$

$$\mu = \frac{73.5}{245}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg = 25 \times 9.8 = 245\,\text{N}$$

At the point of sliding, $F = \mu R$, so

$$\mu = \frac{F}{R} = \frac{73.5}{245} = 0.3$$

The coefficient is 0.3. It has no unit, because it is a force divided by a
force.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A crate of mass $8$ kg rests on a rough horizontal floor. The coefficient
of friction is $0.5$. Find the greatest friction force the floor can provide.
Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The greatest friction is the limiting friction, $F = \mu R$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 8 \times 9.8 = 78.4\,\text{N}$$

$$F = 0.5 \times 78.4$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg = 8 \times 9.8 = 78.4\,\text{N}$$

$$F = \mu R = 0.5 \times 78.4 = 39.2\,\text{N}$$

The greatest friction is 39.2 N. A push smaller than this leaves the crate at
rest, with friction matching the push.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A block rests on a rough plane inclined at $45^\circ$ and is on the point
of sliding. Find the coefficient of friction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the point of sliding, $\mu = \tan\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\mu = \tan 45^\circ$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\mu = \tan 45^\circ = 1$$

The coefficient is 1. At this angle, the limiting friction equals the normal
reaction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A block rests on a rough plane inclined at $30^\circ$ and is on the point
of sliding. Find the coefficient of friction, to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle at which sliding starts gives the coefficient directly.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\mu = \tan 30^\circ = 0.5774$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At the point of sliding, $mg\sin 30^\circ = \mu mg\cos 30^\circ$. The mass and
$g$ cancel, so

$$\mu = \tan 30^\circ = 0.577$$

to three significant figures. The mass of the block does not affect the
answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A crate of mass $12$ kg rests on a rough horizontal floor. The
coefficient of friction is $0.35$. Find the greatest friction force. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $F = \mu R$ with $R = mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 12 \times 9.8 = 117.6\,\text{N}$$

$$F = 0.35 \times 117.6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg = 12 \times 9.8 = 117.6\,\text{N}$$

$$F = \mu R = 0.35 \times 117.6 = 41.16\,\text{N}$$

The greatest friction is 41.16 N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A block of mass $10$ kg slides down a rough plane inclined at
$30^\circ$. The coefficient of friction is $0.3$. Find the friction force, to
four significant figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The normal reaction on a slope is $mg\cos\theta$, not $mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$R = 10 \times 9.8 \times 0.8660 = 84.87\,\text{N}$$

$$F = 0.3 \times 84.87$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$R = mg\cos 30^\circ = 10 \times 9.8 \times 0.8660 = 84.87\,\text{N}$$

The block is sliding, so friction is limiting:

$$F = \mu R = 0.3 \times 84.87 = 25.46\,\text{N}$$

Friction acts up the plane, against the sliding.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why the coefficient of friction has no unit, and say what it
depends on.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the two quantities in $\mu = \dfrac{F}{R}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both $F$ and $R$ are forces, measured in newtons.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The coefficient is $\mu = \dfrac{F}{R}$. The friction force $F$ is measured in
newtons, and the normal reaction $R$ is measured in newtons. Dividing one by
the other cancels the unit, so $\mu$ is a pure number.

The coefficient depends on the two surfaces in contact, and on how rough and
how dry they are. It does not depend on the mass of the object or on the area
of contact, in this model.

</details>
</details>
</details>
