## Practise with help

**1.** An object of mass $0.4$ kg falls with $D = kv$, where $k = 0.2$ kg/s.
Find its terminal velocity. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the terminal speed the drag balances the weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$mg = kv_T$$

$$v_T = \frac{0.4 \times 9.8}{0.2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_T = \frac{mg}{k} = \frac{0.4 \times 9.8}{0.2} = \frac{3.92}{0.2} =
19.6\,\text{m/s}$$

The terminal velocity is 19.6 m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A skydiver of mass $50$ kg falls with $D = kv^2$, where $k = 0.5$ kg/m.
Find the terminal velocity, to four significant figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v_T = \sqrt{\dfrac{mg}{k}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_T = \sqrt{\frac{50 \times 9.8}{0.5}} = \sqrt{980}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_T = \sqrt{\frac{mg}{k}} = \sqrt{\frac{50 \times 9.8}{0.5}} = \sqrt{980} =
31.30\,\text{m/s}$$

The terminal velocity is 31.30 m/s, to four significant figures.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** An object of mass $60$ kg falls with $D = kv^2$, where $k = 0.15$ kg/m.
Find the terminal velocity, to four significant figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute into $v_T = \sqrt{\dfrac{mg}{k}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_T = \sqrt{\frac{60 \times 9.8}{0.15}} = \sqrt{3920}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_T = \sqrt{\frac{60 \times 9.8}{0.15}} = \sqrt{\frac{588}{0.15}} =
\sqrt{3920} = 62.61\,\text{m/s}$$

The terminal velocity is 62.61 m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A parachutist of mass $70$ kg reaches a terminal velocity of $5$ m/s. The
drag is $D = kv^2$. Find $k$. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $kv_T^2 = mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$k = \frac{mg}{v_T^2} = \frac{70 \times 9.8}{25}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$k = \frac{mg}{v_T^2} = \frac{70 \times 9.8}{25} = \frac{686}{25} =
27.44\,\text{kg/m}$$

A large $k$ means a lot of drag at a given speed, which is what the parachute
provides. The unit of $k$ is kg/m, as the drag law requires.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A block of mass $10$ kg slides down a smooth plane inclined at
$30^\circ$. The drag is $D = kv^2$ with $k = 0.5$ kg/m. Find the terminal speed,
to three significant figures. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The plane is smooth, so there is no friction. The driving force is
$mg\sin 30^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$mg\sin 30^\circ = 10 \times 9.8 \times 0.5 = 49\,\text{N}$$

$$0.5v_T^2 = 49$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The plane is smooth, so the only force along it is the weight component:

$$mg\sin 30^\circ = 10 \times 9.8 \times 0.5 = 49\,\text{N}$$

At the terminal speed the drag balances this:

$$v_T = \sqrt{\frac{49}{0.5}} = \sqrt{98} = 9.90\,\text{m/s}$$

The terminal speed is 9.90 m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** An object of mass $80$ kg falls with $D = kv^2$ and $k = 0.2$ kg/m.
Find the terminal velocity, to four significant figures, and the drag force at
that speed. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s².

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the terminal speed, the drag equals the weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v_T = \sqrt{\frac{80 \times 9.8}{0.2}} = \sqrt{3920}$$

The drag at that speed is $mg$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v_T = \sqrt{\frac{mg}{k}} = \sqrt{\frac{80 \times 9.8}{0.2}} = \sqrt{3920} =
62.61\,\text{m/s}$$

At the terminal speed the drag balances the weight, so

$$D = mg = 80 \times 9.8 = 784\,\text{N}$$

The terminal velocity is 62.61 m/s, and the drag there is 784 N.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain what is meant by terminal velocity, and why the acceleration is
zero when an object reaches it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the two forces on a falling object as the speed grows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The drag grows until it equals the weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Terminal velocity is the constant speed an object reaches when the drag force
upwards has grown until it equals the weight downwards. The resultant force is
then zero.

Newton's second law gives $F = ma$. With $F = 0$, the acceleration is zero. The
object keeps moving at a steady speed, because there is no reason for it to
speed up or slow down. The forces have not disappeared; they balance.

</details>
</details>
</details>
