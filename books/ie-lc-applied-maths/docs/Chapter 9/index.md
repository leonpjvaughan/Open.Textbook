# Chapter 9: The modelling project

The modelling project is worth 20% of your final mark. You choose a real
situation, build a mathematical model of it, test the model against
measurements, and report what you found. This chapter is your guide through
every stage of that work.

## The chapter problem

**Your own model.** This is the one chapter where the problem is yours. You will
choose a real situation, build a mathematical model of it, test it against data,
and report honestly on where it fails.

Before you decide anything, write down three things and keep them.

- One situation in your own life where a number would help you decide.
- What you would measure, and with what instrument.
- A first guess at the answer, before any mathematics.

The three notes are the start of your project. You will add to them in every
section and return to them in the Review.

Now the questions you will be able to answer by the end of the chapter.

- What makes a problem suitable for a project?
- How do you write a question with a unit?
- How do you list the assumptions and say what each one leaves out?
- How do you test a model against data?
- How do you improve a model without fooling yourself?
- How do you report the result so another person can check it?
- What are the limits of any model, including yours?

Your first guess is not marked. It is there so that you can compare it with the
finished model, and the difference between them is often the most useful thing
you learn.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- read the SEC brief for its context, its task words and the data it allows;
- turn a broad context into a precise question with a unit;
- test a candidate problem for measurability, available data and size;
- build a model from letters, units and relationships;
- state each assumption and say what it leaves out;
- collect data and compare it with the model's prediction;
- read the sign and the size of a residual;
- change one assumption at a time and measure what the change did;
- decide when a model is good enough to stop refining;
- write a report with its six parts, in the voice of an applied mathematician;
- evaluate a model, including your own, and say where it fails.

## Where it is used

The modelling project, and every real problem after school. A hospital plans a
ward with a model of arrivals. A delivery company plans routes with a model of
the road network. An engineer tests a design with a model before anything is
built. Every one of those models leaves something out, and every one of them is
reported so that someone else can check it.

## Your turn to connect it

What situation in your own life would a model help you decide about?

A bus that is often late, the queue at a shop, the battery in your phone, the
water in a tank, the price of something you buy. Choose one and write down the
question you would like answered. Keep it with the three notes above. By the end
of the chapter you will be able to model it and report it.
