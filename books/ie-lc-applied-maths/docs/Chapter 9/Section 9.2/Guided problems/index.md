# 9.2 Building the model and stating assumptions — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Choose a letter for the speed of a car and write its unit beside it.
2. Check the units of the relationship $d = vt$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. For example, $v$ is the speed in metres each second, written m/s. Any letter
   works, as long as its unit is written down.
2. On the right, $v$ has unit m/s and $t$ has unit s, so $vt$ has unit m. The
   left side is a distance in metres. Both sides match, so the relationship
   balances.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A phone battery is at 100% when it is unplugged. Two hours later it is at
70%. Find the rate at which the charge falls, in percent each hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rate is the fall divided by the time it took.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The charge fell by $100 - 70 = 30$ percent in 2 hours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{rate} = \frac{100 - 70}{2} = \frac{30}{2} = 15\ \text{percent each hour}$$

The model assumes the charge falls at this steady rate. That is the first
assumption, and it leaves out the way a real battery falls faster when it is
running a heavy app.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Using the rate of 15 percent each hour, find the time for the charge to
fall from 100% to 0%, in hours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the total fall by the rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$100 \div 15$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{100}{15} = \frac{20}{3} \approx 6.67\ \text{hours}$$

The model predicts about 6.67 hours, or 6 hours 40 minutes. The answer is
written as an exact fraction first, so the rounding happens once, at the end.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The phone model uses $t$ for the time in hours and $c$ for the charge in
percent. Write the relationship for the charge after $t$ hours, and state the
starting charge.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The charge starts at 100 and falls by 15 each hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$c = 100 - 15t$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = 100 - 15t$$

The starting charge is the value at $t = 0$, which is $c = 100$. The relationship
claims that the charge falls at a steady 15 percent each hour. This is a linear
model, so it draws a straight line on a graph of charge against time.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The relationship for a falling object is $v = u + at$, where $u$ is the
starting speed and $a$ is the acceleration. Check that the units balance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the unit of each letter, then work out the unit of $at$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$u$ has unit m/s and $a$ has unit m/s². So $at$ has unit
$\text{m/s}^2 \times \text{s} = \text{m/s}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Every term on the right has unit m/s, and $v$ on the left is a speed in m/s.
Both sides match, so the relationship balances. A relationship whose sides do not
match cannot be right, whatever the numbers say.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A model of a shower uses $V$ for the volume in litres, $r$ for the flow
rate in litres each minute and $t$ for the time in minutes. Write the
relationship, and state what the model assumes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The volume is the rate multiplied by the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$V = rt$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$V = rt$$

The model assumes the flow rate stays the same for the whole shower. That
assumption leaves out a shower that is turned down halfway through, or a shower
whose pressure changes when another tap is opened.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student lists the assumption "the temperature of the room is steady".
Write the second half of the assumption, which says what it leaves out.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What would change the room temperature during the measurement?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Think about a door, a window or a heater.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

"The temperature of the room is steady, so a room that warms up when the heating
comes on is not modelled." The second half names the real situation the
assumption leaves out. A reader can then decide whether the assumption is safe
for their own room.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 3

**7.** A cyclist starts at 4 m/s and accelerates at 0.5 m/s² for 6 s. Find the
final speed, and state the unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v = u + at$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$v = 4 + 0.5 \times 6$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = 4 + 3 = 7\ \text{m/s}$$

The final speed is 7 m/s. This is the same relationship you checked in question
4, and it is a model: it assumes the acceleration is steady for the whole 6 s.

</details>
</details>
</details>
