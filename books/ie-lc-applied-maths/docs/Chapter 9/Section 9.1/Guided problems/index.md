# 9.1 Reading the brief and choosing a problem — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Name the five stages of the modelling cycle, in order.
2. Write the question "the bus is often late" as a precise question with a unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. Formulate, translate, compute, evaluate, refine. Each stage turns the work
   into something the next stage can use.
2. For example: "How many minutes late is the 08:15 bus on a school day?" The
   quantity is a time, the unit is minutes, and the moment is 08:15 on a school
   day.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A student writes the question "Is the canteen queue long?" Explain why
this question cannot be marked, and rewrite it with a unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A marker needs a number, and a number needs a unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The word *long* has no number attached to it. Name the quantity you want and the
unit you will measure it in.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Two readers would disagree about what counts as *long*, so nobody can say
whether the answer is right. A markable version is "How many minutes does a
student wait in the canteen queue at 12:30?" The quantity is a time, the unit is
minutes, and the moment is 12:30.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A project plans to take a temperature reading every 2 minutes for
30 minutes. How many readings will the student record if the first reading is
taken at the start?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Count the readings at the start of each interval, and include the reading at
time zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

There are $30 \div 2 = 15$ intervals, and each interval has one reading at its
start.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$15 + 1 = 16$$

The student records 16 readings. The extra one is the reading at the start,
before any time has passed. Forgetting it is a common error, and it leaves the
first value of the model without a measurement.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A student has four weeks for the project and can work three hours each
week. How many hours are available in total?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the weeks by the hours in each week.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$4 \times 3$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$4 \times 3 = 12$$

Twelve hours. A project that needs 40 hours of measurement fails the manageable
test, and it is better to discover that now than in the last week.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A candidate problem is "How much water does the school use in a year?"
The water meter is read once a month. Test this problem against the three tests,
and say which test it fails.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three tests are measurable, data available and manageable.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The question is measurable, because the answer is a volume in litres or cubic
metres. Check whether the data supports a year.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The question is measurable, because the answer has a unit. It fails the data
test. One reading each month gives 12 numbers for the year, which is enough for
a rough total but not enough to see how the use changes. It also fails the
manageable test for a short project, because a year is longer than the time
available. Narrow it to a month, or to one building, so the data can be
collected.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Narrow the broad topic "sport and scoring" into a question with a
quantity, a unit and a place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Choose one sport, one player or team, and one quantity that can be counted.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For example, count the shots a basketball player takes before scoring.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A narrowed question is "How many shots does one basketball player take, on
average, before scoring a basket at training?" The quantity is a count of shots,
the unit is shots, and the place is the training session. Another student might
choose free throws, or goals in hockey. Any of them can be measured.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student says a project can be measured quickly because the numbers are
already on a website. Explain what else the student must check before using
them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A published number still has to be the right number, from a source you can name.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Check the units, the date, and whether the website explains how the numbers were
produced.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The student must check that the numbers measure the quantity in the question,
that the units match, and that the source can be named. A published average may
cover a different year, a different place or a different definition. If the
source cannot be named, the data cannot be checked, and the project fails the
data test.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** A queue holds 60 students, students join at 4 each minute, and the till
serves 12 students each minute. Find the clear time, in minutes. Then say which
of the three tests this question passes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The clear time is the queue divided by the net rate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$12 - 4 = 8\ \text{students each minute}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{Q}{s - a} = \frac{60}{12 - 4} = \frac{60}{8} = 7.5$$

The clear time is 7.5 minutes. The question is measurable, because the answer is
a time in minutes. The data is available, because the three rates can be
counted. It is manageable, because the model is one division. This is the kind
of question Chapter 1 asked, and it is the kind your project should ask.

</details>
</details>
</details>
