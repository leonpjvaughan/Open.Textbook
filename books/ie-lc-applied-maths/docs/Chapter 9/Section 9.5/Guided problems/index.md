# 9.5 Communicating the result — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Name the first three parts of a modelling report.
2. Round 63.333 to 3 significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The question, the assumptions and the model. The solution, the evaluation and
   the conclusion follow.
2. 63.3. The fourth figure is a 3, so the third figure stays.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Three readings are 62, 65 and 63. Find their mean, correct to 3
significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the readings, divide by three, then round once.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$\frac{62 + 65 + 63}{3} = \frac{190}{3}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{190}{3} = 63.333\ldots \approx 63.3$$

The mean is 63.3 to 3 significant figures. Rounding once at the end keeps the
value as accurate as the readings allow.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A model predicts a clear time of 7.916666 minutes. Explain why reporting
that number is a mistake, and give a better way to report it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about how precisely the arrival and service rates were measured.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The rates were counted, not measured to six decimal places, so the answer cannot
be that precise.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The number claims a precision the data does not have. The rates were counted in
whole students each minute, so the answer is only known to the nearest minute or
so. A better report gives the answer to 2 significant figures, about 7.9
minutes, or as a range such as 7 to 9 minutes.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A table has a column headed "Time" with the entries 0, 2, 4, 6. Explain
what is missing, and write the header again.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The reader must know the unit of every entry.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the quantity and its unit in the header.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The unit is missing. The header should read "Time / min", so every entry in the
column is a time in minutes. Putting the unit in the header is shorter than
writing "min" after each number, and it cannot be forgotten on one row.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A model gives a clear time of 8 minutes, but the arrival rate is only
known to lie between two values, which give 7 minutes and 10 minutes. Report the
clear time as a range, and explain why the range is better than the single
number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the two times from the two possible rates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The answer lies between 7 minutes and 10 minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The clear time is between 7 and 10 minutes. The range is better than the single
number because it tells the reader how much the answer depends on the arrival
rate. The single number 8 minutes hides that dependence, and a reader might
treat it as exact.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A car travels at 10 m/s. Convert this to kilometres each hour, so the
report can use a familiar unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 1000 metres in a kilometre and 3600 seconds in an hour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$10 \times \frac{3600}{1000}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$10 \times 3.6 = 36\ \text{km/h}$$

The speed is 36 km/h. Converting to a familiar unit helps the reader picture the
answer, and the conversion does not change the model.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student's conclusion reads "The clear time is 8 minutes." Explain what
the conclusion is missing, and add the two missing parts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The conclusion needs three jobs done.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the assumption the answer depends on most, and state one limit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The conclusion gives the answer but not its main assumption or its limit. A
fuller version is: "The clear time is about 8 minutes on a normal lunchtime. The
answer depends most on the arrival rate, so a heavier rush would make it longer.
The model assumes steady rates, so it cannot say how long one particular day
takes."

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**7.** A report gives a wait of 3.5 minutes but lists no assumptions. Write one
sentence that explains why the report is incomplete.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A reader cannot check the answer or apply it without the assumptions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The assumptions say what the model treated as true.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The report is incomplete because a reader cannot check the answer, or tell
whether it applies to their own canteen, without knowing what the model treated
as true. Chapter 1 asked the same question, and the answer has not changed.

</details>
