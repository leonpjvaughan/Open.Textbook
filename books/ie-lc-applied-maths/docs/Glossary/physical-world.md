# Glossary — Modelling the Physical World

**Acceleration** — the rate of change of velocity.

**Amplitude** — the greatest distance a body reaches from its equilibrium
position.

**Angular velocity** — the rate at which an angle changes, measured in radians
per second.

**Antiderivative** — a function whose derivative is the given function.

**Average velocity** — total displacement divided by the time taken.

**Banked track** — a track tilted so that the normal reaction helps supply the
centripetal force.

**Centripetal acceleration** — the acceleration of a body moving in a circle,
directed towards the centre.

**Centripetal force** — the resultant force that keeps a body moving in a circle.

**Chain rule** — the rule for differentiating a function of a function.

**Coefficient of friction** — the constant $\mu$ that links the limiting friction
to the normal reaction.

**Coefficient of restitution** — the ratio of the relative speed after a
collision to the relative speed before it.

**Complementary angles** — two angles that add to $90^\circ$.

**Conical pendulum** — a body moving in a horizontal circle on the end of a
string that sweeps out a cone.

**Constant acceleration** — acceleration that does not change during the motion.

**Deceleration** — acceleration that reduces the speed.

**Definite integral** — an integral worked out between two limits, which gives a
number.

**Derivative** — the rate of change of a function, or the gradient of its graph.

**Design speed** — the speed at which a banked track needs no friction to hold a
vehicle on the curve.

**Displacement** — the distance moved in a stated direction; a vector.

**Displacement–time graph** — a graph of displacement against time.

**Distance** — the total length of the path travelled; a scalar.

**Drag** — a resistive force that opposes motion through a fluid.

**Elastic collision** — a collision in which kinetic energy is conserved.

**Elastic limit** — the point beyond which a stretched string does not return to
its natural length.

**Equilibrium** — the state of a body when the resultant force on it is zero.

**Equilibrium position** — the position where a body would rest with no resultant
force.

**Extension** — the increase in length of a spring or string from its natural
length.

**Force** — a push or a pull; a vector measured in newtons.

**Free-body diagram** — a diagram showing one body alone, with an arrow for every
force acting on it.

**Frequency** — the number of complete oscillations or revolutions each second.

**Friction** — a contact force that opposes sliding between two surfaces.

**Fundamental theorem of calculus** — the result that links integration to the
antiderivative.

**Gradient** — the change in the vertical coordinate divided by the change in the
horizontal coordinate.

**Hooke's law** — the extension of a spring is proportional to the force applied,
up to the elastic limit.

**Impulse** — the product of a force and the time for which it acts.

**Impulse–momentum theorem** — the result that the impulse on a body equals its
change in momentum.

**Inclined plane** — a sloping surface on which a body rests or moves.

**Inelastic collision** — a collision in which kinetic energy is not conserved.

**Instantaneous velocity** — the velocity of a body at one particular moment.

**Kinetic energy** — the energy a body has because of its motion.

**Limiting friction** — the greatest friction a surface can provide before
sliding starts.

**Linear speed** — the speed of a body moving along a circle, measured along the
path.

**Magnitude** — the size of a vector, without its direction.

**Mass** — the quantity of matter in a body, measured in kilograms.

**Modulus of elasticity** — a constant that measures how stiff an elastic string
is.

**Momentum** — the product of a body's mass and velocity.

**Natural length** — the length of a spring or string when no force acts on it.

**Newton's laws** — the three laws that link force, mass and motion.

**Normal reaction** — the force a surface exerts perpendicular to itself on a
body resting on it.

**Perfectly elastic** — having a coefficient of restitution of $1$.

**Perfectly inelastic** — having a coefficient of restitution of $0$.

**Period** — the time for one complete oscillation or revolution.

**Projectile** — a body moving under gravity alone after being launched.

**Pulley** — a wheel over which a string passes, used to change the direction of
a force.

**Range** — the horizontal distance a projectile travels before returning to its
starting height.

**Resolving** — splitting a force into two components at right angles.

**Resultant force** — the single force that has the same effect as all the forces
acting together.

**Scalar** — a quantity that has a size but no direction.

**Simple harmonic motion** — motion where the acceleration is proportional to the
displacement and directed towards the equilibrium position.

**Smooth** — having no friction.

**Speed** — the rate of change of distance, without a direction.

**Stiffness** — another name for the spring constant of a spring.

**Tension** — the pulling force in a string or rod.

**Terminal velocity** — the constant speed reached when the drag equals the
driving force.

**Thrust** — a push force, often from an engine or a rod.

**Time of flight** — the time a projectile spends in the air.

**Vector** — a quantity that has both a size and a direction.

**Velocity** — the rate of change of displacement; a vector.

**Velocity–time graph** — a graph of velocity against time.

**Weight** — the force of gravity on a body, equal to its mass times the
gravitational field strength.
