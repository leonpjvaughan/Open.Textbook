# 2.9 Graphs, the modulus function, asymptotes and intersections — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. For a modulus equation, write the two cases
down before you solve either one, and test each answer against its case.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $|-4|$ and $|7|$.
2. Factorise $x^2 + x - 6$.
3. Solve $x^2 + x - 6 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $|-4| = 4$ and $|7| = 7$.
2. $x^2 + x - 6 = (x + 3)(x - 2)$.
3. From the factorisation, $x = -3$ or $x = 2$.

If question 1 was hard, revisit the meaning of the modulus: it is a distance from
zero, so it is never negative.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $|x + 5| = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The expression inside the modulus is either $3$ or $-3$. Write two equations.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Case 1: $x + 5 = 3$. Case 2: $x + 5 = -3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Case 1:

$$
x + 5 = 3, \qquad x = -2.
$$

Case 2:

$$
x + 5 = -3, \qquad x = -8.
$$

Both values are solutions, so $x = -2$ or $x = -8$.
</details>

**2.** Solve $|4x - 3| = 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the two cases, then solve each linear equation.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4x - 3 = 9$ gives one value, and $4x - 3 = -9$ gives the other.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Case 1:

$$
4x - 3 = 9, \qquad 4x = 12, \qquad x = 3.
$$

Case 2:

$$
4x - 3 = -9, \qquad 4x = -6, \qquad x = -\frac{3}{2}.
$$

Both values satisfy the equation.
</details>

**3.** Solve $|x - 2| \leq 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The inequality says the distance from $x$ to $2$ is at most $4$. Write it as a
double inequality.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$-4 \leq x - 2 \leq 4$. Add $2$ to every part.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
-4 \leq x - 2 \leq 4.
$$

Add $2$ to each part:

$$
-2 \leq x \leq 6.
$$

The solution set is $x \in [-2, 6]$.
</details>

**4.** Solve $|2x + 1| > 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A greater-than modulus splits into two separate inequalities, joined by "or".
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2x + 1 > 5$ or $2x + 1 < -5$. Solve each.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Split into two cases:

$$
2x + 1 > 5 \quad \text{or} \quad 2x + 1 < -5.
$$

First case: $2x > 4$, so $x > 2$. Second case: $2x < -6$, so $x < -3$. Therefore

$$
x < -3 \quad \text{or} \quad x > 2,
$$

that is $x \in (-\infty, -3) \cup (2, \infty)$.
</details>

**5.** Find the points where $y = x^2 - 1$ meets $y = 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set the two expressions for $y$ equal: $x^2 - 1 = 3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Solve $x^2 = 4$ for both values of $x$, then find each $y$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Set the expressions equal:

$$
x^2 - 1 = 3, \qquad x^2 = 4, \qquad x = 2 \text{ or } x = -2.
$$

The line is $y = 3$, so both points have $y = 3$. The graphs meet at $(2, 3)$ and
$(-2, 3)$.
</details>

**6.** The curve $y = \dfrac{2}{x}$ has two asymptotes. Describe both, and work
out where the curve meets the line $y = 1$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The vertical asymptote is where the denominator is zero. For the horizontal
asymptote, ask what happens to $\frac{2}{x}$ as $x$ grows large.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the meeting point, solve $\frac{2}{x} = 1$ by multiplying both sides by $x$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The vertical asymptote is $x = 0$, where the curve is undefined. As
$x \to \infty$, the fraction $\frac{2}{x} \to 0$, so the horizontal asymptote is
$y = 0$.

For the meeting point:

$$
\frac{2}{x} = 1, \qquad 2 = x.
$$

The curve meets the line $y = 1$ at $(2, 1)$.
</details>
