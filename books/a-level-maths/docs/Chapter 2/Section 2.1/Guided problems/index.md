# 2.1 Laws of indices for rational exponents — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Reveal a hint only if you are stuck, the next
step to see the first move, and the full working only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $2^5$ and $3^3$.
2. Work out $\sqrt{64}$ and $\sqrt[3]{64}$.
3. Write $\dfrac{1}{4}$ as a power of $2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $2^5 = 32$ and $3^3 = 27$.
2. $\sqrt{64} = 8$, and $\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$ because $4^3 = 64$.
3. $\dfrac{1}{4} = \dfrac{1}{2^2} = 2^{-2}$.

If question 3 was hard, revisit the negative-index law before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Work out $64^{\frac{1}{3}}$ and $64^{\frac{2}{3}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The denominator $3$ names the cube root. Start with the cube root of $64$, then
use the numerator.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $64 = 4^3$, so that $\sqrt[3]{64} = 4$. Then raise $4$ to the numerator.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The cube root of $64$ is $4$, because $4^3 = 64$.

$$
64^{\frac{1}{3}} = 4.
$$

For the second power, raise that root to the power $2$:

$$
64^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left(64^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)^2 = 4^2 = 16.
$$
</details>

**2.** Work out $100^{-\frac{3}{2}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The negative sign first turns the expression upside down. The denominator $2$
then names the square root.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $100^{-\frac{3}{2}} = \dfrac{1}{100^{\frac{3}{2}}}$ and work out
$100^{\frac{3}{2}}$ on its own.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The negative exponent gives a reciprocal:

$$
100^{-\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{1}{100^{\frac{3}{2}}}.
$$

Now $100^{\frac{3}{2}} = \left(\sqrt{100}\right)^3 = 10^3 = 1000$, so

$$
\frac{1}{100^{\frac{3}{2}}} = \frac{1}{1000}.
$$
</details>

**3.** Simplify $\dfrac{x^{\frac{7}{2}}}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}}$, where $x > 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is division with the same base, so subtract the exponents.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract the fraction: $\frac{7}{2} - \frac{3}{2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Subtract the exponents:

$$
\frac{x^{\frac{7}{2}}}{x^{\frac{3}{2}}} = x^{\frac{7}{2} - \frac{3}{2}} = x^{\frac{4}{2}} = x^{2}.
$$
</details>

**4.** Simplify $\left(27x^3\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}$, where $x > 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The power $\frac{2}{3}$ applies to the number $27$ and to the letter $x^3$
separately.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $27^{\frac{2}{3}}$ first, then use the power-of-a-power law on
$\left(x^3\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Split the bracket:

$$
\left(27x^3\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = 27^{\frac{2}{3}} \times \left(x^3\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}.
$$

The cube root of $27$ is $3$, so $27^{\frac{2}{3}} = 3^2 = 9$. The power of a
power multiplies the exponents:

$$
\left(x^3\right)^{\frac{2}{3}} = x^{3 \times \frac{2}{3}} = x^{2}.
$$

So the expression simplifies to $9x^2$.
</details>

**5.** Solve $x^{\frac{2}{3}} = 16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Raise both sides to the reciprocal power $\frac{3}{2}$. Then think about a
negative base.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $16^{\frac{3}{2}}$ by taking the square root of $16$ and cubing it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Raise both sides to the power $\frac{3}{2}$:

$$
x = 16^{\frac{3}{2}} = \left(\sqrt{16}\right)^3 = 4^3 = 64.
$$

The numerator $2$ is even, so a negative base also works:

$$
(-64)^{\frac{2}{3}} = \left(\sqrt[3]{-64}\right)^2 = (-4)^2 = 16.
$$

So $x = 64$ or $x = -64$. Both values check in the original equation.
</details>

**6.** The side length $s$ of a cube with volume $V$ is $s = V^{\frac{1}{3}}$.
Work out the side length of a cube whose volume is $\frac{27}{64}$ m$^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The power $\frac{1}{3}$ is a cube root. Take the cube root of the numerator and
the cube root of the denominator.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\left(\frac{27}{64}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}$ as
$\dfrac{27^{\frac{1}{3}}}{64^{\frac{1}{3}}}$, then find each root.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The cube root of a fraction is the fraction of the cube roots:

$$
s = \left(\frac{27}{64}\right)^{\frac{1}{3}} = \frac{27^{\frac{1}{3}}}{64^{\frac{1}{3}}} = \frac{3}{4}.
$$

The side length is $\frac{3}{4}$ m. Check the answer by cubing it:
$\left(\frac{3}{4}\right)^3 = \frac{27}{64}$, which is the volume we started
with.
</details>
