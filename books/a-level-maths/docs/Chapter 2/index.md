# Chapter 2: Algebra and functions

## The chapter problem: will the lorry fit under the arch?

A new road passes through an arched tunnel. The road across the tunnel is level,
and the middle of the road is at $x = 0$. The height of the arch above the road
is modelled by

$$
y = 4 - \frac{x^2}{4}, \qquad -4 \le x \le 4,
$$

where $x$ is the distance in metres from the middle of the road and $y$ is the
height in metres.

A lorry is $2.5$ m wide and $3.2$ m tall. It drives along the middle of the road.
The drivers must have at least $0.2$ m of clear space above the roof of the
lorry. Does the lorry fit?

By the end of the chapter you will answer that question exactly. You will find
where the arch meets the road, how high the arch rises, and how much room the
lorry really has. You will then change one feature of the design at a time and
see how the answer moves.

> **First contact — no working needed yet.** Put $x = 0$ and $x = 2$ into the
> model and work out the two heights. Sketch the arch roughly on paper. At which
> value of $x$ is the arch highest? Write one sentence saying what you would need
> to work out before you could be sure that the lorry fits.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>If you are not sure where to start</summary>

The lorry is $2.5$ m wide and sits in the middle, so its sides are at
$x = 1.25$ and $x = -1.25$. Substituting $x = 0$ gives the height at the centre
of the road. Substituting $x = 4$ gives the height at the edge of the arch.

For $x = 2$ the model gives $y = 4 - \frac{2^2}{4} = 4 - 1 = 3$. The road is
level because the height does not change as you cross it.
</details>

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- use the laws of indices for fractional and negative exponents;
- simplify surds and rationalise a denominator;
- complete the square, read the vertex of a parabola, and use the discriminant;
- solve quadratic equations, including equations that are quadratic in a
  function of the unknown;
- solve simultaneous equations by elimination and by substitution, including
  one linear and one quadratic equation;
- solve linear and quadratic inequalities and write the solution in set
  notation;
- divide polynomials, use the factor theorem, and factorise a cubic;
- simplify rational expressions and split one into partial fractions;
- sketch graphs including the modulus function and the asymptotes of
  $y = \frac{a}{x}$ and $y = \frac{a}{x^2}$;
- form composite and inverse functions and describe their domains;
- apply the graph transformations $y = af(x)$, $y = f(x) + a$, $y = f(x + a)$
  and $y = f(ax)$;
- build and refine a function model, and say what its limitations are.

## Where it is used

Algebra is the language the rest of the course is written in. Differentiation
needs the index laws for every negative and fractional power. Integration needs
partial fractions. Coordinates need simultaneous equations. Statistics and
mechanics both start by turning a situation into a function and then asking what
that function does. The tasks in this chapter — solve, factorise, complete the
square, choose a method — are the tasks that appear on every later page.
