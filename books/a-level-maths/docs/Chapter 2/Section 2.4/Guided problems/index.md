# 2.4 Solving quadratic equations, including in a function of the unknown — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Before you start, check whether a quick
factorisation is possible; if it is not, reach for completing the square or the
formula.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Factorise $x^2 + 7x + 12$.
2. Work out the value of the discriminant of $x^2 + 2x + 5$.
3. Work out $2^3$ and $2^{-1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $x^2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)$.
2. $b^2 - 4ac = 2^2 - 4 \times 1 \times 5 = 4 - 20 = -16$. It is negative, so
   there are no real roots.
3. $2^3 = 8$ and $2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}$.

If question 1 was hard, revisit the factor pairs of $12$: you need a pair that
adds to $7$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $x^2 - 7x + 10 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look for two numbers that multiply to $10$ and add to $-7$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the equation as $(x - 5)(x - 2) = 0$, then set each factor to zero.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Factorise:

$$
x^2 - 7x + 10 = (x - 5)(x - 2) = 0.
$$

So $x - 5 = 0$ or $x - 2 = 0$, giving $x = 5$ or $x = 2$.
</details>

**2.** Solve $x^2 + 6x + 2 = 0$, giving the answer in surd form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The roots are not whole numbers, so complete the square. Half of $6$ is $3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $x^2 + 6x + 2 = (x + 3)^2 - 7$, set it to zero, and rearrange to
$(x + 3)^2 = 7$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Complete the square:

$$
x^2 + 6x + 2 = (x + 3)^2 - 9 + 2 = (x + 3)^2 - 7.
$$

So $(x + 3)^2 = 7$, and $x + 3 = \pm\sqrt{7}$. Therefore

$$
x = -3 \pm \sqrt{7}.
$$

</details>

**3.** Solve $3x^2 - 5x - 2 = 0$ by the formula.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Identify $a = 3$, $b = -5$ and $c = -2$, then substitute into
$x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the discriminant first: $(-5)^2 - 4 \times 3 \times (-2) = 25 + 24 = 49$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Substitute into the formula:

$$
x = \frac{5 \pm \sqrt{49}}{6} = \frac{5 \pm 7}{6}.
$$

So $x = \frac{12}{6} = 2$ or $x = \frac{-2}{6} = -\frac{1}{3}$. Check by
factorising: $3x^2 - 5x - 2 = (3x + 1)(x - 2)$.
</details>

**4.** Solve $x^4 - 10x^2 + 9 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The expression is a quadratic in $x^2$. Let $t = x^2$ and solve for $t$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

With $t = x^2$ the equation becomes $t^2 - 10t + 9 = 0$, which factorises as
$(t - 1)(t - 9) = 0$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $t = x^2$. Then

$$
t^2 - 10t + 9 = (t - 1)(t - 9) = 0,
$$

so $t = 1$ or $t = 9$. Replace $t$ with $x^2$:

$$
x^2 = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad x^2 = 9.
$$

Taking both signs gives $x = \pm 1$ and $x = \pm 3$, so there are four solutions:
$x = 1$, $x = -1$, $x = 3$ and $x = -3$.
</details>

**5.** Solve $3^{2x} - 10 \times 3^x + 9 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Here $3^{2x} = \left(3^x\right)^2$, so let $t = 3^x$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Solve $t^2 - 10t + 9 = 0$, then replace $t$ with $3^x$ and match both sides to
powers of $3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Let $t = 3^x$. Then

$$
t^2 - 10t + 9 = (t - 1)(t - 9) = 0,
$$

so $t = 1$ or $t = 9$. Replace $t$:

$$
3^x = 1 \quad \text{or} \quad 3^x = 9.
$$

Since $3^0 = 1$ and $3^2 = 9$, the solutions are $x = 0$ and $x = 2$.
</details>

**6.** Solve $\sqrt{2x + 3} = x$, and reject any value that does not satisfy the
original equation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square both sides to remove the root. Squaring can create a value that does not
work, so every answer must be checked.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Squaring gives $2x + 3 = x^2$, so rearrange to $x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0$ and factorise.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Square both sides:

$$
2x + 3 = x^2, \qquad x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0, \qquad (x - 3)(x + 1) = 0.
$$

The two values are $x = 3$ and $x = -1$. Check the original equation. For $x = 3$:
$\sqrt{2 \times 3 + 3} = \sqrt{9} = 3$, which matches the right side. For
$x = -1$: $\sqrt{1} = 1$, but the right side is $-1$, so $x = -1$ fails. The
only solution is $x = 3$.
</details>
