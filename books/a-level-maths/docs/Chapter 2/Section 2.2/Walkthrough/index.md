# 2.2 Surds and rationalising the denominator — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the index laws from 2.1; square numbers and their factors;
expanding brackets; the difference of two squares.

**By the end of this section you can:** simplify a surd; add, subtract and
multiply surds; remove a surd from the denominator of a fraction, including a
denominator of the form $a + \sqrt{b}$; and explain why a simplified surd is an
exact answer.

## 1. What a surd is

A **surd** is a root that cannot be written as an exact fraction. The number
$\sqrt{2}$ is a surd: it is about $1.414$, but no fraction equals it exactly.
Leaving an answer as $\sqrt{2}$ keeps it exact, while $1.414$ is only an
approximation.

Two laws let you move factors in and out of a root:

$$
\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\,\sqrt{b} \qquad \text{and} \qquad \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}.
$$

The first law in reverse is how a surd is simplified. Look for a square factor
inside the root.

**Example 1.** Simplify $\sqrt{50}$.

Write $50$ as $25 \times 2$, and $25$ is a square:

$$
\sqrt{50} = \sqrt{25 \times 2} = \sqrt{25}\,\sqrt{2}.
$$

Now take the square root of $25$:

$$
\sqrt{25}\,\sqrt{2} = 5\sqrt{2}.
$$

The number $5\sqrt{2}$ is the simplest exact form.

## 2. Adding and subtracting surds

Surds behave like algebraic terms. You can add $3x$ and $5x$ because they are
like terms; you can add $3\sqrt{2}$ and $5\sqrt{2}$ for the same reason. Surds
with different numbers under the root are not like terms, but they may simplify
to like terms.

**Example 2.** Simplify $\sqrt{12} + \sqrt{75}$.

Simplify each surd first:

$$
\sqrt{12} = \sqrt{4 \times 3} = 2\sqrt{3},
$$

and

$$
\sqrt{75} = \sqrt{25 \times 3} = 5\sqrt{3}.
$$

Now the two surds are like terms, so add them:

$$
2\sqrt{3} + 5\sqrt{3} = 7\sqrt{3}.
$$

## 3. Multiplying surds

Multiply the numbers inside the roots. A product of two surds often contains a
square factor, so simplify again at the end.

**Example 3.** Work out $\sqrt{8} \times \sqrt{18}$.

Multiply inside the roots:

$$
\sqrt{8} \times \sqrt{18} = \sqrt{8 \times 18} = \sqrt{144}.
$$

Then take the square root:

$$
\sqrt{144} = 12.
$$

## 4. Rationalising the denominator

A fraction is in its simplest form when its denominator contains no surd. To
remove a single surd from the denominator, multiply the top and the bottom by
that surd.

**Example 4.** Write $\dfrac{6}{\sqrt{3}}$ with a whole-number denominator.

Multiply the top and the bottom by $\sqrt{3}$:

$$
\frac{6}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{6 \times \sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}}.
$$

The denominator becomes $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 3$:

$$
\frac{6\sqrt{3}}{3} = 2\sqrt{3}.
$$

When the denominator is $a + \sqrt{b}$, multiply by its **conjugate**
$a - \sqrt{b}$. The product is the difference of two squares, and the surd
disappears.

$$
\left(a + \sqrt{b}\right)\left(a - \sqrt{b}\right) = a^2 - b.
$$

**Example 5.** Write $\dfrac{1}{3 + \sqrt{2}}$ with a rational denominator.

Multiply the top and the bottom by the conjugate $3 - \sqrt{2}$:

$$
\frac{1}{3 + \sqrt{2}} = \frac{3 - \sqrt{2}}{\left(3 + \sqrt{2}\right)\left(3 - \sqrt{2}\right)}.
$$

The denominator is a difference of two squares:

$$
\left(3 + \sqrt{2}\right)\left(3 - \sqrt{2}\right) = 3^2 - 2 = 9 - 2 = 7.
$$

So

$$
\frac{1}{3 + \sqrt{2}} = \frac{3 - \sqrt{2}}{7}.
$$

> **Watch out.** The trap is applying a product law to a sum.
>
> $\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$, but $\sqrt{9} + \sqrt{16} = 3 + 4 = 7$.
> The laws $\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}$ and $\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}$
> hold for a product and a quotient. There is no rule for a sum.
>
> **Re-check:** after simplifying, square your answer, or substitute a decimal
> approximation into both the original and the answer. If the two decimals agree,
> the simplification is sound.

## You should now be able to

- simplify a surd by removing its largest square factor;
- add and subtract surds that are like terms;
- multiply surds and simplify the result;
- rationalise a denominator of the form $\sqrt{a}$ or $a \pm \sqrt{b}$;
- explain why a simplified surd is exact, while a decimal is not.
