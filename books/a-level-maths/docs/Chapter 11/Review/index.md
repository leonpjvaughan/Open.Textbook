# Chapter 11: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The bus company claims a mean journey time of 25 minutes on the 07:30 service. A
passenger group timed 30 journeys. The times, in minutes, are

$$
\begin{aligned}
&12,\ 15,\ 16,\ 17,\ 18,\ 18,\ 19,\ 20,\ 20,\ 21,\ 21,\ 22,\ 22,\ 23,\ 23,\\
&23,\ 24,\ 24,\ 25,\ 25,\ 26,\ 27,\ 28,\ 29,\ 30,\ 32,\ 34,\ 36,\ 41,\ 58.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Step 1. Name the population and the sample

The population is every journey made by that service. The sample is the 30
journeys the group timed. The mean of the sample is an estimate of the mean of
the population, not a measurement of it.

### Step 2. Draw the picture

The times are grouped for a histogram. The class widths are not all equal, so
the bars are drawn with frequency density.

| Class (minutes) | Frequency | Class width | Frequency density |
|---|---|---|---|
| $10$–$20$ | 7 | 10 | 0.7 |
| $20$–$25$ | 11 | 5 | 2.2 |
| $25$–$30$ | 6 | 5 | 1.2 |
| $30$–$40$ | 4 | 10 | 0.4 |
| $40$–$60$ | 2 | 20 | 0.1 |

The frequencies add to 30. The tallest bar is the class $20$–$25$, so the modal
class is $20$ to $25$ minutes. The distribution has a longer tail on the right,
so it is positively skewed.

### Step 3. Find the mean and the standard deviation

The mean is

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{749}{30} = 24.97 \ (2 \text{ d.p.}).
$$

The standard deviation, dividing by $n$, is

$$
\sigma = \sqrt{\frac{\sum (x - \bar{x})^2}{30}} = \frac{\sqrt{69389}}{30}
= 8.78 \ (2 \text{ d.p.}).
$$

The sample mean of 24.97 minutes is very close to the claimed 25 minutes. The
standard deviation of 8.78 minutes is large, so the individual journeys vary a
great deal around that mean.

### Step 4. Look for an outlier

In order, the 30 values give $Q_1 = 19.75$ and $Q_3 = 28.25$, so

$$
\text{IQR} = 28.25 - 19.75 = 8.5.
$$

The fences are

$$
19.75 - 1.5(8.5) = 7
\qquad \text{and} \qquad
28.25 + 1.5(8.5) = 41.
$$

The value 58 is above 41, so 58 is a possible outlier. The group should check the
original record. If the value is genuine, keep it; a journey can really take 58
minutes. If it is a recording error and the true value is known, correct it.

If 58 is removed, the remaining 29 journeys have mean

$$
\frac{691}{29} = 23.83 \ (2 \text{ d.p.})
$$

and standard deviation

$$
\frac{2\sqrt{8585}}{29} = 6.39 \ (2 \text{ d.p.}).
$$

Removing the outlier lowers the mean by about 1.1 minutes and lowers the standard
deviation by about 2.4 minutes. The change must be reported, with the number of
values removed.

### Step 5. Read the scatter diagram

The group also recorded the distance of each journey. On a scatter diagram of
distance against time, the points rise from left to right and lie close to a
straight line, so the correlation is positive and strong. Longer journeys tend
to take longer, which is expected.

The correlation does not prove that distance causes the time. A crowded bus, road
works and the time of day also change the journey time. Distance is one
explanatory variable among several.

### Step 6. Answer the claim

The sample mean of 24.97 minutes supports the claim of 25 minutes. On this
evidence, the group cannot say that the claim is wrong.

At the first contact you predicted how many of the 30 journeys took longer
than 30 minutes. The data give 5 such journeys. Compare that number with your
prediction, and say whether the count changes your view of the service.

The conclusion is limited in four ways. The sample has only 30 journeys. It comes
from one week, so a school holiday or an accident would change it. The sample was
taken by the group at the stop, which is an opportunity sample, so it may miss
passengers who boarded earlier or who gave up waiting. The single value 58
affects the mean and the standard deviation a great deal. A larger random sample
over several weeks would give a more reliable estimate.

## What you can now do

- explain the difference between a population and a sample, and draw an informal
  inference about the population;
- choose or criticise a simple random, systematic or stratified sample;
- read a histogram and use frequency density, remembering that area is
  frequency;
- describe correlation and interpret a line of best fit, including why
  correlation does not imply causation;
- calculate the mean, median, mode, range, interquartile range and standard
  deviation;
- use the mean and the standard deviation to compare two distributions;
- recognise and deal with outliers, missing values and errors;
- use a large data set and write a conclusion with its limitations.
