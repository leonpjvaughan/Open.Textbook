# 11.2 Sampling techniques and their critique — Practise with help

Work each question closed book first. For a criticism, name the part of the
population that the method is most likely to miss.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $20\%$ of $350$.
2. Simplify $\dfrac{480}{800}$.
3. Write the next number in the pattern $7, 27, 47, \ldots$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $0.2 \times 350 = 70$.
2. $\dfrac{480}{800} = \dfrac{3}{5}$ (divide both parts by 160).
3. $67$. The pattern adds $20$ each time, so a systematic sample with interval
   $k = 20$ follows it.

If question 2 was hard, revise equivalent fractions before you start.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A club has 240 members. A simple random sample of 30 is chosen. Find the
probability that a given member is in the sample.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

In a simple random sample, the probability is the sample size divided by the
population size.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\dfrac{30}{240}$ and simplify it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\frac{30}{240} = \frac{1}{8}.
$$

Every member has chance $\dfrac{1}{8}$, because every member of the frame is
equally likely to be chosen.
</details>

**2.** A population of 900 is sampled systematically with sample size 45. Find
the interval, then list the first three members if the start is chosen at
random as 4.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The interval is the population size divided by the sample size.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$k = \dfrac{900}{45} = 20$. Add $20$ twice to the starting value 4.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
k = \frac{900}{45} = 20.
$$

Starting at 4, the first three members are 4, 24 and 44.
</details>

**3.** A school has 600 girls and 400 boys. A stratified sample of 50 is needed.
Find the number of girls and the number of boys in the sample.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The population is $600 + 400 = 1000$. Each group contributes in proportion to
its size.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the girls, work out $\dfrac{600}{1000} \times 50$. Then do the same for the
boys.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
n_{\text{girls}} = \frac{600}{1000} \times 50 = 30,
\qquad
n_{\text{boys}} = \frac{400}{1000} \times 50 = 20.
$$

The two sample sizes add to 50, which is the check that the allocation is
complete.
</details>

**4.** A researcher surveys the first 60 people who leave a gym between 6 pm and
7 pm. Name the sampling method and give one reason the sample may be biased.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The researcher chose the people who were easiest to reach.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Compare the people at the gym in that hour with everyone the researcher wants to
describe.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

This is an opportunity sample. It is biased because it only reaches people who
use that gym, at that time. People who exercise elsewhere, at other times, or not
at all are missing. The sample is large, but size does not remove bias.
</details>

**5.** A population of 3000 is split into strata of sizes 1200, 900 and 900. A
stratified sample of 100 is taken. Find each stratum sample size.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $n_i = \dfrac{N_i}{N} \times n$ for each stratum.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the first stratum, work out $\dfrac{1200}{3000} \times 100 = 40$. Repeat with
900 for the other two.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\frac{1200}{3000} \times 100 = 40,
\qquad
\frac{900}{3000} \times 100 = 30,
\qquad
\frac{900}{3000} \times 100 = 30.
$$

The three parts add to $40 + 30 + 30 = 100$, which matches the total sample size.
</details>

**6.** A researcher wants to know the mean number of books read by adults in a
town. He asks people in a library. Explain why his estimate is likely to be too
high, and suggest one better method.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about who visits a library, and who does not.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Compare library users with the whole population of adults in the town.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

People in a library are more likely to read than adults in general, so the
estimate of the mean is likely to be too high. A better method is a simple random
sample from a complete sampling frame, such as the electoral register, or a
stratified sample with strata that cover the whole town. A random method reaches
readers and non-readers alike.
</details>
