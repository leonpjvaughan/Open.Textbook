# 16.4 Newton's third law, equilibrium and connected particles — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Draw each body on its own before you write any
equation. Give the two bodies the same size of acceleration, and use one symbol
for the tension. Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Solve the pair of equations $2x + y = 11$ and $3x - y = 4$ together.
2. Work out $5 \times 9.8$.
3. A resultant force of $20$ N acts on a mass of $5$ kg. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Adding gives $5x = 15$, so $x = 3$. Then $y = 11 - 6 = 5$.
2. $5 \times 9.8 = 49$.
3. $a = \dfrac{20}{5} = 4$ m s$^{-2}$.

If question 1 was hard, revise solving simultaneous equations in Section 2.5
before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Masses of $2$ kg and $4$ kg hang over a smooth pulley on a light
inextensible string and are released. Find the acceleration and the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The heavier mass moves down and the lighter mass moves up. The difference in the
weights drives both masses.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The driving force is $(4 - 2) \times 9.8 = 19.6$ N and the mass moved is
$2 + 4 = 6$ kg. Then use $T - 2g = 2a$ for the lighter mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{(4 - 2) \times 9.8}{2 + 4} = \frac{19.6}{6} = \frac{49}{15} \approx 3.27 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

For the $2$ kg mass, moving up:

$$
T = 2(9.8 + 3.27) = 26.13 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**2.** A block of mass $3$ kg rests on a smooth horizontal table. It is joined by
a light inextensible string over a smooth pulley at the table edge to a hanging
mass of $2$ kg. Find the acceleration and the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Only the hanging weight drives the system, and the total mass moved is the sum
of the two masses.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The hanging weight is $2 \times 9.8 = 19.6$ N and the total mass is $5$ kg. Then
use $T = 3a$ for the block on the table.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{2 \times 9.8}{3 + 2} = \frac{19.6}{5} = 3.92 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

$$
T = 3a = 3 \times 3.92 = 11.76 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**3.** A book rests on a table. A student says the weight of the book and the
normal reaction on the book are a Newton's third law pair. Explain why the
student is wrong.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A third-law pair acts on two different bodies. Ask which body each force acts
on here.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both the weight and the normal reaction act on the book. Find the real pair: the
force the book exerts on the table, and the force the table exerts on the book.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The weight and the normal reaction both act on the book, so they are forces on
one body, not a third-law pair. They happen to be equal in size because the book
is in equilibrium.

The third-law pair is the contact force between the book and the table. The book
pushes down on the table, and the table pushes up on the book with the same size
of force.
</details>

**4.** Masses of $6$ kg and $4$ kg hang over a smooth pulley on a light
inextensible string and are released. Find the acceleration and the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is the same pattern as question 1, with different masses.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The driving force is $(6 - 4) \times 9.8 = 19.6$ N and the mass moved is
$10$ kg. Then use the lighter mass for the tension.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{(6 - 4) \times 9.8}{6 + 4} = \frac{19.6}{10} = 1.96 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

For the $4$ kg mass, moving up:

$$
T = 4(9.8 + 1.96) = 47.04 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**5.** A block of mass $5$ kg rests on a smooth table and is joined over a
smooth pulley to a hanging mass of $1$ kg. Find the acceleration and the
tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The hanging weight is the only driving force.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The total mass is $6$ kg and the driving force is $9.8$ N. Then use $T = 5a$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{1 \times 9.8}{5 + 1} = \frac{9.8}{6} = \frac{49}{30} \approx 1.63 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

$$
T = 5a = 5 \times 1.633 \approx 8.17 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**6.** A block on a smooth table is joined over a smooth pulley to a hanging
mass of $3$ kg. The acceleration is $1.96$ m s$^{-2}$. Find the mass of the
block.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write $F = ma$ for the whole system, with the block's mass as the unknown.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The hanging weight is $3 \times 9.8 = 29.4$ N, so $29.4 = (m + 3) \times 1.96$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
29.4 = (m + 3) \times 1.96,
$$

so $m + 3 = \dfrac{29.4}{1.96} = 15$, giving $m = 12$ kg.
</details>

**7.** Masses of $7$ kg and $3$ kg hang over a smooth pulley on a light
inextensible string and are released. Find the acceleration and the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Follow the four steps: draw each mass, choose the direction, write two
equations, then solve.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The driving force is $(7 - 3) \times 9.8 = 39.2$ N and the mass moved is
$10$ kg. Then use the lighter mass for the tension.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{(7 - 3) \times 9.8}{7 + 3} = \frac{39.2}{10} = 3.92 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

For the $3$ kg mass, moving up:

$$
T = 3(9.8 + 3.92) = 41.16 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**8.** A particle is in equilibrium under three forces. Explain what this says
about the forces, and what a triangle of forces looks like.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Equilibrium means the resultant force is zero.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

If the three force arrows are placed head to tail, their result is the zero
vector. What shape does that make?

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

In equilibrium the resultant force is zero. Placing the three arrows head to
tail returns to the starting point, so the arrows form a closed triangle. Each
force is balanced by the sum of the other two.
</details>
