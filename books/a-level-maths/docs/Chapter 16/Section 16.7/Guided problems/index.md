# 16.7 Friction and the F <= mu R model — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Find the normal reaction before you find any
limit. Compare the driving force with $\mu R$, then decide whether the body
stays at rest or slides. Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $0.4 \times 419.4$.
2. Find the component of a weight of $441$ N acting down a slope of $18^\circ$.
3. Which is larger, $136.3$ or $167.8$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $0.4 \times 419.4 = 167.76$.
2. $441\sin 18^\circ \approx 136.3$ N.
3. $167.8$ is larger.

If question 2 was hard, revise resolving a weight on a slope in Section 16.5
before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A block of mass $8$ kg rests on a rough horizontal floor. The coefficient
of friction is $0.5$. A horizontal push of $30$ N is applied. Find the friction
force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the normal reaction and the limit $\mu R$ first.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = 8 \times 9.8 = 78.4$ N, so $\mu R = 0.5 \times 78.4 = 39.2$ N. Compare the
push with the limit.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The push of $30$ N is below the limit of $39.2$ N, so the block stays at rest.
Static friction matches the push:

$$
F = 30 \text{ N}
$$

backwards.
</details>

**2.** The same block is pushed with $50$ N instead. Find the friction force and
the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The limit is unchanged at $39.2$ N. Compare the new push with it.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Since $50 > 39.2$, the block slides and friction takes its greatest value.
Subtract the friction from the push, then divide by the mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F = \mu R = 39.2 \text{ N},
$$

$$
a = \frac{50 - 39.2}{8} = \frac{10.8}{8} = 1.35 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$
</details>

**3.** A block of mass $10$ kg is released on a rough slope of $20^\circ$. The
coefficient of friction is $0.6$. Show that the block stays at rest, and find the
friction force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On a slope the normal reaction is $mg\cos\theta$, not $mg$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The weight is $98$ N. The downhill component is $98\sin 20^\circ = 33.52$ N and
the limit is $0.6 \times 98\cos 20^\circ = 55.25$ N.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The downhill component, $33.52$ N, is below the limit of $55.25$ N, so the block
stays at rest. Friction matches the downhill component:

$$
F = 33.52 \text{ N}
$$

up the slope.
</details>

**4.** A block of mass $6$ kg is released on a rough slope of $35^\circ$. The
coefficient of friction is $0.4$. Show that the block slides, and find its
acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the downhill component with the limit. If the component is larger, the
block slides.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The weight is $58.8$ N. Downhill: $58.8\sin 35^\circ = 33.73$ N. Limit:
$0.4 \times 58.8\cos 35^\circ = 19.27$ N. The block slides, so friction is
$19.27$ N.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{33.73 - 19.27}{6} = \frac{14.46}{6} \approx 2.41 \text{ m s}^{-2}
$$

down the slope.
</details>

**5.** A block on a rough slope of $25^\circ$ is in limiting equilibrium. Find
the coefficient of friction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At limiting equilibrium the downhill component equals the greatest friction.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $mg\sin 25^\circ = \mu\,mg\cos 25^\circ$, then divide.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\mu = \tan 25^\circ \approx 0.466.
$$

The mass cancels, so the answer does not depend on the mass.
</details>

**6.** A block of mass $15$ kg rests on a rough horizontal floor. The coefficient
of friction is $0.35$. Find the smallest horizontal force that will start the
block moving.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The block starts to move when the push reaches the limiting friction.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = 15 \times 9.8 = 147$ N, and the limiting friction is $0.35 \times 147$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F_{\min} = \mu R = 0.35 \times 147 = 51.45 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**7.** A block of mass $10$ kg is pulled along a rough horizontal floor by a rope
at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal with a tension of $60$ N. The coefficient of
friction is $0.4$. Find the normal reaction and the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rope has an upward component, so the normal reaction is less than the weight.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = 98 - 60\sin 30^\circ = 68$ N, so the limit is $0.4 \times 68 = 27.2$ N. The
horizontal pull is $60\cos 30^\circ = 51.96$ N, which beats the limit.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = 98 - 30 = 68 \text{ N}, \qquad F = 0.4 \times 68 = 27.2 \text{ N}.
$$

$$
a = \frac{51.96 - 27.2}{10} = \frac{24.76}{10} \approx 2.48 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$
</details>

**8.** A block of mass $20$ kg on a rough horizontal floor is on the point
of moving when a horizontal force of $50$ N is applied. Find the coefficient of
friction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the point of moving, the push equals the limiting friction.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = 20 \times 9.8 = 196$ N and $50 = \mu \times 196$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\mu = \frac{50}{196} \approx 0.255.
$$
</details>
