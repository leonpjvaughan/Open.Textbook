# 16.6 Resultant forces and dynamics in a plane — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Choose one axis along the acceleration and one
perpendicular to it. Resolve in both directions, then use the perpendicular
equation for the normal reaction and the other equation for the acceleration.
Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the horizontal and vertical components of a force of $50$ N acting at
   $40^\circ$ above the horizontal.
2. Add the vectors $(3, 4)$ and $(-1, 2)$.
3. Solve $5a = 12.5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Horizontal $= 50\cos 40^\circ \approx 38.30$ N. Vertical
   $= 50\sin 40^\circ \approx 32.14$ N.
2. $(3 - 1, 4 + 2) = (2, 6)$.
3. $a = 2.5$.

If question 1 was hard, revise resolving forces in Section 16.5 before you
continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A force of $40$ N acts due east and a force of $30$ N acts due north. Find
the size of the resultant and its direction from the east direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two forces are perpendicular, so add them as the sides of a right-angled
triangle.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The size is $\sqrt{40^2 + 30^2}$. The direction uses the tangent of the north
force over the east force.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = \sqrt{40^2 + 30^2} = 50 \text{ N},
$$

$$
\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{30}{40}\right) = 36.87^\circ
$$

north of east.
</details>

**2.** A body of mass $20$ kg is pulled along a horizontal floor by a rope at
$30^\circ$ to the horizontal with a tension of $150$ N. A resistance of $50$ N
opposes the motion. Find the normal reaction and the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Resolve vertically first. The rope has an upward component, so the normal
reaction is less than the weight.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Vertically: $R + 150\sin 30^\circ = 20 \times 9.8$. Horizontally, the resultant
is $150\cos 30^\circ - 50$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = 196 - 75 = 121 \text{ N}.
$$

$$
a = \frac{150\cos 30^\circ - 50}{20} = \frac{129.90 - 50}{20} \approx 4.00 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

</details>

**3.** A block of mass $6$ kg is released on a smooth slope of $25^\circ$. Find
the acceleration and the normal reaction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

On a smooth slope the only force along the slope is the component of the weight.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The weight is $6 \times 9.8 = 58.8$ N. Down the slope the resultant is
$58.8\sin 25^\circ$. Perpendicular to the slope the reaction is
$58.8\cos 25^\circ$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{58.8\sin 25^\circ}{6} = 9.8\sin 25^\circ \approx 4.14 \text{ m s}^{-2},
$$

$$
R = 58.8\cos 25^\circ \approx 53.29 \text{ N}.
$$

</details>

**4.** A block of mass $5$ kg is pulled up a smooth slope of $35^\circ$ by a rope
parallel to the slope with a tension of $60$ N. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Along the slope the tension acts up and the weight component acts down.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The weight component down the slope is $5 \times 9.8\sin 35^\circ = 28.10$ N.
Subtract it from the tension, then divide by the mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{60 - 49\sin 35^\circ}{5} = \frac{60 - 28.10}{5} \approx 6.38 \text{ m s}^{-2}
$$

up the slope.
</details>

**5.** A block of mass $4$ kg rests on a horizontal table. It is joined by a
light inextensible string over a smooth pulley at the table edge to a hanging
mass of $3$ kg. A resistance of $8$ N acts on the block. Find the acceleration
and the tension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The hanging weight drives the system. The resistance opposes the motion, so it
subtracts from the driving force.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The driving force is $3 \times 9.8 = 29.4$ N and the total mass is $7$ kg. Then
use $T - 8 = 4a$ for the block.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{29.4 - 8}{7} = \frac{21.4}{7} \approx 3.06 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

$$
T = 4a + 8 = 12.23 + 8 \approx 20.23 \text{ N}.
$$

</details>

**6.** Forces of $8$ N due east, $6$ N due north and $3$ N due west act at a
point. Find the resultant and its size.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the east and west forces first, since they act along the same line.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The east-west resultant is $8 - 3 = 5$ N east. Now combine $5$ N and $6$ N at
right angles.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The resultant is $(5, 6)$ N. Its size is

$$
\sqrt{5^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{61} \approx 7.81 \text{ N}.
$$

</details>

**7.** Two forces of $200$ N and $150$ N act at a point with an angle of
$60^\circ$ between them. Find the size of the resultant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the cosine rule form for two forces at an angle:
$R^2 = F_1^2 + F_2^2 + 2F_1F_2\cos\theta$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $F_1 = 200$, $F_2 = 150$ and $\cos 60^\circ = 0.5$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R^2 = 200^2 + 150^2 + 2(200)(150)(0.5) = 40000 + 22500 + 30000 = 92500,
$$

$$
R = \sqrt{92500} \approx 304.14 \text{ N}.
$$

</details>

**8.** A block of mass $12$ kg slides down a slope of $20^\circ$. A friction
force of $15$ N acts up the slope. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The friction opposes the sliding, so it acts up the slope and reduces the
downhill resultant.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The weight component down the slope is $12 \times 9.8\sin 20^\circ = 40.22$ N.
Subtract the friction, then divide by the mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{12 \times 9.8\sin 20^\circ - 15}{12} = \frac{40.22 - 15}{12} \approx 2.10 \text{ m s}^{-2}
$$

down the slope.
</details>
