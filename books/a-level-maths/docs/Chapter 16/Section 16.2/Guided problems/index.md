# 16.2 Newton's second law and F = ma — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Choose the direction of motion as positive.
Write down the resultant force along that direction, then use $F = ma$.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $30 - 12$ and $12 - 30$.
2. Solve $5a = 20$.
3. A car speeds up from $10$ m s$^{-1}$ to $22$ m s$^{-1}$ in $4$ s. Find its
   acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $30 - 12 = 18$ and $12 - 30 = -18$.
2. $a = 4$.
3. $a = \dfrac{22 - 10}{4} = 3$ m s$^{-2}$.

If question 3 was hard, revise the constant-acceleration formulae in
Section 15.4 before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Forces of $30$ N and $12$ N act in opposite directions on a mass of
$8$ kg. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The resultant is the difference between the two forces, because they oppose each
other.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The resultant is $30 - 12 = 18$ N in the direction of the $30$ N force. Now use
$a = \dfrac{F}{m}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{30 - 12}{8} = \frac{18}{8} = 2.25 \text{ m s}^{-2}
$$

in the direction of the $30$ N force.
</details>

**2.** A crate of mass $20$ kg is pushed along a horizontal floor by a force of
$90$ N. A resistance of $10$ N opposes the motion. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The resistance is a force in the negative direction.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The resultant force is $90 - 10 = 80$ N. Then divide by the mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{90 - 10}{20} = \frac{80}{20} = 4 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$
</details>

**3.** A mass of $7$ kg accelerates at $3$ m s$^{-2}$. Find the resultant force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This time the force is the unknown in $F = ma$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the mass by the acceleration.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F = ma = 7 \times 3 = 21 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**4.** A resultant force of $144$ N gives a body an acceleration of
$6$ m s$^{-2}$. Find the mass of the body.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $F = ma$ to make the mass the subject.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The mass is the force divided by the acceleration.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
m = \frac{F}{a} = \frac{144}{6} = 24 \text{ kg}.
$$
</details>

**5.** A car of mass $1500$ kg has a driving force of $4000$ N and a total
resistance of $1600$ N. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the resultant force along the road first.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The resultant is $4000 - 1600 = 2400$ N. Then use $a = \dfrac{F}{m}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{4000 - 1600}{1500} = \frac{2400}{1500} = 1.6 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$
</details>

**6.** A car of mass $800$ kg brakes with a force of $2400$ N. Find the
deceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The braking force is the only horizontal force, and it points backwards.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the braking force by the mass. The minus sign in the algebra stands for a
deceleration.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Taking the direction of motion as positive,

$$
a = \frac{-2400}{800} = -3 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

The car decelerates at $3$ m s$^{-2}$.
</details>

**7.** A trolley of mass $5$ kg accelerates from rest to $15$ m s$^{-1}$ in
$3$ s. Find the acceleration and the resultant force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is two steps: first find the acceleration from the motion, then use
$F = ma$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The acceleration is $\dfrac{15 - 0}{3}$. Multiply the answer by the mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{15 - 0}{3} = 5 \text{ m s}^{-2},
\qquad
F = ma = 5 \times 5 = 25 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**8.** A body of mass $12$ kg accelerates at $0.5$ m s$^{-2}$ against a
resistance of $8$ N. Find the driving force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the resultant force the acceleration needs. The driving force is
larger than the resultant when a resistance is present.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The resultant is $12 \times 0.5 = 6$ N. Add the resistance to find the driving
force.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F_{\text{resultant}} = ma = 12 \times 0.5 = 6 \text{ N},
$$

so the driving force is $6 + 8 = 14$ N.
</details>

**9.** Forces of $6$ N and $8$ N act at right angles on a mass of $4$ kg. Find
the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two forces are perpendicular, so find their resultant with Pythagoras
before you use $F = ma$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The resultant force is $\sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10$ N. Divide by the mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F = \sqrt{6^2 + 8^2} = 10 \text{ N},
\qquad
a = \frac{10}{4} = 2.5 \text{ m s}^{-2},
$$

in the direction of the resultant force.
</details>
