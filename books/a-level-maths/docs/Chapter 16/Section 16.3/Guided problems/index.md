# 16.3 Weight and motion under gravity — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Ask whether the question wants a mass in
kilograms or a force in newtons. Take the direction of motion as positive, and
write $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$ unless the question says otherwise.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $25 \times 9.8$.
2. Rearrange $W = mg$ to make $m$ the subject.
3. A resultant force of $30$ N acts on a mass of $6$ kg. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $25 \times 9.8 = 245$.
2. $m = \dfrac{W}{g}$.
3. $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{30}{6} = 5$ m s$^{-2}$.

If question 3 was hard, revise Newton's second law in Section 16.2 before you
continue.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Find the weight of a suitcase of mass $25$ kg. Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Weight is a force, so the answer is in newtons. Use $W = mg$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the mass by $9.8$, and say that the weight acts downwards.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
W = mg = 25 \times 9.8 = 245 \text{ N}
$$

acting downwards. The mass stays $25$ kg.
</details>

**2.** A body has a weight of $735$ N. Find its mass. Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This time the mass is the unknown, so divide.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $m = \dfrac{W}{g}$ and divide $735$ by $9.8$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
m = \frac{W}{g} = \frac{735}{9.8} = 75 \text{ kg}.
$$

</details>

**3.** A bag of mass $2$ kg is taken to the Moon, where $g = 1.6$ m s$^{-2}$.
Find its mass and its weight on the Moon.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The mass does not change when the bag moves. Only the weight changes.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The mass is still $2$ kg. Use $W = mg$ with the Moon value of $g$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Mass $= 2$ kg.

$$
W = mg = 2 \times 1.6 = 3.2 \text{ N}.
$$

The bag weighs less on the Moon, but its mass is unchanged.
</details>

**4.** A ball of mass $0.4$ kg falls with an air resistance of $0.6$ N. Find its
acceleration. Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take downwards as positive. The weight acts down and the air resistance acts up.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The weight is $0.4 \times 9.8 = 3.92$ N. The resultant is $3.92 - 0.6$ N. Then
divide by the mass.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{0.4 \times 9.8 - 0.6}{0.4} = \frac{3.32}{0.4} = 8.3 \text{ m s}^{-2}
$$

downwards.
</details>

**5.** A skydiver of mass $75$ kg falls at terminal velocity. Find the air
resistance. Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At terminal velocity the velocity is constant.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A constant velocity means zero resultant force, so the air resistance balances
the weight.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = mg = 75 \times 9.8 = 735 \text{ N}
$$

acting upwards, balancing the weight.
</details>

**6.** A person of mass $65$ kg stands in a lift that accelerates upwards at
$2$ m s$^{-2}$. Find the normal reaction from the floor. Take
$g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The acceleration is upwards, so the reaction is larger than the weight.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $R = m(g + a)$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = 65(9.8 + 2) = 65 \times 11.8 = 767 \text{ N}.
$$

</details>

**7.** A person of mass $65$ kg stands in a lift that accelerates downwards at
$2$ m s$^{-2}$. Find the normal reaction from the floor. Take
$g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The acceleration is downwards now, so the reaction is smaller than the weight.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $R = m(g - a)$ for a downward acceleration.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = 65(9.8 - 2) = 65 \times 7.8 = 507 \text{ N}.
$$

The person feels lighter than usual.
</details>

**8.** A person of mass $65$ kg stands in a lift that moves at a constant
velocity. Find the normal reaction from the floor. Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A constant velocity means no acceleration, so this case is the same as standing
still.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $R = mg$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = mg = 65 \times 9.8 = 637 \text{ N}.
$$

The reading is the same as when the lift is at rest.
</details>

**9.** A stone is dropped and falls with air resistance negligible. Explain why
its acceleration does not depend on its mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write down the resultant force on the stone, then put it into $F = ma$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The only force is the weight $mg$. Substitute that for $F$ in $F = ma$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The resultant force is the weight, $F = mg$. Newton's second law gives

$$
mg = ma,
$$

and dividing both sides by $m$ gives $a = g$. The mass cancels, so every freely
falling body has the same acceleration.
</details>
