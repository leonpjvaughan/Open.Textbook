# Chapter 16: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The crate has a mass of $45$ kg and $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$, so its weight is

$$
W = 45 \times 9.8 = 441 \text{ N}.
$$

**Step 1 (16.1). The crate on the floor.** Two forces act on the crate: its
weight $441$ N downwards and the normal reaction from the floor upwards. The
crate is at rest, so by Newton's first law the resultant force is zero. The two
forces are equal in size and opposite in direction, so the normal reaction is
$441$ N.

**Step 2 (16.2). The two workers push.** The workers push with
$120$ N and $95$ N in the same direction, and a resistance of $65$ N opposes the
motion. The resultant along the floor is

$$
120 + 95 - 65 = 150 \text{ N}.
$$

Newton's second law gives

$$
a = \frac{150}{45} = \frac{10}{3} \approx 3.33 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

**Step 3 (16.3). Mass and weight.** The crate's mass stays $45$ kg wherever it
is. Its weight is a force that depends on $g$: on the Earth it is $441$ N, and on
the Moon, where $g = 1.6$ m s$^{-2}$, it would be $45 \times 1.6 = 72$ N.

**Step 4 (16.4). The rope over the pulley on the smooth ramp.** A light
inextensible string runs up the ramp, over a smooth pulley at the top, to a
hanging mass of $20$ kg. The hanging weight is

$$
20 \times 9.8 = 196 \text{ N}.
$$

The component of the crate's weight down the ramp is

$$
441\sin 18^\circ \approx 136.28 \text{ N}.
$$

The hanging weight is larger, so the crate accelerates up the ramp. The
resultant is

$$
196 - 136.28 = 59.72 \text{ N},
$$

and the total mass moved is $45 + 20 = 65$ kg, so

$$
a = \frac{59.72}{65} \approx 0.92 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

The tension follows from the hanging mass, moving down:

$$
20g - T = 20a, \qquad T = 20(9.8 - 0.92) \approx 177.6 \text{ N}.
$$

**Step 5 (16.5). The rope holding the crate on the smooth ramp.** With the ramp
smooth and the rope parallel to it, resolve along and perpendicular to the ramp.

Along the ramp, the tension balances the downhill component:

$$
T = 441\sin 18^\circ \approx 136.3 \text{ N}.
$$

Perpendicular to the ramp, the normal reaction balances the other component:

$$
R = 441\cos 18^\circ \approx 419.4 \text{ N}.
$$

**Step 6 (16.6). The crate pulled along the floor.** A rope at $25^\circ$ to the
floor pulls with a tension of $200$ N against a resistance of $80$ N.

Resolve vertically. There is no vertical acceleration:

$$
R + 200\sin 25^\circ = 441, \qquad R = 441 - 84.52 = 356.48 \text{ N}.
$$

Resolve horizontally. The horizontal component of the tension is
$200\cos 25^\circ = 181.26$ N, so the resultant is

$$
181.26 - 80 = 101.26 \text{ N},
$$

and

$$
a = \frac{101.26}{45} \approx 2.25 \text{ m s}^{-2}.
$$

**Step 7 (16.7). The rough ramp.** With the rope slack and the coefficient of
friction $0.4$, the crate is released on the ramp.

Perpendicular to the ramp, the normal reaction is still $R \approx 419.4$ N, so
the greatest friction is

$$
\mu R = 0.4 \times 419.4 \approx 167.76 \text{ N}.
$$

The component down the ramp is $136.28$ N. Since

$$
136.28 \leq 167.76,
$$

the downhill component is within the limit, so the crate **stays at rest**.
Friction takes the value it needs, and is $136.3$ N up the ramp.

**Step 8 (16.8). The plank on the trestles.** The uniform plank $AB$ has length
$4.0$ m and mass $20$ kg, so its weight of $196$ N acts at its midpoint, $2$ m
from $A$. The crate of weight $441$ N acts $1.5$ m from $A$.

Take moments about $A$. The reaction at $B$ acts $4$ m from $A$:

$$
4R_B = 196 \times 2 + 441 \times 1.5 = 392 + 661.5 = 1053.5,
$$

$$
R_B = 263.375 \text{ N}.
$$

The total load is $196 + 441 = 637$ N, so

$$
R_A = 637 - 263.375 = 373.625 \text{ N}.
$$

The trestle nearer the crate, at $A$, carries the larger reaction of about
$374$ N.

**The chapter's answer.** The crate stays at rest on the floor, with a normal
reaction of $441$ N. Pushed by the two workers, it accelerates at about
$3.33$ m s$^{-2}$. Its mass is always $45$ kg, while its weight is $441$ N on the
Earth. On a smooth ramp at $18^\circ$ a rope holds it with a tension of
$136.3$ N and the ramp pushes back with $419.4$ N. A $20$ kg hanging mass over a
smooth pulley accelerates the crate up the ramp at about $0.92$ m s$^{-2}$ with a
tension of $177.6$ N. Pulled along the floor by a rope at $25^\circ$, it
accelerates at about $2.25$ m s$^{-2}$, and the floor pushes up with $356.5$ N.
On the rough ramp it stays at rest, because the greatest friction of about
$167.8$ N beats the downhill pull of $136.3$ N. On the plank, the trestles carry
about $374$ N and $263$ N.

## What you can now do

- list the forces on a body and use Newton's first law;
- use $F = ma$ with a resultant force;
- separate mass from weight and use $W = mg$;
- describe free fall and terminal velocity, and find an apparent weight in a
  lift;
- state Newton's third law and identify an action–reaction pair;
- solve connected-particles problems for a pulley, a table and a ramp;
- resolve a force into two perpendicular components;
- write and solve the two equilibrium equations for a particle in a plane;
- find the resultant of forces that are not at right angles;
- use Newton's second law in two directions;
- use the model $F \leq \mu R$ and decide whether a body slides;
- take moments and use the principle of moments to find forces and distances.
