# 16.5 Resolving forces and coplanar equilibrium — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Choose the two axes before you write anything.
Resolve every force, then put the sum in each direction equal to zero. Take
$g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down $\cos 30^\circ$ and $\sin 30^\circ$ exactly.
2. In a right-angled triangle, the hypotenuse is $10$ cm and one angle is
   $40^\circ$. Find the side adjacent to that angle, to $2$ d.p.
3. Work out $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$, $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
2. $10\cos 40^\circ \approx 7.66$ cm.
3. $\sqrt{25} = 5$.

If question 2 was hard, revise the sine and cosine ratios before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Resolve a force of $70$ N acting at $25^\circ$ above the horizontal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is measured from the horizontal, so the horizontal component uses the
cosine.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The horizontal component is $70\cos 25^\circ$ and the vertical component is
$70\sin 25^\circ$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{horizontal} = 70\cos 25^\circ \approx 63.44 \text{ N},
$$

$$
\text{vertical} = 70\sin 25^\circ \approx 29.58 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**2.** A weight of $200$ N acts on a body on a slope of $20^\circ$. Find the
component down the slope and the component perpendicular to the slope, each to
$2$ d.p.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle at the slope is measured from the slope surface, so the sine goes with
the component down the slope.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Down the slope: $200\sin 20^\circ$. Perpendicular to the slope:
$200\cos 20^\circ$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{down the slope} = 200\sin 20^\circ \approx 68.40 \text{ N},
$$

$$
\text{perpendicular} = 200\cos 20^\circ \approx 187.94 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**3.** A block of mass $10$ kg rests on a smooth slope of $30^\circ$. A light
rope parallel to the slope holds it in equilibrium. Find the tension and the
normal reaction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the weight, then resolve it along and perpendicular to the slope.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The weight is $10 \times 9.8 = 98$ N. The tension balances the component down the
slope.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
T = 98\sin 30^\circ = 49 \text{ N},
$$

$$
R = 98\cos 30^\circ = 49\sqrt{3} \approx 84.87 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**4.** A particle of weight $60$ N hangs in equilibrium from two light strings
that each make $45^\circ$ with the horizontal. Find the tension in each string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two strings are symmetrical, so the two tensions are equal.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Resolve vertically: $T\sin 45^\circ + T\sin 45^\circ = 60$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
2T\sin 45^\circ = 60, \qquad T = \frac{60}{2 \times 0.7071} \approx 42.43 \text{ N}.
$$

Each tension is about $42.43$ N.
</details>

**5.** A particle of weight $100$ N hangs in equilibrium from two light strings.
One makes $30^\circ$ with the horizontal and the other makes $60^\circ$ with the
horizontal. Find the two tensions.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are two unknown tensions, so two equations are needed: one horizontal and
one vertical.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The horizontal equation is $T_1\cos 30^\circ = T_2\cos 60^\circ$. The vertical
equation is $T_1\sin 30^\circ + T_2\sin 60^\circ = 100$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

From the horizontal equation, $T_1 = \dfrac{\cos 60^\circ}{\cos 30^\circ}T_2
= \dfrac{T_2}{\sqrt{3}}$. Substitute into the vertical equation:

$$
\frac{T_2}{\sqrt{3}} \times \frac{1}{2} + T_2 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 100.
$$

Multiply every term by $2\sqrt{3}$:

$$
T_2 + 3T_2 = 200\sqrt{3}, \qquad 4T_2 = 200\sqrt{3}, \qquad T_2 = 50\sqrt{3} \approx 86.60 \text{ N}.
$$

Then $T_1 = 50$ N. The string closer to the vertical carries the larger tension.
</details>

**6.** A body of weight $196$ N is held in equilibrium on a smooth slope of
$30^\circ$ by a horizontal force $P$. Find $P$ and the normal reaction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Resolve along the slope first. The weight has a component down the slope and the
horizontal force has a component up the slope.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Along the slope: $P\cos 30^\circ = 196\sin 30^\circ$, so $P = 196\tan 30^\circ$.
Then resolve perpendicular to the slope for the normal reaction.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
P = 196\tan 30^\circ \approx 113.16 \text{ N}.
$$

$$
R = 196\cos 30^\circ + P\sin 30^\circ \approx 169.74 + 56.58 = 226.32 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**7.** A particle is in equilibrium under four forces. Three of them are
$(4, 0)$ N, $(-1, 5)$ N and $(0, -3)$ N. Find the fourth force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the three known forces as vectors first.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The three forces add to $(3, 2)$ N. The fourth force must cancel that.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The fourth force is $-(3, 2) = (-3, -2)$ N. Its size is
$\sqrt{3^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{13} \approx 3.61$ N.
</details>

**8.** A sign of weight $49$ N hangs from the end of a horizontal rod. A wire
from the wall to the end of the rod makes $40^\circ$ with the horizontal and
holds the sign in equilibrium. Find the tension in the wire and the thrust in
the rod.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the end of the rod, three forces meet: the weight, the wire tension and the
rod thrust. Resolve vertically first.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The vertical component of the tension balances the weight:
$T\sin 40^\circ = 49$. Then the horizontal component balances the rod thrust.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
T = \frac{49}{\sin 40^\circ} \approx 76.23 \text{ N}.
$$

$$
\text{thrust} = T\cos 40^\circ \approx 58.40 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>
