# 10.2 Magnitude and direction; component and magnitude/direction form — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Before you find a direction, decide which
quadrant the vector is in. Before you find a magnitude, write down the squares.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\cos 60^\circ$ and $\sin 60^\circ$.
2. Work out $\tan^{-1}(1)$ in degrees.
3. Work out $\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\cos 60^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and $\sin 60^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
2. $\tan 45^\circ = 1$, so $\tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$.
3. $\sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.

If question 1 was hard, revise the exact trigonometric values before you
continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Find the magnitude of $\mathbf{a} = 5\mathbf{i} + 12\mathbf{j}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The magnitude is the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle whose two shorter
sides are the components.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Square each component, add the two squares, then take the square root.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
|\mathbf{a}| = \sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{25 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13.
$$

The vector has length $13$.
</details>

**2.** Find the magnitude of $\mathbf{b} = -8\mathbf{i} + 6\mathbf{j}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The negative sign belongs to a direction, not to a length. Squaring removes it.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $(-8)^2 = 64$ and $6^2 = 36$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
|\mathbf{b}| = \sqrt{(-8)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{64 + 36} = \sqrt{100} = 10.
$$

The magnitude is $10$.
</details>

**3.** Find the magnitude of $\mathbf{c} = 2\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j} + 6\mathbf{k}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

In three dimensions there are three squares to add before the square root.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $2^2 + (-3)^2 + 6^2$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
|\mathbf{c}| = \sqrt{2^2 + (-3)^2 + 6^2} = \sqrt{4 + 9 + 36} = \sqrt{49} = 7.
$$

The magnitude is $7$.
</details>

**4.** Find the direction of $\mathbf{a} = \mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}$ as an angle
from the positive $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both components are equal, so the vector lies on a familiar line through the
origin.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{1}{1} = 1$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both components are positive, so the vector is in the first quadrant.
$\tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$, so the direction is $45^\circ$ from the positive
$x$-axis.
</details>

**5.** Find the direction of $\mathbf{c} = -\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}$ as an angle
from the positive $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$-component is negative and the $y$-component is positive. Sketch it to
choose the quadrant.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The inverse tangent gives $45^\circ$ to the negative $x$-axis. Subtract it from
$180^\circ$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The vector is in the second quadrant. Its angle to the negative $x$-axis is
$\tan^{-1}(1) = 45^\circ$, so its direction from the positive $x$-axis is

$$
180^\circ - 45^\circ = 135^\circ.
$$
</details>

**6.** Find the direction of $\mathbf{d} = -3\mathbf{j}$ as an angle from the
positive $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$-component is $0$, so the formula for $\tan\theta$ divides by zero. Draw
the vector instead.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The vector points straight down, which is $90^\circ$ clockwise from the positive
$x$-axis.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The vector points straight downwards. As an anticlockwise angle from the
positive $x$-axis, that is $270^\circ$. It can also be written as $-90^\circ$.
Both name the same direction.
</details>

**7.** A force of $25$ N acts at $40^\circ$ above the horizontal. Write the
force in component form, correct to $3$ significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The horizontal component uses cosine and the vertical component uses sine.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $25\cos 40^\circ$ and $25\sin 40^\circ$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F_x = 25\cos 40^\circ = 19.2 \text{ N},
\qquad
F_y = 25\sin 40^\circ = 16.1 \text{ N}.
$$

The force is $19.2\mathbf{i} + 16.1\mathbf{j}$ N.
</details>

**8.** A walker walks $15$ km on a bearing of $060^\circ$. Find the displacement
from the start, with east as $\mathbf{i}$ and north as $\mathbf{j}$, giving an
exact answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A bearing is measured clockwise from north. The east component uses sine and the
north component uses cosine.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $15\sin 60^\circ$ and $15\cos 60^\circ$ with exact values.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{east} = 15\sin 60^\circ = 15 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{15\sqrt{3}}{2},
\qquad
\text{north} = 15\cos 60^\circ = 15 \times \frac{1}{2} = \frac{15}{2}.
$$

The displacement is $\dfrac{15\sqrt{3}}{2}\mathbf{i} + \dfrac{15}{2}\mathbf{j}$
km. Both components are positive, so the walker finishes north-east of the
start.
</details>

**9.** Find the unit vector in the direction of $\mathbf{a} = 5\mathbf{i} + 12\mathbf{j}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A unit vector has magnitude $1$. Divide the vector by its own magnitude.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Question 1 gives $|\mathbf{a}| = 13$. Divide each component by $13$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\hat{\mathbf{a}} = \frac{1}{13}(5\mathbf{i} + 12\mathbf{j}) = \frac{5}{13}\mathbf{i} + \frac{12}{13}\mathbf{j}.
$$

Check: $\sqrt{(5/13)^2 + (12/13)^2} = \sqrt{25/169 + 144/169} = 1$, so the unit
vector has magnitude $1$.
</details>
