# 10.4 Position vectors and the distance between two points — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Before you calculate, decide whether the
question wants a displacement vector, a distance or a midpoint. Write the
subtraction in the order the letters give.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down $5 - 1$ and $1 - 5$.
2. Find the magnitude of $3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$.
3. Work out the midpoint of $2$ and $8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $5 - 1 = 4$ and $1 - 5 = -4$.
2. $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5$.
3. The midpoint is $\dfrac{2 + 8}{2} = 5$.

If question 1 was hard, revise subtracting directed numbers before you
continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write down the position vector of $A(3, -2)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The position vector of a point runs from the origin to the point. Its components
are the coordinates.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Read the coordinates into a column: $3$ on top, $-2$ below.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\vec{OA} = 3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j} =
\begin{pmatrix} 3 \\ -2 \end{pmatrix}.
$$
</details>

**2.** The points are $A(2, 1)$ and $B(7, 4)$. Find $\vec{AB}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\vec{AB}$ is the position vector of $B$ minus the position vector of $A$. The
letter that comes second is the one you subtract from.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $7 - 2$ and $4 - 1$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\vec{AB} = \mathbf{b} - \mathbf{a} =
(7 - 2)\mathbf{i} + (4 - 1)\mathbf{j} = 5\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}.
$$
</details>

**3.** Find the distance between $A(1, -1)$ and $B(4, 3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find $\vec{AB}$, then find its magnitude.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\vec{AB} = (4 - 1)\mathbf{i} + (3 - (-1))\mathbf{j} = 3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$.
Now take the square root of the sum of squares.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\vec{AB} = 3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j},
\qquad
AB = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5.
$$
</details>

**4.** Find the distance between $A(2, 0, 1)$ and $B(5, 4, 3)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are three coordinates, so there are three squares under the square root.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The difference of the $z$-coordinates is $3 - 1 = 2$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\vec{AB} = 3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} + 2\mathbf{k},
\qquad
AB = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 4} = \sqrt{29}.
$$

The distance is $\sqrt{29} \approx 5.39$.
</details>

**5.** Find the distance of $P(-3, 4, 12)$ from the origin.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The distance from the origin is the magnitude of the position vector.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Square each coordinate, including $(-3)^2$ and $12^2$, then add.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
OP = \sqrt{(-3)^2 + 4^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16 + 144} = \sqrt{169} = 13.
$$
</details>

**6.** Find the midpoint of $A(1, 3)$ and $B(7, -1)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The midpoint is the average of the two position vectors. Average each coordinate
separately.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\dfrac{1 + 7}{2}$ and $\dfrac{3 + (-1)}{2}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\mathbf{m} = \frac{1}{2}\left((1 + 7)\mathbf{i} + (3 - 1)\mathbf{j}\right) =
4\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}.
$$

The midpoint is $(4, 1)$.
</details>

**7.** Show that the triangle with vertices $A(1, 1)$, $B(4, 5)$ and $C(8, 2)$
is isosceles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An isosceles triangle has two equal sides. Work out all three side lengths and
look for a matching pair.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$AB$ uses $\vec{AB} = 3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$ and $BC$ uses
$\vec{BC} = 4\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
AB = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = 5,
\qquad
BC = \sqrt{4^2 + (-3)^2} = 5,
$$

$$
AC = \sqrt{7^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{50}.
$$

Two sides are equal, so the triangle is isosceles.
</details>

**8.** A point $P$ has position vector $2\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j}$ and a point
$Q$ has position vector $6\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}$. Find $\vec{PQ}$ and the
distance $PQ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Subtract the position vector of $P$ from the position vector of $Q$, then take
the magnitude.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\vec{PQ} = (6 - 2)\mathbf{i} + (2 - (-1))\mathbf{j}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\vec{PQ} = 4\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j},
\qquad
PQ = \sqrt{4^2 + 3^2} = 5.
$$
</details>
