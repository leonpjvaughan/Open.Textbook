# 3.3 The equation of a circle and its properties — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Decide at the start whether the question has
given you the centre and the radius, or an expanded equation that hides them.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $x^2 - 10x + 7$ in completed-square form.
2. Work out the distance between $(1, 2)$ and $(4, 6)$.
3. Work out the gradient of the line $3x + 4y = 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Half of $-10$ is $-5$: $(x - 5)^2 - 25 + 7 = (x - 5)^2 - 18$.
2. $\sqrt{(4 - 1)^2 + (6 - 2)^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
3. $4y = 12 - 3x$, so $y = 3 - \dfrac{3}{4}x$. The gradient is $-\dfrac{3}{4}$.

If question 1 was hard, revisit completing the square in Section 2.3 before you
continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write the equation of the circle with centre $(2, -5)$ and radius $6$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute the centre and the radius into $(x - a)^2 + (y - b)^2 = r^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The centre gives $a = 2$ and $b = -5$. Subtracting a negative gives a plus on
the left: $(y - (-5))^2 = (y + 5)^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y - (-5))^2 = 6^2,
$$

so the equation is

$$
(x - 2)^2 + (y + 5)^2 = 36.
$$

The radius is $6$, and $6^2 = 36$ is the number on the right.
</details>

**2.** Find the centre and the radius of
$x^2 + y^2 - 8x + 2y - 8 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Complete the square twice, once in $x$ and once in $y$. Remember to subtract the
number you add.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x^2 - 8x = (x - 4)^2 - 16$ and $y^2 + 2y = (y + 1)^2 - 1$. Put those into the
equation and collect the constants.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
(x - 4)^2 - 16 + (y + 1)^2 - 1 - 8 = 0.
$$

Add the constants:

$$
(x - 4)^2 + (y + 1)^2 - 25 = 0, \qquad (x - 4)^2 + (y + 1)^2 = 25.
$$

The centre is $(4, -1)$ and the radius is $5$.
</details>

**3.** The circle is $(x - 2)^2 + (y - 1)^2 = 25$. Decide whether the point
$(7, 3)$ is inside, on, or outside the circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute the point into the left side and compare the answer with $25$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $(7 - 2)^2 + (3 - 1)^2$, then compare with $r^2 = 25$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
(7 - 2)^2 + (3 - 1)^2 = 25 + 4 = 29.
$$

Since $29 > 25$, the point is outside the circle. The distance from the centre
is $\sqrt{29} \approx 5.39$, which is greater than the radius $5$.
</details>

**4.** Find the tangent to the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 169$ at the point $(5, 12)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the gradient of the radius from the centre to the point. The tangent has the
negative reciprocal gradient.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The radius has gradient $\frac{12}{5}$, so the tangent has gradient
$-\frac{5}{12}$. Use the point $(5, 12)$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
y - 12 = -\frac{5}{12}(x - 5).
$$

Multiply by $12$:

$$
12y - 144 = -5x + 25, \qquad 5x + 12y = 169.
$$

Check the point: $5 \times 5 + 12 \times 12 = 25 + 144 = 169$.
</details>

**5.** Find where the line $y = 3$ meets the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute $y = 3$ into the circle equation. The result is a quadratic in $x$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x^2 + 9 = 25$, so $x^2 = 16$. Take both square roots.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
x^2 + 3^2 = 25, \qquad x^2 + 9 = 25, \qquad x^2 = 16, \qquad x = 4 \text{ or } x = -4.
$$

The line meets the circle at $(4, 3)$ and $(-4, 3)$. The chord is horizontal and
$8$ units long.
</details>

**6.** The line $x + y = 19$ cuts the circle $(x - 6)^2 + (y - 8)^2 = 25$ at
$(11, 8)$ and $(6, 13)$. Work out the length of the chord.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The chord length is the distance between the two points. Use the distance
formula.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\sqrt{(11 - 6)^2 + (8 - 13)^2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\sqrt{(11 - 6)^2 + (8 - 13)^2} = \sqrt{25 + 25} = \sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}.
$$

The chord is $5\sqrt{2} \approx 7.07$ units long. The midpoint is
$\left(\frac{17}{2}, \frac{21}{2}\right)$, and the perpendicular from the centre
$(6, 8)$ passes through it.
</details>

**7.** The circle $x^2 + y^2 = 25$ has $A(-5, 0)$, $B(5, 0)$ and $C(-3, 4)$ on
it. Show that angle $ACB$ is a right angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$AB$ is a diameter. For a right angle, the two gradients $CA$ and $CB$ must
multiply to $-1$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The gradient of $CA$ is $\dfrac{4 - 0}{-3 - (-5)} = \dfrac{4}{2} = 2$. Work out
the gradient of $CB$ the same way.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
m_{CA} = \frac{4 - 0}{-3 - (-5)} = \frac{4}{2} = 2,
$$

$$
m_{CB} = \frac{4 - 0}{-3 - 5} = \frac{4}{-8} = -\frac{1}{2}.
$$

The product is $2 \times \left(-\frac{1}{2}\right) = -1$, so the two lines meet
at a right angle. This is the angle in a semicircle: $AB$ is a diameter, and any
point $C$ on the circle gives a right angle at $C$.
</details>
