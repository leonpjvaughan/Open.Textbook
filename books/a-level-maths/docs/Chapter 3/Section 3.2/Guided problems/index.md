# 3.2 Parallel and perpendicular lines — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Read the gradient of the given line before you
write anything down, and rearrange the line if it is not in the form
$y = mx + c$.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out the gradient of the line through $(0, 5)$ and $(4, 13)$.
2. Rearrange $2x + 3y = 6$ to make $y$ the subject.
3. Work out the midpoint of the segment from $(2, 4)$ to $(8, 10)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $m = \dfrac{13 - 5}{4 - 0} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$.
2. $3y = 6 - 2x$, so $y = 2 - \dfrac{2}{3}x$. The gradient is $-\dfrac{2}{3}$.
3. $M = \left(\dfrac{2 + 8}{2}, \dfrac{4 + 10}{2}\right) = (5, 7)$.

If question 2 was hard, revisit rearranging a formula before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Decide whether $y = 5x + 1$ and $y = 5x - 9$ are parallel. Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Parallel lines point in the same direction. Which number in $y = mx + c$ sets
the direction?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Read the coefficient of $x$ in each equation. Both are $5$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both lines have gradient $5$, and parallel lines have equal gradients. So the
lines are parallel. They are not the same line, because their intercepts, $1$ and
$-9$, are different.
</details>

**2.** A line has gradient $\frac{3}{4}$. Work out the gradient of a line
perpendicular to it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Turn the fraction upside down and change its sign.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The reciprocal of $\frac{3}{4}$ is $\frac{4}{3}$. Change the sign.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
m = -\frac{4}{3}.
$$

Check: $\frac{3}{4} \times \left(-\frac{4}{3}\right) = -1$, so the two lines are
perpendicular.
</details>

**3.** Find the equation of the line through $(2, 7)$ perpendicular to
$y = \frac{1}{2}x + 3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The given gradient is $\frac{1}{2}$. Work out the negative reciprocal first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The perpendicular gradient is $-2$. Now substitute the point $(2, 7)$ into
$y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The perpendicular gradient is $-2$, because $\frac{1}{2} \times (-2) = -1$.

$$
y - 7 = -2(x - 2) = -2x + 4.
$$

Add $7$:

$$
y = -2x + 11.
$$

Check the point $(2, 7)$: $-2 \times 2 + 11 = 7$.
</details>

**4.** Find the equation of the line through $(4, -1)$ parallel to
$3x - y = 5$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $3x - y = 5$ to read off its gradient. A parallel line has the same
gradient.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rearranging gives $y = 3x - 5$, so the gradient is $3$. Substitute the point
$(4, -1)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ with $m = 3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
y - (-1) = 3(x - 4) = 3x - 12.
$$

So

$$
y + 1 = 3x - 12, \qquad y = 3x - 13.
$$

The gradient is $3$, the same as the given line, so the lines are parallel.
</details>

**5.** Work out the shortest distance from $(0, 0)$ to the line
$3x + 4y = 20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The shortest distance is measured along the perpendicular. You can find the
perpendicular line and intersect it, or use the distance formula.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the line as $3x + 4y - 20 = 0$. Then use
$\dfrac{|a x_1 + b y_1 + c|}{\sqrt{a^2 + b^2}}$ with $a = 3$, $b = 4$, $c = -20$
and $(x_1, y_1) = (0, 0)$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\frac{|3 \times 0 + 4 \times 0 - 20|}{\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}}
= \frac{20}{\sqrt{25}} = \frac{20}{5} = 4.
$$

The shortest distance is $4$ units. The foot of the perpendicular is the point
$(2.4, 3.2)$, which is $\sqrt{2.4^2 + 3.2^2} = 4$ from the origin.
</details>

**6.** Show that $y = 4x - 3$ and $y = -\frac{1}{4}x + 2$ are perpendicular.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The test for perpendicular lines is the product of the two gradients.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the two gradients, $4$ and $-\frac{1}{4}$, and compare the answer with
$-1$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
4 \times \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = -1.
$$

The product of the gradients is $-1$, so the lines meet at a right angle.
</details>
