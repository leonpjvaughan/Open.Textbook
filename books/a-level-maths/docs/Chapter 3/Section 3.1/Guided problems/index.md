# 3.1 Straight lines, gradients and straight-line models — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Decide whether the question gives you a
gradient, a point, or both, before you write anything down.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Rearrange $3x + 2y = 12$ to make $y$ the subject.
2. Work out the value of $\dfrac{11 - 5}{7 - 2}$.
3. Solve $2x - 1 = 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Subtract $3x$ from both sides: $2y = 12 - 3x$. Divide by $2$:
   $y = 6 - \dfrac{3}{2}x$.
2. $\dfrac{6}{5}$.
3. Add $1$ to both sides: $2x = 10$, so $x = 5$.

If question 1 was hard, revisit rearranging a formula before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Work out the gradient of the line through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 14)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The gradient is the change in $y$ divided by the change in $x$. Take the second
point's coordinates minus the first point's, on the top and on the bottom.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The change in $y$ is $14 - 2 = 12$. The change in $x$ is $5 - 1 = 4$. Divide.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
m = \frac{14 - 2}{5 - 1} = \frac{12}{4} = 3.
$$

The line rises $3$ units for each unit across, so the gradient is $3$.
</details>

**2.** Work out the gradient of the line through $(-2, 6)$ and $(3, -4)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Substitute carefully: the change in $y$ is $-4 - 6$, and the change in $x$ is
$3 - (-2)$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the top and the bottom separately before you divide:
$-4 - 6 = -10$ and $3 - (-2) = 5$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
m = \frac{-4 - 6}{3 - (-2)} = \frac{-10}{5} = -2.
$$

The gradient is negative, so the line falls as it moves right.
</details>

**3.** Find the equation of the line through $(1, 5)$ with gradient $3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$ with $x_1 = 1$, $y_1 = 5$ and $m = 3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $y - 5 = 3(x - 1)$, then expand the bracket on the right.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
y - 5 = 3(x - 1) = 3x - 3.
$$

Add $5$ to both sides:

$$
y = 3x + 2.
$$

Check with the point $(1, 5)$: $3 \times 1 + 2 = 5$. The point fits.
</details>

**4.** Find the equation of the line through $(2, 1)$ and $(6, 9)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is two jobs. Work out the gradient first, then use one of the points.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The gradient is $\dfrac{9 - 1}{6 - 2} = 2$. Now put $m = 2$ and the point
$(2, 1)$ into $y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
m = \frac{9 - 1}{6 - 2} = \frac{8}{4} = 2.
$$

$$
y - 1 = 2(x - 2) = 2x - 4.
$$

Add $1$:

$$
y = 2x - 3.
$$

Check the other point $(6, 9)$: $2 \times 6 - 3 = 9$. Both points fit.
</details>

**5.** A plumber charges a fixed £40 plus £25 for each hour of work. Write a
model for the cost, and work out the charge for a visit of $3$ hours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which number is paid once, whatever the time? That is the intercept. Which
number is paid for each hour? That is the gradient.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Let $h$ be the number of hours and $C$ the cost in pounds. The model starts from
$C = 40 + 25h$. Substitute $h = 3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
C = 40 + 25h.
$$

The intercept $40$ is the fixed charge, paid before any work starts. The
gradient $25$ is the charge for each extra hour, in pounds per hour.

For a visit of $3$ hours,

$$
C = 40 + 25 \times 3 = 40 + 75 = 115.
$$

The charge is £115.
</details>

**6.** Write $y = \dfrac{2}{3}x - 4$ in the form $ax + by + c = 0$, where
$a$, $b$ and $c$ are whole numbers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The fraction is the only obstacle. Multiply every term by $3$ first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiplying by $3$ gives $3y = 2x - 12$. Now move every term to one side so that
the equation reads $= 0$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Multiply both sides by $3$:

$$
3y = 2x - 12.
$$

Subtract $3y$ from both sides:

$$
0 = 2x - 3y - 12,
$$

which is the same as

$$
2x - 3y - 12 = 0.
$$

So $a = 2$, $b = -3$ and $c = -12$.
</details>

**7.** Find where the line $4x - 3y = 24$ crosses each axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The $x$-intercept is the point with $y = 0$. The $y$-intercept is the point with
$x = 0$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Put $y = 0$ to get $4x = 24$. Then put $x = 0$ to get $-3y = 24$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Put $y = 0$:

$$
4x = 24, \qquad x = 6.
$$

Put $x = 0$:

$$
-3y = 24, \qquad y = -8.
$$

The line crosses the $x$-axis at $(6, 0)$ and the $y$-axis at $(0, -8)$.
</details>
