# 5.9 The forms r cos(theta +/- alpha) and r sin(theta +/- alpha) — Practise with help

Work out $R$ first. Then find the quadrant of $\alpha$ from the signs of the two
coefficients. Then solve or state the range.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2}$.
2. Work out $\arctan 1$ in radians.
3. Write down the value of $\cos 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.
2. $\arctan 1 = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
3. $\cos 0 = 1$.

If question 1 was hard, revise Pythagoras before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write $3\cos\theta + 4\sin\theta$ in the form $R\cos(\theta - \alpha)$,
with $\alpha$ acute, and state the greatest value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$ with $a = 3$ and $b = 4$. Then $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{b}{a}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = \sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$ and $\tan\alpha = \dfrac{4}{3}$, so
$\alpha \approx 0.9273$. The greatest value is $R$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
3\cos\theta + 4\sin\theta = 5\cos(\theta - 0.9273).
$$

The greatest value is $5$, reached when $\theta = 0.9273$.
</details>

**2.** Write $5\cos\theta - 12\sin\theta$ in the form
$R\cos(\theta - \alpha)$, and state the greatest value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Here $a = 5$ and $b = -12$. The negative $b$ puts $\alpha$ in the fourth
quadrant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = \sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$. Also
$\tan\alpha = \dfrac{-12}{5}$, so the related acute angle is about $1.176$ and
$\alpha \approx -1.176$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
5\cos\theta - 12\sin\theta = 13\cos(\theta + 1.176).
$$

The greatest value is $13$.
</details>

**3.** Write $-\cos\theta + \sqrt{3}\sin\theta$ in the form
$R\cos(\theta - \alpha)$, in exact form.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$a = -1$, so cosine is negative, and $b = \sqrt{3}$ is positive. That puts
$\alpha$ in the second quadrant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = 2$. The related acute angle is $\arctan\sqrt{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, so
$\alpha = \pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
-\cos\theta + \sqrt{3}\sin\theta = 2\cos\left(\theta - \frac{2\pi}{3}\right).
$$
</details>

**4.** Solve $3\cos\theta + 4\sin\theta = 2$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Replace the left-hand side with $5\cos(\theta - 0.9273)$ from question 1.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos(\theta - 0.9273) = 0.4$. The principal value is $1.1593$ radians, and
cosine is positive at the negative of that value as well.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\theta - 0.9273 = 1.1593 \quad \text{or} \quad \theta - 0.9273 = -1.1593.
$$

$$
\theta = 2.0866 \quad \text{or} \quad \theta = -0.2320.
$$

The second is outside the interval, so add $2\pi$ to get
$\theta = 6.0512$. The solutions are $\theta \approx 2.087$ and
$\theta \approx 6.051$.
</details>

**5.** The height of the tide is
$h = 12\cos\theta + 5\sin\theta$ metres. Find the greatest height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The greatest height is $R = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = \sqrt{144 + 25} = \sqrt{169}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = \sqrt{144 + 25} = 13.
$$

The greatest height is $13$ m. The tide is never as high as $12 + 5 = 17$ m,
because the two waves do not peak at the same angle.
</details>

**6.** Write $4\sin\theta + 3\cos\theta$ in the form
$R\sin(\theta + \beta)$, with $\beta$ acute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For the sine form, $\sin\beta = \dfrac{a}{R}$ and $\cos\beta = \dfrac{b}{R}$,
where $a$ is the cosine coefficient and $b$ the sine coefficient.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = 5$. Here the cosine coefficient is $3$ and the sine coefficient is $4$, so
$\sin\beta = \dfrac{3}{5}$ and $\cos\beta = \dfrac{4}{5}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
4\sin\theta + 3\cos\theta = 5\sin(\theta + \beta),
$$

where $\beta \approx 0.6435$ radians, because $\sin\beta = \dfrac{3}{5}$.
</details>
