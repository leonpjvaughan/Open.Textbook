# 5.12 Trigonometry in context: vectors, kinematics and forces — Practise with help

Draw the vector triangle before you calculate. Mark the angle you have been
given, and check whether it is between the two vectors or between a vector and a
direction.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides $3$
   and $4$.
2. For an angle $\theta$ in a right-angled triangle, write down $\tan\theta$ in
   terms of the opposite and adjacent sides.
3. Work out $10\cos 60^\circ$ and $10\sin 60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\sqrt{9 + 16} = 5$.
2. $\tan\theta = \dfrac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}$.
3. $10\cos 60^\circ = 10 \times \dfrac{1}{2} = 5$ and
   $10\sin 60^\circ = 10 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.66$.

If question 2 was hard, revise right-angled trigonometry before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Resolve a force of $20$ N at $40^\circ$ above the horizontal into its
horizontal and vertical components, to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The horizontal component uses cosine. The vertical component uses sine.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $20\cos 40^\circ$ and $20\sin 40^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{horizontal} = 20\cos 40^\circ \approx 15.3 \text{ N}.
$$

$$
\text{vertical} = 20\sin 40^\circ \approx 12.9 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**2.** Two forces of $5$ N and $9$ N act at $90^\circ$ to each other. Find the
magnitude of the resultant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At $90^\circ$ the cosine is zero, so the resultant uses Pythagoras' theorem.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R = \sqrt{5^2 + 9^2} = \sqrt{25 + 81}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R = \sqrt{25 + 81} = \sqrt{106} \approx 10.3 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**3.** Two forces of $4$ N and $7$ N act at $50^\circ$ to each other. Find the
magnitude of the resultant, to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $R^2 = p^2 + q^2 + 2pq\cos\theta$ with $\theta = 50^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$R^2 = 16 + 49 + 2 \times 4 \times 7 \times \cos 50^\circ = 65 + 56 \times
0.6428$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
R^2 = 16 + 49 + 56 \times 0.6428 \approx 65 + 36.0 = 101.0.
$$

$$
R \approx \sqrt{101.0} \approx 10.0 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**4.** A swimmer heads due east at $1.2$ m s$^{-1}$ in a current of
$0.5$ m s$^{-1}$ due south. Find the size of the resultant velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two velocities are perpendicular. Draw them nose to tail.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \sqrt{1.2^2 + 0.5^2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
v = \sqrt{1.44 + 0.25} = \sqrt{1.69} = 1.3 \text{ m s}^{-1}.
$$
</details>

**5.** A block of weight $120$ N is on a slope of $20^\circ$. Find the component
of the weight down the slope.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The component down the slope uses the sine of the slope angle.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $120\sin 20^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
120\sin 20^\circ \approx 120 \times 0.3420 = 41.0 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**6.** Two forces of $6$ N and $8$ N act at $60^\circ$ to each other. The
resultant has magnitude $12.2$ N. Find the angle between the resultant and the
$8$ N force, to one decimal place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the sine rule in the vector triangle: $\dfrac{\sin\alpha}{6} =
\dfrac{\sin 60^\circ}{12.2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin\alpha = \dfrac{6\sin 60^\circ}{12.2} = \dfrac{5.196}{12.2} \approx 0.4259$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\alpha = \sin^{-1}(0.4259) \approx 25.2^\circ.
$$
</details>
