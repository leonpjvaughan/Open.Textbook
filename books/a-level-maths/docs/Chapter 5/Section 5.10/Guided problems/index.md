# 5.10 Solving trigonometric equations in an interval — Practise with help

Sketch the curve over the interval before you solve. Count the solutions you
expect, then find them all. For a multiple angle, change the interval first.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the value of $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
2. Factorise $x^2 - x - 2$.
3. Solve $x^2 - x - 2 = 0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Both equal $\dfrac{1}{2}$.
2. $x^2 - x - 2 = (x - 2)(x + 1)$.
3. $x = 2$ or $x = -1$.

If question 2 was hard, revise factorising a quadratic before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Solve $\sin\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Sine is positive in the first and second quadrants. One solution is
$\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The second solution is $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\theta = \frac{\pi}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad \theta = \pi - \frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{2\pi}{3}.
$$
</details>

**2.** Solve $\cos\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Cosine is negative in the second and third quadrants. The related acute angle is
$\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The two angles are $\pi - \dfrac{\pi}{3}$ and $\pi + \dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\theta = \frac{2\pi}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad \theta = \frac{4\pi}{3}.
$$
</details>

**3.** Solve $\tan\theta = \sqrt{3}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Tangent has period $\pi$. The principal value is $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $\pi$ to the principal value to get the second solution, then check it is
still below $2\pi$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\theta = \frac{\pi}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad \theta = \frac{\pi}{3} + \pi = \frac{4\pi}{3}.
$$
</details>

**4.** Solve $\sin 2\theta = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Let $x = 2\theta$. The new interval is $0 \le x < 4\pi$, which is two full
turns.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

In $0 \le x < 4\pi$, sine takes $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ at
$x = \dfrac{\pi}{3}, \dfrac{2\pi}{3}, \dfrac{7\pi}{3}, \dfrac{8\pi}{3}$. Divide
each by $2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\theta = \frac{\pi}{6}, \quad \frac{\pi}{3}, \quad \frac{7\pi}{6}, \quad \frac{4\pi}{3}.
$$
</details>

**5.** Solve $2\cos^2\theta - \cos\theta = 0$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Factorise the left-hand side. Do not divide by $\cos\theta$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos\theta(2\cos\theta - 1) = 0$, so $\cos\theta = 0$ or
$\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

For $\cos\theta = 0$:

$$
\theta = \frac{\pi}{2} \quad \text{or} \quad \theta = \frac{3\pi}{2}.
$$

For $\cos\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$:

$$
\theta = \frac{\pi}{3} \quad \text{or} \quad \theta = \frac{5\pi}{3}.
$$

The solutions are $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$, $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ and
$\dfrac{5\pi}{3}$.
</details>

**6.** Solve $\sin^2\theta = \dfrac{1}{4}$ for $0 \le \theta < 2\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$ or $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Each gives two
solutions.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For $\sin\theta = \dfrac{1}{2}$, the angles are $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ and
$\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$. For $\sin\theta = -\dfrac{1}{2}$, they are
$\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ and $\dfrac{11\pi}{6}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\theta = \frac{\pi}{6}, \quad \frac{5\pi}{6}, \quad \frac{7\pi}{6}, \quad \frac{11\pi}{6}.
$$
</details>
