# 5.1 The sine and cosine rules and the area of a triangle — Practise with help

Work each question closed book first. Before you substitute, decide whether the
sine rule or the cosine rule fits the information you have.

## Warm-up: remember

1. In a right-angled triangle the side opposite the right angle is $13$ cm and
   one shorter side is $5$ cm. Work out the third side.
2. For the same triangle, work out $\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle
   between the $5$ cm side and the hypotenuse.
3. Make $x$ the subject of $4x = 10\sin 40^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Pythagoras: $13^2 - 5^2 = 169 - 25 = 144$, so the third side is $12$ cm.
2. The side opposite $\theta$ is $12$ cm, so $\sin\theta = \dfrac{12}{13}$.
3. Divide by $4$: $x = \dfrac{10\sin 40^\circ}{4} \approx 1.61$.

If question 3 was hard, revise rearranging a formula before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** In triangle $ABC$, angle $A = 50^\circ$, angle $B = 70^\circ$ and side
$a = 11$ cm. Find side $b$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two angles and a side are given. That is a job for the sine rule, with $b$
paired with $B$ and $a$ paired with $A$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\dfrac{b}{\sin 70^\circ} = \dfrac{11}{\sin 50^\circ}$, then multiply both
sides by $\sin 70^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
b = \frac{11\sin 70^\circ}{\sin 50^\circ}
\approx \frac{11 \times 0.9397}{0.7660} \approx 13.5 \text{ cm}.
$$

The side opposite the larger angle is longer, and $13.5 > 11$, which agrees.
</details>

**2.** In triangle $ABC$, side $b = 8$ cm, side $c = 11$ cm and angle
$A = 62^\circ$. Find side $a$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle sits between the two sides you know. That is the cosine rule, finding
the third side.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute into $a^2 = b^2 + c^2 - 2bc\cos A$ with $b = 8$, $c = 11$ and
$A = 62^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a^2 = 8^2 + 11^2 - 2 \times 8 \times 11 \times \cos 62^\circ.
$$

$$
a^2 = 64 + 121 - 176 \times 0.4695 \approx 185 - 82.6 = 102.4.
$$

$$
a \approx \sqrt{102.4} \approx 10.1 \text{ cm}.
$$
</details>

**3.** A triangle has sides $a = 6$ cm, $b = 7$ cm and $c = 10$ cm. Find angle
$C$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

All three sides are known. Use the cosine rule, rearranged to give the angle
opposite side $c$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

With $C$ opposite $c = 10$, write
$\cos C = \dfrac{6^2 + 7^2 - 10^2}{2 \times 6 \times 7}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\cos C = \frac{36 + 49 - 100}{84} = \frac{-15}{84} \approx -0.1786.
$$

$$
C = \cos^{-1}(-0.1786) \approx 100.3^\circ.
$$

The angle is obtuse, which is why $\cos C$ is negative.
</details>

**4.** Find the area of the triangle with $b = 9$ cm, $c = 14$ cm and included
angle $A = 48^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use area $= \tfrac{1}{2}bc\sin A$. The angle you need is the one between the two
sides.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute the two sides and the angle: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 14 \times
\sin 48^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{area} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 9 \times 14 \times \sin 48^\circ
= 63 \times 0.7431 \approx 46.8 \text{ cm}^2.
$$
</details>

**5.** In triangle $PQR$, side $p = 9$ cm, side $q = 12$ cm and angle
$P = 40^\circ$. Find the two possible sizes of angle $Q$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is the sine rule for an angle, so the ambiguous case applies. The
calculator will give one acute answer.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\dfrac{\sin Q}{12} = \dfrac{\sin 40^\circ}{9}$, giving
$\sin Q \approx 0.8570$. The acute answer is about $59.0^\circ$. The other is
$180^\circ - 59.0^\circ = 121.0^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\sin Q = \frac{12\sin 40^\circ}{9} \approx \frac{12 \times 0.6428}{9} \approx 0.8570.
$$

The two candidates are $Q \approx 59.0^\circ$ and $Q \approx 121.0^\circ$. Both
fit here: with $P = 40^\circ$, the remaining angles are $81.0^\circ$ and
$19.0^\circ$, and both are positive. So both triangles are possible.
</details>

**6.** A surveyor stands at point $A$. The distance to a tree $T$ is $80$ m and
the distance to a post $P$ is $110$ m. The angle $TAP$ is $65^\circ$. Find the
distance $TP$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw the triangle $ATP$ and label the two known sides and the angle between
them. That is the cosine rule.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$TP^2 = 80^2 + 110^2 - 2 \times 80 \times 110 \times \cos 65^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
TP^2 = 6400 + 12100 - 17600 \times 0.4226 \approx 18500 - 7438 = 11062.
$$

$$
TP \approx \sqrt{11062} \approx 105 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>
