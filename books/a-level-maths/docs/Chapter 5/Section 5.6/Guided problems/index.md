# 5.6 Secant, cosecant, cotangent and the inverse trigonometric functions — Practise with help

Write each new function as a fraction before you calculate. Then the reciprocal
and the domain are both visible.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out the reciprocal of $\dfrac{4}{5}$ and of $\dfrac{3}{4}$.
2. Use $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$ to find $\sin\theta$ when
   $\cos\theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$ and $\theta$ is acute.
3. Write down the value of $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The reciprocals are $\dfrac{5}{4}$ and $\dfrac{4}{3}$.
2. $\sin^2\theta = 1 - \dfrac{25}{169} = \dfrac{144}{169}$, so
   $\sin\theta = \dfrac{12}{13}$.
3. $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$.

If question 1 was hard, revise reciprocals before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write down the value of $\sec\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Secant is the reciprocal of cosine.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so take its reciprocal.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\sec\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{1}{\cos\frac{\pi}{3}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}} = 2.
$$
</details>

**2.** Write down the value of $\operatorname{cosec}\dfrac{\pi}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Cosecant is the reciprocal of sine.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = \dfrac{1}{2}$, so take its reciprocal.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\operatorname{cosec}\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{6}} = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}} = 2.
$$
</details>

**3.** Write down the value of $\cot\dfrac{\pi}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\cot\theta = \dfrac{\cos\theta}{\sin\theta}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both $\cos\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ and $\sin\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ equal $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
Divide them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\cot\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\cos\frac{\pi}{4}}{\sin\frac{\pi}{4}}
= \frac{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}}{\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}} = 1.
$$
</details>

**4.** Simplify $\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta - \cot^2\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the identity $1 + \cot^2\theta = \operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Replace $\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta$ with $1 + \cot^2\theta$, then subtract
$\cot^2\theta$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\operatorname{cosec}^2\theta - \cot^2\theta
= (1 + \cot^2\theta) - \cot^2\theta = 1.
$$
</details>

**5.** Given that $\cos\theta = \dfrac{5}{13}$ and $\theta$ is acute, find
$\sec\theta$ and $\operatorname{cosec}\theta$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Secant is immediate. For cosecant, find sine first with
$\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sec\theta = \dfrac{13}{5}$. Also
$\sin^2\theta = 1 - \dfrac{25}{169} = \dfrac{144}{169}$, so
$\sin\theta = \dfrac{12}{13}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\sec\theta = \frac{1}{\cos\theta} = \frac{13}{5}.
$$

$$
\operatorname{cosec}\theta = \frac{1}{\sin\theta} = \frac{13}{12}.
$$
</details>

**6.** Work out $\arcsin\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ in radians, giving the principal
value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask which angle in the principal range has a sine of $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The principal range of arcsine is $\left[-\dfrac{\pi}{2}, \dfrac{\pi}{2}\right]$.
Find the angle in that range whose sine is $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\arcsin\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = \frac{\pi}{3}.
$$

This is $60^\circ$, and it lies inside the principal range.
</details>
