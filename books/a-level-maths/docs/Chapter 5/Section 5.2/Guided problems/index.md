# 5.2 Radian measure, arc length and sector area — Practise with help

Before each question, write down whether the angle is in degrees or radians. The
arc and sector formulas need radians, so convert first when you need to.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out the circumference of a circle of radius $7$ cm, in terms of $\pi$.
2. Work out the area of the same circle, in terms of $\pi$.
3. Simplify $\dfrac{120}{180}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $C = 2\pi r = 14\pi$ cm.
2. $A = \pi r^2 = 49\pi$ cm$^2$.
3. $\dfrac{120}{180} = \dfrac{2}{3}$.

If question 3 was hard, revise simplifying fractions before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Change $135^\circ$ into radians, in terms of $\pi$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the number of degrees by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $135 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180}$ and simplify the fraction $\dfrac{135}{180}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
135 \times \frac{\pi}{180} = \frac{135\pi}{180} = \frac{3\pi}{4}.
$$
</details>

**2.** Change $\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ radians into degrees.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $\dfrac{180}{\pi}$. The $\pi$ cancels.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\dfrac{5}{6} \times 180$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\frac{5\pi}{6} \times \frac{180}{\pi} = \frac{5 \times 180}{6} = 150^\circ.
$$
</details>

**3.** A circle has radius $12$ cm. Find the length of the arc that subtends
$0.6$ radians at the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is already in radians, so use $l = r\theta$ directly.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $r = 12$ and $\theta = 0.6$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
l = r\theta = 12 \times 0.6 = 7.2 \text{ cm}.
$$
</details>

**4.** A sector has radius $9$ cm and angle $2$ radians. Find its area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$ with the angle in radians.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The radius squared is $81$. Work out $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 81 \times 2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 9^2 \times 2 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 81 \times 2 = 81 \text{ cm}^2.
$$
</details>

**5.** A sector has radius $10$ cm and angle $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ radians. Find the
length of the arc and the area of the sector.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is two jobs. Use $l = r\theta$ first, then $A = \tfrac{1}{2}r^2\theta$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the arc, work out $10 \times \dfrac{\pi}{4}$. For the area, work out
$\tfrac{1}{2} \times 100 \times \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
l = 10 \times \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{5\pi}{2} \approx 7.85 \text{ cm}.
$$

$$
A = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 100 \times \frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{25\pi}{2} \approx 39.3 \text{ cm}^2.
$$
</details>

**6.** A circle has radius $5$ cm. Find the area of the segment cut off by an
arc of angle $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ radians.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the sector, then subtract the triangle formed by the two radii. The
triangle uses $\tfrac{1}{2}r^2\sin\theta$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Sector $= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Triangle
$= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times \sin\dfrac{\pi}{6}$. Subtract.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{sector} = \frac{25\pi}{12} \approx 6.545.
$$

$$
\text{triangle} = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 25 \times \tfrac{1}{2} = 6.25.
$$

$$
\text{segment} = \frac{25\pi}{12} - 6.25 \approx 0.295 \text{ cm}^2.
$$
</details>
