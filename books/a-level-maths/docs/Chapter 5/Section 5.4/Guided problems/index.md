# 5.4 Exact trigonometric values — Practise with help

For each angle, find the quadrant first. Then find the related acute angle. Then
attach the sign. Write the three steps down so the method stays visible.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Simplify $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2}$ and $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3}$.
2. Find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with both shorter sides $1$.
3. Work out $180 - 150$ and $\dfrac{180}{150}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\sqrt{2} \times \sqrt{2} = 2$ and $\sqrt{3} \times \sqrt{3} = 3$.
2. $1^2 + 1^2 = 2$, so the hypotenuse is $\sqrt{2}$.
3. $180 - 150 = 30$ and $\dfrac{180}{150} = \dfrac{6}{5}$.

If question 1 was hard, revise simplifying surds before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write down the exact values of $\sin 60^\circ$ and $\cos 60^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

These come from the half-equilateral triangle with sides $1$, $\sqrt{3}$ and
$2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the $60^\circ$ angle, the side opposite it is $\sqrt{3}$ and the side next
to it is $1$. The hypotenuse is $2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \qquad \cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2}.
$$
</details>

**2.** Write down the exact value of $\tan 30^\circ$, in the form with a
rational denominator.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sin 30^\circ}{\cos 30^\circ}$, or read it from the
half-equilateral triangle.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

From the triangle, the side opposite $30^\circ$ is $1$ and the side next to it
is $\sqrt{3}$, so $\tan 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$. Rationalise the
denominator.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.
$$
</details>

**3.** Find the exact value of $\sin\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\dfrac{2\pi}{3}$ is $120^\circ$, in the second quadrant. Only sine is positive
there.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The related acute angle is $\pi - \dfrac{2\pi}{3} = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$, which is
$60^\circ$. So the answer is $+\sin 60^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\sin\frac{2\pi}{3} = +\sin\frac{\pi}{3} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}.
$$
</details>

**4.** Find the exact value of $\cos\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\dfrac{5\pi}{4}$ is $225^\circ$, in the third quadrant. Only tangent is
positive there.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The related acute angle is $\dfrac{5\pi}{4} - \pi = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$, which is
$45^\circ$. Cosine is negative in the third quadrant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\cos\frac{5\pi}{4} = -\cos\frac{\pi}{4} = -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.
$$
</details>

**5.** Find the exact value of $\tan\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ is $150^\circ$, in the second quadrant. Tangent is negative
there.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The related acute angle is $\pi - \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$, which is
$30^\circ$. So the answer is $-\tan 30^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\tan\frac{5\pi}{6} = -\tan 30^\circ = -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}.
$$
</details>

**6.** Find the exact value of $\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{6} + \cos\dfrac{\pi}{3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work out each term on its own. $\dfrac{7\pi}{6}$ is $210^\circ$, in the third
quadrant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin\dfrac{7\pi}{6} = -\sin\dfrac{\pi}{6} = -\dfrac{1}{2}$. Also
$\cos\dfrac{\pi}{3} = \dfrac{1}{2}$. Add the two values.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\sin\frac{7\pi}{6} + \cos\frac{\pi}{3} = -\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = 0.
$$
</details>
