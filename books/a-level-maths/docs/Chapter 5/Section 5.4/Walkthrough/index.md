# 5.4 Exact trigonometric values — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Pythagoras' theorem; simplifying surds; the four quadrants of
the coordinate plane; the graphs of sine, cosine and tangent.

**By the end of this section you can:** derive the exact values of sine, cosine
and tangent at $30^\circ$, $45^\circ$ and $60^\circ$; use them at any angle with
the correct sign; convert between degrees and radians for these angles.

## 1. Two special triangles

Two right-angled triangles give every exact value in this section.

**The half-square.** A square of side $1$ cut along a diagonal gives a
right-angled triangle with two $45^\circ$ angles. Its hypotenuse is
$\sqrt{1^2 + 1^2} = \sqrt{2}$. Therefore

$$
\sin 45^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \qquad
\cos 45^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \qquad
\tan 45^\circ = 1.
$$

**The half-equilateral triangle.** An equilateral triangle of side $2$ cut in
half gives a right-angled triangle with angles $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$. Its
sides are $1$, $\sqrt{3}$ and $2$. Therefore

$$
\sin 30^\circ = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad
\cos 30^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \qquad
\tan 30^\circ = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{3},
$$

$$
\sin 60^\circ = \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}, \qquad
\cos 60^\circ = \frac{1}{2}, \qquad
\tan 60^\circ = \sqrt{3}.
$$

## 2. The table to remember

| $\theta$ | $0$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{6}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{4}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{3}$ | $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| degrees | $0^\circ$ | $30^\circ$ | $45^\circ$ | $60^\circ$ | $90^\circ$ |
| $\sin\theta$ | $0$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $1$ |
| $\cos\theta$ | $1$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2}$ | $\dfrac{1}{2}$ | $0$ |
| $\tan\theta$ | $0$ | $\dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{3}$ | $1$ | $\sqrt{3}$ | undefined |

The sine row increases from $0$ to $1$. The cosine row decreases from $1$ to
$0$. That pattern is the quickest way to remember which surd belongs where.

## 3. Signs in the four quadrants

The exact values above are all positive. For angles outside the first quadrant,
find the **related acute angle** with the horizontal axis, then attach the sign.

| Quadrant | Angle range | Positive functions |
|---|---|---|
| first | $0$ to $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ | all |
| second | $\dfrac{\pi}{2}$ to $\pi$ | sine only |
| third | $\pi$ to $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ | tangent only |
| fourth | $\dfrac{3\pi}{2}$ to $2\pi$ | cosine only |

A common way to record this is the word **CAST**, read anticlockwise from the
fourth quadrant: cosine, all, sine, tangent.

**Example 1.** Find the exact value of $\sin\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$.

$\dfrac{5\pi}{6}$ is $150^\circ$, in the second quadrant, where sine is positive.
The related acute angle is $\pi - \dfrac{5\pi}{6} = \dfrac{\pi}{6}$. So

$$
\sin\frac{5\pi}{6} = +\sin\frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{1}{2}.
$$

**Example 2.** Find the exact value of $\cos\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$.

$\dfrac{4\pi}{3}$ is $240^\circ$, in the third quadrant, where cosine is
negative. The related acute angle is $\dfrac{4\pi}{3} - \pi = \dfrac{\pi}{3}$. So

$$
\cos\frac{4\pi}{3} = -\cos\frac{\pi}{3} = -\frac{1}{2}.
$$

**Example 3.** Find the exact value of $\tan\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$.

$\dfrac{7\pi}{4}$ is $315^\circ$, in the fourth quadrant, where tangent is
negative. The related acute angle is $2\pi - \dfrac{7\pi}{4} = \dfrac{\pi}{4}$.
So

$$
\tan\frac{7\pi}{4} = -\tan\frac{\pi}{4} = -1.
$$

> **Watch out.** Three mistakes are common with exact values.
>
> **Swapping sine and cosine.** At $30^\circ$, $\sin 30^\circ = \dfrac{1}{2}$ and
> $\cos 30^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$. The larger value goes with the smaller
> angle for sine. Check against the graph: sine starts near $0$ and cosine starts
> near $1$.
>
> **Losing the minus sign.** Find the quadrant first, then attach the sign. An
> answer such as $\cos 150^\circ = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ is missing a minus,
> because $150^\circ$ lies in the second quadrant and cosine is negative there.
>
> **Writing a value for $\tan 90^\circ$.** At $90^\circ$ the cosine is $0$, and
> division by zero is undefined. The tangent has an asymptote there. **Re-check:**
> for each answer, ask which quadrant the angle is in, and whether the function
> is positive there.

## You should now be able to

- derive the exact values from the half-square and the half-equilateral
  triangle;
- state the exact value of sine, cosine and tangent at $0^\circ$, $30^\circ$,
  $45^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$;
- use the same values in radians;
- find the related acute angle and attach the correct sign for any quadrant;
- explain why $\tan 90^\circ$ is undefined.
