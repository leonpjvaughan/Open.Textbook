# Chapter 4: Sequences and series

## The chapter problem: will the village hall roof be paid for?

The village hall needs a new roof. It will cost £9,000.

Two fundraising streams run at the same time. They both start in month 1.

- **The quiz night.** It raises £150 in month 1. Each month it raises £50 more
  than the month before.
- **The online appeal.** It raises £600 in month 1. Each month it raises 80% of
  the month before, because the appeal fades.

A former resident gives £2,000 at the start. The committee puts the gift in an
account that pays 6% compound interest each year.

By the end of the chapter you will answer all of this exactly. You will find how
much each stream raises in a chosen month, and how much each has raised after
one year. You will find the most the online appeal can ever raise. You will
decide whether the fund reaches £9,000, and when. You will also work out what
the £2,000 gift becomes, and estimate its value between whole years.

> **First contact — no working needed yet.** Work out the income for months 1,
> 2 and 3 of each stream. For the quiz night, add £50 each time. For the online
> appeal, work out 80% of the month before. Write the first three numbers of
> each stream, then write one sentence saying which stream you expect to raise
> more over a year.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>If you are not sure where to start</summary>

The quiz night starts at £150. The next month is $150 + 50 = 200$, and the
month after is $200 + 50 = 250$.

The online appeal starts at £600. Work out 80% of £600, which is
$0.80 \times 600 = 480$. Then work out 80% of that amount. The amounts fall,
but they fall by a smaller amount each time, so the appeal keeps going for a
long time.
</details>

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- use factorials and the notation $\binom{n}{r}$;
- expand $(a + bx)^n$ when $n$ is a positive whole number;
- find one term or one coefficient without expanding a whole bracket;
- expand $(1 + x)^n$ when $n$ is a fraction or negative, and state when the
  expansion is valid;
- use the first terms of an expansion to approximate a value;
- generate a sequence from a formula, or from a rule that links each term to
  the one before;
- decide whether a sequence increases, decreases or repeats;
- read and write a sum in sigma notation;
- use the standard results for $\sum r$, $\sum r^2$ and $\sum r^3$;
- find the $n$th term and the sum of an arithmetic sequence;
- find the $n$th term and the sum of a geometric sequence;
- find the sum to infinity of a geometric series when $|r| < 1$;
- build a sequence model, state its assumptions, and refine it.

## Where it is used

Sequences describe anything that changes in steps. Savings, populations,
medicine in the blood and the value of a machine all follow a rule from one
value to the next. Compound interest is a geometric sequence. The binomial
expansion is how a calculator estimates a power or a root. The coefficient
$\binom{n}{r}$ returns in statistics, where it counts the ways a result can
happen. Later chapters use these series as the building blocks of calculus.
