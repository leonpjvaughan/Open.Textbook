# Glossary — Statistics

**Addition rule** — the rule for finding the probability that at least one of two
events happens. For mutually exclusive events, the probabilities are added.

**Alternative hypothesis** — the claim that a hypothesis test suspects instead of
the null hypothesis, written $H_1$.

**Binomial distribution** — the distribution of the number of successes in $n$
independent trials, each with the same probability $p$ of success.

**Bivariate data** — data with two values recorded for each item.

**Conditional probability** — the probability of one event when you know that
another event has happened.

**Continuity correction** — the adjustment made when a whole-number binomial
variable is approximated by a continuous Normal variable.

**Correlation** — a measure of how two variables move together.

**Correlation coefficient** — a number between $-1$ and $1$ that measures the
strength and direction of a linear relationship, written $r$.

**Critical region** — the set of test statistic values that lead to rejecting the
null hypothesis.

**Critical value** — the value that separates the critical region from the
acceptance region.

**Data cleaning** — the process of finding and dealing with values that are wrong
or missing.

**Discrete random variable** — a variable whose value depends on the outcome of
an experiment and takes only separate, listed values.

**Explanatory variable** — the variable you choose or control in bivariate data;
it goes on the horizontal axis.

**Frequency density** — the frequency of a class divided by the class width; it
gives the height of a histogram bar.

**Hypothesis test** — a procedure that uses sample evidence to decide between two
claims about a population.

**Independent events** — two events where the chance of one does not change when
you know the other has happened.

**Interquartile range** — the difference between the upper and lower quartiles;
it measures the spread of the middle half of the data.

**Mutually exclusive** — two events that cannot both happen at the same time.

**Normal distribution** — a continuous model with a symmetric, bell-shaped curve,
fixed by its mean and its standard deviation.

**Null hypothesis** — the claim assumed at the start of a hypothesis test,
written $H_0$.

**Outlier** — a value that is unusually far from the rest of the data.

**p-value** — the probability of a result at least as extreme as the one
observed, assuming the null hypothesis is true.

**Population** — the whole group you want to know about.

**Probability distribution** — a list of the possible values of a discrete random
variable and their probabilities.

**Random variable** — a number whose value depends on the outcome of an
experiment.

**Regression line** — a straight line drawn through the middle of the points on a
scatter diagram, used to summarise the trend.

**Response variable** — the variable you measure in bivariate data.

**Sample** — a part of the population that you measure.

**Sample space** — the set of all possible outcomes of an experiment.

**Sampling frame** — the list of the population that you choose a sample from.

**Sampling variability** — the natural variation between the results of
different samples from the same population.

**Significance level** — the small probability, written $\alpha$, that a test is
allowed to reject a true null hypothesis by mistake.

**Standard deviation** — a measure of spread found from the root mean square
deviation from the mean.

**Standard error** — the standard deviation of a sample mean, equal to
$\dfrac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$ for a sample of size $n$.

**Standardising** — the change of variable $z = \dfrac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$ that
turns a Normal variable into the standard Normal variable.

**Stratified sampling** — a method that splits the population into groups and
takes a random sample from each group.

**Systematic sample** — a sample taken by choosing a random start and then every
$k$th member of an ordered list.

**Test statistic** — the value calculated from the sample and compared with the
hypothesis.
