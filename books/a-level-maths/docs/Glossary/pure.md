# Glossary — Pure Mathematics

**Amplitude** — the distance from the middle line of a wave graph to a peak.

**Arithmetic sequence** — a sequence that rises or falls by the same amount at
every step.

**Assumption** — in a statement of the form if A then B, the part A that is
taken as true.

**Asymptote** — a line that a curve approaches but never reaches.

**Chain rule** — the rule for differentiating a function of a function.

**Common ratio** — the fixed multiplier between consecutive terms of a geometric
sequence, written $r$.

**Conclusion** — in a statement of the form if A then B, the part B that follows
from the assumption.

**Counter example** — a single value that makes a "for all" statement false, and
so disproves it.

**Definite integral** — an integral worked out between two limits, which gives a
number.

**Derivative** — the gradient of a curve at a point, or the rate of change of one
quantity with respect to another.

**Differential equation** — an equation that links a derivative to the quantities
it describes.

**Discriminant** — the value $b^2 - 4ac$, which tells you how many real roots the
equation $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ has.

**Exponential function** — a function with the variable in the index, such as
$y = 2^x$ or $y = e^x$.

**Factor theorem** — the result that $(x - a)$ is a factor of a polynomial $f(x)$
exactly when $f(a) = 0$.

**Factorial** — the product of all the whole numbers from $1$ up to a given whole
number, written $n!$.

**Geometric sequence** — a sequence made by multiplying by the same amount at
every step.

**Gradient** — how steeply a line or curve rises; the change in the vertical
coordinate divided by the change in the horizontal coordinate.

**Iterative method** — a method that finds a root by applying the same rule again
and again, using each answer as the next input.

**Logarithm** — the power to which a base must be raised to give a number;
$\log_a x$ asks what power of $a$ gives $x$.

**Magnitude** — the length or size of a vector, written $|\mathbf{a}|$.

**Modulus** — the distance of a number from zero, so it is never negative.

**Natural logarithm** — the logarithm to base $e$, written $\ln x$.

**Newton–Raphson** — an iterative method for finding a root that uses the tangent
to the curve at each step.

**Parametric equations** — a pair of equations that give $x$ and $y$ separately
in terms of a third variable, called the parameter.

**Partial fractions** — the simpler fractions that a rational expression is split
into.

**Period** — the length of the interval over which a repeating graph repeats.

**Point of inflection** — a point where a curve changes from convex to concave,
or from concave to convex.

**Polynomial** — a sum of terms of the form $ax^n$, where $n$ is a whole number.

**Position vector** — the vector from the origin to a point.

**Product rule** — the rule for differentiating a product of two functions.

**Proof by contradiction** — a proof that assumes the statement is false and
works until it reaches an impossibility.

**Proof by deduction** — a proof that works from the assumption with algebra to
reach the conclusion.

**Proof by exhaustion** — a proof that checks every case in a finite set.

**Quotient rule** — the rule for differentiating a quotient of two functions.

**Radian** — the angle at the centre of a circle that cuts off an arc equal in
length to the radius.

**Recurrence relation** — a rule that links each term of a sequence to the one
before it.

**Remainder theorem** — the result that dividing a polynomial $f(x)$ by $(x - a)$
leaves the remainder $f(a)$.

**Resultant** — a single vector that has the same effect as two or more vectors
together.

**Root** — a value of the variable that makes the two sides of an equation equal.

**Scalar** — a quantity that has a size but no direction.

**Sequence** — an ordered list of numbers.

**Series** — the total of the terms of a sequence.

**Sigma notation** — a short way of writing a sum, using the symbol $\sum$.

**Statement** — a sentence that is either true or false.

**Stationary point** — a point on a curve where the gradient is zero.

**Surd** — a root that cannot be written as an exact fraction.

**Tangent** — a straight line that touches a curve at a point and has the same
gradient as the curve there.

**Trapezium rule** — a formula that estimates a definite integral by adding the
areas of trapezium strips.

**Vector** — a quantity that has both a size and a direction.
