# 15.4 The constant-acceleration formulae — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Write down $u$, $v$, $a$, $s$ and $t$ and
mark the three you know. That tells you which formula to use.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Rearrange $v = u + at$ to make $t$ the subject.
2. Work out $5 \times 4 + \frac{1}{2} \times 3 \times 16$.
3. Solve $0 = 30 - 5t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $v - u = at$, so $t = \dfrac{v - u}{a}$.
2. $20 + 24 = 44$.
3. $5t = 30$, so $t = 6$.

If question 1 was hard, revise rearranging formulae before you continue. Every
formula on this page has to be rearranged at some point.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A cyclist is moving at $4$ m/s and accelerates at $6$ m/s$^2$ for $5$ s.
Find the final velocity and the displacement.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You know $u = 4$, $a = 6$ and $t = 5$. Use the formula that has those three
letters and the one you want.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the velocity, use $v = u + at$. For the displacement, use
$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
v = 4 + 6 \times 5 = 34 \text{ m/s},
$$

$$
s = 4 \times 5 + \frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 5^2 = 20 + 75 = 95 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>

**2.** A car starts from rest and accelerates at $4$ m/s$^2$ for $7$ s. Find its
final velocity and the distance travelled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

"From rest" means the initial velocity is $0$. So $u = 0$, $a = 4$ and
$t = 7$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

With $u = 0$, the formula $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ simplifies to
$s = \frac{1}{2}at^2$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
v = 0 + 4 \times 7 = 28 \text{ m/s},
$$

$$
s = 0 \times 7 + \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 7^2 = 98 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>

**3.** A car travelling at $20$ m/s brakes uniformly and stops in $8$ s. Find
the acceleration and the distance travelled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The final velocity is $0$, because the car stops. So $u = 20$, $v = 0$ and
$t = 8$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v = u + at$ for the acceleration. For the distance, use
$s = \dfrac{u + v}{2}t$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
0 = 20 + 8a,
\qquad
a = -2.5 \text{ m/s}^2,
$$

$$
s = \frac{20 + 0}{2} \times 8 = 80 \text{ m}.
$$

The acceleration is negative, as expected for braking.
</details>

**4.** A train moves $100$ m while accelerating uniformly from $8$ m/s to
$28$ m/s. Find the acceleration and the time taken.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You know $u = 8$, $v = 28$ and $s = 100$. The time is not wanted first, so
use the formula without $t$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ for the acceleration. Then use $v = u + at$ for the time.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
28^2 = 8^2 + 2a \times 100,
\qquad
784 = 64 + 200a,
\qquad
a = \frac{720}{200} = 3.6 \text{ m/s}^2.
$$

$$
28 = 8 + 3.6t,
\qquad
t = \frac{20}{3.6} = \frac{200}{36} = \frac{50}{9} \approx 5.56 \text{ s}.
$$
</details>

**5.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at $15$ m/s. Find its velocity and
its height after $2$ s. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$ and upwards as positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

With upwards positive, $u = 15$ and $a = -9.8$. Use $t = 2$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v = u + at$ for the velocity, then $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ for the
height.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
v = 15 - 9.8 \times 2 = 15 - 19.6 = -4.6 \text{ m/s},
$$

$$
s = 15 \times 2 - \frac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times 4 = 30 - 19.6 = 10.4 \text{ m}.
$$

The velocity is negative, so the ball is already falling at $2$ s.
</details>

**6.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at $19.6$ m/s. Find the greatest
height it reaches and the time it takes to get there. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the greatest height the ball is instantaneously at rest, so $v = 0$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ with $v = 0$ and $a = -9.8$ to find $s$. Then use
$v = u + at$ to find $t$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
0 = 19.6^2 + 2(-9.8)s,
\qquad
19.6s = 384.16,
\qquad
s = 19.6 \text{ m}.
$$

$$
0 = 19.6 - 9.8t,
\qquad
t = 2 \text{ s}.
$$
</details>

**7.** A car travelling at $25$ m/s brakes at $5$ m/s$^2$ until it stops. Find
the stopping distance and the time taken.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The car stops, so $v = 0$. With forwards positive, $a = -5$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ for the distance, then $v = u + at$ for the time.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
0 = 25^2 + 2(-5)s,
\qquad
10s = 625,
\qquad
s = 62.5 \text{ m}.
$$

$$
0 = 25 - 5t,
\qquad
t = 5 \text{ s}.
$$
</details>
