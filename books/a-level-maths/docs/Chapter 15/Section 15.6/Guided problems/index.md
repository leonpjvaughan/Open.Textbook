# 15.6 Motion under gravity and projectiles using vectors — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Resolve the initial velocity before you touch
any formula, and write down the positive direction. The two components share one
time $t$.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Resolve a velocity of $10$ m/s at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal into its
   components.
2. Write $20\cos 45^\circ$ as an exact surd.
3. Solve $0 = 10 - 9.8t$, giving $t$ to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Horizontal $= 10\cos 30^\circ = 5\sqrt{3} \approx 8.66$ m/s, and vertical
   $= 10\sin 30^\circ = 5$ m/s.
2. $20 \times \dfrac{\sqrt{2}}{2} = 10\sqrt{2}$.
3. $t = \dfrac{10}{9.8} \approx 1.02$.

If question 1 was hard, revise resolving vectors from Section 5.12 before you
continue. Every projectile on this page starts with that step.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A ball is projected at $24$ m/s at $30^\circ$ to the horizontal. Find
the horizontal and vertical components of its initial velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $u_x = U\cos\theta$ and $u_y = U\sin\theta$. The angle is measured from the
horizontal.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $24\cos 30^\circ$ and $24\sin 30^\circ$. Keep the surds exact.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
u_x = 24\cos 30^\circ = 24 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} = 12\sqrt{3}
\approx 20.8 \text{ m/s},
$$

$$
u_y = 24\sin 30^\circ = 24 \times \frac{1}{2} = 12 \text{ m/s}.
$$
</details>

**2.** For the ball in question 1, find the time to the greatest height. Take
$g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the greatest height the vertical velocity is zero. Use the vertical
component only.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Set $0 = u_y - gt$ with $u_y = 12$ and $g = 9.8$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
0 = 12 - 9.8t,
\qquad
t = \frac{12}{9.8} = \frac{120}{98} = \frac{60}{49} \approx 1.22 \text{ s}.
$$
</details>

**3.** For the ball in question 1, find the total time of flight and the range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The ball lands at the height it started from, so the total time is twice the
time to the greatest height. The range is the horizontal velocity times the
total time.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Double the time from question 2. Then multiply $u_x = 12\sqrt{3}$ by that total
time.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
T = 2 \times \frac{60}{49} = \frac{120}{49} \approx 2.45 \text{ s},
$$

$$
R = u_x T = 12\sqrt{3} \times \frac{120}{49} =
\frac{1440\sqrt{3}}{49} \approx 50.9 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>

**4.** For the ball in question 1, find the greatest height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the vertical component with $v_y = 0$ at the top, and the formula
$v_y^2 = u_y^2 - 2gy$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0 = 12^2 - 2 \times 9.8 \times H$. Rearrange for $H$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
0 = 144 - 19.6H,
\qquad
H = \frac{144}{19.6} = \frac{360}{49} \approx 7.35 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>

**5.** A ball is kicked at $30$ m/s at $60^\circ$ to the horizontal. Find its
vertical velocity and its height after $2$ s. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$ and upwards
as positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Resolve to get $u_y = 30\sin 60^\circ = 15\sqrt{3}$. Then use $v_y = u_y - gt$
and $y = u_y t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

At $t = 2$: $v_y = 15\sqrt{3} - 19.6$ and
$y = 30\sqrt{3} - 19.6$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
v_y = 15\sqrt{3} - 9.8 \times 2 = 15\sqrt{3} - 19.6 \approx 6.38 \text{ m/s},
$$

$$
y = 15\sqrt{3} \times 2 - \frac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times 2^2 =
30\sqrt{3} - 19.6 \approx 32.4 \text{ m}.
$$

The vertical velocity is still positive, so the ball is rising at $2$ s.
</details>

**6.** A ball is hit horizontally at $12$ m/s from the top of a cliff that is
$25$ m high. Find the time it takes to reach the ground and the horizontal
distance travelled. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The initial vertical velocity is $0$. Take downwards as positive for the fall
and use $s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$ with $s = 25$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$25 = 4.9t^2$, so $t^2 = \dfrac{25}{4.9} = \dfrac{250}{49}$. Then use
$x = 12t$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{250}{49}} = \frac{5\sqrt{10}}{7} \approx 2.26 \text{ s},
$$

$$
x = 12 \times \frac{5\sqrt{10}}{7} = \frac{60\sqrt{10}}{7} \approx 27.1 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>

**7.** A ball is kicked from the ground at $20$ m/s at $45^\circ$. Find its
height after it has travelled $30$ m horizontally. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First find the time from the horizontal equation $x = u_x t$, with
$u_x = 10\sqrt{2}$. Then substitute that time into the vertical equation.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$30 = 10\sqrt{2}\,t$, so $t = \dfrac{3}{\sqrt{2}} = \dfrac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}$.
Substitute this into $y = 10\sqrt{2}\,t - 4.9t^2$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
t = \frac{30}{10\sqrt{2}} = \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2},
$$

$$
y = 10\sqrt{2} \times \frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}

- 4.9 \times \left(\frac{3\sqrt{2}}{2}\right)^2
= 30 - 4.9 \times \frac{18}{4}
= 30 - 22.05 = 7.95 \text{ m}.
$$

The ball is about $7.95$ m high when it has travelled $30$ m horizontally.
</details>
