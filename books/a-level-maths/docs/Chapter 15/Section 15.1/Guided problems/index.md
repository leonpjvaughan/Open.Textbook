# 15.1 Fundamental and derived SI quantities and units — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Before you write a number, decide which
quantity it measures and which unit belongs with it.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $1000 \div 3600$ and give the answer as a fraction in its lowest
   terms.
2. Work out $2.5 \times 1000$ and $45 \div 1000$.
3. A car travels $120$ m in $8$ s. Work out its average speed in m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\dfrac{1000}{3600} = \dfrac{5}{18}$.
2. $2.5 \times 1000 = 2500$, and $45 \div 1000 = 0.045$.
3. Average speed $= \dfrac{120}{8} = 15$ m/s.

If question 1 was hard, revise simplifying fractions before you continue.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write down the SI unit of length, the SI unit of mass and the SI unit of
time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

These three units are the base of every other unit in mechanics. They measure a
distance, an amount of matter and a duration.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Give the name and the symbol of each unit. The base unit of mass is the
kilogram, not the gram.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Length is measured in metres, m. Mass is measured in kilograms, kg. Time is
measured in seconds, s.
</details>

**2.** Write the unit of acceleration in terms of the base units of length and
time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Acceleration is the change in velocity each second. Build it from the unit of
velocity first.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Velocity is metres divided by seconds, so its unit is m/s. Divide that by
seconds again for acceleration.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{acceleration} = \frac{\text{velocity}}{\text{time}} =
\frac{\text{m/s}}{\text{s}} = \text{m/s}^2 = \text{m s}^{-2}.
$$
</details>

**3.** Convert $4.5$ km to metres, and $60$ cm to metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The prefix tells you the multiplier. Kilo means $1000$, and centi means
$0.01$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the kilometres by $1000$. Divide the centimetres by $100$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
4.5 \text{ km} = 4.5 \times 1000 = 4500 \text{ m},
\qquad
60 \text{ cm} = 60 \div 100 = 0.6 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>

**4.** Convert $54$ km/h to m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply by $\dfrac{5}{18}$ to change km/h into m/s. If you forget the
fraction, remember that $1$ km/h is $1000$ m in $3600$ s.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $54 \times 5$ first, then divide by $18$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
54 \times \frac{5}{18} = \frac{270}{18} = 15 \text{ m/s}.
$$
</details>

**5.** Find the weight of a mass of $12$ kg. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Weight is a force, so use $W = mg$. The answer carries the unit of force.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $m = 12$ and $g = 9.8$ into $W = mg$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
W = mg = 12 \times 9.8 = 117.6 \text{ N}.
$$
</details>

**6.** Find the moment of a force of $15$ N acting at a perpendicular distance
of $0.3$ m from a pivot.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A moment is a force multiplied by a perpendicular distance. The unit is the
newton metre.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply $15$ by $0.3$. Keep the unit of force and the unit of distance
together as N m.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{moment} = 15 \times 0.3 = 4.5 \text{ N m}.
$$
</details>

**7.** A force of $24$ N acts on a mass of $6$ kg. Find the acceleration, and
state its unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start from $F = ma$ and rearrange it for the acceleration.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the force by the mass: $a = \dfrac{F}{m}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{24}{6} = 4 \text{ m/s}^2.
$$

The unit is m/s$^2$, which comes from dividing a force in N by a mass in kg.
</details>

**8.** A car travels $150$ m in $12$ s. Find its average speed in m/s, then
convert that speed to km/h.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the speed first. For the second part, m/s is the smaller unit, so the
number in km/h must be larger.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide $150$ by $12$ for the speed. Then multiply the speed by $\dfrac{18}{5}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{speed} = \frac{150}{12} = 12.5 \text{ m/s}.
$$

$$
12.5 \times \frac{18}{5} = 12.5 \times 3.6 = 45 \text{ km/h}.
$$

The answer is larger than $12.5$, as expected, because one hour is a long time
and $1$ km is a long distance.
</details>
