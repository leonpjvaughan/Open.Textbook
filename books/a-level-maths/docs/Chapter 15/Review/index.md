# Chapter 15: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

A free kick is taken from the ground, $40$ m from the goal line. The ball leaves
the ground at $20$ m/s at $45^\circ$ to the horizontal. The crossbar is $2.44$ m
high. Model the ball as a particle, ignore air resistance, and take
$g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

**Step 1. Check the units.** Length is measured in metres, m; time in seconds,
s; velocity in m/s; acceleration in m/s$^2$. The mass of the ball is not needed,
because the motion is fixed by the initial velocity and gravity alone.

**Step 2. Resolve the initial velocity.** The launch angle is $45^\circ$, so
the two components are equal:

$$
u_x = 20\cos 45^\circ = 10\sqrt{2} \approx 14.1 \text{ m/s},
\qquad
u_y = 20\sin 45^\circ = 10\sqrt{2} \approx 14.1 \text{ m/s}.
$$

There is no horizontal acceleration, so $v_x = 10\sqrt{2}$ throughout the
flight.

**Step 3. Find the time to the goal line.** The horizontal distance is $40$ m,
and $x = u_x t$:

$$
40 = 10\sqrt{2} t,
\qquad
t = \frac{40}{10\sqrt{2}} = \frac{4}{\sqrt{2}} = 2\sqrt{2} \approx 2.83 \text{ s}.
$$

**Step 4. Find the height at that time.** Use the vertical equation with
upwards positive, $a = -9.8$:

$$
y = u_y t - \frac{1}{2}gt^2 =
10\sqrt{2} \times 2\sqrt{2} - \frac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times (2\sqrt{2})^2.
$$

Now $10\sqrt{2} \times 2\sqrt{2} = 40$, and $(2\sqrt{2})^2 = 8$, so

$$
y = 40 - 4.9 \times 8 = 40 - 39.2 = 0.8 \text{ m}.
$$

**Step 5. Compare with the crossbar.** The ball is $0.8$ m above the ground when
it reaches the goal line. The crossbar is $2.44$ m above the ground. Since

$$
0.8 < 2.44,
$$

the ball passes under the crossbar. It does not go over the bar.

**Step 6. Check with the vertical velocity.** From $v_y = u_y - gt$,

$$
v_y = 10\sqrt{2} - 9.8 \times 2\sqrt{2} = (10 - 19.6)\sqrt{2} = -9.6\sqrt{2}
\approx -13.6 \text{ m/s}.
$$

The vertical velocity is negative, so the ball is falling as it crosses the
line. It is on its way down and still below the bar, which agrees with Step 5.

**Step 7. The rest of the flight.** The greatest height is

$$
H = \frac{u_y^2}{2g} = \frac{200}{19.6} \approx 10.2 \text{ m},
$$

reached at $t = \dfrac{u_y}{g} = \dfrac{10\sqrt{2}}{9.8} \approx 1.44$ s. The
range over level ground is

$$
R = \frac{U^2 \sin 2\theta}{g} = \frac{400}{9.8} \approx 40.8 \text{ m}.
$$

So the ball crosses the goal line at $0.8$ m and would land $40.8$ m from the
kick, past the line.

**Step 8. Check the model.** The calculation treats the ball as a particle and
ignores air resistance. Drag would reduce both the height and the range, so a
real ball would be lower at the goal line. The conclusion that the ball passes
under the bar is therefore safe for this kick. The same model would need
adjusting for a strong wind or a spinning ball.

**The chapter's answer.** The ball reaches the goal line at $0.8$ m above the
ground, below the $2.44$ m crossbar. The free kick does not go over the bar. It
passes under the bar, so it can be a goal. The greatest height of the flight is
about $10.2$ m, and the ball would land about $40.8$ m from where it was kicked.

## What you can now do

- name the fundamental SI quantities and build derived units from them;
- convert between units, including km/h and m/s;
- use the language of position, displacement, distance, speed, velocity and
  acceleration;
- read the gradient of a displacement–time graph and the gradient and area of a
  velocity–time graph;
- choose and use the constant-acceleration formulae;
- move between displacement, velocity and acceleration with calculus;
- treat a projectile as two constant-acceleration motions sharing one time;
- resolve an initial velocity, and find a time of flight, a greatest height, a
  range and a height at a given distance;
- check a model and say what has been assumed.
