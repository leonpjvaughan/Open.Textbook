# 15.2 The language of kinematics — Learn

**Prerequisites:** positive and negative numbers on a number line; using
$\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$; the SI units of length
and time from Section 15.1.

**By the end of this section you can:** describe the position and the
displacement of a moving object; tell distance travelled apart from
displacement; tell speed apart from velocity; find an average speed, an average
velocity and an acceleration; use a sign convention correctly.

## 1. Position and displacement

Every moving object needs a starting point. Choose a fixed point, called the
**origin**, and a positive direction along a straight line. The **position** of
the object is its place on that line, measured from the origin.

The **displacement** is the change in position. Displacement is a vector: it has
a size and a direction. Along a line, the direction is carried by the sign.
Moving in the positive direction gives a positive displacement; moving the other
way gives a negative displacement.

If an object starts at position $s_0$ and finishes at position $s_1$, then

$$
\text{displacement} = s_1 - s_0.
$$

**Example 1.** A cyclist starts at a point $O$ and rides $5$ km east, then
$2$ km west. Taking east as positive, find the final position and the
displacement.

Start at $0$. Riding east adds $5$ km, and riding west subtracts $2$ km:

$$
s_1 = 0 + 5 - 2 = 3 \text{ km}.
$$

The final position is $3$ km east of $O$. The displacement is $3$ km east, or
$+3$ km.

## 2. Distance travelled

The **distance travelled** is the total length of the path, whatever the
direction. It is a scalar, so it has a size but no direction. Distance travelled
is never negative.

In Example 1 the cyclist rides $5$ km and then $2$ km, so the distance travelled
is

$$
5 + 2 = 7 \text{ km}.
$$

The distance is $7$ km, while the displacement is $3$ km east. They are equal
only when the object moves in one direction without turning back.

**Example 2.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards and caught at the same
height. Find the distance travelled and the displacement when the ball has risen
$4$ m.

The ball rises $4$ m and falls $4$ m, so the distance travelled is $8$ m. It
finishes at the height it started from, so the displacement is $0$ m.

## 3. Speed and velocity

**Speed** is the rate at which distance is covered. **Velocity** is the rate of
change of displacement. Velocity is a vector, so it can be positive or negative.
The two differ whenever the object turns around.

For motion over a time $t$:

$$
\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{distance travelled}}{t},
\qquad
\text{average velocity} = \frac{\text{displacement}}{t}.
$$

The unit of both is m/s. Speed is the magnitude of velocity, so speed is never
negative.

**Example 3.** A car travels $240$ m due east in $12$ s, then $60$ m due west
in $4$ s. Find the average speed and the average velocity over the whole journey.

The distance travelled is $240 + 60 = 300$ m, over a total time of $16$ s:

$$
\text{average speed} = \frac{300}{16} = 18.75 \text{ m/s}.
$$

The displacement is $240 - 60 = 180$ m due east, so

$$
\text{average velocity} = \frac{180}{16} = 11.25 \text{ m/s due east}.
$$

The average speed is larger, because the car doubled back.

## 4. Acceleration

**Acceleration** is the rate of change of velocity. Like velocity, it is a
vector, so its sign shows its direction along the line.

For an object whose velocity changes from $u$ to $v$ in a time $t$:

$$
\text{average acceleration} = \frac{v - u}{t}.
$$

The unit is m/s$^2$. A positive acceleration means the velocity is increasing in
the positive direction. A negative acceleration means the velocity is changing
in the negative direction.

**Example 4.** A train slows from $30$ m/s to $10$ m/s in $5$ s while travelling
east. Taking east as positive, find its acceleration.

$$
a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{10 - 30}{5} = \frac{-20}{5} = -4 \text{ m/s}^2.
$$

The acceleration is $-4$ m/s$^2$. The negative sign means the acceleration
points west, opposite to the direction of travel. The train is slowing down.

**Example 5.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at $12$ m/s. It rises, stops
for an instant and falls back. Taking upwards as positive, write down its
acceleration while it is in the air.

Gravity acts downwards throughout, even at the highest point. Taking upwards as
positive, the acceleration is $-9.8$ m/s$^2$ at every moment of the flight.

## 5. Try it: distance and displacement

**Predict first.** A walker starts at $0$ and walks to a turning point, then
walks back to a finishing point. Before you move the sliders, predict when the
distance travelled and the displacement are equal. Write down your prediction.

:::figure ch15-distance-displacement

**Test.** Move the two sliders so the turning point is to the right of the
finishing point, then so it is to the left. Watch both readouts.

**Explain.** Write two sentences. The first says why the displacement can be
smaller than the distance travelled. The second says what the readouts show when
the walker finishes back at $0$.

> **Watch out.** Three mistakes are common with this vocabulary.
>
> **Distance used as displacement.** The distance travelled is the length of the
> path, and the displacement is the change in position. A runner who runs one
> lap of a track travels $400$ m but has a displacement of $0$ m, because the
> finish is the starting point.
>
> **A negative velocity read as a negative speed.** Speed is never negative.
> A velocity of $-8$ m/s means the object moves at $8$ m/s in the negative
> direction. The size is $8$ m/s.
>
> **A negative acceleration called slowing down.** The sign of the acceleration
> says which way the velocity is changing, not whether the object is slowing.
> A ball thrown upwards has a negative acceleration on the way up and on the way
> down. **Re-check:** decide the positive direction first, then give every
> vector its sign before you calculate.

## You should now be able to

- define position, displacement and distance travelled;
- find a displacement from a starting position and a finishing position;
- find an average speed and an average velocity;
- tell when the average speed and the average velocity of a journey differ;
- find an average acceleration from a change in velocity;
- use a sign convention and interpret a negative velocity or acceleration.
