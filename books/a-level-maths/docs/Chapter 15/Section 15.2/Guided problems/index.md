# 15.2 The language of kinematics — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Before you calculate, choose a positive
direction and write down the sign of every vector you will use.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Convert $36$ km/h to m/s.
2. Work out $(-3) + 7$ and $4 - 9$.
3. A car travels $180$ m in $9$ s. Work out its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $36 \times \dfrac{5}{18} = 10$ m/s.
2. $(-3) + 7 = 4$, and $4 - 9 = -5$.
3. Average speed $= \dfrac{180}{9} = 20$ m/s.

If question 2 was hard, revise adding and subtracting negative numbers before
you continue. Every sign in this section depends on that skill.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A walker walks $300$ m north, then turns and walks $100$ m south. Find
the distance travelled and the displacement. Take north as positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Distance travelled adds the lengths of the two stages. Displacement uses the
signs of the two stages.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $300$ and $100$ for the distance. For the displacement, add $+300$ and
$-100$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Distance travelled $= 300 + 100 = 400$ m.

Displacement $= (+300) + (-100) = +200$ m, which is $200$ m north.

The walker doubled back, so the distance is larger than the size of the
displacement.
</details>

**2.** A runner completes one lap of a $400$ m track in $80$ s. Find the
distance travelled, the displacement and the average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The finish of a lap is the starting point. Think about the change in position
over the whole lap.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The distance is the whole lap. For the displacement, compare the finish with
the start.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Distance travelled $= 400$ m. The runner finishes where they started, so the
displacement is $0$ m.

Average speed $= \dfrac{400}{80} = 5$ m/s.

The average velocity is $0$ m/s, because the displacement is zero.
</details>

**3.** A car travels $150$ m due east in $10$ s, then continues $50$ m due east
in $5$ s. Find the average speed and the average velocity over the whole
journey.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The car does not turn around, so the distance travelled and the size of the
displacement are equal for this journey.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the two distances and the two times. Then divide.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Total distance $= 150 + 50 = 200$ m, and total time $= 10 + 5 = 15$ s.

$$
\text{average speed} = \frac{200}{15} = 13\frac{1}{3} \text{ m/s}.
$$

The displacement is $200$ m due east, so the average velocity is also
$13\frac{1}{3}$ m/s due east.
</details>

**4.** A cyclist speeds up from $5$ m/s to $20$ m/s in $3$ s. Find the
acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time. Subtract the
starting velocity from the finishing velocity.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The change in velocity is $20 - 5 = 15$ m/s. Divide by $3$ s.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{20 - 5}{3} = \frac{15}{3} = 5 \text{ m/s}^2.
$$

The acceleration is positive, and the cyclist is speeding up.
</details>

**5.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at $9.8$ m/s. It returns to the
thrower's hand $2.0$ s later, moving downwards at $9.8$ m/s. Taking upwards as
positive, find the acceleration of the ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The starting velocity is $+9.8$ m/s and the finishing velocity is $-9.8$ m/s.
Use the acceleration formula with those signs.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The change in velocity is $(-9.8) - (+9.8) = -19.6$ m/s. Divide by $2.0$ s.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{-9.8 - 9.8}{2.0} = \frac{-19.6}{2.0} =
-9.8 \text{ m/s}^2.
$$

The acceleration is $-9.8$ m/s$^2$ throughout the flight. The negative sign
means gravity points downwards, opposite to the positive direction.
</details>

**6.** A cyclist rides east at $6$ m/s, turns around and later rides west at
$6$ m/s. Taking east as positive, write down the velocity before and after the
turn. Has the speed changed?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Velocity needs a direction, and the sign carries it. Speed is the size of the
velocity.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Before the turn, the velocity is in the positive direction. After the turn, it
is in the negative direction with the same size.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Before the turn the velocity is $+6$ m/s, or $6$ m/s east. After the turn the
velocity is $-6$ m/s, or $6$ m/s west. The speed is $6$ m/s in both cases, so
the speed has not changed but the velocity has.
</details>

**7.** A car has velocity $-15$ m/s and acceleration $+3$ m/s$^2$. Is the car
speeding up or slowing down? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the directions of the velocity and the acceleration. The velocity is
negative, so the car moves in the negative direction.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The acceleration is positive, which is opposite to the velocity. Decide what
happens to the size of the velocity when the two point in opposite directions.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The velocity is $-15$ m/s, so the car moves in the negative direction. The
acceleration is $+3$ m/s$^2$, which points in the positive direction, opposite
to the motion. The acceleration reduces the size of the velocity, so the car is
slowing down. After $5$ s the velocity would be $-15 + 3 \times 5 = 0$ m/s.
</details>
