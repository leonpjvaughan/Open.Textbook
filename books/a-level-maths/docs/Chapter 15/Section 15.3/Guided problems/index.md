# 15.3 Displacement–time and velocity–time graphs — Practise with help

Work each one first, closed book. Before you use a graph, decide whether the
gradient or the area answers the question. Watch the sign of every area below
the time axis.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Find the gradient of the straight line through $(0, 3)$ and $(4, 11)$.
2. Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides $6$ and $10$ and height $5$.
3. Work out $\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Gradient $= \dfrac{11 - 3}{4 - 0} = \dfrac{8}{4} = 2$.
2. Area $= \dfrac{6 + 10}{2} \times 5 = 8 \times 5 = 40$.
3. $\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 8 = 24$.

If question 1 was hard, revise the gradient of a straight line before you
continue. Every velocity and acceleration on this page comes from a gradient.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A displacement–time graph is a straight line through $(0, 0)$ and
$(5, 15)$, with $s$ in metres and $t$ in seconds. Find the velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The velocity is the gradient of the displacement–time graph.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the change in displacement by the change in time:
$\dfrac{15 - 0}{5 - 0}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
v = \frac{15 - 0}{5 - 0} = 3 \text{ m/s}.
$$

The line is straight, so the velocity is constant.
</details>

**2.** A displacement–time graph is horizontal from $t = 2$ s to $t = 9$ s at
$s = 12$ m. Find the velocity in that interval.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A horizontal line has zero gradient. The displacement does not change.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The change in displacement is $0$ m over $7$ s.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
v = \frac{12 - 12}{9 - 2} = 0 \text{ m/s}.
$$

The object is at rest from $2$ s to $9$ s.
</details>

**3.** A velocity–time graph is a straight line from $(0, 8)$ to $(4, 20)$, with
$v$ in m/s and $t$ in s. Find the acceleration and the distance travelled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the gradient for the acceleration and the area for the distance.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The gradient is $\dfrac{20 - 8}{4}$. The area is the trapezium
$\dfrac{8 + 20}{2} \times 4$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{20 - 8}{4} = 3 \text{ m/s}^2,
\qquad
s = \frac{8 + 20}{2} \times 4 = 14 \times 4 = 56 \text{ m}.
$$
</details>

**4.** A velocity–time graph is a straight line from $(0, 15)$ to $(5, 0)$.
Find the acceleration and the distance travelled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The line falls, so the gradient is negative. The area is still a triangle,
because the line stays above the axis.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The gradient is $\dfrac{0 - 15}{5}$. The area is
$\frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 15$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
a = \frac{0 - 15}{5} = -3 \text{ m/s}^2,
\qquad
s = \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 15 = 37.5 \text{ m}.
$$

The negative acceleration shows the object is slowing down.
</details>

**5.** A velocity–time graph has three straight segments: $(0, 0)$ to
$(4, 8)$, $(4, 8)$ to $(10, 8)$, and $(10, 8)$ to $(14, 0)$. Find the total
distance travelled and the acceleration on the last segment.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Split the graph into a triangle, a rectangle and a triangle. The acceleration on
the last segment comes from its gradient.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The areas are $\frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 8$, $6 \times 8$ and
$\frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 8$. The last gradient is $\dfrac{0 - 8}{14 - 10}$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{distance} = 16 + 48 + 16 = 80 \text{ m},
\qquad
a = \frac{0 - 8}{4} = -2 \text{ m/s}^2.
$$

The train speeds up, travels at a steady speed, then slows down.
</details>

**6.** A velocity–time graph is a straight line from $(0, 6)$ to $(2, -6)$,
with $v$ in m/s. Find the displacement and the distance travelled over the two
seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are two triangles, one above the axis and one below it. Give the lower
one a negative sign for the displacement.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The upper triangle has area $\frac{1}{2} \times 1 \times 6 = 3$, and the lower
triangle has the same size.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Displacement $= 3 - 3 = 0$ m, and distance travelled $= 3 + 3 = 6$ m.

The line crosses the axis at $t = 1$ s, so the object turns around and returns
to its starting point.
</details>

**7.** A velocity–time graph is a straight line from $(0, 12)$ to $(6, 0)$, and
then a straight line from $(6, 0)$ to $(10, -4)$. Find the displacement and the
distance travelled over the whole $10$ s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The first area is above the axis and the second is below it. Add the second with
a negative sign for the displacement.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The first triangle has area $\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 12 = 36$. The second
has area $\frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 4 = 8$.

</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Displacement $= 36 - 8 = 28$ m. Distance travelled $= 36 + 8 = 44$ m.

The object moves forward, stops at $6$ s, then moves backwards.
</details>
