# 6.1 Superposition and interference — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Keep the wavelength and the path difference in the same unit, and
work in metres.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wave has a frequency of 250 Hz. Find its period.
2. Write 0.60 mm in metres.
3. A car travels 150 m in 6.0 s. Find its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $T = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{250} = 4.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{s}$.
2. $0.60\,\text{mm} = 6.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}$.
3. $v = \dfrac{s}{t} = \dfrac{150}{6.0} = 25\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, remember that the period is the time for one wave, so
it is one divided by the frequency. If question 2 goes wrong, the prefix
milli- means $10^{-3}$. If question 3 goes wrong, divide the distance by the
time.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A sound wave has a frequency of 425 Hz and travels at
$340\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find its wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wave equation links the speed, the frequency and the wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rearrange $v = f\lambda$ to make the wavelength the subject, then divide 340 by
425.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= f\lambda \\
\lambda &= \frac{v}{f} = \frac{340}{425} = 0.80\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The wavelength is 0.80 m.

</details>

**2.** A wave has a wavelength of 0.25 m and a frequency of 680 Hz. Find its
speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed of a wave is the distance one wave travels each second.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the frequency by the wavelength: $v = f\lambda$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ v = f\lambda = 680 \times 0.25 = 170\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} $$

The wave travels at $170\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.

</details>

**3.** Two points on a wave are 0.80 m apart. The wavelength is 2.0 m. Find the
phase difference between them, in degrees.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One whole wavelength is $360^\circ$. The phase difference is the same fraction
of a full cycle as the distance is of a wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the distance by the wavelength, then multiply by $360^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ \Delta\phi = \frac{\Delta x}{\lambda} \times 360^\circ
   = \frac{0.80}{2.0} \times 360^\circ = 144^\circ $$

The points are $144^\circ$ out of step.

</details>

**4.** Two loudspeakers are driven in phase at 850 Hz. The speed of sound is
$340\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. A listener is 4.30 m from one speaker and 3.70 m
from the other. Decide whether the sound is loud or quiet, and give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the wavelength first, then the path difference. Compare the path difference
with the wavelength, not with the frequency.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The wavelength is $\dfrac{340}{850} = 0.40\,\text{m}$. The path difference is
$4.30 - 3.70 = 0.60\,\text{m}$. Divide the path difference by the wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{v}{f} = \frac{340}{850} = 0.40\,\text{m} \\
\Delta x &= 4.30 - 3.70 = 0.60\,\text{m} \\
\frac{\Delta x}{\lambda} &= \frac{0.60}{0.40} = 1.5
\end{aligned}
$$

The path difference is one and a half wavelengths, so the two waves arrive
antiphase. The sound at the listener is quiet.

</details>

**5.** A laser of wavelength 590 nm passes through two slits 0.40 mm apart. The
screen is 1.80 m away. Find the spacing of the bright fringes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $w = \dfrac{\lambda D}{s}$. Change the nanometres and the millimetres to
metres before you substitute.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\lambda = 5.90 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$ and
$s = 4.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}$, then substitute into the fringe formula.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
w &= \frac{\lambda D}{s} \\
  &= \frac{5.90 \times 10^{-7} \times 1.80}{4.0 \times 10^{-4}} \\
  &= 2.7 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The fringes are $2.7\,\text{mm}$ apart, to two significant figures.

</details>

**6.** Explain what is meant by coherent sources, and explain why two separate
lamps cannot produce a steady interference pattern, even when they look the same
colour.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Coherence is about the phase between the two waves, and about the frequency.
Think about how an ordinary lamp emits its light.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that coherent sources have the same frequency and a constant phase
difference. Then say what an ordinary lamp does instead.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Coherent sources have the same frequency and a constant phase difference, so the
position of the bright and dark fringes stays fixed. Two separate lamps emit
light in short, unrelated bursts. The phase difference between them changes many
times each second, so the pattern shifts from moment to moment. The eye sees only
the average, which has no visible fringes. A single source feeding both slits, or
one laser, is needed because the light is then coherent.

</details>
