# 1.1 SI units and prefixes — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write 3500 m in kilometres.
2. Write 0.045 s in milliseconds.
3. Work out $0.5 \times 1200 \times 15^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $3.5\,\text{km}$.
2. $45\,\text{ms}$.
3. $0.5 \times 1200 \times 225 = 135000$.

If question 1 or 2 goes wrong, look again at the prefix table in Section 2 of the
Learn page. Kilo means $10^{3}$ and milli means $10^{-3}$. If question 3 goes
wrong, work out the power before you multiply.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write $25\,\text{cm}^2$ in $\text{m}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The prefix applies to the metre, and the whole unit is squared. Change the
centimetre to a metre first, then square the result.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $1\,\text{cm} = 10^{-2}\,\text{m}$, so $1\,\text{cm}^2 = (10^{-2})^2\,\text{m}^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
25\,\text{cm}^2 &= 25 \times (10^{-2})^2\,\text{m}^2 \\
                &= 25 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^2 \\
                &= 2.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

The answer is much smaller than 25, which is what a square centimetre should be.
</details>

**2.** A capacitor is marked $4.7\,\mu\text{F}$. Write its capacitance in farads.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the symbol $\mu$ in the prefix table and write its factor in place of the
symbol.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The factor for micro is $10^{-6}$, so replace $\mu$ by $10^{-6}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ 4.7\,\mu\text{F} = 4.7 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F} $$

There is no power on the unit here, so the factor is used once.
</details>

**3.** Show that $s = ut + \tfrac{1}{2}at^2$ is homogeneous.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are two terms on the right. Each term must have the same base units as the
left side.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the base units of $ut$ and of $at^2$ separately before you compare them
with $s$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The left side, $s$, is a distance, so its base units are $\text{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
ut &= \text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \times \text{s} = \text{m} \\
at^2 &= \text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2} \times \text{s}^2 = \text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

Both terms are $\text{m}$, the same as $s$, so the equation is homogeneous. The
$\tfrac{1}{2}$ is a pure number and has no unit.
</details>

**4.** The energy of a photon is $E = hf$. Use base units to find the unit of the
Planck constant $h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange for $h$ first. The frequency $f$ is measured in hertz, and
$1\,\text{Hz} = 1\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Dividing by $f$ gives $h = \dfrac{E}{f}$, so write the unit of a joule divided by
the unit of a hertz.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
h &= \frac{E}{f} \\
  &= \frac{\text{kg}\,\text{m}^2\,\text{s}^{-2}}{\text{s}^{-1}} \\
  &= \text{kg}\,\text{m}^2\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the same as a joule second, $\text{J}\,\text{s}$.
</details>

**5.** A solid has a density of $7.8\,\text{g}\,\text{cm}^{-3}$. Write the density
in $\text{kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the gram to a kilogram and the cubic centimetre to a cubic metre. Do the
two changes in separate steps so neither is lost.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $7.8\,\text{g} = 7.8 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}$ and
$1\,\text{cm}^3 = 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
7.8\,\text{g}\,\text{cm}^{-3}
  &= \frac{7.8 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{kg}}{10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3} \\
  &= 7.8 \times 10^{3}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}
\end{aligned}
$$

So the density is $7800\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}^{-3}$. The cubic centimetre is a
million times smaller than the cubic metre, so the number becomes much larger.
</details>

**6.** The force in a spring is $F = kx$, where $x$ is the extension. One student
writes the unit of the spring constant $k$ as $\text{N}\,\text{m}$. Another
writes $\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}$. Decide which student is correct, and explain
your choice.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange the equation to make $k$ the subject before you decide.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Dividing by $x$ gives $k = \dfrac{F}{x}$, so $k$ is a force divided by an
extension.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}} = \text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}
$$

The second student is correct. A spring with a large $k$ needs a large force for
a small extension, so the unit is newtons per metre. In base units this is
$\text{kg}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
</details>
