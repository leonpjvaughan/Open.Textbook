# Chapter 1: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The pendulum in the church tower has a length of 0.800 m from the pivot to the
centre of the bob. The time for 20 oscillations was measured three times as
35.90 s, 35.85 s and 35.95 s. The metre rule reads to 1 mm, so each reading
carries an uncertainty of 0.0005 m, and the length carries
$0.0005 + 0.0005 = 0.001\,\text{m}$.

**1. The mean time for 20 oscillations.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{mean} &= \frac{35.90 + 35.85 + 35.95}{3} = 35.90\,\text{s} \\
\text{range} &= 35.95 - 35.85 = 0.10\,\text{s} \\
\text{uncertainty} &= \frac{0.10}{2} = 0.05\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The time is $35.90 \pm 0.05\,\text{s}$.

**2. The period and its absolute uncertainty.** Dividing by 20 divides both the
time and its uncertainty.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac{35.90}{20} = 1.795\,\text{s} \\
\Delta T &= \frac{0.05}{20} = 0.0025\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

**3. The percentage uncertainty in the period.**

$$ \frac{0.05}{35.90} \times 100\% = 0.139\% $$

**4. The percentage uncertainty in the length.** The length is
$0.800 \pm 0.001\,\text{m}$.

$$ \frac{0.001}{0.800} \times 100\% = 0.125\% $$

**5. The acceleration due to gravity.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
g &= \frac{4\pi^2 L}{T^2} \\
  &= \frac{4\pi^2 \times 0.800}{1.795^2} \\
  &= 9.802\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**6. The percentage uncertainty in $g$.** The period is squared, so its
percentage uncertainty is doubled, and the exact constant $4\pi^2$ adds nothing.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{percentage uncertainty in } g &= 0.125\% + 2 \times 0.139\% \\
                                     &= 0.40\%
\end{aligned}
$$

**7. The absolute uncertainty and the quoted result.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta g &= 9.802 \times \frac{0.40}{100} = 0.04\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2} \\
g &= 9.80 \pm 0.04\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$

**8. Does the experiment agree with the accepted value?** The measured range is
9.76 to 9.84 m s⁻². The accepted value, 9.81 m s⁻², lies inside the range, so the
experiment agrees with it within the stated uncertainty.

**9. The length the clock needs.** A clock keeps time when its pendulum has a
period of exactly 2.000 s.

$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \\
L &= \frac{gT^2}{4\pi^2} \\
  &= \frac{9.81 \times 4.000}{4\pi^2} \\
  &= 0.994\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The clock pendulum must be about 0.994 m long. The 0.800 m pendulum has a period
of 1.795 s, which is shorter than 2.000 s, so the clock would run fast. The
technician must lengthen the pendulum from 0.800 m to about 0.994 m.

**10. The judgement.** The measurement is consistent with the accepted value, so
it gives no reason to doubt the accepted value. It does not prove that the value
is right. The uncertainty allows for random scatter, and a systematic error in
the length, at the centre of the bob, would not appear in it. This final
judgement is a reasoned answer. It is not the kind of claim a computer can check,
so compare your reasoning with the evidence above.
