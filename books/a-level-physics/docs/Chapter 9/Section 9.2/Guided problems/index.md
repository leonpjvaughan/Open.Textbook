# 9.2 Electric fields — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Change microcoulombs and nanocoulombs to coulombs, and
millimetres to metres, before you substitute.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A current of 0.50 A flows through a lamp for 20 s. Find the charge that
   passes through it.
2. An electron is accelerated through a potential difference of 200 V. Find the
   energy it gains, in joules.
3. A $5.0\,\mu\text{F}$ capacitor is charged to 12 V. Find the energy stored in
   it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $Q = It = 0.50 \times 20 = 10\,\text{C}$.
2. $E = eV = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 200 = 3.2 \times 10^{-17}\,\text{J}$.
3. $E = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^{2} = 0.5 \times 5.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 12^{2}
   = 3.6 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{J}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, revisit charge and current in Section 5.1. If
question 2 goes wrong, remember that the energy gained is the charge multiplied
by the potential difference. If question 3 goes wrong, revisit the energy stored
in a capacitor in Section 5.4.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A charge of $+3.0\,\mu\text{C}$ and a charge of $-2.0\,\mu\text{C}$ are
0.50 m apart in air. Find the magnitude of the force between them, and state its
direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two charges have opposite signs, so the force is attractive.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Change both charges to coulombs and use $F = \dfrac{kQq}{r^{2}}$. The product
$Qq$ is negative, which tells you the force is attractive; use the size of the
answer.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= \frac{kQq}{r^{2}}
   = \frac{8.99 \times 10^{9} \times 3.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-6}}{0.50^{2}} \\
  &= \frac{5.39 \times 10^{-2}}{0.25} = 0.22\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The force is $0.22\,\text{N}$, and it pulls the two charges together.
</details>

**2.** Find the electric field strength at a distance of 0.20 m from a point
charge of $+5.0\,\text{nC}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A nanocoulomb is $10^{-9}\,\text{C}$, and the field of a point charge follows an
inverse-square law.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $E = \dfrac{kQ}{r^{2}}$, and square $0.20$ before you divide.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= \frac{kQ}{r^{2}}
   = \frac{8.99 \times 10^{9} \times 5.0 \times 10^{-9}}{0.20^{2}} \\
  &= \frac{44.95}{0.040} = 1.1 \times 10^{3}\,\text{N}\,\text{C}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The field strength is $1.1 \times 10^{3}\,\text{N}\,\text{C}^{-1}$, directed
away from the charge.
</details>

**3.** Two parallel plates are 4.0 mm apart and have a potential difference of
240 V between them. Find the electric field strength and the force on an
electron that is between the plates.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The field between parallel plates is uniform. Change the separation to metres.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $E = \dfrac{V}{d}$, then multiply by the charge on an electron,
$1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
E = \frac{V}{d} = \frac{240}{4.0 \times 10^{-3}} = 6.0 \times 10^{4}\,\text{V}\,\text{m}^{-1}
$$

$$
F = QE = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 6.0 \times 10^{4}
  = 9.6 \times 10^{-15}\,\text{N}
$$

The field strength is $6.0 \times 10^{4}\,\text{V}\,\text{m}^{-1}$, and the
force on the electron is $9.6 \times 10^{-15}\,\text{N}$ towards the positive
plate.
</details>

**4.** Find the electric potential at a distance of 0.20 m from the
$+5.0\,\text{nC}$ charge in question 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The potential of a point charge is proportional to $\dfrac{1}{r}$, not
$\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $V = \dfrac{kQ}{r}$ with the same charge and distance, and do not square the
distance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
V = \frac{kQ}{r}
  = \frac{8.99 \times 10^{9} \times 5.0 \times 10^{-9}}{0.20}
  = 2.2 \times 10^{2}\,\text{V}
$$

The potential is $2.2 \times 10^{2}\,\text{V}$, or
$2.2 \times 10^{2}\,\text{J}\,\text{C}^{-1}$.
</details>

**5.** An electron is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of
250 V. Find the kinetic energy it gains and its final speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The energy gained is the electron charge multiplied by the potential
difference.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $E_{k} = eV$ first, then use $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^{2} = E_{k}$ with the mass of
an electron.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
E_{k} = eV = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 250 = 4.0 \times 10^{-17}\,\text{J}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \sqrt{\frac{2E_{k}}{m}}
   = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 4.0 \times 10^{-17}}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}}} \\
  &= \sqrt{8.78 \times 10^{13}}
   = 9.4 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The electron gains $4.0 \times 10^{-17}\,\text{J}$ and reaches
$9.4 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
</details>

**6.** A charge of $+4.0\,\mu\text{C}$ moves through a potential difference of
500 V. Find the work done on the charge.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The work done depends on the charge and the potential difference, not on the
distance travelled.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $W = q\,\Delta V$ with the charge in coulombs.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
W = q\,\Delta V = 4.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 500 = 2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{J}
$$

The work done is $2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{J}$, which is 2.0 mJ.
</details>

**7.** Explain why the electric force between two electrons is much stronger
than the gravitational force between them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both forces follow an inverse-square law with the same distance, so compare the
constants and the charges.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write both force laws for the same separation $r$, then compare $k e^{2}$ with
$G m_{e}^{2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both forces follow an inverse-square law and use the same distance $r$, so the
ratio of the electric force to the gravitational force is
$\dfrac{ke^{2}}{Gm_{e}^{2}}$. The Coulomb constant $k$ is about $10^{20}$ times
larger than $G$. The charge and the mass of the electron make up the rest of the
ratio, which works out at about $4 \times 10^{42}$. The electric force is
enormously stronger. Gravity becomes noticeable only when the mass is very
large, as it is for a planet.
</details>

**8.** A positively charged particle enters a uniform electric field at right
angles to the field lines. Describe the shape of its path and explain how the
force on it changes during the motion.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the situation with projectile motion under gravity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which component of the velocity changes and which stays constant, and state
whether the force changes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The force is $F = QE$, and the field is uniform, so the force is constant and
points along the field lines. The component of the velocity along the field
lines changes at a steady rate, so the particle accelerates in that direction.
The component at right angles stays constant. The path is a parabola, the same
shape as the path of a projectile under gravity.
</details>
