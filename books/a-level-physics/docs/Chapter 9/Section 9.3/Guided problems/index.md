# 9.3 Magnetic fields — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Draw the field, the current and the force for every question that
asks for a direction, and use Fleming's left-hand rule.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wire carries a current of 4.0 A. Find the charge that passes a point in the
   wire in 30 s.
2. A particle moves in a circle of radius 0.50 m at a constant speed of
   $3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find its centripetal acceleration.
3. Resolve a force of 20 N acting at $30^{\circ}$ to the horizontal into a
   horizontal and a vertical component.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $Q = It = 4.0 \times 30 = 120\,\text{C}$.
2. $a = \dfrac{v^{2}}{r} = \dfrac{3.0^{2}}{0.50} = 18\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
3. Horizontal $= 20\cos 30^{\circ} = 17\,\text{N}$; vertical
   $= 20\sin 30^{\circ} = 10\,\text{N}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, revisit charge and current in Section 5.1. If
question 2 goes wrong, revisit circular motion in Section 3.5. If question 3
goes wrong, revisit resolving vectors in Section 2.2.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A straight wire of length 0.50 m carries a current of 2.0 A at right
angles to a magnetic field of flux density 0.40 T. Find the force on the wire.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wire is at right angles to the field, so no angle factor is needed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $F = BIL$ with the flux density in tesla, the current in amperes and the
length in metres.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F = BIL = 0.40 \times 2.0 \times 0.50 = 0.40\,\text{N}
$$

The force is $0.40\,\text{N}$, at right angles to both the wire and the field.
</details>

**2.** The wire in question 1 is turned so that it makes an angle of
$40^{\circ}$ with the field. Find the force on it now.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Only the component of the wire at right angles to the field carries the force.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $F = BIL\sin\theta$ with $\theta = 40^{\circ}$, and check that the answer is
smaller than the answer to question 1.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
F = BIL\sin 40^{\circ} = 0.40 \times 0.643 = 0.26\,\text{N}
$$

The force is $0.26\,\text{N}$. It is smaller than when the wire was at right
angles, because only part of the wire is at right angles to the field.
</details>

**3.** A proton moves at $2.0 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ at right
angles to a magnetic field of flux density 0.30 T. Find the force on the proton.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A single moving charge obeys a different equation from a current-carrying wire.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $F = BQv$ with the charge on a proton,
$Q = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= BQv = 0.30 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 2.0 \times 10^{6} \\
  &= 9.6 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The force is $9.6 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{N}$, at right angles to the velocity
and to the field.
</details>

**4.** Find the radius of the circular path of the proton in question 3. The
mass of a proton is $1.67 \times 10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The magnetic force provides the centripetal force.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Set $BQv = \dfrac{mv^{2}}{r}$ and rearrange for $r$, or use
$r = \dfrac{mv}{BQ}$ directly.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
r &= \frac{mv}{BQ}
   = \frac{1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 2.0 \times 10^{6}}{0.30 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}} \\
  &= \frac{3.34 \times 10^{-21}}{4.80 \times 10^{-20}}
   = 7.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The proton travels in a circle of radius 7.0 cm.
</details>

**5.** A coil of 150 turns has an area of $3.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^{2}$. It
is placed at right angles to a magnetic field of flux density 0.20 T. Find the
magnetic flux through one turn and the flux linkage.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Flux is for one turn; flux linkage includes all the turns.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $\Phi = BA$ first, then multiply by $N = 150$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\Phi = BA = 0.20 \times 3.0 \times 10^{-3} = 6.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{Wb}
$$

$$
N\Phi = 150 \times 6.0 \times 10^{-4} = 0.090\,\text{Wb}
$$

The flux through one turn is $6.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{Wb}$ and the flux
linkage is $0.090\,\text{Wb}$.
</details>

**6.** Explain why the speed of a charged particle moving in a magnetic field
stays constant, even though a force acts on it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the angle between the force and the velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what the force is at right angles to, and what work done depends on.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The magnetic force is always at right angles to the velocity of the particle.
Work done is force multiplied by the distance moved in the direction of the
force. The particle does not move in the direction of the force, so the force
does no work on it. Its kinetic energy does not change, so its speed stays
constant. The force only changes the direction of the velocity.
</details>

**7.** Explain how a mass spectrometer uses a magnetic field to separate the
isotopes of an element.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Isotopes have the same charge but different masses.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Start from $r = \dfrac{mv}{BQ}$ and say which quantities are the same for each
isotope and which one differs.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The ions are all given the same charge and accelerated to the same speed. They
enter the magnetic field at right angles. The radius of the circular path is
$r = \dfrac{mv}{BQ}$. The charge $Q$, the speed $v$ and the flux density $B$ are
the same for every isotope, so the radius depends on the mass $m$. A heavier
isotope travels in a larger circle and lands further along the detector, so the
isotopes are separated.
</details>

**8.** A wire carrying a current is placed at an angle of $35^{\circ}$ to a
magnetic field. Explain how you would find the force on the wire.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Only one component of the current feels a force.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which equation you would use, and then how you would find the direction.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Find the component of the wire that is at right angles to the field, or use
$F = BIL\sin\theta$ with $\theta = 35^{\circ}$. Then use Fleming's left-hand
rule: point the first finger along the field, the second finger along the
current, and the thumb gives the direction of the force. The force is at right
angles to both the wire and the field.
</details>
