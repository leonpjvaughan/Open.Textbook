# 4.2 Force-extension graphs and stored energy — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. For each graph, decide whether the line is straight before you
choose a formula.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 12 \times 0.30$.
2. Find the area of a triangle with base $0.040\,\text{m}$ and height
   $8.0\,\text{N}$.
3. Find the gradient of the line through $(0, 0)$ and $(0.050\,\text{m},\, 15\,\text{N})$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $1.8$.
2. $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.040 \times 8.0 = 0.16$. The unit would be
   $\text{N}\,\text{m}$, which is the joule.
3. $\dfrac{15}{0.050} = 300$.

If question 1 or 2 goes wrong, work on the arithmetic before the physics. If
question 3 goes wrong, the gradient is the change in the vertical value divided
by the change in the horizontal value.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A spring extends by $0.060\,\text{m}$ under a force of $12\,\text{N}$.
Find the spring constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Hooke's law connects the force, the spring constant and the extension. Make the
spring constant the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $k = \dfrac{F}{\Delta L}$, then substitute $F = 12$ and
$\Delta L = 0.060$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ k = \frac{F}{\Delta L} = \frac{12}{0.060} = 200\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1} $$

The spring constant is $200\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}$.
</details>

**2.** A spring of constant $300\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}$ is stretched by
$0.050\,\text{m}$. Find the force and the energy stored.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the force first with $F = k\Delta L$. Then use the force in the energy
formula.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $F = 300 \times 0.050$, then put that force into
$E = \tfrac{1}{2}F\Delta L$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= k\Delta L = 300 \times 0.050 = 15\,\text{N} \\
E &= \frac{1}{2}F\Delta L = \frac{1}{2} \times 15 \times 0.050 = 0.375\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The force is $15\,\text{N}$ and the energy stored is $0.375\,\text{J}$.
</details>

**3.** A force-extension graph is a straight line through $(0, 0)$ and
$(0.025\,\text{m},\, 10\,\text{N})$. Find the gradient and the energy stored at
$10\,\text{N}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The gradient is the spring constant. The energy stored is the area under the
line, and the line is straight, so the area is a triangle.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $k = \dfrac{10}{0.025}$ for the gradient, then use
$E = \tfrac{1}{2}F\Delta L$ with the same two values.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ k = \frac{10}{0.025} = 400\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1} $$

$$ E = \frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 0.025 = 0.125\,\text{J} $$

The gradient is $400\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}$ and the energy stored is
$0.125\,\text{J}$.
</details>

**4.** A wire has a Young modulus of $2.0 \times 10^{11}\,\text{Pa}$, a
cross-sectional area of $1.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}^2$ and a length of
$2.00\,\text{m}$. Find its stiffness and the energy stored when it extends by
$1.0\,\text{mm}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The stiffness of a wire is $k = \dfrac{EA}{L}$. Change the extension to metres
before you find the energy.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $k$ from the first three values, then use
$E = \tfrac{1}{2}k(\Delta L)^2$ with $\Delta L = 1.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
k &= \frac{EA}{L} = \frac{2.0 \times 10^{11} \times 1.0 \times 10^{-7}}{2.00}
   = 1.0 \times 10^{4}\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1} \\
E &= \frac{1}{2}k(\Delta L)^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1.0 \times 10^{4} \times (1.0 \times 10^{-3})^2 \\
  &= 5.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The stiffness is $1.0 \times 10^{4}\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}$ and the energy
stored is $5.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{J}$.
</details>

**5.** A force-extension graph bends. Explain why the energy stored is not
$\tfrac{1}{2}F\Delta L$, and say how to find it instead.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the shape of the area under the graph, and about the shape the
formula assumes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

State the shape that $\tfrac{1}{2}F\Delta L$ gives, then say how the real area
differs.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The formula $\tfrac{1}{2}F\Delta L$ is the area of a triangle. A triangle is
the right shape only when the graph is a straight line through the origin. If
the graph bends, the area under it is not a triangle, so the formula gives the
wrong value. Find the true area by counting squares, or by splitting the area
into strips and adding the areas of the strips.
</details>

**6.** A spring stores $0.90\,\text{J}$ when it is extended by $0.060\,\text{m}$.
The graph is a straight line through the origin. Find the final force and the
spring constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start from $E = \tfrac{1}{2}F\Delta L$ and rearrange it for the force. Then use
the force and the extension to find the spring constant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $F = \dfrac{2E}{\Delta L}$, then find $k = \dfrac{F}{\Delta L}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= \frac{2E}{\Delta L} = \frac{2 \times 0.90}{0.060} = 30\,\text{N} \\
k &= \frac{F}{\Delta L} = \frac{30}{0.060} = 500\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The final force is $30\,\text{N}$ and the spring constant is
$500\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}$.
</details>
