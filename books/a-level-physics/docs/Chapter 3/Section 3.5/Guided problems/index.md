# 3.5 Circular motion — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. In every problem, name the force that provides the centripetal
force.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A car of mass 1200 kg accelerates at $3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$. Find the
   resultant force.
2. Write $90^\circ$ in radians.
3. A ball of mass 0.40 kg moves at $5.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find its
   kinetic energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $F = ma = 1200 \times 3.0 = 3600\,\text{N}$.
2. $90 \times \dfrac{\pi}{180} = \dfrac{\pi}{2} = 1.57\,\text{rad}$.
3. $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.40 \times 5.0^2 = 5.0\,\text{J}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, use $F = ma$. If question 2 goes wrong, multiply the
degrees by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$. If question 3 goes wrong, use
$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write $60^\circ$ and $200^\circ$ in radians.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the angle in degrees by $\dfrac{\pi}{180}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For $60^\circ$ this gives $\dfrac{60\pi}{180}$, which simplifies. For $200^\circ$
it gives $\dfrac{200\pi}{180}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
60^\circ &= \frac{60\pi}{180} = \frac{\pi}{3} = 1.05\,\text{rad} \\
200^\circ &= \frac{200\pi}{180} = \frac{10\pi}{9} = 3.49\,\text{rad}
\end{aligned}
$$

The radian is a pure number, so there is no unit to write after it.
</details>

**2.** A wheel of radius 0.30 m rotates at 4.0 revolutions per second. Find its
angular velocity and the speed of a point on the rim.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The frequency is 4.0 Hz, and $\omega = 2\pi f$. The rim speed is $v = \omega r$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $\omega = 2\pi \times 4.0$ first, then multiply by the radius.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega &= 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 4.0 = 25.1\,\text{rad}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
v &= \omega r = 25.1 \times 0.30 = 7.5\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

A point on the rim moves at $7.5\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, and a point nearer
the centre moves more slowly.
</details>

**3.** A car of mass 1400 kg goes round a bend of radius 50 m at a constant
$15\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find the centripetal acceleration and the
friction force on the car.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The friction force provides the centripetal force. Use $a = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$,
then $F = ma$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $a = \dfrac{15^2}{50}$ first, then multiply by the mass.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{15^2}{50} = 4.5\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2} \\
F &= ma = 1400 \times 4.5 = 6300\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The friction force is $6300\,\text{N}$ towards the centre of the bend. On ice,
the friction could not reach this size, and the car would slide outwards.
</details>

**4.** A ball of mass 0.25 kg is whirled in a horizontal circle of radius 0.60 m
on a string. The maximum tension the string can take is 6.0 N. Find the maximum
speed of the ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the maximum speed the tension equals the centripetal force, so
$T = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rearrange to $v^2 = \dfrac{Tr}{m}$ and substitute the values.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^2 &= \frac{Tr}{m} = \frac{6.0 \times 0.60}{0.25} = 14.4 \\
v &= 3.8\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

If the ball moves faster than $3.8\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, the tension needed
would pass 6.0 N and the string would break.
</details>

**5.** A bucket of water is swung in a vertical circle of radius 0.80 m. Find
the minimum speed at the top for the water to stay in the bucket. Take
$g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the minimum speed the water is held by its own weight alone, so
$mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Cancel the mass and rearrange to $v^2 = gr$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
mg &= \frac{mv^2}{r} \\
v &= \sqrt{gr} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 0.80} \\
  &= 2.8\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

At any speed lower than $2.8\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, the water would leave
the bucket at the top.
</details>

**6.** A passenger on a roundabout feels pushed outwards, towards the rim. The
passenger says this shows there is an outward force. Explain what is really
happening, and name the force that keeps the passenger moving in a circle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The passenger is moving in a circle, so the acceleration points towards the
centre. Think about what happens to the passenger's body when the roundabout
turns.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the real force that points towards the centre, and say what the passenger
would do if that force disappeared.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The passenger is moving in a circle, so the acceleration points towards the
centre. The only real horizontal force on the passenger is the friction from the
seat, which points towards the centre and provides the centripetal force.

The passenger feels pushed outwards because the body tends to keep moving in a
straight line, by Newton's first law. When the roundabout turns, the body
continues along the tangent for a moment, and the seat presses inwards on it.
The outward feeling is the sensation of the inward push, not a real outward
force. If the friction disappeared, the passenger would fly off along the
tangent, not radially outwards.

There is no outward centrifugal force on the passenger in the ground frame.
</details>
