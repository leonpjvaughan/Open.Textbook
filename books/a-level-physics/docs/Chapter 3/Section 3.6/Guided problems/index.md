# 3.6 Oscillations and simple harmonic motion — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Remember that the acceleration is not constant, so the equations
of motion from Section 3.1 do not apply.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A pendulum has a length of 0.500 m. Use $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$ to find
   its period. Take $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
2. A resultant force of 3.0 N acts on a mass of 0.60 kg. Find the acceleration.
3. A mass of 0.20 kg moves at $3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find its kinetic
   energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{0.500}{9.81}} = 2\pi \times 0.226 = 1.42\,\text{s}$.
2. $a = \dfrac{3.0}{0.60} = 5.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
3. $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.20 \times 3.0^2 = 0.90\,\text{J}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, divide the length by $g$ before taking the square
root. If question 2 goes wrong, use $F = ma$. If question 3 goes wrong, use
$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A mass on a spring oscillates with amplitude 0.080 m and period 0.40 s.
Find the angular frequency, the maximum speed and the maximum acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angular frequency is $\omega = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $\omega$ first, then use $v_{\max} = A\omega$ and $a_{\max} = A\omega^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\omega &= \frac{2\pi}{0.40} = 15.7\,\text{rad}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
v_{\max} &= A\omega = 0.080 \times 15.7 = 1.3\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
a_{\max} &= A\omega^2 = 0.080 \times 15.7^2 = 20\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The maximum speed is at the equilibrium position. The maximum acceleration is at
the extremes of the motion.
</details>

**2.** A mass of 0.40 kg hangs on a spring of spring constant
$25\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{-1}$. Find the period of the oscillation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The period of a mass on a spring is $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{m}{k}}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the fraction under the square root first, then multiply by $2\pi$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{0.40}{25}} \\
  &= 2\pi \times 0.126 = 0.79\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The period does not depend on the amplitude. A larger amplitude gives a larger
maximum speed, but the same period.
</details>

**3.** A simple pendulum has a length of 1.20 m. Find its period. Take
$g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The period of a simple pendulum is $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide 1.20 by 9.81, take the square root, then multiply by $2\pi$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{1.20}{9.81}} = 2\pi\sqrt{0.1223} \\
  &= 2\pi \times 0.3498 = 2.20\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The formula assumes a small amplitude. A large swing would take slightly longer
than this.
</details>

**4.** A mass oscillates with amplitude 0.060 m and angular frequency
$6.0\,\text{rad}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find its speed when the displacement is
0.020 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $A^2 - x^2$ first, then take the square root.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2} \\
  &= 6.0\sqrt{0.060^2 - 0.020^2} \\
  &= 6.0\sqrt{0.0032} = 6.0 \times 0.0566 \\
  &= 0.34\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The speed is smaller than the maximum speed of $A\omega = 0.36\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$,
because the mass is not at the equilibrium position.
</details>

**5.** A mass of 0.30 kg oscillates with amplitude 0.040 m and angular frequency
$10\,\text{rad}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find the total energy and the kinetic energy
when the displacement is 0.020 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total energy is $E = \tfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 A^2$, and the potential energy at
displacement $x$ is $E_p = \tfrac{1}{2}m\omega^2 x^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the total energy and the potential energy separately, then subtract.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{total}} &= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.30 \times 10^2 \times 0.040^2 = 0.024\,\text{J} \\
E_p &= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 0.30 \times 10^2 \times 0.020^2 = 0.0060\,\text{J} \\
E_k &= 0.024 - 0.0060 = 0.018\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

Halfway between the centre and the extreme, the energy is shared in the ratio
three to one, kinetic to potential.
</details>

**6.** The pendulum from Chapter 1 has a length of 0.800 m and a measured period
of 1.795 s. Explain why a pendulum clock uses a small swing, and find the length
a pendulum needs for a period of exactly 2.000 s. Take
$g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The simple-pendulum formula uses $\sin\theta \approx \theta$, which is accurate
only for small angles. For the length, rearrange $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Squaring the period equation gives $T^2 = \dfrac{4\pi^2 l}{g}$, so
$l = \dfrac{gT^2}{4\pi^2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The formula $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\dfrac{l}{g}}$ comes from the approximation
$\sin\theta \approx \theta$, which holds only for small angles. A clock uses a
small swing so that the period stays constant as the swing gradually dies away,
and the clock keeps time.

For a period of exactly 2.000 s,

$$
l = \frac{gT^2}{4\pi^2} = \frac{9.81 \times 4.000}{4\pi^2} = 0.994\,\text{m}
$$

The 0.800 m pendulum is too short, so its period is less than 2.000 s and the
clock would run fast. The technician must lengthen the pendulum to about 0.994 m.
</details>
