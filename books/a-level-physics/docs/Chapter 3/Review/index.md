# Chapter 3: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The car and riders have a total mass of 500 kg. The first drop is a straight
track at 60° to the horizontal, with a vertical descent of 45 m. The loop has a
radius of 12 m. The car is released from rest, and at the end the brakes stop it
from $29.7\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ in 3.0 s. Air resistance and friction are
negligible on the first drop. Take $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$. Here $u$
is the speed at the start of a stage and $\Delta$ means the change in a
quantity.

**1. The acceleration down the first drop.** Only the component of the weight $W$
along the slope accelerates the car.

$$
a = g\sin 60^\circ = 9.81 \times 0.866 = 8.50\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}
$$

**2. The length of the slope.** The vertical descent is the opposite side of a
right-angled triangle whose hypotenuse is the slope.

$$
s = \frac{45}{\sin 60^\circ} = \frac{45}{0.866} = 52.0\,\text{m}
$$

**3. The speed at the bottom.** From rest, use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^2 &= 0 + 2 \times 8.50 \times 52.0 = 884 \\
v &= 29.7\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The same result comes from energy. The height matters, not the angle.

**4. The time down the slope.**

$$
t = \frac{v - u}{a} = \frac{29.7}{8.50} = 3.5\,\text{s}
$$

**5. The energy at the top and at the bottom.** Taking the bottom of the loop as
the zero of potential energy,

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_p(\text{top}) &= mg\Delta h = 500 \times 9.81 \times 45 = 2.21 \times 10^5\,\text{J} \\
E_k(\text{bottom}) &= 2.21 \times 10^5\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

All the potential energy has become kinetic energy, so the car has
$2.21 \times 10^5\,\text{J}$ at the bottom.

**6. The forces on the slope.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= mg = 500 \times 9.81 = 4905\,\text{N} \\
W_{\parallel} &= W\sin 60^\circ = 4248\,\text{N} \\
W_{\perp} &= W\cos 60^\circ = 2453\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The normal reaction from the track balances the perpendicular component, so it is
$2453\,\text{N}$. Dividing the along-slope force by the mass recovers
$a = 8.50\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

**7. The top of the loop.** The top is $2r = 24\,\text{m}$ above the bottom, so
the car has lost some of its kinetic energy on the way up.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_k(\text{top}) &= 2.21 \times 10^5 - 500 \times 9.81 \times 24 \\
                &= 2.21 \times 10^5 - 1.18 \times 10^5 = 1.03 \times 10^5\,\text{J} \\
v_{\text{top}} &= \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1.03 \times 10^5}{500}} = 20.3\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

**8. The force at the top of the loop.** The centripetal acceleration is

$$
a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{20.3^2}{12} = 34.3\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}
$$

At the top, the track force $N$ and the weight both act downwards, towards the
centre, so

$$
\begin{aligned}
N + mg &= \frac{mv^2}{r} \\
N &= 500(34.3 - 9.81) = 1.2 \times 10^4\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The track pushes down on the car with about $1.2 \times 10^4\,\text{N}$.

**9. The margin for staying on the track.** The car needs at least
$v^2 = gr$ at the top. That is

$$
v_{\min} = \sqrt{9.81 \times 12} = 10.8\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
$$

The actual speed of $20.3\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ is well above this, so the
car stays in contact. Energy conservation gives the least release height:

$$
mgH = \tfrac{1}{2}mgr + mg(2r) \quad\Rightarrow\quad H = \tfrac{5}{2}r = 30\,\text{m}
$$

The actual release height is 45 m, so the car has a margin of 15 m.

**10. The braking force.** The momentum at the bottom is

$$
p = mv = 500 \times 29.7 = 1.49 \times 10^4\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
$$

The change in momentum as the car stops is
$-1.49 \times 10^4\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, so

$$
F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{-1.49 \times 10^4}{3.0} = -5.0 \times 10^3\,\text{N}
$$

The average braking force is about 5000 N, opposite to the motion.

**11. The judgement.** The ride works, and it has a safe margin on the loop. A
real roller coaster is not like this ideal one in three ways.

- A real loop is **not a circle**. A circular loop would give a very large force
  at the bottom, where the radius is smallest. Real loops are teardrop-shaped, so
  the radius is larger where the speed is greatest, and the forces stay within
  safe limits.
- **Friction and air resistance** remove energy on every part of the track, so
  the car is slower than the ideal calculation. The lift hill must be taller than
  30 m to leave a margin.
- The **brakes** do not apply a constant force, so 5000 N is an average. The peak
  force is higher.

This final judgement is a reasoned answer. It is not the kind of claim a
computer can check, so compare your reasoning with the evidence above.

## What you can now do

- use the equations of motion for constant acceleration, and read motion graphs;
- analyse free fall and projectiles;
- state Newton's three laws and apply $F = ma$ to a resultant force;
- draw a free-body diagram and find the apparent weight in a lift;
- calculate work, kinetic energy, potential energy and power;
- apply conservation of energy, including energy transferred to thermal stores;
- use momentum, impulse and conservation of momentum;
- find angular velocity and use the equations of circular motion;
- apply the condition for simple harmonic motion and find the period of a spring
  and a pendulum;
- calculate the energy in an oscillation.
