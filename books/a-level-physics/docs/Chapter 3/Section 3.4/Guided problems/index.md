# 3.4 Momentum — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. For every momentum problem, write the positive direction before
you substitute.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A resultant force of 240 N acts on an object of mass 60 kg. Find the
   acceleration.
2. A car of mass 1000 kg travels at $12\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find its
   kinetic energy.
3. A ball of mass 5.0 kg is dropped from rest from a height of 3.0 m. Find its
   speed just before it lands. Take $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $a = \dfrac{240}{60} = 4.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
2. $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 1000 \times 12^2 = 72000\,\text{J} = 72\,\text{kJ}$.
3. $v = \sqrt{2g\Delta h} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 3.0} = 7.7\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, use $F = ma$. If question 2 goes wrong, use
$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. If question 3 goes wrong, use conservation of energy
with $mg\Delta h = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A car of mass 1500 kg travels at $20\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ due north.
Find its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Momentum is mass multiplied by velocity. It carries the direction of the
velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute into $p = mv$ and state the direction with the answer.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ p = mv = 1500 \times 20 = 3.0 \times 10^4\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \ \text{north} $$

The momentum is a vector, so the direction is part of the answer.
</details>

**2.** A force of 250 N acts for 0.40 s on a ball of mass 2.0 kg that is
initially at rest. Find the impulse and the final speed of the ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The impulse is $F\Delta t$, and it equals the change in momentum.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the impulse first, then use $\Delta p = mv - 0$ to find the final speed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F\Delta t &= 250 \times 0.40 = 100\,\text{N}\,\text{s} \\
\Delta p &= 100\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
v &= \frac{100}{2.0} = 50\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The impulse is $100\,\text{N}\,\text{s}$ and the final speed is
$50\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
</details>

**3.** A ball of mass 0.15 kg hits a wall at $12\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ and
rebounds at $8.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find the change in momentum of the
ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the direction towards the wall as positive. The rebound velocity is
negative.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $\Delta p = m(v - u)$ with $u = +12$ and $v = -8.0$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta p &= m(v - u) \\
        &= 0.15(-8.0 - 12) \\
        &= -3.0\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The change in momentum is $3.0\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ away from
the wall. The size of the change is larger than the ball's initial momentum,
because the ball reverses direction.
</details>

**4.** A trolley of mass 3.0 kg moves at $4.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ towards
a second trolley of mass 1.0 kg, which is at rest. The trolleys collide and stick
together. Find their common velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = (m_1 + m_2)v$ and substitute the masses and the
initial velocities.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
3.0 \times 4.0 + 1.0 \times 0 &= (3.0 + 1.0)v \\
12 &= 4.0v \\
v &= 3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The trolleys move together at $3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ in the original
direction of the first trolley.
</details>

**5.** An object of mass 0.80 kg is at rest and explodes into two pieces. One
piece has a mass of 0.30 kg and moves at $4.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find the
velocity of the other piece.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The object is at rest before the explosion, so its total momentum is zero.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $0 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2$ and put in the known mass and velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
0 &= 0.30 \times 4.0 + 0.50v \\
v &= -\frac{1.2}{0.50} = -2.4\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The other piece moves at $2.4\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ in the opposite
direction to the first piece. The total momentum stays at zero.
</details>

**6.** The chapter problem. The car and riders have a mass of 500 kg and reach
$29.7\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ at the bottom of the first drop. At the end of
the ride the brakes bring the car to rest in 3.0 s. Find the momentum at the
bottom and the average braking force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The change in momentum is the final momentum minus the initial momentum. At the
end the car is at rest.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $p = mv$ at the bottom, then use $F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$ with
$\Delta t = 3.0$ s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= mv = 500 \times 29.7 = 1.49 \times 10^4\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
\Delta p &= 0 - 1.49 \times 10^4 = -1.49 \times 10^4\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
F &= \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{-1.49 \times 10^4}{3.0} = -5.0 \times 10^3\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The average braking force is $5.0 \times 10^3\,\text{N}$, about 5000 N, opposite
to the motion.
</details>
