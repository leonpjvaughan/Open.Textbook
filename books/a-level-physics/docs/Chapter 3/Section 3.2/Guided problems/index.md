# 3.2 Dynamics and Newton's laws of motion — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A car accelerates uniformly from $12\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ to
   $30\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ in 6.0 s. Find its acceleration.
2. A stone falls from rest for 1.5 s. Find its speed. Take
   $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
3. A force of 40 N acts at 25° to the horizontal. Find its horizontal and
   vertical components.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $a = \dfrac{30 - 12}{6.0} = 3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
2. $v = gt = 9.81 \times 1.5 = 14.7\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
3. $40\cos 25^\circ = 36.3\,\text{N}$ and $40\sin 25^\circ = 16.9\,\text{N}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, use $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$. If question 2 goes wrong,
the stone starts from rest and the acceleration is $g$. If question 3 goes
wrong, the angle is measured from the horizontal, so that component uses the
cosine.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A resultant force of 36 N acts on an object of mass 12 kg. Find the
acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The force given is already the resultant, so it can be used directly in
$F = ma$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rearrange $F = ma$ to make the acceleration the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{36}{12} = 3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2} $$

The acceleration is in the same direction as the resultant force.
</details>

**2.** A car of mass 1500 kg has a driving force of 4000 N. A drag and friction
force of 2500 N acts against the motion. Find the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take the direction of motion as positive. The 4000 N drives forwards and the
2500 N acts backwards.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the resultant force first, then divide by the mass.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= 4000 - 2500 = 1500\,\text{N} \\
a &= \frac{1500}{1500} = 1.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}
\end{aligned}
$$

The acceleration is $1.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$ in the direction of motion.
</details>

**3.** A crate of mass 20 kg is released on a frictionless slope at 15° to the
horizontal. Find its acceleration down the slope and the normal reaction on it.
Take $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Resolve the weight into a component along the slope and a component
perpendicular to it. Only the along-slope component accelerates the crate.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The along-slope component is $mg\sin 15^\circ$, so $a = g\sin 15^\circ$. The
perpendicular component is balanced by the normal reaction.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= g\sin 15^\circ = 9.81 \times 0.259 = 2.5\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2} \\
N &= mg\cos 15^\circ = 20 \times 9.81 \times 0.966 = 190\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The acceleration does not depend on the mass: doubling the mass doubles both the
force and the inertia. The normal reaction is less than the weight, because the
slope is tilted.
</details>

**4.** A passenger of mass 80 kg stands in a lift that accelerates downwards at
$0.80\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$. Find the normal reaction on the passenger.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The acceleration is downwards, so the weight is larger than the normal reaction.
The resultant is $mg - N$ downwards.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write Newton's second law downwards: $mg - N = ma$, then rearrange for $N$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
mg - N &= ma \\
N &= m(g - a) \\
  &= 80(9.81 - 0.80) \\
  &= 721\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The weight is $785\,\text{N}$ and the normal reaction is $721\,\text{N}$, so the
passenger feels lighter while the lift accelerates downwards.
</details>

**5.** A ball is thrown at $15\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ at 40° above the
horizontal. Find the maximum height, the time of flight and the range. Take
$g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Resolve the launch velocity into a horizontal component and a vertical
component. The horizontal component stays constant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

At the highest point the vertical velocity is zero, so use $v_y^2 = u_y^2 - 2gh$.
The time of flight is twice the time to the highest point.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
u_x &= 15\cos 40^\circ = 11.5\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
u_y &= 15\sin 40^\circ = 9.64\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
h_{\max} &= \frac{u_y^2}{2g} = \frac{9.64^2}{19.62} = 4.7\,\text{m} \\
t_{\text{top}} &= \frac{u_y}{g} = \frac{9.64}{9.81} = 0.983\,\text{s} \\
t_{\text{flight}} &= 2 \times 0.983 = 1.97\,\text{s} \\
\text{range} &= u_x t_{\text{flight}} = 11.5 \times 1.97 = 23\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The range is $23\,\text{m}$ to two significant figures.
</details>

**6.** A raindrop falls from a cloud and eventually moves at a constant speed.
Explain how the forces on the drop lead to this terminal velocity, and say what
the resultant force is at terminal velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Name the two forces on the drop. One is constant and the other grows with speed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Describe what happens to the resultant force, and so to the acceleration, as the
speed rises.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Two forces act on the drop: its weight downwards, which is constant, and the
drag upwards, which grows as the drop speeds up.

At first the drop is slow, so the drag is small. The resultant is close to the
weight, and the acceleration is close to $g$. As the drop speeds up, the drag
grows. The resultant force falls, so the acceleration falls. When the drag
becomes equal to the weight, the resultant is zero and the drop stops
accelerating. It then falls at a constant terminal velocity.

At terminal velocity the resultant force is zero, so the net force is zero and
the drop moves at constant speed.
</details>
