# 3.3 Work, energy and power — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A car accelerates uniformly from $8.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ to
   $24\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ in 4.0 s. Find its acceleration and the distance
   travelled.
2. A person of mass 60 kg stands in a lift that accelerates downwards at
   $2.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$. Find the normal reaction. Take
   $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.
3. A force of 100 N acts at 35° to the horizontal. Find its horizontal and
   vertical components.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $a = 4.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$ and $s = 64\,\text{m}$.
2. $N = 60(9.81 - 2.0) = 469\,\text{N}$.
3. $100\cos 35^\circ = 81.9\,\text{N}$ and $100\sin 35^\circ = 57.4\,\text{N}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, use $a = \dfrac{v - u}{t}$ and
$s = \dfrac{(u + v)t}{2}$. If question 2 goes wrong, the acceleration is
downwards, so $mg - N = ma$. If question 3 goes wrong, the angle is measured
from the horizontal, so that component uses the cosine.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A force of 150 N moves a crate 8.0 m along the direction of the force.
Find the work done.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The force and the displacement are in the same direction, so the angle between
them is zero.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $W = Fs$, because $\cos 0 = 1$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ W = Fs = 150 \times 8.0 = 1200\,\text{J} $$

The work done is 1.2 kJ.
</details>

**2.** A force of 200 N acts at 40° to the horizontal and drags a box 15 m along
the ground. Find the work done by the force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Only the component of the force along the displacement does work.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $W = Fs\cos\theta$ with $\theta = 40^\circ$, and put your calculator in
degree mode.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= Fs\cos 40^\circ \\
  &= 200 \times 15 \times 0.766 \\
  &= 2300\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The vertical component of the force does no work, because the box moves
horizontally.
</details>

**3.** A stone of mass 4.0 kg is released from rest and falls 12 m. Find the
gravitational potential energy it loses and its speed just before it lands.
Take $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The potential energy lost becomes kinetic energy. You can write
$mg\Delta h = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h$ first, then use $v = \sqrt{2g\Delta h}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E_p &= mg\Delta h = 4.0 \times 9.81 \times 12 = 471\,\text{J} \\
v &= \sqrt{2g\Delta h} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.81 \times 12} \\
  &= 15.3\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The mass cancels in the speed, so a heavier stone would land at the same speed.
</details>

**4.** A ball of mass 0.50 kg is thrown vertically upwards at
$12\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Use an energy method to find the maximum height
it reaches. Take $g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the highest point the kinetic energy is zero, so all the kinetic energy has
become gravitational potential energy.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = mgh$ and cancel the mass before you substitute.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 &= mgh \\
h &= \frac{v^2}{2g} = \frac{12^2}{2 \times 9.81} \\
  &= 7.3\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The energy method gives the same height as $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ from Section 3.1,
because both come from the same conservation law.
</details>

**5.** A motor lifts a load of mass 250 kg through 20 m in 12 s. Find the work
done against gravity and the useful power of the motor. Take
$g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The work done against gravity is $mg\Delta h$. Power is work divided by time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the work first, then divide by 12 s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= mg\Delta h = 250 \times 9.81 \times 20 = 49050\,\text{J} \\
P &= \frac{W}{t} = \frac{49050}{12} = 4088\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

The useful power is about 4.1 kW. A real motor draws more power than this,
because some energy is transferred to thermal stores.
</details>

**6.** A cyclist of total mass 80 kg freewheels from rest down a hill,
descending 20 m. The cyclist reaches $15\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ at the
bottom. Find the energy transferred to thermal stores. Take
$g = 9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the gravitational potential energy lost with the kinetic energy gained.
The difference has gone somewhere.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h$ and $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ separately, then
subtract.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E_p &= 80 \times 9.81 \times 20 = 15696\,\text{J} \\
E_k &= \tfrac{1}{2} \times 80 \times 15^2 = 9000\,\text{J} \\
\text{thermal energy} &= 15696 - 9000 = 6696\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

About 6700 J has been transferred to thermal stores by friction and air
resistance, so the cyclist is slower than the frictionless prediction.
</details>
