# 8.4 Nuclear energy and E = mc² — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Decide first whether the mass is in kilograms or in atomic mass
units, because the two routes use different constants.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write an energy of 2.0 MeV in joules.
2. A mass of $1.0 \times 10^{-30}\,\text{kg}$ is converted completely into
   energy. Find the energy released.
3. Find the energy of a photon of energy 0.511 MeV in joules.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $2.0 \times 10^{6} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} = 3.2 \times 10^{-13}\,\text{J}$.
2. $E = mc^2 = 1.0 \times 10^{-30} \times (3.00 \times 10^{8})^2 = 9.0 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{J}$.
3. $0.511 \times 10^{6} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19} = 8.18 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{J}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, one megaelectronvolt is $1.60 \times 10^{-13}\,\text{J}$.
If question 2 goes wrong, use $E = mc^2$ with the speed of light squared. If
question 3 goes wrong, change mega to $10^{6}$ and multiply by the charge of the
electron.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A reaction converts a mass of $3.0 \times 10^{-29}\,\text{kg}$ into
energy. Find the energy released.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the mass-energy relation with the speed of light squared.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E = mc^2$ with $m = 3.0 \times 10^{-29}\,\text{kg}$ and
$c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= mc^2 = 3.0 \times 10^{-29} \times (3.00 \times 10^{8})^2 \\
  &= 3.0 \times 10^{-29} \times 9.00 \times 10^{16} \\
  &= 2.7 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The energy released is $2.7 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{J}$.
</details>

**2.** A nucleus has a mass defect of 0.050 u. Find its binding energy in MeV.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the energy equivalent of one atomic mass unit from the data sheet.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the mass defect by 931.5 MeV per u.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ E = 0.050 \times 931.5 = 46.6\,\text{MeV} $$

The binding energy is 46.6 MeV.
</details>

**3.** A helium-4 nucleus has a mass defect of 0.0304 u. Find its binding energy
in MeV and its binding energy per nucleon.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the total binding energy first, then divide by the number of nucleons.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply 0.0304 by 931.5, then divide by 4.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= 0.0304 \times 931.5 = 28.3\,\text{MeV} \\
\text{per nucleon} &= \frac{28.3}{4} = 7.08\,\text{MeV}
\end{aligned}
$$

The binding energy is 28.3 MeV, or 7.08 MeV per nucleon.
</details>

**4.** A fission reaction releases 200 MeV. Find the mass defect in kilograms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the energy to joules, then divide by $c^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E = 200 \times 10^{6} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$, then use
$\Delta m = \dfrac{E}{c^2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= 200 \times 10^{6} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}
   = 3.20 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{J} \\
\Delta m &= \frac{3.20 \times 10^{-11}}{(3.00 \times 10^{8})^2}
         = \frac{3.20 \times 10^{-11}}{9.00 \times 10^{16}} \\
         &= 3.56 \times 10^{-28}\,\text{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

The mass defect is $3.56 \times 10^{-28}\,\text{kg}$.
</details>

**5.** A positron and an electron annihilate. Each has a rest energy of
0.511 MeV. Find the total energy released and the energy of each photon, in
joules.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two rest energies add. Two photons share the total energy equally.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $2 \times 0.511\,\text{MeV}$ in joules, then halve it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_{\text{total}} &= 2 \times 0.511 = 1.022\,\text{MeV} \\
                 &= 1.022 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-13}
                  = 1.64 \times 10^{-13}\,\text{J} \\
E_{\text{photon}} &= \frac{1.64 \times 10^{-13}}{2}
                    = 8.2 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

Each photon carries $8.2 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{J}$, or 0.511 MeV.
</details>

**6.** Explain why energy is released when a very heavy nucleus undergoes
fission.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the binding energy per nucleon of the heavy nucleus with that of the
products.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say where the heavy nucleus sits on the binding energy curve, and where the
products sit. Then link a larger binding energy per nucleon to a smaller mass.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A very heavy nucleus sits on the right-hand side of the binding energy curve,
where the binding energy per nucleon is lower than at the peak. Its fission
products are nearer the peak, so they have a larger binding energy per nucleon.
A more tightly bound set of nucleons has less mass. The missing mass is released
as the kinetic energy of the products, through $E = mc^2$.
</details>

**7.** Explain why energy is released when two light nuclei fuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the same curve, but start at the far left.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Compare the binding energy per nucleon of the light nuclei with that of the
heavier product.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Two light nuclei sit on the steep left-hand part of the binding energy curve,
where the binding energy per nucleon is small. The heavier product is closer to
the peak, so its binding energy per nucleon is larger. The nucleons are more
tightly bound in the product, so the product has less mass than the two nuclei
together. The mass difference is released as energy, through $E = mc^2$.
</details>

**8.** The Sun converts about $4.3 \times 10^{9}\,\text{kg}$ of mass into energy
each second. Find the energy released each second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E = mc^2$ with the mass converted each second.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply $4.3 \times 10^{9}$ by $(3.00 \times 10^{8})^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= mc^2 = 4.3 \times 10^{9} \times (3.00 \times 10^{8})^2 \\
  &= 4.3 \times 10^{9} \times 9.00 \times 10^{16} \\
  &= 3.9 \times 10^{26}\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The Sun releases about $3.9 \times 10^{26}\,\text{J}$ each second.
</details>
