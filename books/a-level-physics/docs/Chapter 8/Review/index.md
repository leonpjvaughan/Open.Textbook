# Chapter 8: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The hospital prepares a fluorine-18 tracer for a scan. Its half-life is
110 minutes. At the start of the morning its activity is
$8.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{Bq}$, and the scan needs at least
$2.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{Bq}$. Each positron annihilates with an electron and
produces two 511 keV gamma photons. The detector photocathode has a work
function of 2.00 eV, and the team tests it with 250 nm ultraviolet light. The
teaching electron gun runs at 150 V.

**1. The energy and frequency of a gamma photon.** The photon energy is 511 keV.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= 511 \times 10^{3} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19}
   = 8.18 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{J} \\
f &= \frac{E}{h} = \frac{8.18 \times 10^{-14}}{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}
   = 1.23 \times 10^{20}\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

Its wavelength is $\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f} = 2.43 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{m}$.

**2. The ultraviolet test.** The photon energy of 250 nm light is

$$ E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}
   = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.00 \times 10^{8}}{250 \times 10^{-9}}
   = 7.96 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J} = 4.97\,\text{eV} $$

This is above the 2.00 eV work function, so electrons are released. Their
maximum kinetic energy is

$$ E_{k(\text{max})} = hf - \phi = 4.97 - 2.00 = 2.97\,\text{eV} $$

The stopping potential is 2.97 V. The threshold wavelength is 622 nm, so the
250 nm light is well above the threshold and a red lamp would not release
electrons.

**3. The electron gun.** An electron accelerated through 150 V gains

$$ E_k = eV = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 150 = 2.40 \times 10^{-17}\,\text{J} $$

Its momentum is

$$ p = \sqrt{2mE_k}
   = \sqrt{2 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 2.40 \times 10^{-17}}
   = 6.61 \times 10^{-24}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} $$

and its de Broglie wavelength is

$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{6.61 \times 10^{-24}}
   = 1.00 \times 10^{-10}\,\text{m} $$

**4. The decay constant of fluorine-18.** The half-life is
$110 \times 60 = 6600\,\text{s}$, so

$$ \lambda = \frac{\ln 2}{T_{1/2}} = \frac{0.693}{6600}
   = 1.05 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{s}^{-1} $$

**5. The activity through the morning.** 330 minutes is three half-lives.

$$ A = \frac{8.0 \times 10^{8}}{2^{3}} = 1.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{Bq} $$

550 minutes is five half-lives, so
$A = \dfrac{8.0 \times 10^{8}}{2^{5}} = 2.5 \times 10^{7}\,\text{Bq}$.

**6. The number of undecayed nuclei at the start.**

$$ N = \frac{A}{\lambda} = \frac{8.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.05 \times 10^{-4}}
   = 7.6 \times 10^{12} $$

**7. The energy from the annihilation.** The electron and the positron together
have a rest energy of $2 \times 0.511 = 1.022\,\text{MeV}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= 1.022 \times 1.60 \times 10^{-13} = 1.64 \times 10^{-13}\,\text{J} \\
E_{\text{photon}} &= \frac{1.64 \times 10^{-13}}{2} = 8.18 \times 10^{-14}\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

Each photon carries 0.511 MeV. This matches the gamma photons detected by the
scanner, so the 511 keV photons really do come from the annihilation.

**8. The judgement on the tracer.** The activity must be at least
$2.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{Bq}$. This is one quarter of the starting activity, so
it is reached after two half-lives, or 220 minutes. At 220 minutes the tracer is
at its weakest usable level. At 330 minutes the activity has fallen to
$1.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{Bq}$, which is too low. The team should start the scan
within 220 minutes of preparing the tracer, or prepare a fresh sample. After
that, a longer scan would deliver too small a signal.

**9. The first prediction.** The activity after 330 minutes is not a half, a
quarter or a thousandth. It is one eighth, because 330 minutes contains three
intervals of 110 minutes and each interval halves the activity.
