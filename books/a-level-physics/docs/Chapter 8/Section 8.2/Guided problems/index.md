# 8.2 Wave-particle duality — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Keep the mass in kilograms, the speed in metres per second and
the wavelength in metres.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A ball of mass 0.20 kg moves at $3.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find its
   momentum.
2. Find the energy of a photon of wavelength 500 nm.
3. An electron is accelerated through $200\,\text{V}$. Find its kinetic energy
   in joules.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $p = mv = 0.20 \times 3.0 = 0.60\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
2. $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda} = \dfrac{6.63 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.00 \times 10^{8}}{5.00 \times 10^{-7}} = 3.98 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$.
3. $E_k = eV = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 200 = 3.2 \times 10^{-17}\,\text{J}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, revisit the definition of momentum in Section 3.4. If
question 2 goes wrong, use $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$ from Section 8.1. If
question 3 goes wrong, the kinetic energy is the charge multiplied by the
potential difference.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** An electron moves at $2.0 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. Find
its de Broglie wavelength. The mass of an electron is
$9.11 \times 10^{-31}\,\text{kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the momentum first, then divide the Planck constant by it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $p = mv = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 2.0 \times 10^{6}$, then use
$\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= mv = 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 2.0 \times 10^{6}
   = 1.82 \times 10^{-24}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
\lambda &= \frac{h}{p} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{1.82 \times 10^{-24}}
        = 3.6 \times 10^{-10}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The de Broglie wavelength is $3.6 \times 10^{-10}\,\text{m}$.
</details>

**2.** Find the de Broglie wavelength of a proton moving at
$1.0 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$. The mass of a proton is
$1.67 \times 10^{-27}\,\text{kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The proton is much heavier than the electron, so expect a much shorter
wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the same two steps as question 1, but with the proton mass.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= 1.67 \times 10^{-27} \times 1.0 \times 10^{6}
   = 1.67 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
\lambda &= \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{1.67 \times 10^{-21}}
        = 4.0 \times 10^{-13}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The proton's wavelength is $4.0 \times 10^{-13}\,\text{m}$.
</details>

**3.** An electron has a de Broglie wavelength of
$1.0 \times 10^{-10}\,\text{m}$. Find its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange the de Broglie equation to make the momentum the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $p = \dfrac{h}{\lambda}$ and substitute the two values.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ p = \frac{h}{\lambda} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{1.0 \times 10^{-10}}
   = 6.6 \times 10^{-24}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} $$

The momentum is $6.6 \times 10^{-24}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
</details>

**4.** An electron is accelerated through $500\,\text{V}$. Find its de Broglie
wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the kinetic energy in joules, then the momentum, then the wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E_k = 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 500$, then
$p = \sqrt{2mE_k}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_k &= 1.60 \times 10^{-19} \times 500 = 8.0 \times 10^{-17}\,\text{J} \\
p &= \sqrt{2 \times 9.11 \times 10^{-31} \times 8.0 \times 10^{-17}}
   = 1.21 \times 10^{-23}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
\lambda &= \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{1.21 \times 10^{-23}}
        = 5.5 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The wavelength is $5.5 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{m}$.
</details>

**5.** Find the de Broglie wavelength of a 0.050 kg ball moving at
$20\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$, and explain why this wavelength is never
observed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the same equation, with the ball's mass and speed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the momentum, then divide the Planck constant by it. Compare the wavelength
with the size of any gap you could make.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ \lambda = \frac{h}{p} = \frac{6.63 \times 10^{-34}}{0.050 \times 20}
   = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}\,\text{m} $$

The wavelength is about $10^{-34}\,\text{m}$, far smaller than an atom. To show
diffraction, a gap or a crystal must be about the same size as the wavelength.
No such gap can be made, so the wave nature of the ball is never observed.
</details>

**6.** Describe the electron diffraction experiment, and explain how it shows
that electrons have wave properties.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think of the beam, the target and the pattern on the screen.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the beam, the graphite film or crystal, and the rings. Then link the ring
spacing to a wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A beam of electrons is fired through a thin film of graphite, or at a crystal of
nickel, and the electrons that pass through are recorded on a screen. The screen
shows a pattern of concentric rings, with a dark centre. Only waves produce such
a pattern when they pass through a regular array of gaps. The spacing of the
rings matches the de Broglie wavelength, which is calculated from
$\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p}$. The experiment therefore shows that electrons behave
as waves.
</details>

**7.** In an electron diffraction tube, the rings move closer together when the
accelerating voltage is increased. Explain why.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work through the chain: voltage, kinetic energy, momentum, wavelength, angle of
diffraction.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what a larger voltage does to the kinetic energy and the momentum. Then say
what a larger momentum does to the wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A larger accelerating voltage gives each electron more kinetic energy. More
kinetic energy means more momentum, since $p = \sqrt{2mE_k}$. The de Broglie
wavelength is $\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p}$, so a larger momentum gives a shorter
wavelength. A shorter wavelength diffracts through a smaller angle, so the
rings move closer together.
</details>
