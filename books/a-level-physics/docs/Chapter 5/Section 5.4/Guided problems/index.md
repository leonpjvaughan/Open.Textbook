# 5.4 Capacitance — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Write the unit at every step.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A $6.0\,\Omega$ resistor and a $3.0\,\Omega$ resistor are connected in
   parallel. Find the equivalent resistance.
2. A battery of emf $9.0\,\text{V}$ drives a current of $1.5\,\text{A}$ through
   an external circuit. Find the power delivered to the external circuit.
3. Write $220\,\mu\text{F}$ in farads.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\dfrac{1}{R} = \dfrac{1}{6.0} + \dfrac{1}{3.0} = \dfrac{1}{2.0}$, so
   $R = 2.0\,\Omega$.
2. $P = VI = 9.0 \times 1.5 = 13.5\,\text{W}$.
3. $220 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{F} = 2.20 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{F}$.

If question 1 goes wrong, look again at Section 5.3 and remember that the
reciprocals add. If question 2 goes wrong, use $P = VI$. If question 3 goes
wrong, remember that micro means $10^{-6}$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A capacitor stores a charge of $6.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{C}$ when the pd
across it is $12\,\text{V}$. Find its capacitance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Capacitance is the charge divided by the potential difference.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $C = \dfrac{Q}{V}$ with $Q = 6.0 \times 10^{-4}$ and $V = 12$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ C = \frac{Q}{V} = \frac{6.0 \times 10^{-4}}{12} = 5.0 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{F} $$

The capacitance is $5.0 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{F}$, which is $50\,\mu\text{F}$.
</details>

**2.** A capacitor of capacitance $470\,\mu\text{F}$ is charged to a pd of
$300\,\text{V}$. Find the charge stored.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the capacitance to farads first, then use $Q = CV$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $470\,\mu\text{F} = 4.70 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{F}$, then multiply by $300$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= CV \\
  &= 4.70 \times 10^{-4} \times 300 \\
  &= 0.141\,\text{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

The charge stored is $0.141\,\text{C}$.
</details>

**3.** A capacitor of capacitance $220\,\mu\text{F}$ is charged to $50\,\text{V}$.
Find the energy stored.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2$. Change microfarads to farads and square the pd.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $C = 220 \times 10^{-6}$ and $V = 50$ into $E = \tfrac{1}{2}CV^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= \frac{1}{2}CV^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 220 \times 10^{-6} \times 50^2 \\
  &= \frac{1}{2} \times 220 \times 10^{-6} \times 2500 \\
  &= 0.275\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The energy stored is about $0.28\,\text{J}$.
</details>

**4.** A capacitor of capacitance $100\,\mu\text{F}$ is discharged through a
$200\,\Omega$ resistor. Find the time constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The time constant is the product of the resistance and the capacitance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\tau = RC$ with $R = 200$ and $C = 100 \times 10^{-6}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ \tau = RC = 200 \times 100 \times 10^{-6} = 2.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\text{s} $$

The time constant is $2.0 \times 10^{-2}\,\text{s}$, or $20\,\text{ms}$.
</details>

**5.** A capacitor of capacitance $50\,\mu\text{F}$ is charged to $100\,\text{V}$
and then discharged through a $1.0\,\text{k}\Omega$ resistor. Find the time
constant and the potential difference after one time constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $\tau = RC$ first. After one time constant the pd is $\dfrac{1}{e}$ of its
starting value.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\tau = 1000 \times 50 \times 10^{-6}$, then find
$100\,e^{-1}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\tau &= RC = 1000 \times 50 \times 10^{-6} = 0.050\,\text{s} \\
V &= V_0 e^{-1} = 100 \times 0.368 = 36.8\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

The time constant is $0.050\,\text{s}$, and after one time constant the pd is
about $37\,\text{V}$.
</details>

**6.** A parallel plate capacitor has plates of area $0.020\,\text{m}^2$
separated by $5.0 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{m}$. The dielectric has a relative
permittivity of $3.0$. Find the capacitance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d}$ with
$\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{F}\,\text{m}^{-1}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute the four values. Keep the area on the top and the separation on the
bottom.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
C &= \frac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d} \\
  &= \frac{8.85 \times 10^{-12} \times 3.0 \times 0.020}{5.0 \times 10^{-5}} \\
  &= 1.1 \times 10^{-8}\,\text{F}
\end{aligned}
$$

The capacitance is about $1.1 \times 10^{-8}\,\text{F}$, or $11\,\text{nF}$.
</details>
