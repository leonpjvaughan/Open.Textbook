# 5.1 Current, charge and emf — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Write the unit at every step.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $240\,\text{mA}$ in amperes.
2. A car travels at a steady $15\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$ for $8.0\,\text{s}$.
   Find the distance it travels.
3. Write $1.60 \times 10^{-19}$ as an ordinary decimal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $0.24\,\text{A}$. The prefix milli means $10^{-3}$.
2. Distance is speed multiplied by time: $15 \times 8.0 = 120\,\text{m}$.
3. $0.000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,000\,16$.

If question 1 goes wrong, look again at the prefix table in Section 1.1. If
question 2 goes wrong, check which quantity is a rate and multiply it by the time.
If question 3 goes wrong, count the places after the decimal point.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A current of $0.25\,\text{A}$ flows for $40\,\text{s}$. Find the charge
that flows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Charge is current multiplied by time. The current is already in amperes and the
time is in seconds.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\Delta Q = I\,\Delta t$ with $I = 0.25$ and $\Delta t = 40$, then work
out the product.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ \Delta Q = I\,\Delta t = 0.25 \times 40 = 10\,\text{C} $$

The charge is $10\,\text{C}$.
</details>

**2.** A charge of $6.0\,\text{C}$ passes through a point in a circuit in
$12\,\text{s}$. Find the current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Current is the rate of flow of charge, so divide the charge by the time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $I = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}$ with $\Delta Q = 6.0$ and
$\Delta t = 12$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ I = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t} = \frac{6.0}{12} = 0.50\,\text{A} $$

The current is $0.50\,\text{A}$.
</details>

**3.** Find the number of electrons that carry a charge of $4.8\,\text{C}$. Use
$e = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Each electron carries one elementary charge. Divide the total charge by the
charge on one electron.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $n = \dfrac{Q}{e}$ with $Q = 4.8$ and $e = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= \frac{Q}{e} = \frac{4.8}{1.60 \times 10^{-19}} \\
  &= 3.0 \times 10^{19}
\end{aligned}
$$

So $3.0 \times 10^{19}$ electrons carry the charge.
</details>

**4.** A battery has an emf of $9.0\,\text{V}$ and an internal resistance of
$0.50\,\Omega$. It drives a current of $1.5\,\text{A}$. Find the terminal
potential difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The terminal pd is the emf minus the lost volts inside the battery.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

First find the lost volts $Ir = 1.5 \times 0.50$, then subtract them from the
emf.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \varepsilon - Ir \\
  &= 9.0 - (1.5 \times 0.50) \\
  &= 9.0 - 0.75 = 8.25\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

The terminal pd is $8.25\,\text{V}$, which is $8.3\,\text{V}$ to two significant
figures.
</details>

**5.** A component has a potential difference of $6.0\,\text{V}$ across it and a
current of $0.50\,\text{A}$ through it. Find the power and the energy transferred
in $20\,\text{s}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Power is current multiplied by potential difference. Energy is power multiplied
by time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $P = VI = 6.0 \times 0.50$, then use $W = P\,\Delta t$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= VI = 6.0 \times 0.50 = 3.0\,\text{W} \\
W &= P\,\Delta t = 3.0 \times 20 = 60\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The power is $3.0\,\text{W}$ and the energy is $60\,\text{J}$.
</details>

**6.** A battery of emf $12\,\text{V}$ and internal resistance $0.20\,\Omega$ is
connected to a $5.80\,\Omega$ resistor. Find the current and the terminal
potential difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The emf drives the current through both resistances, so use
$\varepsilon = I(R + r)$ with the total resistance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the resistances to get $R + r$, then divide the emf by that total.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{\varepsilon}{R + r} = \frac{12}{5.80 + 0.20} = \frac{12}{6.00}
   = 2.0\,\text{A} \\
V &= \varepsilon - Ir = 12 - (2.0 \times 0.20) = 11.6\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

The current is $2.0\,\text{A}$ and the terminal pd is $11.6\,\text{V}$.
</details>
