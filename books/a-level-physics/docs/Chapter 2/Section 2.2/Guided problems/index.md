# 2.2 Resolving and adding vectors — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Say which direction each angle is measured from before you use a
sine or a cosine.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 5 and 12. Find the hypotenuse.
2. Work out $50\cos 60^\circ$.
3. Work out $50\sin 60^\circ$ to three significant figures.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\sqrt{5^2 + 12^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13$.
2. $50 \times 0.5 = 25$.
3. $50 \times 0.866 = 43.3$.

If question 1 goes wrong, add the squares of the two shorter sides and take the
square root. If question 2 or 3 goes wrong, check which ratio goes with the
angle. The side opposite the angle uses the sine. The side next to it uses the
cosine.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Resolve a force of 60 N at 40° to the horizontal into horizontal and
vertical components.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is measured from the horizontal, so the horizontal component is the
one that uses the cosine.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $F_x = 60\cos 40^\circ$ and $F_y = 60\sin 40^\circ$, then evaluate each.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_x &= 60\cos 40^\circ = 46.0\,\text{N} \\
F_y &= 60\sin 40^\circ = 38.6\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

Both components are smaller than the 60 N force, which is a useful check.
</details>

**2.** A vector has components $-3.0$ and $+4.0$. Find its magnitude and its
direction measured anticlockwise from the positive $x$-axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the magnitude with Pythagoras first. Then use the tangent to find the angle
inside the triangle, and place the vector in the correct quadrant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The magnitude is $\sqrt{(-3.0)^2 + 4.0^2} = 5.0$. The negative $x$-component and
positive $y$-component put the vector in the second quadrant.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{magnitude} &= \sqrt{(-3.0)^2 + 4.0^2} = 5.0 \\
\text{angle in the triangle} &= \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{4.0}{3.0}\right) = 53.1^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

In the second quadrant the direction from the positive $x$-axis is
$180^\circ - 53.1^\circ = 126.9^\circ$.
</details>

**3.** Two forces of 7.0 N and 24 N act at right angles. Find the magnitude and
direction of the resultant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two forces are already perpendicular components of their resultant, so this
is a right-angled triangle to solve.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $R = \sqrt{7.0^2 + 24^2}$, then $\tan\theta = \dfrac{24}{7.0}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \sqrt{7.0^2 + 24^2} = \sqrt{49 + 576} = \sqrt{625} = 25\,\text{N} \\
\theta &= \tan^{-1}\!\left(\frac{24}{7.0}\right) = 73.7^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

The resultant is 25 N at 73.7° to the 7.0 N force.
</details>

**4.** A weight of 800 N acts on a slope at 20° to the horizontal. Find the
component along the slope and the component perpendicular to the slope.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The weight $W$ acts vertically down. The slope makes 20° with the horizontal, so
the weight makes 20° with the perpendicular to the slope.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The component along the slope uses the sine of the slope angle, and the
perpendicular component uses the cosine.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
W_{\parallel} &= 800\sin 20^\circ = 273.6\,\text{N} \\
W_{\perp} &= 800\cos 20^\circ = 751.8\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The perpendicular component is the larger one, because the slope is shallow.
</details>

**5.** Resolve a velocity of 15 m s⁻¹ at 35° above the horizontal into horizontal
and vertical components.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle is measured from the horizontal. Decide which component uses the
cosine before you reach for the calculator.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $v_x = 15\cos 35^\circ$ and $v_y = 15\sin 35^\circ$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_x &= 15\cos 35^\circ = 12.3\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
v_y &= 15\sin 35^\circ = 8.60\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

Check that $\sqrt{12.3^2 + 8.60^2} = 15.0$, which recovers the original speed.
</details>

**6.** Explain why Pythagoras cannot be used on its own to add two vectors at
55°, and name a method that works.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the triangle formed by the two vectors and their resultant, and about
the condition Pythagoras needs.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

State the condition, then say whether the 55° triangle meets it. Then name a
method that does not need a right angle.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Pythagoras applies only to a right-angled triangle. Two vectors at 55° do not
form a right angle, so Pythagoras cannot give the resultant on its own. A scale
drawing works for any angle: draw the vectors head to tail, then measure the
resultant. Resolving each vector into perpendicular components and adding them
also works.
</details>
