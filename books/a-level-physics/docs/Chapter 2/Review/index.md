# Chapter 2: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The ferry moves at 4.0 m s⁻¹ through the water, and the river is 120 m wide. The
current flows at 3.0 m s⁻¹, at right angles to the crossing.

**1. The velocity $v$ over the ground on the first crossing.** The ferry points
straight across, so its 4.0 m s⁻¹ is one perpendicular component and the 3.0 m s⁻¹
current is the other.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \sqrt{4.0^2 + 3.0^2} \\
  &= \sqrt{16 + 9} \\
  &= \sqrt{25} = 5.0\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\tan\theta = \frac{3.0}{4.0}, \qquad \theta = 36.9^\circ
$$

The velocity over the ground is 5.0 m s⁻¹ at 36.9° to the straight-across
direction, on the downstream side.

**2. The crossing time $t$.** The crossing time depends on the across component
and the width of the river. The current has no across component, so it does not
change the time.

$$
t = \frac{120}{4.0} = 30\,\text{s}
$$

**3. The distance downstream.** The current carries the ferry downstream for the
whole 30 s.

$$
\text{distance downstream} = 3.0 \times 30 = 90\,\text{m}
$$

The ferry lands 90 m downstream of the point directly opposite.

**4. The heading to land directly opposite.** To land directly opposite, the
ferry must have no downstream component over the ground. Its upstream component
must cancel the current, so $4.0\sin\theta = 3.0$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\sin\theta &= \frac{3.0}{4.0} = 0.75 \\
\theta &= \sin^{-1}(0.75) = 48.6^\circ
\end{aligned}
$$

The ferry heads 48.6° upstream of the straight-across direction.

**5. The crossing time with that heading.** The across component is now the
cosine of the heading.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{\text{across}} &= 4.0\cos 48.6^\circ = 2.65\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1} \\
t &= \frac{120}{2.65} = 45\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

**6. The judgement.** The two crossings are different trades. Pointing straight
across reaches the far bank in 30 s, but the ferry lands 90 m downstream.
Heading 48.6° upstream lands directly opposite, but takes 45 s, because part of
the ferry speed is used to fight the current. The operator chooses between time
and a straight route. This final judgement is a reasoned answer. It is not the
kind of claim a computer can check, so compare your reasoning with the evidence
above.
