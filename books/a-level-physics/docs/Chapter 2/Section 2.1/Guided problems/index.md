# 2.1 Scalars and vectors — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write $72\,\text{km}\,\text{h}^{-1}$ in metres per second.
2. Write $0.000\,45\,\text{m}$ in standard form to two significant figures.
3. A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 3 and 4. Find the hypotenuse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $72\,\text{km}\,\text{h}^{-1} = 20\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
2. $4.5 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}$.
3. The hypotenuse is $\sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{25} = 5$.

If question 1 goes wrong, multiply by 1000 and divide by 3600. If question 2
goes wrong, count the places the decimal point moves: four places to the right
after the 4. If question 3 goes wrong, use $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$, where $c$ is the
hypotenuse.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Classify each quantity as a scalar or a vector: energy, acceleration,
temperature, weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask whether the quantity needs a direction to make sense in space. Energy and
temperature do not; a force does.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Weight is a force, and acceleration has a direction. Energy and temperature have
a magnitude only.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Energy and temperature are scalars. Acceleration and weight are vectors.
Acceleration of $9.81\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-2}$ downwards is complete; a weight
of 50 N needs a direction, such as 50 N downwards.
</details>

**2.** A hiker walks 4.0 km north, then 2.5 km south. Find the resultant
displacement.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two displacements are along the same line but in opposite directions. Choose
north as positive.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write 4.0 km as $+4.0$ and 2.5 km south as $-2.5$, then add the signed numbers.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= (+4.0) + (-2.5) \\
  &= +1.5\,\text{km}
\end{aligned}
$$

The resultant displacement is 1.5 km north. The distance travelled is 6.5 km.
</details>

**3.** A car drives at a steady 15 m s⁻¹ round a bend. Explain why its speed is
constant but its velocity is changing.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at what the two words mean. Speed is the magnitude of the velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the direction of the car's motion as it goes round the bend.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The speed is a scalar, and it is the magnitude of the velocity. A steady speed of
15 m s⁻¹ means the magnitude does not change. The velocity is a vector, and its
direction changes as the car goes round the bend. A change in direction is a
change in velocity, so the velocity changes while the speed stays constant.
</details>

**4.** A cyclist rides 8.0 km east, then 6.0 km west. Find the resultant
displacement.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take east as positive and give the westward displacement a negative sign.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $+8.0$ and $-6.0$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ s = (+8.0) + (-6.0) = +2.0\,\text{km} $$

The resultant displacement is 2.0 km east. The distance travelled is 14.0 km.
</details>

**5.** Explain why electric current is a scalar even though it has a direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about how two currents combine at a junction, and compare that with the
triangle rule for vectors.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

State the test a quantity must pass to be a vector, then say whether current
passes it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A quantity is a vector only if it adds by the triangle rule. Two currents meeting
at a junction add as ordinary numbers, and a current is not drawn as an arrow
with a length. Current fails the vector test, so it is a scalar.
</details>

**6.** A dog runs 50 m east, then 50 m west. Find its resultant displacement.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two displacements are equal in size and opposite in direction.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add $+50$ and $-50$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ s = (+50) + (-50) = 0\,\text{m} $$

The resultant displacement is zero, because the dog finishes where it started.
The distance travelled is 100 m.
</details>
