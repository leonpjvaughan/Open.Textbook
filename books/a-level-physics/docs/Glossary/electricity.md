# Glossary — Electricity

**Capacitance** — the charge stored on one plate of a capacitor divided by the
potential difference across the capacitor, $C = \dfrac{Q}{V}$. The unit is the
farad.

**Capacitor** — a component that stores charge on two separated conductors.

**Charge carrier** — a particle that carries electric charge through a material,
such as an electron in a metal or an ion in a solution.

**Conventional current** — the direction in which positive charge would flow, from
the positive terminal of a source around the circuit to the negative terminal. It
is opposite to the flow of electrons.

**Dielectric** — the insulating material between the plates of a capacitor.

**Electric current** — the rate of flow of electric charge,
$I = \dfrac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}$. The unit is the ampere.

**Electromotive force (emf)** — the energy transferred to each unit of charge by a
source, $\varepsilon = \dfrac{W}{Q}$. The unit is the volt.

**Elementary charge** — the smallest packet of charge, carried by one electron or
one proton, $e = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$.

**Farad** — the unit of capacitance. One farad is one coulomb per volt.

**Internal resistance** — the resistance inside a source, which makes the
terminal potential difference less than the emf when a current flows.

**Kirchhoff's junction rule** — the total current entering a junction equals the
total current leaving it, because charge is conserved.

**Kirchhoff's loop rule** — around any closed loop in a circuit, the sum of the
emfs equals the sum of the potential differences, because energy is conserved.

**Light-dependent resistor (LDR)** — a component whose resistance falls as the
light falling on it increases. It is used in a potential divider as a light
sensor.

**Lost volts** — the potential difference $Ir$ across the internal resistance of a
source. It is the part of the emf that is not available to the external circuit.

**Ohm** — the unit of resistance. One ohm is one volt per ampere.

**Ohm's law** — for a metallic conductor at constant temperature, the current
through the conductor is proportional to the potential difference across it.

**Parallel** — describing components connected across the same two points, so the
potential difference across each component is the same.

**Parallel plate capacitor** — a capacitor made of two parallel plates separated
by a dielectric, with $C = \dfrac{\varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r A}{d}$.

**Potential difference (pd)** — the energy transferred from each unit of charge by
a component, $V = \dfrac{W}{Q}$. The unit is the volt.

**Potential divider** — two or more components in series that share a supply
potential difference. The output is
$V_{\text{out}} = V_{\text{in}}\dfrac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}$.

**Resistance** — the potential difference across a component divided by the
current through it, $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$. The unit is the ohm.

**Resistivity** — the resistance of a piece of material of unit length and unit
cross-sectional area, $\rho = \dfrac{RA}{L}$. The unit is the ohm metre.

**Series** — describing components connected in a single path, so the same current
flows through each component.

**Superconductor** — a material whose resistance falls to zero below a critical
temperature.

**Terminal potential difference** — the potential difference across the terminals
of a source when a current flows. It equals the emf minus the lost volts.

**Thermistor** — a component whose resistance falls as its temperature rises. It
is used in a potential divider as a temperature sensor.

**Time constant** — the product $RC$ for a capacitor discharging through a
resistor. It is the time for the charge or the potential difference to fall to
$\dfrac{1}{e}$ of its starting value.
