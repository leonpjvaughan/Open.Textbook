# Glossary — Thermal physics and matter

**Absolute zero** — the lowest temperature that is possible, at which the volume
of an ideal gas would fall to zero. It is 0 K, or about −273 °C.

**Avogadro constant** — the number of particles in one mole of a substance,
$N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$.

**Avogadro's law** — equal volumes of any gas at the same temperature and
pressure contain the same number of molecules.

**Boltzmann constant** — the constant
$k = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\,\text{J}\,\text{K}^{-1}$ that links the average
kinetic energy of a gas molecule to the temperature. It is related to the molar
gas constant by $R = N_A k$.

**Boyle's law** — for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature, the pressure
multiplied by the volume is constant, so $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$.

**Change of state** — a change from one state of matter to another, such as
melting, boiling, freezing or condensing. The temperature stays the same during
the change.

**Charles's law** — for a fixed mass of gas at constant pressure, the volume
divided by the temperature in kelvin is constant, so
$\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}$.

**Gas laws** — the three laws that link the pressure, the volume and the
temperature of a fixed mass of gas: Boyle's law, Charles's law and the pressure
law.

**Heating curve** — a graph of temperature against time for a substance heated at
a steady rate. The sloping parts show the substance warming up, and the flat
parts show a change of state.

**Ideal gas equation** — the equation $pV = nRT$, or $pV = NkT$, that links the
pressure, the volume, the amount of gas and the temperature. The temperature must
be in kelvin.

**Ideal gas model** — a model of a gas as a very large number of identical
molecules in random motion, with a negligible volume and negligible forces
between them except during elastic collisions.

**Internal energy** — the total kinetic energy plus the total potential energy of
all the particles in a system.

**Kelvin** — the SI unit of temperature. The kelvin scale starts at absolute
zero, and $T(\text{K}) = \theta(^\circ\text{C}) + 273$. A change of 1 K is the
same size as a change of 1 °C.

**Kinetic theory** — the theory that explains the pressure and the temperature of
a gas from the motion and the collisions of its molecules.

**Molar gas constant** — the constant
$R = 8.31\,\text{J}\,\text{K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ that appears in
$pV = nRT$. It is related to the Boltzmann constant by $R = N_A k$.

**Molar mass** — the mass of one mole of a substance, in
$\text{kg}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$. It is written $M$ in
$c_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}}$.

**Mole** — the amount of a substance that contains the Avogadro constant of
particles, $6.02 \times 10^{23}$. Its symbol is mol.

**Molecule** — a group of atoms joined together. In a gas, the molecules move at
random and collide with one another and with the walls of the container.

**Plateau** — the flat part of a heating curve, where a substance changes state
and the temperature stays the same.

**Pressure** — the force per unit area on a surface. A gas exerts a pressure
because very many molecules collide with the walls of the container each second.

**Pressure law** — for a fixed mass of gas at constant volume, the pressure
divided by the temperature in kelvin is constant, so
$\dfrac{p_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{p_2}{T_2}$.

**Root-mean-square speed** — the square root of the mean of the squares of the
speeds of the molecules in a gas, written $c_{\text{rms}}$. It appears in
$pV = \tfrac{1}{3}Nm(c_{\text{rms}})^2$ and is larger than the average speed.

**Specific heat capacity** — the energy needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg
of a substance by 1 K. In symbols $E = mc\Delta\theta$, and the unit is
$\text{J}\,\text{kg}^{-1}\,\text{K}^{-1}$.

**Specific latent heat** — the energy needed to change the state of 1 kg of a
substance with no change in temperature. In symbols $E = mL$, and the unit is
$\text{J}\,\text{kg}^{-1}$. There are two kinds: the specific latent heat of
fusion and the specific latent heat of vaporisation.

**Specific latent heat of fusion** — the specific latent heat for melting or
freezing.

**Specific latent heat of vaporisation** — the specific latent heat for boiling
or condensing.

**System** — the object, or the group of objects, that is being studied.

**Temperature** — a measure of the average random kinetic energy of the particles
in a system. It does not measure the total energy stored.

**Vapour pressure** — the pressure of the vapour above a liquid. A liquid boils
when its vapour pressure equals the pressure acting on it.
