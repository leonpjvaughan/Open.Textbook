# Glossary — Quantum and nuclear physics

**Activity** — the number of nuclei that decay each second, $A = \dfrac{\Delta
N}{\Delta t}$. Its unit is the becquerel.

**Alpha particle** — a helium nucleus, $^{4}_{2}\text{He}$, emitted by some
unstable nuclei. It has 2 protons and 2 neutrons.

**Antiparticle** — a particle with the same mass as another particle but the
opposite charge. The antiparticle of the electron is the positron.

**Atomic mass unit** — a unit of mass used for nuclei,
$1\,\text{u} = 1.66 \times 10^{-27}\,\text{kg} = 931.5\,\text{MeV}$.

**Background count rate** — the count rate a detector records when no source is
present, caused by nearby materials and cosmic rays. It is subtracted from a
measured count rate.

**Becquerel** — the unit of activity, $\text{Bq}$. One becquerel is one decay
per second.

**Beta-minus decay** — decay in which a neutron turns into a proton and the
nucleus emits an electron and an antineutrino. The proton number rises by 1.

**Beta-plus decay** — decay in which a proton turns into a neutron and the
nucleus emits a positron and a neutrino. The proton number falls by 1.

**Binding energy** — the energy needed to separate a nucleus into its separate
protons and neutrons, equal to the mass defect multiplied by $c^2$.

**Binding energy per nucleon** — the binding energy of a nucleus divided by its
nucleon number. It is largest near iron-56.

**Count rate** — the number of particles a detector records each second. It is
smaller than the activity, because not every decay is detected.

**de Broglie wavelength** — the wavelength of a moving particle,
$\lambda = \dfrac{h}{p} = \dfrac{h}{mv}$.

**Decay constant** — the probability that a given nucleus decays in one second,
written $\lambda$. Its unit is $\text{s}^{-1}$, and $T_{1/2} = \dfrac{\ln
2}{\lambda}$.

**Electron diffraction** — the pattern of rings formed when a beam of electrons
passes through a thin crystal. It shows that electrons have wave properties.

**Energy level** — one of the fixed energies an electron can have in an atom. A
photon is emitted when an electron moves between two levels, with
$hf = E_1 - E_2$.

**Gamma radiation** — a high-energy photon emitted by an unstable nucleus. The
nucleon number and the proton number do not change.

**Half-life** — the average time for half of the unstable nuclei in a sample to
decay, or for the activity to fall to half its starting value.

**Isotope** — one of two or more atoms of the same element with the same proton
number but different nucleon numbers.

**Line spectrum** — the set of bright lines of particular wavelengths emitted by
an element, one for each allowed energy transition.

**Mass defect** — the difference between the mass of a nucleus and the total
mass of its separate protons and neutrons.

**Nucleon** — a proton or a neutron, the particles that make up a nucleus.

**Nucleon number** — the total number of protons and neutrons in a nucleus,
written $A$.

**Nuclide** — a particular nucleus with a given proton number and nucleon
number, written $^{A}_{Z}\text{X}$.

**Photoelectric effect** — the release of electrons from a metal surface when
light of a high enough frequency falls on it.

**Photocurrent** — the electric current formed by the photoelectrons released in
the photoelectric effect.

**Photoelectron** — an electron released from a metal surface by the
photoelectric effect.

**Photon** — a packet of electromagnetic energy. Its energy is $E = hf$.

**Planck constant** — the constant $h = 6.63 \times 10^{-34}\,\text{J}\,\text{s}$
that links a photon's energy to its frequency.

**Positron** — the antiparticle of the electron. It has the same mass as an
electron and a positive charge.

**Proton number** — the number of protons in a nucleus, written $Z$. It decides
which element the atom is.

**Radioactive decay** — the spontaneous change of an unstable nucleus into a
more stable one, with the release of radiation.

**Spontaneous** — describing a decay that happens without anything outside the
nucleus setting it off.

**Stopping potential** — the potential difference that brings the fastest
photoelectrons to rest, $eV_s = E_{k(\text{max})}$.

**Threshold frequency** — the lowest frequency of light that releases electrons
from a metal surface, $f_0 = \dfrac{\phi}{h}$.

**Threshold wavelength** — the longest wavelength of light that releases
electrons from a metal surface, $\lambda_0 = \dfrac{hc}{\phi}$.

**Wave-particle duality** — the idea that light and matter show both wave
properties and particle properties, depending on the experiment.

**Work function** — the minimum energy an electron needs to escape from a metal
surface, written $\phi$.
