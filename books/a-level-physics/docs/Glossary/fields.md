# Glossary — Fields

**Coulomb constant** — the constant $k = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}
= 8.99 \times 10^{9}\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{2}\,\text{C}^{-2}$, which appears in
Coulomb's law and in the field of a point charge.

**Coulomb's law** — the electric force between two point charges in a vacuum,
$F = \dfrac{kQq}{r^{2}}$. The force is attractive for opposite charges and
repulsive for like charges.

**Cyclotron** — a machine that sends charged particles round a circle many times
in a magnetic field so that they gain energy each time they cross an electric
field.

**Eddy current** — a current induced in a piece of metal by a changing magnetic
field. Eddy currents can be useful, as in an induction hob, or wasteful, as in a
transformer core.

**Electric field** — the region around a charge in which another charge feels a
force. Its field lines point away from a positive charge and towards a negative
charge.

**Electric field strength** — the force per unit positive charge at a point,
$E = \dfrac{F}{Q}$. Its unit is the newton per coulomb, or the volt per metre.

**Electric potential** — the energy per unit positive charge at a point, taking
the potential as zero at infinity. For a point charge,
$V = \dfrac{kQ}{r}$. Its unit is the volt.

**Electromagnetic induction** — the creation of an emf in a conductor by a
changing magnetic flux.

**Equipotential surface** — a surface on which the potential is the same at
every point. No work is done moving a charge along it, and the field lines are
at right angles to it.

**Escape speed** — the smallest speed a body needs to leave a planet and never
return, $v_{\text{esc}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{2GM}{R}}$.

**Faraday's law** — the induced emf equals the rate of change of the magnetic
flux linkage, $\varepsilon = -N\dfrac{\Delta\Phi}{\Delta t}$.

**Field line** — a line drawn to show the direction of a field. The direction of
the line is the direction of the force on a small test body placed at that
point, and the lines are closer together where the field is stronger.

**Fleming's left-hand rule** — the rule for the direction of the force on a
current-carrying wire in a magnetic field. The first finger points along the
field, the second finger along the current, and the thumb gives the force.

**Flux linkage** — the magnetic flux through a coil multiplied by the number of
turns, $N\Phi = NBA$. Its unit is the weber.

**Force field** — a region in which a body feels a force without anything
touching it, such as a gravitational, electric or magnetic field.

**Gravitational constant** — the constant
$G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{2}\,\text{kg}^{-2}$, which
appears in Newton's law of gravitation.

**Gravitational field strength** — the gravitational force per unit mass at a
point, $g = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{GM}{r^{2}}$. Its unit is the newton per
kilogram.

**Gravitational potential** — the energy per unit mass needed to move a small
mass from infinity to a point, $V = -\dfrac{GM}{r}$. It is negative, because
the potential is zero at infinity.

**Induced emf** — the emf created by a changing magnetic flux, by Faraday's law.
It drives an induced current if the conductor forms a complete circuit.

**Lenz's law** — the induced current flows in the direction that opposes the
change that produced it. The law is a statement of the conservation of energy.

**Magnetic field** — the region around a magnet, a current or a moving charge in
which a magnetic force can be felt. Its field lines are closed loops.

**Magnetic flux** — the product of the magnetic flux density and the area at
right angles to the field, $\Phi = BA$. Its unit is the weber.

**Magnetic flux density** — the quantity that describes the strength of a
magnetic field, symbol $B$, measured in tesla. It is defined by $F = BIL$ for a
wire at right angles to the field.

**Mass spectrometer** — an instrument that accelerates ions, deflects them in a
magnetic field, and separates them by mass. The radius of the path is
$r = \dfrac{mv}{BQ}$.

**Newton's law of gravitation** — the gravitational force between two point
masses, $F = \dfrac{GMm}{r^{2}}$. The force is always attractive.

**Permittivity of free space** — the constant
$\varepsilon_0 = 8.85 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{F}\,\text{m}^{-1}$, which appears
in the Coulomb constant $k = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$.

**Radial field** — a field whose lines point towards or away from a single
point, such as the gravitational field of a sphere or the electric field of a
point charge.

**Search coil** — a small coil of many turns used to measure a changing magnetic
field from the emf it induces,
$\varepsilon = NA\dfrac{\Delta B}{\Delta t}$.

**Tesla** — the unit of magnetic flux density, $\text{T}$. One tesla is the flux
density that gives a force of one newton on a wire of length one metre carrying
a current of one ampere at right angles to the field.

**Transformer** — two coils wound on the same core, used to change an
alternating voltage. The turns ratio sets the voltage ratio,
$\dfrac{V_s}{V_p} = \dfrac{N_s}{N_p}$.

**Uniform field** — a field whose strength and direction are the same at every
point, drawn with parallel, equally spaced field lines.

**Weber** — the unit of magnetic flux, $\text{Wb}$. One weber is one tesla metre
squared, $\text{T}\,\text{m}^{2}$.
