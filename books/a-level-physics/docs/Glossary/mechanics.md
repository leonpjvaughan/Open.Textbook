# Glossary — Mechanics

**Acceleration** — the rate of change of velocity. Acceleration is a vector, so
it has a direction as well as a magnitude.

**Amplitude** — the maximum displacement of an oscillation from its equilibrium
position.

**Angular frequency** — the rate of change of the phase of an oscillation,
$\omega = 2\pi f = \dfrac{2\pi}{T}$, measured in radians per second.

**Angular velocity** — the rate of change of angle for an object moving in a
circle, $\omega = \dfrac{\Delta\theta}{\Delta t}$, measured in radians per
second.

**Centripetal acceleration** — the acceleration of an object moving in a circle,
directed towards the centre, with size $a = \dfrac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r$.

**Centripetal force** — the resultant force directed towards the centre of a
circle that produces the centripetal acceleration,
$F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} = m\omega^2 r$. It is provided by a real force such as
tension, friction or gravity.

**Component** — one of two perpendicular vectors that together have the same
effect as a single vector. A vector $v$ at an angle $\theta$ to the $x$-axis has
components $v\cos\theta$ along $x$ and $v\sin\theta$ along $y$.

**Damping** — the loss of energy from an oscillation to the surroundings, which
makes the amplitude decay over time.

**Displacement** — the straight-line distance and direction from a start point to
a finish point. Displacement is a vector.

**Elastic collision** — a collision in which both momentum and kinetic energy are
conserved.

**Equilibrium position** — the position of an oscillating object when the
resultant force on it is zero, and where it would rest.

**Frequency** — the number of complete oscillations per second, $f = \dfrac{1}{T}$,
measured in hertz.

**Free fall** — motion under gravity alone, with air resistance taken as
negligible.

**Gravitational potential energy** — the energy stored by an object because of
its position in a gravitational field. Near the Earth, the change is
$\Delta E_p = mg\Delta h$.

**Impulse** — the product of a force and the time for which it acts, $F\Delta t$.
The impulse on an object equals its change in momentum.

**Inelastic collision** — a collision in which momentum is conserved but kinetic
energy is not.

**Inertia** — the tendency of an object to keep its velocity. A larger mass has
more inertia.

**Kinetic energy** — the energy an object has because of its motion,
$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.

**Magnitude** — the size of a vector, written without a direction. The magnitude
of a velocity is a speed.

**Momentum** — the product of mass and velocity, $p = mv$. Momentum is a vector,
measured in $\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.

**Newton's first law** — an object stays at rest, or keeps moving in a straight
line at constant velocity, unless a resultant force acts on it.

**Newton's second law** — the resultant force on an object equals its mass
multiplied by its acceleration, $F = ma$. In terms of momentum,
$F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$.

**Newton's third law** — if object A exerts a force on object B, then object B
exerts an equal and opposite force on object A. The two forces act on different
objects.

**Period** — the time for one complete oscillation, $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$.

**Power** — the rate of transfer of energy, $P = \dfrac{W}{t} = Fv$, measured in
watts.

**Projectile** — an object moving under gravity alone after it is launched, with
air resistance taken as negligible.

**Radian** — the angle at the centre of a circle that cuts off an arc equal in
length to the radius. A full circle is $2\pi$ radians, so
$2\pi\ \text{rad} = 360^\circ$.

**Resolving** — replacing a vector by two perpendicular components that have the
same combined effect.

**Resultant** — the single vector that has the same effect as two or more
vectors added together.

**Scalar** — a quantity with a magnitude only, such as mass, time, temperature or
energy.

**Scale drawing** — a drawing in which vectors are represented by arrows whose
lengths are in a fixed proportion to their magnitudes. It adds vectors at any
angle.

**Simple harmonic motion** — an oscillation in which the acceleration is
proportional to the displacement from the equilibrium position and directed
towards it, $a = -\omega^2 x$.

**Terminal velocity** — the constant velocity reached by an object falling
through a fluid when the drag force equals its weight.

**Triangle rule** — the rule for adding two vectors: place the tail of the second
at the tip of the first, and draw the resultant from the tail of the first to the
tip of the second. A quantity is a vector only if it adds by this rule.

**Vector** — a quantity with a magnitude and a direction, such as displacement,
velocity, acceleration or force.

**Velocity** — the rate of change of displacement. Velocity is a vector, so it
has a direction as well as a magnitude.

**Weight** — the gravitational force on an object, $W = mg$. Weight is a vector,
measured in newtons.

**Work** — the energy transferred when a force moves its point of application,
$W = Fs\cos\theta$, measured in joules.
