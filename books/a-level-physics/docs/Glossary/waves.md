# Glossary — Waves

**Antinode** — a point on a stationary wave where the amplitude is largest.

**Coherence** — a property of two sources that have the same frequency and a
constant phase difference, so their interference pattern stays still.

**Compression** — a place in a longitudinal wave where the particles are closer
together than usual.

**Constructive interference** — the meeting of two waves in phase, so their
displacements add and the resultant amplitude is larger.

**Destructive interference** — the meeting of two waves antiphase, so their
displacements subtract and the resultant amplitude is smaller.

**Diffraction** — the spreading of a wave when it passes through a gap or past an
edge.

**Diffraction grating** — a slide with many parallel slits, close together, used
to split light into its wavelengths. Its maxima are at $d\sin\theta = n\lambda$.

**Fringe spacing** — the distance from one bright fringe to the next in a
two-slit pattern, $w = \dfrac{\lambda D}{s}$.

**Fundamental frequency** — the lowest frequency at which a string or an air
column can form a stationary wave. It is also called the first harmonic.

**Harmonic** — one of the frequencies at which a string or an air column forms a
stationary wave. The $n$th harmonic has $n$ half wavelengths on the string or
$n$ times the fundamental frequency.

**Longitudinal wave** — a wave in which the particles vibrate along the direction
in which the wave travels. Sound is a longitudinal wave.

**Node** — a point on a stationary wave where the displacement is always zero.

**Order (of a maximum)** — the number $n$ in the grating equation
$d\sin\theta = n\lambda$. Order 0 is the straight-through direction, order 1 is
the first maximum on each side, and so on.

**Path difference** — the difference in the distances travelled by two waves from
their sources to the same point. It decides whether the waves reinforce or
cancel.

**Phase** — the stage a wave has reached in its cycle, measured as an angle.

**Phase difference** — how far apart in the cycle two points on a wave are. One
wavelength is $360^\circ$ or $2\pi$ radians.

**Plane polarised** — describes light whose electric field vibrates in one plane
only.

**Polarisation** — the restricting of a transverse wave's vibration to one plane.
Only transverse waves can be polarised.

**Polarising filter** — a filter that transmits only the component of a
transverse wave's vibration along its axis.

**Progressive wave** — a wave that carries energy from one place to another
without carrying matter with it.

**Rarefaction** — a place in a longitudinal wave where the particles are further
apart than usual.

**Stationary wave** — a wave formed when two waves of the same frequency travel
in opposite directions and superpose. It has nodes and antinodes and transfers
no net energy.

**Superposition, principle of** — when two waves meet, the resultant displacement
at each point is the sum of the displacements the waves would have on their own.

**Transverse wave** — a wave in which the particles vibrate at right angles to
the direction in which the wave travels. Light is a transverse wave.

**Wavelength** — the distance between two neighbouring points on a wave that are
in step, such as two crests.
