# Chapter 7: Thermal physics and matter

## The chapter problem: the pressure cooker

A pressure cooker is a strong pan with a lid that clamps shut. A valve in the lid
holds the pressure inside higher than the pressure of the air. A cook uses one to
cook food faster.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- The cooker holds **1.50 kg** of water at **20 °C**.
- The water boils at **121 °C**, because the pressure inside is higher than
  normal.
- The specific heat capacity of water is **4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹**.
- The specific latent heat of vaporisation of water is **2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹**.
- The steam above the water fills a space of volume **2.00 × 10⁻³ m³**.
- That steam is at a pressure of **2.00 × 10⁵ Pa** and a temperature of
  **121 °C**. Treat the steam as an ideal gas.
- The molar gas constant is **R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**, the Boltzmann constant is
  **k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹**, and the Avogadro constant is
  **N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹**.
- The cooker boils for **12 minutes**, and in that time **0.050 kg** of water
  turns into steam.

By the end of the chapter you will find the energy needed to heat the water and
to turn some of it into steam. You will find how many moles and how many
molecules of steam are in the space. You will find the average kinetic energy of
a steam molecule and its rms speed. Then you will explain why the cooker reaches
121 °C, and why the lid is clamped shut.

> **First contact: no working yet.** Predict. The lid is clamped shut, so the
> steam cannot escape. As the water is heated, what happens to the pressure
> inside the cooker? Do you think the water can get hotter than 100 °C before it
> boils? Write down your answers and one sentence of reasoning.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>If you are not sure where to start</summary>

The lid is clamped on, so the volume of the space above the water cannot change.
As the water gets hotter, the molecules in the steam move faster. Think about
what faster molecules do to the force on the inside of the lid.
</details>

## By the end of this chapter you will be able to

- explain what internal energy is, and how it differs from temperature;
- use $E = mc\Delta\theta$ for the energy that changes a temperature;
- find a specific heat capacity from a heating experiment;
- explain what a change of state is, and why the temperature stays constant;
- use $E = mL$ for the energy that changes a state;
- read a heating curve, and explain its flat parts;
- describe the ideal gas model and its assumptions;
- use the gas laws for a fixed mass of gas;
- convert between the Celsius and kelvin scales;
- explain what absolute zero is;
- use $pV = nRT$ and $pV = NkT$;
- explain the pressure of a gas in terms of molecular collisions;
- use $pV = \dfrac{1}{3}Nm(c_{\text{rms}})^2$;
- find the average molecular kinetic energy and the rms speed of a gas;
- describe how to test Boyle's law.

## Where it is used

A chef uses a pressure cooker to raise the boiling point of water and cook food
faster. A power station boils water into steam and uses the steam to turn a
turbine. A weather forecaster explains why a balloon expands as it rises into
lower pressure. A nurse follows the safe pressure limits printed on a gas
cylinder, because a warm cylinder holds a higher pressure. A fridge moves energy
from the food compartment to the room by boiling and condensing a refrigerant.
Every engine that burns fuel has to manage the heat it produces.
