# 7.2 Ideal gases and molecular kinetic theory — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check. Keep the temperature in kelvin, and keep the volume in cubic
metres when you use the ideal gas equation.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A molecule of mass $4.8 \times 10^{-26}\,\text{kg}$ moves at 500 m s⁻¹. Find
   its kinetic energy.
2. The same molecule strikes a wall at 500 m s⁻¹ and rebounds at 500 m s⁻¹. Find
   the change in its momentum.
3. Write 200 cm³ in cubic metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.5 \times 4.8 \times 10^{-26} \times 500^2 = 6.0 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J}$.
2. The velocity reverses, so $\Delta p = 2mv = 2 \times 4.8 \times 10^{-26} \times 500 = 4.8 \times 10^{-23}\,\text{kg}\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}$.
3. $200\,\text{cm}^3 = 200 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3 = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3$.

If question 1 goes wrong, remember that kinetic energy is
$\tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. If question 2 goes wrong, the momentum changes direction,
so the change is twice the momentum. If question 3 goes wrong, one metre is
100 cm, so one cubic metre is $10^{6}\,\text{cm}^{3}$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A gas occupies $4.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^{3}$ at a pressure of
$1.0 \times 10^{5}\,\text{Pa}$. It is compressed at constant temperature to
$1.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^{3}$. Find the new pressure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The temperature is constant, so this is Boyle's law.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$ and make $p_2$ the subject: $p_2 = \dfrac{p_1V_1}{V_2}$.
The volume falls to one quarter, so predict a pressure four times larger before
you substitute.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_2 &= \frac{p_1V_1}{V_2} \\
    &= \frac{1.0 \times 10^{5} \times 4.0 \times 10^{-3}}{1.0 \times 10^{-3}} \\
    &= 4.0 \times 10^{5}\,\text{Pa}
\end{aligned}
$$

The new pressure is $4.0 \times 10^{5}\,\text{Pa}$.

</details>

**2.** A fixed mass of gas at 290 K occupies $2.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^{3}$.
At constant pressure it is warmed to 348 K. Find its new volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The pressure is constant, so this is Charles's law. The temperature must be in
kelvin, and it already is.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $\dfrac{V_1}{T_1} = \dfrac{V_2}{T_2}$ and make $V_2$ the subject:
$V_2 = \dfrac{V_1T_2}{T_1}$. The temperature rises by a factor of
$\dfrac{348}{290}$, so the volume rises by the same factor.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_2 &= \frac{V_1T_2}{T_1} \\
    &= \frac{2.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 348}{290} \\
    &= 2.4 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^{3}
\end{aligned}
$$

The new volume is $2.4 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^{3}$.

</details>

**3.** Find the number of moles in $0.020\,\text{m}^{3}$ of gas at
$1.0 \times 10^{5}\,\text{Pa}$ and 300 K. Use
$R = 8.31\,\text{J}\,\text{K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the ideal gas equation $pV = nRT$. The pressure, volume and temperature are
already in SI units.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Make $n$ the subject: $n = \dfrac{pV}{RT}$. Work out the top line and the bottom
line separately before you divide.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= \frac{pV}{RT} \\
  &= \frac{1.0 \times 10^{5} \times 0.020}{8.31 \times 300} \\
  &= 0.802\,\text{mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

The gas contains 0.802 mol.

</details>

**4.** Find the number of molecules in the gas in the previous problem. Use
$n = 0.802\,\text{mol}$ and $N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One mole contains the Avogadro constant, so multiply the number of moles by
$N_A$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $N = nN_A = 0.802 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ N = nN_A = 0.802 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 4.83 \times 10^{23} $$

The gas contains $4.83 \times 10^{23}$ molecules.

</details>

**5.** Find the average random kinetic energy of a gas molecule at 300 K. Use
$k = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\,\text{J}\,\text{K}^{-1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The average molecular kinetic energy is $\dfrac{3}{2}kT$. The temperature is
already in kelvin.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Substitute $k$ and $T$ into $\dfrac{3}{2}kT$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ \tfrac{1}{2}m(c_{\text{rms}})^2 = \tfrac{3}{2}kT = 1.5 \times 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \times 300 = 6.21 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J} $$

The average molecular kinetic energy is $6.21 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J}$.

</details>

**6.** Find the rms speed of nitrogen molecules at 300 K. The molar mass of
nitrogen is $0.028\,\text{kg}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$ and
$R = 8.31\,\text{J}\,\text{K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $c_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{3RT}{M}}$. Keep the molar mass in kilograms
per mole.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the top line: $3 \times 8.31 \times 300 = 7479$. Then divide by 0.028
and take the square root.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
c_{\text{rms}} &= \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} \\
  &= \sqrt{\frac{3 \times 8.31 \times 300}{0.028}} \\
  &= 517\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

The rms speed is 517 m s⁻¹.

</details>

**7.** Explain, in terms of the molecules, how a gas exerts a pressure on the
walls of its container.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what happens to a molecule's momentum when it hits a wall and
bounces back, and about how many molecules hit the wall each second.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the molecules move at random and hit the wall. Then link the change in
momentum to the force on the wall.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The molecules move quickly and at random. They collide with the walls of the
container. When a molecule hits a wall it rebounds, so its momentum changes. The
change in momentum means the wall feels a force from that molecule. There are
very many collisions every second, so the wall feels a steady force. Pressure is
force divided by area, so the gas exerts a steady pressure on the walls.

</details>
