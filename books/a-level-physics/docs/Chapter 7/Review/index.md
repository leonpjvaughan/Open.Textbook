# Chapter 7: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

A pressure cooker holds 1.50 kg of water at 20 °C. The water boils at 121 °C. The
specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹ and the specific latent heat
of vaporisation is $2.26 \times 10^{6}\,\text{J}\,\text{kg}^{-1}$. The steam
above the water is at $2.00 \times 10^{5}\,\text{Pa}$ and 121 °C, in a space of
volume $2.00 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^{3}$. The cooker boils for 12 minutes, and
0.050 kg of water turns into steam. The constants are
$R = 8.31\,\text{J}\,\text{K}^{-1}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$,
$k = 1.38 \times 10^{-23}\,\text{J}\,\text{K}^{-1}$ and
$N_A = 6.02 \times 10^{23}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$.

**1. The temperature change.** The water starts at 20 °C and boils at 121 °C.

$$ \Delta\theta = 121 - 20 = 101\,\text{K} $$

**2. The energy to heat the water.**

$$ E = mc\Delta\theta = 1.50 \times 4200 \times 101 = 6.36 \times 10^{5}\,\text{J} $$

**3. The energy to turn some water into steam.**

$$ E = mL = 0.050 \times 2.26 \times 10^{6} = 1.13 \times 10^{5}\,\text{J} $$

**4. The total energy.**

$$ E_{\text{total}} = 6.36 \times 10^{5} + 1.13 \times 10^{5} = 7.49 \times 10^{5}\,\text{J} $$

**5. The average rate of energy transfer while boiling.** The boiling lasts
$12 \times 60 = 720\,\text{s}$.

$$ P = \frac{E}{t} = \frac{1.13 \times 10^{5}}{720} = 157\,\text{W} $$

**6. The number of moles of steam.** The temperature is $121 + 273 = 394\,\text{K}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n &= \frac{pV}{RT} \\
  &= \frac{2.00 \times 10^{5} \times 2.00 \times 10^{-3}}{8.31 \times 394} \\
  &= 0.122\,\text{mol}
\end{aligned}
$$

**7. The number of molecules.**

$$ N = nN_A = 0.122 \times 6.02 \times 10^{23} = 7.36 \times 10^{22} $$

**8. The average kinetic energy of a steam molecule.**

$$ \tfrac{3}{2}kT = 1.5 \times 1.38 \times 10^{-23} \times 394 = 8.16 \times 10^{-21}\,\text{J} $$

**9. The rms speed of a steam molecule.** The molar mass of water is
$0.018\,\text{kg}\,\text{mol}^{-1}$.

$$
c_{\text{rms}} = \sqrt{\frac{3RT}{M}} = \sqrt{\frac{3 \times 8.31 \times 394}{0.018}} = 739\,\text{m}\,\text{s}^{-1}
$$

**10. The judgement.** The cooker reaches 121 °C because the lid is clamped shut
and the valve holds the pressure at $2.00 \times 10^{5}\,\text{Pa}$. Water boils
when its vapour pressure matches the pressure above it, so a higher pressure
raises the boiling temperature above 100 °C. The hotter water cooks the food
faster. The steam cannot escape, so it pushes outwards on the lid, and the clamp
must be strong enough to hold it.

**11. The first prediction.** As the water is heated with the lid clamped shut,
the pressure rises. The volume of the space is fixed, and the temperature rises,
so $pV = NkT$ means the pressure must rise. The water can get hotter than 100 °C
before it boils, because the higher pressure raises the boiling point.

- use $E = mc\Delta\theta$ for a temperature change;
- find a specific heat capacity from an electrical heating experiment;
- explain what happens to the particles during a change of state;
- use $E = mL$ for melting, boiling, freezing and condensing;
- read a heating curve and explain its flat parts;
- describe the ideal gas model;
- use Boyle's law, Charles's law and the pressure law;
- convert a temperature between degrees Celsius and kelvin;
- explain absolute zero;
- use $pV = nRT$ and $pV = NkT$;
- explain pressure in terms of molecular collisions;
- find the average molecular kinetic energy and the rms speed of a gas;
- test Boyle's law and read the graph;
- explain why a pressure cooker cooks food faster.
