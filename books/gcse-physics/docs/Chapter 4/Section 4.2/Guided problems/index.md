# 4.2 Sound waves and waves for detection and exploration — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wave has a frequency of 2.0 Hz and a wavelength of 1.5 m. Calculate the
   wave speed.
2. A sound wave has a frequency of 500 Hz. Calculate its period.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $v = f\lambda = 2.0 \times 1.5 = 3.0\,\text{m/s}$.
2. $T = \dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{500} = 0.0020\,\text{s}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at the wave equation in Section 4.1. If
question 2 went wrong, remember that the period is the reciprocal of the
frequency.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A pulse of ultrasound travels through tissue at 1500 m/s. The reflection
returns after 0.00020 s. Calculate the distance to the boundary.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The pulse travels to the boundary and back, so the time covers twice the
distance you want.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $s = \dfrac{v t}{2}$, then work out $\dfrac{1500 \times 0.00020}{2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= \frac{v t}{2} \\
  &= \frac{1500 \times 0.00020}{2} \\
  &= \frac{0.30}{2} \\
  &= 0.15\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A ship sends a pulse of sound down through water at 1500 m/s. The
reflection returns after 0.040 s. Calculate the depth of the water.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This is the same shape of problem as question 1. The echo time is for the trip
down and back.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $s = \dfrac{v t}{2}$, and put in $v = 1500$ and $t = 0.040$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= \frac{v t}{2} \\
  &= \frac{1500 \times 0.040}{2} \\
  &= \frac{60}{2} \\
  &= 30\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** An earthquake P-wave travels 45 km in 15 s. Calculate the speed of the
P-wave.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed is distance divided by time, but the distance is given in kilometres.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

First write $45\,\text{km} = 45000\,\text{m}$. Then work out
$\dfrac{45000}{15}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
45\,\text{km} &= 45000\,\text{m} \\
v &= \frac{s}{t} \\
  &= \frac{45000}{15} \\
  &= 3000\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** An ultrasound scanner works at a frequency of 2.5 MHz. The speed of sound
in tissue is 1500 m/s. Calculate the wavelength of the ultrasound.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wavelength comes from the wave equation, but the frequency must be in hertz
first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $2.5\,\text{MHz} = 2.5 \times 10^6\,\text{Hz}$, then use
$\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
2.5\,\text{MHz} &= 2.5 \times 10^6\,\text{Hz} \\
\lambda &= \frac{v}{f} \\
  &= \frac{1500}{2.5 \times 10^6} \\
  &= 6.0 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is 0.60 mm.
</details>

**5.** Explain why a sound wave cannot travel through a vacuum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what a longitudinal wave is made of, and what a vacuum contains.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that a sound wave is a series of compressions and rarefactions, then say
whether a vacuum has particles that can vibrate.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A sound wave is a longitudinal wave, so it travels as compressions and
rarefactions of the particles in the medium. The particles vibrate back and
forth, and pass the wave along. A vacuum contains no particles, so there is
nothing to vibrate and the wave cannot travel. This answer is a model answer for
you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** Explain why the ear can detect sound only over a limited range of
frequencies.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Follow the sound from the air to the ear drum, and think about what the ear
drum has to do with the wave.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that sound waves make the ear drum vibrate, then say why that vibration
cannot happen at every frequency.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Sound waves arriving at the ear make the ear drum and other parts of the ear
vibrate. This converts the sound wave into a vibration of a solid, which causes
the sensation of sound. The parts of the ear can only vibrate well over a limited
range of frequencies, so the conversion works only over that range. This sets the
limits of human hearing, which is about 20 Hz to 20 kHz. This answer is a model
answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**7.** Explain how an ultrasound pulse can be used to find the distance to a
boundary inside the body.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what happens when the wave reaches the boundary, and what the
detector measures.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that part of the wave is reflected back, that the detector times the return,
and that the time must be halved.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

When the ultrasound wave meets the boundary between two different tissues, part
of the wave is reflected back towards the detector. The detector measures the
time taken for the reflection to return. The pulse travelled to the boundary and
back, so the distance is half of the speed multiplied by the time. This answer
is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>
