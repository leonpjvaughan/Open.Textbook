# 4.3 Waves at material interfaces — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wave has a frequency of 5.0 Hz and a wavelength of 0.40 m. Calculate the
   wave speed.
2. Convert 40 cm to metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $v = f\lambda = 5.0 \times 0.40 = 2.0\,\text{m/s}$.
2. $40\,\text{cm} = 0.40\,\text{m}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at the wave equation in Section 4.1. If
question 2 went wrong, remember that the prefix centi means one hundredth.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** State the law of reflection for a wave at a surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The law links the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection, and it says
which line both angles are measured from.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the two angles are equal, then say that both are measured from the
normal.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence. Both angles are measured
between the ray and the normal, which is the line at right angles to the surface
at the point where the ray hits it. This answer is a model answer for you to
compare with your own wording.
</details>

**2.** A water wave travels at 2.0 m/s and reflects from a wall 18 m away.
Calculate the time taken for the wave to travel to the wall and back.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wave has to travel to the wall and then back again, so the total distance is
twice the distance to the wall.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the total distance, $2 \times 18 = 36\,\text{m}$. Then use
$t = \dfrac{s}{v}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{total distance} &= 2 \times 18 = 36\,\text{m} \\
t &= \frac{s}{v} \\
  &= \frac{36}{2.0} \\
  &= 18\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A wave of frequency 250 Hz passes from one material into another. Its
speed changes from 5.0 m/s to 8.0 m/s. The frequency does not change. Calculate
the wavelength in each material.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The frequency is the same on both sides of the boundary, so use the wave
equation twice.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the first material, work out $\dfrac{5.0}{250}$. For the second, work out
$\dfrac{8.0}{250}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{first material:}\quad \lambda &= \frac{v}{f} = \frac{5.0}{250} = 0.020\,\text{m} \\
\text{second material:}\quad \lambda &= \frac{v}{f} = \frac{8.0}{250} = 0.032\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The higher speed gives the longer wavelength.
</details>

**4.** When a wave meets a boundary, 20% of its energy is reflected and 30% is
absorbed. Calculate the percentage of the energy that is transmitted.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three percentages must add up to 100%.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the reflected and absorbed percentages, then subtract the total from 100%.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ 100\% - 20\% - 30\% = 50\% $$

Half of the energy is transmitted into the second material.
</details>

**5.** Describe what can happen to a wave when it meets the boundary between two
different materials.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A wave can bounce back, pass through or be taken in by the material. Give the
name for each one.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name reflection, transmission and absorption, and say in one sentence what
happens in each case.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Part of the wave can be reflected, so it bounces back from the boundary. Part of
the wave can be transmitted, so it passes through into the second material.
Part of the wave can be absorbed, so its energy is transferred to the material.
Usually all three happen at once. This answer is a model answer for you to
compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** Explain why the angles in a reflection diagram are measured from the
normal and not from the surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the angle the surface makes with the ray. Does that angle change if
the surface is tilted?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the normal is at right angles to the surface, so it gives a fixed
reference line, then say why the surface does not.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The normal is at right angles to the surface, so it gives a fixed reference line
for measuring the angles. The surface itself lies flat along the boundary, so the
angle between a ray and the surface depends on the ray and cannot be compared
from one diagram to the next. Measuring from the normal gives the angle of
incidence and the angle of reflection in the same way at every point. This answer
is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**7.** A person claps and hears the echo from a wall after 0.50 s. The speed of
sound in air is 340 m/s. Calculate the distance from the person to the wall.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sound travels to the wall and back, so the distance to the wall is half of
the distance the sound covers.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $d = \dfrac{v t}{2}$, then work out $\dfrac{340 \times 0.50}{2}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
d &= \frac{v t}{2} \\
  &= \frac{340 \times 0.50}{2} \\
  &= \frac{170}{2} \\
  &= 85\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>
