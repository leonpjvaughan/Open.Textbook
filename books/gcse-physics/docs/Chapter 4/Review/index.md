# Chapter 4: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

A survey boat checks whether a harbour channel is deep enough for a ferry. The
speed of sound in sea water is 1500 m/s. A pulse returns from the sea floor after
0.020 s. The echo sounder works at 50 kHz. The ripple tank makes waves at
5.0 Hz with a wavelength of 0.12 m.

**1. The depth of the sea floor.** The pulse travels to the sea floor and back,
so the depth is half of the distance the pulse covers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= \frac{v t}{2} \\
  &= \frac{1500 \times 0.020}{2} \\
  &= \frac{30}{2} \\
  &= 15\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The water is 15 m deep. The ferry needs at least 12 m, so the channel is deep
enough.

**2. The wavelength of the echo sounder.** The frequency is 50 kHz, which is
50000 Hz.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= f \lambda \\
\lambda &= \frac{v}{f} \\
  &= \frac{1500}{50000} \\
  &= 0.030\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The wavelength is 0.030 m, which is 3.0 cm.

**3. The speed of the ripples.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= f \lambda \\
  &= 5.0 \times 0.12 \\
  &= 0.60\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

**4. The period of the ripples.**

$$ T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{5.0} = 0.20\,\text{s} $$

**5. The reflection at the sea floor.** When the sound pulse reaches the sea
floor, the boundary between water and sediment, part of the pulse is reflected
back to the boat. The rest is transmitted into the sediment or absorbed by it.
The reflected part is the echo the boat times, so the echo gives the depth.

<!-- tier:h -->
**6. A medical scanner.** The scanner uses ultrasound at 2.0 MHz, which is
$2.0 \times 10^6\,\text{Hz}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{v}{f} \\
  &= \frac{1500}{2.0 \times 10^6} \\
  &= 7.5 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is 0.75 mm. Shorter wavelengths can show smaller details.

**7. A seismic wave.** A P-wave covers 60 km in 12 s.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{s}{t} \\
  &= \frac{60000}{12} \\
  &= 5000\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

P-waves and S-waves give evidence about the size and structure of the Earth's
core, which cannot be seen directly.
<!-- /tier:h -->

The judgement that the channel is deep enough is a reasoned answer. It follows
from the numbers above. Whether a plan is safe is not the kind of claim a
computer can check, so compare your reasoning with the evidence in the working.

## What you can now do

- describe a wave in terms of amplitude, wavelength, frequency and period, and
  give the unit of each;
- use the wave equation $v = f\lambda$ and the period equation $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$;
- tell a transverse wave from a longitudinal wave, with an example of each;
- recall the speed of sound in air, and explain why sound needs a medium;
- describe a method to measure the speed of sound in air and the speed of
  ripples on water;
- describe reflection, transmission and absorption at a material interface;
- state the law of reflection and draw a ray diagram to show it;
- explain what happens to the speed, frequency and wavelength when a wave is
  transmitted into a new material;
- calculate a distance from an echo, and explain how ultrasound and seismic
  waves are used for detection and exploration (Higher tier).
