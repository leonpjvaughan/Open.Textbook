# 4.1 Waves in air, fluids and solids — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A runner covers 400 m in 50 s. Calculate her average speed.
2. A train travels 900 m at a steady speed of 25 m/s. Calculate the time taken.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $v = \dfrac{s}{t} = \dfrac{400}{50} = 8.0\,\text{m/s}$.
2. $t = \dfrac{s}{v} = \dfrac{900}{25} = 36\,\text{s}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at Section 3.1: average speed is the total
distance divided by the total time. If question 2 went wrong, rearrange
$v = \dfrac{s}{t}$ to make $t$ the subject first.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A water wave has a frequency of 2.0 Hz and a wavelength of 0.30 m.
Calculate the wave speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wave speed is the frequency multiplied by the wavelength.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $v = f\lambda$, then put in $f = 2.0$ and $\lambda = 0.30$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= f \lambda \\
  &= 2.0 \times 0.30 \\
  &= 0.60\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A sound wave in air has a frequency of 155 Hz. The speed of sound in air
is 310 m/s. Calculate the wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You are looking for the wavelength, so the wave equation needs rearranging.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Start from $v = f\lambda$ and divide both sides by $f$, so
$\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$. Then divide 310 by 155.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= f \lambda \\
\lambda &= \frac{v}{f} \\
  &= \frac{310}{155} \\
  &= 2.0\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A wave travels along a rope at 15 m/s. The wavelength is 3.0 m. Calculate
the frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Frequency is the number of waves each second. Use the wave equation and make
$f$ the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rearrange $v = f\lambda$ to $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda}$, then divide 15 by 3.0.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= f \lambda \\
f &= \frac{v}{\lambda} \\
  &= \frac{15}{3.0} \\
  &= 5.0\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A wave has a frequency of 2.0 Hz. Calculate its period.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The period is the time for one wave, so it is the reciprocal of the frequency.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$, then work out $\dfrac{1}{2.0}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
T &= \frac{1}{f} \\
  &= \frac{1}{2.0} \\
  &= 0.50\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**5.** A wave travels at 1200 m/s with a frequency of 400 Hz. Calculate the
wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wavelength is the distance the wave travels in the time for one wave.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$, then divide 1200 by 400.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{v}{f} \\
  &= \frac{1200}{400} \\
  &= 3.0\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**6.** Twenty waves pass a point in 5.0 s. Calculate the frequency of the waves.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Frequency is the number of waves in one second, so divide the number of waves by
the time in seconds.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\dfrac{20}{5.0}$. The unit is waves per second, or hertz.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{\text{number of waves}}{\text{time}} \\
  &= \frac{20}{5.0} \\
  &= 4.0\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

The period would be $T = \dfrac{1}{4.0} = 0.25\,\text{s}$.
</details>

**7.** A cork floats on a pond. A water wave passes the cork, and the cork moves
up and down but stays in the same place. Explain what this shows about what the
wave transfers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare what the cork does with what the wave does. The wave moves across the
pond, but does the water?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the water moves up and down and returns to its place, then say what
does travel across the pond.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The cork bobs up and down as each ripple passes, but it always returns to the
same place. The water itself does not travel across the pond. The ripple is the
wave, and it carries energy from one place to another. So the wave transfers
energy without transferring the medium. This answer is a model answer for you to
compare with your own wording.
</details>
