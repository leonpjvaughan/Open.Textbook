# 9.4 Nuclear fission and fusion — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Section 9.1.

1. How many protons and how many neutrons are in a nucleus of
   $^{235}_{92}\text{U}$?
2. What is the electric charge of a neutron?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. 92 protons and $235 - 92 = 143$ neutrons.
2. A neutron has no electric charge.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at the atomic number and mass number in
Section 9.1. If question 2 went wrong, look at the table of particles in Section
9.1.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Define nuclear fission.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The name tells you that something is being split.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what kind of nucleus splits, and what it splits into.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Nuclear fission is the splitting of a large and unstable nucleus, such as a
nucleus of uranium or plutonium, into two smaller nuclei that are roughly equal
in size. Energy is released.
</details>

**2.** Describe what happens when a uranium-235 nucleus absorbs a neutron.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

After the neutron is absorbed, the nucleus becomes unstable and splits.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what the two products are, then say what else is released.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The nucleus absorbs the neutron and becomes unstable. It splits into two smaller
nuclei that are roughly equal in size. Two or three neutrons and gamma rays are
released. Energy is released, and the products carry it away as kinetic energy.
</details>

**3.** Explain how a chain reaction can happen in a sample of uranium-235.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One fission releases more than one neutron. What can each neutron do?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Trace one neutron from a fission to the next fission, then say what happens to
the number of fissions at each step.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Each fission releases two or three neutrons. Each of those neutrons can be
absorbed by another uranium-235 nucleus, which then fissions and releases more
neutrons. The number of fissions grows with each generation, so the reaction
spreads through the sample. This is a chain reaction.
</details>

**4.** Explain why the chain reaction in a nuclear reactor is controlled, and
what would happen if it were not.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about how fast the energy would be released if every neutron caused
another fission.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say how many neutrons are allowed to cause the next fission in a reactor. Then
describe the result if all of them did.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

In a reactor the chain reaction is controlled so that, on average, only one
neutron from each fission causes the next fission. The energy is then released at
a steady rate that can make electricity. If every neutron caused another fission,
the number of fissions would grow very quickly and release a huge amount of
energy in a very short time, as in a nuclear weapon.
</details>

**5.** Define nuclear fusion, and state what happens to some of the mass of the
nuclei.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The name tells you that two nuclei are joining.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what kind of nuclei join and what they form. Then say what the missing mass
becomes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Nuclear fusion is the joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus. Some
of the mass of the nuclei is converted into the energy of radiation.
</details>

**6.** Compare nuclear fission and nuclear fusion, and say where each one
happens.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One process splits a nucleus and the other joins two nuclei.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write one line for fission and one for fusion, then name a place for each.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Fission splits a large nucleus into smaller nuclei, and it happens in a nuclear
reactor in a power station. Fusion joins two light nuclei into a heavier nucleus,
and it happens in the core of a star such as the Sun. Both release a large amount
of energy.
</details>

**7.** Explain why fusion is difficult to use as a source of power on the Earth.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The nuclei repel each other, so they need a very high temperature to join.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what is difficult to reach and what is difficult to hold.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Nuclei are positively charged, so they repel each other. They must move very fast
to get close enough to join, which needs a very high temperature. It is difficult
to reach such a high temperature and to hold it under control, so fusion is not
yet used to generate electricity.
</details>
