# 8.3 Particle model and pressure — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Section 8.2 and Section 2.4.

1. Calculate the energy needed to warm $0.50\,\text{kg}$ of water by
   $40\,^\circ\text{C}$. The specific heat capacity of water is
   $4200\,\text{J/kg}\,^\circ\text{C}$.
2. A force of $60\,\text{N}$ acts on an area of $0.020\,\text{m}^2$. Calculate
   the pressure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\Delta E = mc\Delta\theta = 0.50 \times 4200 \times 40 = 84\,000\,\text{J}$.
2. $p = \dfrac{F}{A} = \dfrac{60}{0.020} = 3000\,\text{Pa}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at Section 8.2: the temperature changes, so
use specific heat capacity. If question 2 went wrong, remember that pressure is
the force on each unit of area.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A fixed mass of gas at $100\,\text{kPa}$ fills $1.2\,\text{m}^3$. The gas
is compressed at constant temperature until its volume is $0.40\,\text{m}^3$.
Calculate the new pressure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The mass of gas and the temperature do not change, so you can use
$p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Make $p_2$ the subject: $p_2 = \dfrac{p_1V_1}{V_2}$. Put in the values and
divide.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_1V_1 &= p_2V_2 \\
p_2 &= \frac{p_1V_1}{V_2} \\
    &= \frac{100 \times 1.2}{0.40} \\
    &= 300\,\text{kPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**2.** A fixed mass of gas at $100\,\text{kPa}$ fills $0.30\,\text{m}^3$. It
expands at constant temperature until its pressure is $120\,\text{kPa}$. Calculate
the new volume.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This time the unknown is the volume. Rearrange $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$ for $V_2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $V_2 = \dfrac{p_1V_1}{p_2}$, then divide $100 \times 0.30$ by $120$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_1V_1 &= p_2V_2 \\
V_2 &= \frac{p_1V_1}{p_2} \\
    &= \frac{100 \times 0.30}{120} \\
    &= 0.25\,\text{m}^3
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**3.** A gas exerts a pressure of $250\,\text{kPa}$ on a flat plate of area
$0.040\,\text{m}^2$. Calculate the force on the plate.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $p = \dfrac{F}{A}$ and make the force the subject. Change the pressure into
pascals first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $F = pA$ with $p = 250\,000\,\text{Pa}$ and $A = 0.040\,\text{m}^2$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= pA \\
  &= 250\,000 \times 0.040 \\
  &= 10\,000\,\text{N} \\
  &= 1.0 \times 10^4\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**4.** Explain, using the particle model, why the pressure of a gas rises when
the gas is compressed at a constant temperature.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The particles now have less space. Think about how often each particle hits the
walls.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the particles are closer together, then link that to the number of
collisions with the walls each second.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

When the gas is compressed, the same number of particles is squeezed into a
smaller volume. The particles are closer to the walls and to each other, so each
particle hits the walls more often. The average kinetic energy of the particles
does not change, because the temperature is constant. The walls receive more
collisions each second, so the pressure rises.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**5.** A sealed metal container holding a gas is warmed. The volume of the
container does not change. Explain what happens to the speed of the particles and
to the pressure of the gas.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the average kinetic energy of the particles when the gas gets hotter.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the particle speed, then say what that does to the number and
the strength of the collisions with the walls.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

When the gas is warmed, the particles gain kinetic energy and move faster. They
hit the walls of the container more often, and each collision is harder. The
pressure of the gas therefore rises.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** Say which relationship you will use, then calculate the pressure when
$0.80\,\text{m}^3$ of gas at $100\,\text{kPa}$ is compressed to $0.20\,\text{m}^3$
at a constant temperature.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The gas is the same fixed mass, and the temperature is constant. That tells you
which relationship applies.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$ with $p_1 = 100\,\text{kPa}$,
$V_1 = 0.80\,\text{m}^3$ and $V_2 = 0.20\,\text{m}^3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p_1V_1 &= p_2V_2 \\
p_2 &= \frac{p_1V_1}{V_2} \\
    &= \frac{100 \times 0.80}{0.20} \\
    &= 400\,\text{kPa}
\end{aligned}
$$

The volume is divided by four, so the pressure is multiplied by four.
</details>

**7.** A student pushes the handle of a bicycle pump quickly. Explain why the air
inside the pump becomes warmer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Pushing the handle moves a force through a distance. That is work.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that work is done on the gas, then say what happens to the internal energy of
the gas and to the particles.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Pushing the handle does work on the trapped air, because the force moves through
a distance. The work done is transferred to the internal energy of the air. The
particles gain kinetic energy, so the air becomes warmer.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>
