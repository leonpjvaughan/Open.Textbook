# Chapter 8: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The weather station stores its water as a block of ice. The block has a volume of
$2.5 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3$ and the density of ice is
$920\,\text{kg/m}^3$.

**1. The mass of the ice.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \rho V \\
  &= 920 \times 2.5 \times 10^{-3} \\
  &= 2.3\,\text{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

**2. The energy needed to melt the ice.** The specific latent heat of fusion of
ice is $3.3 \times 10^5\,\text{J/kg}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= mL \\
  &= 2.3 \times 3.3 \times 10^5 \\
  &= 759\,000\,\text{J} \\
  &= 7.6 \times 10^5\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

**3. The energy needed to warm the meltwater.** The water is warmed from
$0\,^\circ\text{C}$ to $45\,^\circ\text{C}$, so $\Delta\theta = 45\,^\circ\text{C}$.
The specific heat capacity of water is $4200\,\text{J/kg}\,^\circ\text{C}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E &= mc\Delta\theta \\
         &= 2.3 \times 4200 \times 45 \\
         &= 434\,700\,\text{J} \\
         &= 4.3 \times 10^5\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

**4. The total energy.** Add the two energies.

$$ 759\,000 + 434\,700 = 1\,193\,700\,\text{J} = 1.2 \times 10^6\,\text{J} $$

Melting the ice needs more energy than warming the melted water. This is the
prediction from the chapter opener: breaking the forces between the particles
takes more energy than raising their average kinetic energy through
$45\,^\circ\text{C}$.

**5. The weather balloon.** The balloon holds $2.0\,\text{m}^3$ of gas at
$100\,\text{kPa}$ in the valley. High above, the pressure is $25\,\text{kPa}$ and
the temperature of the gas is the same. The mass of gas does not change, so
$p_1V_1 = p_2V_2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_2 &= \frac{p_1V_1}{p_2} \\
    &= \frac{100 \times 2.0}{25} \\
    &= 8.0\,\text{m}^3
\end{aligned}
$$

The balloon expands to $8.0\,\text{m}^3$. The pressure outside has fallen to a
quarter of its starting value, so the volume is four times larger. The particles
inside hit the balloon wall less often, so the gas pushes the balloon outwards
until the pressure inside matches the pressure outside.

**6. The bicycle pump.** The station also uses a bicycle pump. Pushing the handle
does work on the trapped air. The work done increases the internal energy of the
air, so the particles move faster and the air becomes warmer.

## What you can now do

- use the particle model to describe solids, liquids and gases;
- calculate and measure density, and explain why the states have different
  densities;
- describe the changes of state and explain why mass is conserved;
- describe internal energy and explain what heating does to the particles;
- use $\Delta E = mc\Delta\theta$ and $E = mL$;
- tell specific heat capacity and specific latent heat apart, and read a heating
  or cooling graph;
- explain gas pressure using collisions with the walls;
- use $pV = \text{constant}$ for a fixed mass of gas at constant temperature;
- explain how doing work on a gas raises its temperature (Higher tier).
