# 8.1 Changes of state and the particle model — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Chapter 1 and Section 2.4.

1. Change $250\,\text{cm}^3$ into cubic metres.
2. A worker lifts a box of mass $20\,\text{kg}$ through a height of $1.5\,\text{m}$.
   Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$. Calculate the energy gained by the gravitational
   potential store.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $1\,\text{cm}^3 = 10^{-6}\,\text{m}^3$, so
   $250\,\text{cm}^3 = 2.5 \times 10^{-4}\,\text{m}^3$.
2. $E = mgh = 20 \times 9.8 \times 1.5 = 294\,\text{J}$.

If question 1 went wrong, remember that a cubic metre holds
$10^6$ cubic centimetres. If question 2 went wrong, look again at Chapter 1:
multiply the mass, the field strength and the height.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A block of metal has a mass of $0.60\,\text{kg}$ and a volume of
$0.75 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3$. Calculate its density.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You are given a mass and a volume, and you want the density. The equation
$\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$ links all three.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the mass $0.60\,\text{kg}$ by the volume
$0.75 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\rho &= \frac{m}{V} \\
     &= \frac{0.60}{0.75 \times 10^{-3}} \\
     &= 800\,\text{kg/m}^3
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A block of concrete has a density of $2400\,\text{kg/m}^3$ and a volume
of $0.25\,\text{m}^3$. Calculate its mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This time you want the mass. Rearrange $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$ first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $m = \rho V$, then multiply $2400\,\text{kg/m}^3$ by
$0.25\,\text{m}^3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \rho V \\
  &= 2400 \times 0.25 \\
  &= 600\,\text{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A bottle holds $1.2\,\text{kg}$ of water. The density of water is
$1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$. Calculate the volume of the water.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You know the mass and the density, and you want the volume. Make $V$ the subject
of $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $V = \dfrac{m}{\rho}$, then divide $1.2\,\text{kg}$ by
$1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= \frac{m}{\rho} \\
  &= \frac{1.2}{1000} \\
  &= 1.2 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{m}^3
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A stone has a mass of $64.8\,\text{g}$. It is lowered into a measuring
cylinder. The water level rises from $50\,\text{cm}^3$ to $74\,\text{cm}^3$.
Calculate the density of the stone in $\text{g/cm}^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rise in the water level is the volume of the stone. Read the two levels and
subtract them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the volume: $74 - 50 = 24\,\text{cm}^3$. Then divide the mass by this
volume.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 74 - 50 = 24\,\text{cm}^3 \\
\rho &= \frac{m}{V} \\
     &= \frac{64.8}{24} \\
     &= 2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3
\end{aligned}
$$

The stone has a density of $2.7\,\text{g/cm}^3$, which is $2700\,\text{kg/m}^3$.
</details>

**5.** Explain, using the particle model, why a gas has a much lower density than
the same mass of a solid.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare how far apart the particles are in the two states.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say where the particles are in each state, then link that to the volume the same
mass fills.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

In a gas the particles are far apart, with large empty spaces between them. In a
solid the particles are packed closely together. The same mass of gas therefore
fills a much larger volume than the solid, so the gas has a much lower density.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** A student says that when ice melts, some of the ice is lost, so the mass
of the water is less than the mass of the ice. Explain why the student is wrong.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the particles. Are any of them lost during melting?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

State what happens to the particles and what happens to the mass, then give the
reason.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Melting is a change of state, not a chemical change. The same particles are
present before and after melting. They move further apart and change their
arrangement, but none are created or destroyed. The mass is therefore conserved,
so the mass of the water equals the mass of the ice.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**7.** Say which measurements you would take, and which equation you would use,
to find the density of a small irregular stone. Then calculate the density if the
stone has a mass of $39\,\text{g}$ and raises the water level in a measuring
cylinder from $20\,\text{cm}^3$ to $35\,\text{cm}^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

An irregular stone has no simple length, width and height. Think about how to
find its volume with water.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Measure the mass on a balance. Find the volume by displacement. Then use
$\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Measure the mass of the stone on a balance. Find the volume by lowering the stone
into water in a measuring cylinder and reading the rise in the water level. Then
use $\rho = \dfrac{m}{V}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= 35 - 20 = 15\,\text{cm}^3 \\
\rho &= \frac{39}{15} \\
     &= 2.6\,\text{g/cm}^3
\end{aligned}
$$

The stone has a density of $2.6\,\text{g/cm}^3$.
</details>
