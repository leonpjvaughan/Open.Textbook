# Chapter 5: Light and electromagnetic waves

## The chapter problem: the moorland observatory

An observatory stands on a moor far from any town. It studies the sky with radio
waves, infrared radiation and visible light. Each kind of radiation carries
different information about the universe.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- The radio telescope receives a signal with a **frequency of 150 MHz**.
- An infrared camera detects radiation with a **wavelength of 10 μm**.
- A visible-light image uses light with a **wavelength of 600 nm**.
- The observatory walls are painted **white**.
- The visitor centre uses a lens to project a picture of the Moon. The picture is
  **2.0 cm** tall, and its image on the wall is **0.30 m** tall.
- Electromagnetic waves travel at **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s** in a vacuum.

By the end of the chapter you will work out the wavelength of the radio signal,
the frequencies of the infrared and visible radiation, and the magnification of
the projected picture. You will place all three kinds of radiation in the
electromagnetic spectrum. You will also explain why the white walls keep the
building cooler than black walls would.

> **First contact: no working yet.** Estimate. The radio signal has a frequency
> of 150 million hertz. Is its wavelength closer to 20 cm, 2 m or 20 m? Write
> your estimate down.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>If you are not sure where to start</summary>

Every electromagnetic wave travels at the same speed in a vacuum: 300 million
metres each second. A wave with a frequency of 150 million hertz passes
150 million crests each second. Divide the distance travelled each second by the
number of crests to find the distance between them.
</details>

## By the end of this chapter

You will be able to:

- describe the electromagnetic spectrum, and list its groups in order of
  wavelength and frequency;
- state the properties that all electromagnetic waves share;
- use the wave equation $v = f\lambda$ with electromagnetic waves;
- describe how electromagnetic radiation is absorbed, reflected, transmitted and
  refracted;
- describe the uses and the dangers of each group of electromagnetic waves;
- describe the action of convex and concave lenses, and draw ray diagrams;
- calculate the magnification of a lens;
- explain the colour of an object in terms of absorption and reflection;
- explain how the temperature of an object affects the radiation it emits.

## Where it is used

Light and the rest of the electromagnetic spectrum carry almost everything we
know about the world beyond the Earth. Radio telescopes map distant galaxies.
Infrared cameras see warm objects in the dark. X-rays show a broken bone inside
the body. Visible light gives us colour, and the colour of a star tells us how
hot it is.
