# 5.2 Interactions of electromagnetic radiation with matter — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 5.1, especially the wave equation and the order of the
electromagnetic spectrum; the idea that energy can be transferred to a thermal
store.

**By the end of this section you can:** describe how electromagnetic radiation is
absorbed, reflected, transmitted and refracted; and describe a use and a danger
of each group of electromagnetic waves.

## 1. What can happen to a wave

When electromagnetic radiation reaches a material, one or more of four things
happens.

- **Absorption.** The radiation is taken in by the material. Its energy is
  transferred to the thermal store of the material, so the material warms up.
- **Reflection.** The radiation bounces off the surface.
- **Transmission.** The radiation passes through the material.
- **Refraction.** The radiation changes direction as it passes from one material
  into another.

Which of these happens depends on the material and on the wavelength. A material
that is transparent to visible light may absorb infrared radiation. Glass is one
example.

## 2. Absorption and warming

When radiation is absorbed, energy is transferred to the material. The
temperature rise depends on the energy absorbed and on the material itself.

A dark, matt surface absorbs infrared radiation well. A shiny surface reflects
most of it. This is why a black car warms up faster than a white car in the
Sun. It is also why a shiny emergency blanket helps to keep a casualty warm: the
shiny surface reflects infrared radiation back to the body.

**Worked example 1.** A microwave oven uses radiation with a frequency of
2.5 GHz. Calculate its wavelength.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{c}{f} \\
        &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{2.5 \times 10^9} \\
        &= 0.12\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Worked example 2.** A television remote control sends infrared radiation with a
wavelength of 1.0 μm. Calculate its frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{c}{\lambda} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.0 \times 10^{-6}} \\
  &= 3.0 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

## 3. Reflection and refraction

**Reflection** happens at a surface. The ray that arrives is the **incident
ray**, and the ray that bounces off is the **reflected ray**. A line drawn at
right angles to the surface is the **normal**.

The **angle of incidence** is the angle between the incident ray and the normal.
The **angle of reflection** is the angle between the reflected ray and the
normal. The angle of reflection always equals the angle of incidence.

**Refraction** happens when a wave passes from one material into another and its
speed changes.

- Going from air into glass or water, the wave **slows down** and bends **towards
  the normal**.
- Going from glass or water into air, the wave **speeds up** and bends **away
  from the normal**.

Light travels more slowly in glass and in water than in air. The change of speed
at the boundary is what makes the ray change direction.

**Worked example 3.** A ray of light hits a mirror at an angle of incidence of
40°. State the angle of reflection.

The angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence, so the angle of
reflection is 40°.

## 5. Uses and dangers

Each group of the spectrum has uses that follow from how it interacts with
matter. Some groups are also dangerous if a person receives too much.

| Group | A use | A danger |
|---|---|---|
| Radio waves | Broadcasting and communication. | None at normal power. |
| Microwaves | Cooking, and satellite and mobile links. | Heating of body tissue at high power. |
| Infrared | Thermal imaging, remote controls and heaters. | Burns to the skin at high power. |
| Visible light | Seeing, photography and fibre-optic communication. | Bright light can damage the eye. |
| Ultraviolet | Sunbeds, and vitamin D production in the skin. | Skin damage, and damage to the eyes. |
| X-rays | Imaging bones and luggage. | Ionising, so it can damage cells. |
| Gamma rays | Sterilising equipment, and treating cancer. | Ionising, so it can damage cells. |

Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are **ionising**. Ionising radiation has
enough energy to remove electrons from atoms. This is why it can damage living
cells.
