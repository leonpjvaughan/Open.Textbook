# 5.3 Lenses and ray diagrams — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Sections 5.1 and 5.2.

1. Work out $\dfrac{40}{8.0}$.
2. Convert 0.25 m to centimetres.
3. Which group of the spectrum has the higher frequency, red light or violet
   light?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $5.0$.
2. $25\,\text{cm}$.
3. Violet light.

If question 1 went wrong, remember that a ratio has no unit. If question 2 went
wrong, remember that there are 100 cm in 1 m.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A magnifying glass forms an image 20 mm tall of an object 5.0 mm tall.
Calculate the magnification.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Magnification is image height divided by object height. Both heights are already
in millimetres, so the units match.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write magnification $= \dfrac{20\,\text{mm}}{5.0\,\text{mm}}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{magnification} &= \frac{20\,\text{mm}}{5.0\,\text{mm}} \\
                     &= 4.0
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A lens has a magnification of 6.0. A bee in front of it is 1.5 mm long.
Calculate the length of the image of the bee.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange the magnification relationship so that the image height is on its own.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write image height $= \text{magnification} \times \text{object height}$, then put
in $6.0$ and $1.5\,\text{mm}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{image height} &= 6.0 \times 1.5\,\text{mm} \\
                    &= 9.0\,\text{mm}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A slide 5.0 cm tall is projected so that its image is 0.40 m tall.
Calculate the magnification. Take care with the units.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the image height to centimetres before you divide. Make sure both heights
are in the same unit.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$0.40\,\text{m} = 40\,\text{cm}$. Now divide the image height by the object
height.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{magnification} &= \frac{40\,\text{cm}}{5.0\,\text{cm}} \\
                     &= 8.0
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** Describe the two ray rules you use to draw a ray diagram for a convex
lens.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One rule is about a ray that starts parallel to the principal axis. The other is
about a ray that passes through the middle of the lens.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

For the first rule, say which point the ray passes through after the lens. For
the second rule, say whether the ray bends.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Rule 1: a ray that travels parallel to the principal axis is refracted through
the principal focus on the other side of the lens.

Rule 2: a ray that passes through the centre of the lens continues in a straight
line and does not bend.

The two rays meet at the top of the image.
</details>

**5.** A student looks at a small insect through a magnifying glass. The insect
looks larger and the right way up. Explain whether this image is real or virtual.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A real image is inverted and can be put on a screen. Think about what the student
actually sees.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the two properties of the image that the question gives you, and match them
to the properties of a real or a virtual image.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The image is virtual. It is upright, not inverted, and it cannot be put on a
screen. A virtual image forms where the rays only appear to come from, on the
same side of the lens as the object.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>
