# 5.1 The electromagnetic spectrum — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 4.1, especially transverse waves, wavelength,
frequency and the wave equation $v = f\lambda$; writing numbers in standard form;
the prefixes kilo, mega, giga, micro and nano.

**By the end of this section you can:** describe the electromagnetic spectrum;
list its groups in order of wavelength and frequency; and use the wave equation
to find the wavelength or the frequency of any electromagnetic wave.

## 1. What electromagnetic waves are

An **electromagnetic wave** is a transverse wave. It is made of a vibrating
electric field and a vibrating magnetic field. The two fields vibrate at right
angles to each other and at right angles to the direction the wave travels.

An electromagnetic wave carries energy from a source to an absorber. It does not
need a medium, so it can travel through a vacuum. This is how energy reaches the
Earth from the Sun.

There are seven groups of electromagnetic waves. Together they form the
**electromagnetic spectrum**. The spectrum is continuous, so there are no gaps
between the groups.

## 2. The order of the spectrum

The groups are usually listed from the longest wavelength to the shortest. The
same order goes from the lowest frequency to the highest.

| Group | Typical wavelength | Typical frequency |
|---|---|---|
| Radio waves | longer than 1 m | below 3 × 10⁸ Hz |
| Microwaves | 1 mm to 1 m | 3 × 10⁸ Hz to 3 × 10¹¹ Hz |
| Infrared | 700 nm to 1 mm | 3 × 10¹¹ Hz to 4 × 10¹⁴ Hz |
| Visible light | 400 nm to 700 nm | 4 × 10¹⁴ Hz to 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz |
| Ultraviolet | 10 nm to 400 nm | 7.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz to 3 × 10¹⁶ Hz |
| X-rays | 0.01 nm to 10 nm | 3 × 10¹⁶ Hz to 3 × 10¹⁹ Hz |
| Gamma rays | shorter than 0.01 nm | above 3 × 10¹⁹ Hz |

Every group travels at the **same speed** in a vacuum. That speed is
$c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m/s}$. The groups differ in wavelength and in
frequency, not in speed.

Because the speed is the same for every group, wavelength and frequency are
linked. A wave with a longer wavelength must have a lower frequency. A wave with
a shorter wavelength must have a higher frequency.

## 3. Using the wave equation

The wave equation from Section 4.1 works for every electromagnetic wave:

$$ v = f\lambda $$

where $v$ is the speed in metres per second, $f$ is the frequency in hertz and
$\lambda$ is the wavelength in metres. For electromagnetic waves in a vacuum,
$v$ is always $3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m/s}$.

**Worked example 1.** A radio signal has a frequency of 150 MHz. Calculate its
wavelength.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{c}{f} \\
        &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{150 \times 10^6} \\
        &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.5 \times 10^8} \\
        &= 2.0\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

The answer is 2.0 m. A radio wave with this wavelength is a little longer than a
person is tall.

**Worked example 2.** Infrared radiation has a wavelength of 10 μm. Calculate its
frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{c}{\lambda} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{10 \times 10^{-6}} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.0 \times 10^{-5}} \\
  &= 3.0 \times 10^{13}\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Worked example 3.** Green light has a wavelength of 600 nm. Calculate its
frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{c}{\lambda} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{600 \times 10^{-9}} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{6.0 \times 10^{-7}} \\
  &= 5.0 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

Compare this with Worked example 2. The green light has a much shorter
wavelength and a much higher frequency than the infrared radiation.

**Worked example 4.** An X-ray has a wavelength of $1.0 \times 10^{-10}\,\text{m}$.
Calculate its frequency.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{c}{\lambda} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.0 \times 10^{-10}} \\
  &= 3.0 \times 10^{18}\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$

An X-ray has a higher frequency than every other group except gamma rays.

**Explain.** The speed of every electromagnetic wave is the same. Why does a
higher frequency mean a shorter wavelength? Write your answer in two or three
sentences.

> **Watch out.** Wavelength and frequency are not the same thing, and they move
> in opposite directions. When the wavelength goes up, the frequency goes down.
> Also take care with the prefixes: 150 MHz is $1.5 \times 10^8\,\text{Hz}$, and
> 600 nm is $6.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$. Re-check by asking whether your
> wavelength should be bigger or smaller than a metre.

## 5. Progress check

frequency of 150 MHz. Without a calculator, decide whether the wavelength is
closer to 2 cm, 2 m or 200 m. Then work it out with the wave equation.

## You should now be able to

- describe an electromagnetic wave as a transverse wave that carries energy;
- list the seven groups of the spectrum in order of wavelength and frequency;
- state that all electromagnetic waves travel at $3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m/s}$ in
  a vacuum;
- use $v = f\lambda$ to find the wavelength or the frequency of an
  electromagnetic wave;
- explain the link between wavelength and frequency.
