# 5.1 The electromagnetic spectrum — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Section 4.1.

1. Use $v = f\lambda$ to find $v$ when $f = 200\,\text{Hz}$ and
   $\lambda = 1.5\,\text{m}$.
2. Write 600 nm in metres, using standard form.
3. Name the two kinds of wave that pass through a vacuum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $v = 200 \times 1.5 = 300\,\text{m/s}$.
2. $6.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$.
3. Electromagnetic waves pass through a vacuum. Light is one example.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at the wave equation and keep the units with
the numbers. If question 2 went wrong, remember that nano means
$10^{-9}$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** An FM radio station broadcasts at 100 MHz. Calculate the wavelength of
the radio waves.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wave equation is $v = f\lambda$. These waves travel through a vacuum at
the speed of light, $c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m/s}$. You know the speed, and
you want the wavelength. Rearrange the equation so that $\lambda$ is on its own.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$ and convert 100 MHz to $1.0 \times 10^8\,\text{Hz}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{c}{f} \\
        &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.0 \times 10^8} \\
        &= 3.0\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A radar transmitter sends microwaves at 10 GHz. Calculate their
wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed of a microwave in a vacuum is the same as the speed of light. Convert
the frequency to hertz before you divide.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$10\,\text{GHz} = 1.0 \times 10^{10}\,\text{Hz}$. Put this into
$\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{c}{f} \\
        &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{1.0 \times 10^{10}} \\
        &= 0.030\,\text{m} \\
        &= 3.0\,\text{cm}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A detector measures infrared radiation with a frequency of
$6.0 \times 10^{13}\,\text{Hz}$. Calculate its wavelength, and give your answer
in micrometres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the wavelength in metres first. Then convert metres to micrometres. One
micrometre is $1 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\lambda = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{6.0 \times 10^{13}}$, then divide by
$1 \times 10^{-6}$ to change metres into micrometres.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\lambda &= \frac{c}{f} \\
        &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{6.0 \times 10^{13}} \\
        &= 5.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{m} \\
        &= 5.0\,\mu\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** Place these four groups in order of increasing wavelength: gamma rays,
infrared, radio waves, ultraviolet.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Picture the spectrum as one long line. Start at the end with the shortest
wavelength and move along it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength. Radio waves have the longest. Decide
where the other two go between them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Gamma rays, ultraviolet, infrared, radio waves.

Gamma rays have the shortest wavelength and the highest frequency. Radio waves
have the longest wavelength and the lowest frequency. Infrared comes after
visible light, and ultraviolet comes before it, so ultraviolet is shorter than
infrared.
</details>

**5.** A radio wave and a gamma ray travel through a vacuum side by side.
Explain how their speeds compare, and how their wavelengths compare.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

They are both electromagnetic waves. Think about what every electromagnetic wave
has in common in a vacuum.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the one speed they share. Then use the link between wavelength and
frequency to compare the sizes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum, $3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m/s}$.
They have different frequencies. The radio wave has the lower frequency, so it
has the longer wavelength. The gamma ray has the higher frequency, so it has the
much shorter wavelength.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>
