# 5.4 Colour and frequency — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Section 5.1.

1. Which has the shorter wavelength, red light or blue light?
2. Write 400 nm in metres, using standard form.
3. Work out $\dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{6.0 \times 10^{-7}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Blue light has the shorter wavelength.
2. $4.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$.
3. $5.0 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$.

If question 1 went wrong, remember the order of the visible spectrum. If question
3 went wrong, take care with the powers of ten when you divide.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A red shirt is seen in green light. Explain the colour the shirt appears,
and why.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask which colours a red object reflects, and whether green light contains any of
them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the green light when it reaches a surface that reflects only
red.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The shirt appears black. A red object reflects red light and absorbs the other
colours. Green light contains no red, so the shirt reflects almost nothing to the
eye. With no light reflected, the shirt looks black.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**2.** White light shines on a red filter. Describe the light that passes
through, and explain what happens to the other colours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A filter absorbs some wavelengths and transmits others. Think about which
wavelength a red filter lets through.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the colour that passes through. Then say where the energy of the absorbed
colours goes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Red light passes through the filter. The other colours, such as green and blue,
are absorbed by the filter. The absorbed energy is transferred to the thermal
store of the filter.
</details>

**3.** Violet light has a wavelength of 400 nm. Calculate its frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the wave equation as $f = \dfrac{c}{\lambda}$, where
$c = 3.0 \times 10^8\,\text{m/s}$ is the speed of light in a vacuum. Convert the
wavelength to metres first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$400\,\text{nm} = 4.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$. Put this into
$f = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^8}{\lambda}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{c}{\lambda} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{4.0 \times 10^{-7}} \\
  &= 7.5 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** Explain why white light spreads into a spectrum when it passes through a
prism.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

White light is a mixture of colours. Each colour has its own frequency.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Link the frequency of each colour to the amount it is refracted.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

White light contains all the visible wavelengths. Each colour has a different
frequency, so each colour is refracted by a different amount when it enters the
glass. Violet is refracted the most and red the least. The colours therefore
spread out into a band, which is the spectrum.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**5.** A white wall is lit by red light. Explain the colour the wall appears.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A white surface reflects every colour that reaches it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which colours reach the wall, then say which of them are reflected to the
eye.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The wall appears red. A white surface reflects all the visible wavelengths. Only
red light reaches the wall, so only red light is reflected to the eye. The wall
therefore looks red.
</details>
