# 5.5 Black body radiation — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 5.1, especially infrared and the wave equation;
Section 5.2, especially absorption and emission.

**By the end of this section you can:** explain that every object emits and
absorbs radiation; describe how the radiation emitted changes with temperature;
and explain why the temperature of an object can be steady.

## 1. Every object emits radiation

Every object emits electromagnetic radiation. The radiation comes from the motion
of the particles inside the object. The warmer the object, the more radiation it
emits.

A warm object emits mostly **infrared** radiation. Infrared is invisible, but a
thermal camera can detect it. When an object becomes very hot, it also emits
visible light. A piece of iron in a fire first glows dull red, then orange, then
white.

An object can be a good absorber or a poor absorber of radiation. A **black**
surface is a good absorber. A **shiny** surface is a poor absorber, because it
reflects most of the radiation.

A good absorber is also a good emitter. A black surface absorbs infrared
radiation well and emits it well. A shiny surface absorbs little and emits
little.

## 2. Temperature and the radiation emitted

As the temperature of an object rises, two things happen to the radiation it
emits.

1. It emits **more** radiation at every wavelength. The total intensity rises.
2. The wavelength of the **most intense** radiation becomes **shorter**.

A cool object emits most of its radiation at long infrared wavelengths. A hotter
object emits more radiation, and its peak moves to shorter wavelengths. A very
hot object, like the Sun, peaks in the visible range.

**Worked example 1.** A warm object emits most of its radiation at a wavelength
of 8.0 μm. Calculate the frequency of this radiation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
f &= \frac{c}{\lambda} \\
  &= \frac{3.0 \times 10^8}{8.0 \times 10^{-6}} \\
  &= 3.75 \times 10^{13}\,\text{Hz} \\
  &= 3.8 \times 10^{13}\,\text{Hz} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

## 3. Absorbing and emitting at the same time

Every object is absorbing radiation and emitting radiation at the same time.

- If an object absorbs **more** than it emits, it warms up.
- If an object emits **more** than it absorbs, it cools down.
- If the two rates are **equal**, the temperature stays the same.

The Earth receives radiation from the Sun and emits its own infrared radiation.
The Earth's average temperature is steady when these two rates are equal.

- explain that every object emits electromagnetic radiation;

- explain that the peak wavelength becomes shorter as the temperature rises;

- explain why a good absorber is also a good emitter;
- explain how absorption and emission together set the temperature of an object.
