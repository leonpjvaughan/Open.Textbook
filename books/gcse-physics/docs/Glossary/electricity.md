# Glossary — Electricity

**Alternating current (a.c.)** — a current that keeps changing direction. The UK
mains supply is alternating, at 50 Hz and about 230 V.

**Ammeter** — an instrument that measures current, in amperes. It is connected in
series with the component.

**Charge** — a property of matter. Charge comes in two kinds, positive and
negative, and is measured in coulombs.

**Coulomb (C)** — the unit of charge. One coulomb is the charge that passes a
point when a current of one ampere flows for one second.

**Current** — the rate of flow of electric charge, measured in amperes. A current
of one ampere carries one coulomb each second.

**Diode** — a component that allows current to flow in one direction only. In the
reverse direction, its resistance is very high.

**Direct current (d.c.)** — a current that flows in one direction only. A battery
supplies direct current.

**Earth wire** — the green-and-yellow safety wire in a mains cable. It is at 0 V
and carries a current only if there is a fault.

**Electric field** — the region around a charged object where it exerts a force
on another charged object. The field is strongest close to the object.

**Electron** — a particle with a negative charge. Electrons moving through a
conductor carry the charge that makes a current.

**Equivalent resistance** — the single resistance that would give the same
current as a combination of resistors. For resistors in series, it is their sum.

**Filament lamp** — a lamp in which a thin wire is heated until it glows. Its
resistance increases as the filament gets hotter.

**LDR (light-dependent resistor)** — a resistor whose resistance decreases as the
light intensity increases.

**Live wire** — the brown wire in a mains cable. It carries the alternating
potential difference from the supply and is at about 230 V relative to earth.

**National Grid** — the system of cables and transformers that links power
stations to consumers. It transfers power at high potential difference and then
at a lower potential difference for domestic use.

**Neutral wire** — the blue wire in a mains cable. It completes the circuit and
is at, or close to, earth potential.

**Ohm ($\Omega$)** — the unit of resistance. One ohm is one volt per ampere.

**Ohmic conductor** — a conductor whose resistance stays constant as the current
changes, provided its temperature stays constant. The current is directly
proportional to the potential difference.

**Parallel circuit** — a circuit in which components are joined across the same
two points. The potential difference is the same across each component, and the
total current is the sum of the branch currents.

**Potential difference (pd)** — the energy transferred to or from each unit of
charge as it moves between two points in a circuit. It is measured in volts. Many
books and papers also use the word voltage.

**Resistance** — a measure of how strongly a component opposes the flow of
charge. It is found from resistance = potential difference ÷ current, and is
measured in ohms.

**Series circuit** — a circuit in which components are joined one after another.
The current is the same through each component, and the supply potential
difference is shared between them.

**Static electricity** — charge that stays on an insulating material after it has
been rubbed, instead of flowing away.

**Step-down transformer** — a transformer that decreases potential difference, so
that power from the transmission cables can be used in homes and businesses.

**Step-up transformer** — a transformer that increases potential difference, from
a power station to the transmission cables.

**Thermistor** — a resistor whose resistance decreases as its temperature
increases.

**Voltage** — another word for potential difference.

**Voltmeter** — an instrument that measures potential difference, in volts. It is
connected in parallel with the component.
