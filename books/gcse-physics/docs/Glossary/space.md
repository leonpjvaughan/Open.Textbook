# Glossary — Space physics

**Artificial satellite** — an object built by people and put into orbit around a
planet or another body.

**Big Bang** — the model that the universe began from a very small region that
was extremely hot and dense, and has been expanding ever since.

**Cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR)** — a faint microwave signal that
comes from every direction in space, left over from the hot, dense early
universe.

**Dark energy** — the unknown cause of the speeding up of the expansion of the
universe.

**Dark matter** — matter that does not emit electromagnetic radiation but pulls
on galaxies. Its nature is not known.

**Dwarf planet** — a small, roughly spherical body that orbits the Sun but is not
one of the eight planets.

**Galaxy** — a huge group of stars. The Sun is one star in the Milky Way galaxy.

**Main sequence** — the long stage in the life of a star when it fuses hydrogen
into helium, such as the Sun.

**Milky Way** — the galaxy that contains the solar system.

**Moon** — a natural satellite that orbits a planet.

**Nebula** — a cloud of dust and gas. Stars form from a nebula.

**Neutron star** — the small, very dense core left behind after a massive star
explodes as a supernova.

**Orbit** — the path of an object around a larger body, kept in place by gravity.

**Planet** — a large, roughly spherical body that orbits a star.

**Red giant** — the stage in the life of a Sun-sized star when it expands after
the main sequence.

**Red-shift** — the increase in the wavelength of light from a source that is
moving away from the observer.

**Solar system** — the Sun and everything that orbits it: the planets, the dwarf
planets, the moons, the asteroids and the comets.

**Spectrum** — the range of wavelengths in light, crossed by dark lines at fixed
wavelengths.

**Supernova** — the explosion of a massive star at the end of its life. Elements
heavier than iron are made in a supernova.

**White dwarf** — the small, dense, cooling core left behind after a Sun-sized
star becomes a red giant.
