# Glossary — Forces and motion

**Acceleration** — the rate of change of velocity, found from the change in
velocity divided by the time taken. Acceleration is measured in metres per
second squared.

**Atmospheric pressure** — the pressure caused by air molecules colliding with a
surface. It decreases as the height above the ground increases.

**Average speed** — the total distance travelled divided by the total time
taken, for a journey in which the speed changes.

**Braking distance** — the distance a vehicle travels while the brakes are
acting.

**Centre of mass** — the single point at which the weight of an object may be
considered to act.

**Compression** — the decrease in the length of an object when it is pushed.

**Conservation of momentum** — the principle that in a closed system the total
momentum before an event equals the total momentum after it.

**Contact force** — a force that acts when two objects touch, such as friction,
air resistance, tension or the normal contact force.

**Deceleration** — the rate at which an object slows down; a negative
acceleration.

**Density** — the mass of one cubic metre of a substance, measured in kilograms
per cubic metre.

**Distance** — how far an object moves. Distance has no direction, so it is a
scalar.

**Drag** — the resistive force on an object that moves through a fluid.

**Elastic deformation** — a change of shape in which an object returns to its
original shape and size when the forces are removed.

**Extension** — the increase in the length of an object when it is stretched,
measured in metres.

**Fluid** — a liquid or a gas.

**Force** — a push or a pull that acts on an object because of an interaction
with another object. Force is a vector, measured in newtons.

**Free body diagram** — a diagram that shows one object on its own, with an arrow
for every force that acts on it.

**Gear** — a wheel with teeth. Two gears that mesh transmit the turning effect of
a force from one to the other.

**Gravitational field strength** — the force of gravity on each kilogram of an
object, measured in newtons per kilogram. On the Earth it is about 9.8 N/kg.

**Inelastic deformation** — a change of shape in which an object does not return
to its original shape and size when the forces are removed.

**Inertia** — the tendency of an object to stay at rest, or to keep moving at a
constant velocity.

**Inertial mass** — a measure of how difficult it is to change the velocity of
an object, defined as the force divided by the acceleration it produces.

**Lever** — a bar that turns about a pivot and transmits the turning effect of a
force. A lever can multiply a force.

**Limit of proportionality** — the point on a force-extension graph beyond which
the extension is no longer proportional to the force.

**Mass** — the amount of matter in an object, measured in kilograms. Mass does
not change when the object moves to another place.

**Moment** — the turning effect of a force, found from the force multiplied by
the perpendicular distance from the pivot. The unit is the newton-metre.

**Momentum** — the product of the mass of an object and its velocity, measured
in kilogram metres per second. Momentum is a vector.

**Newton** — the unit of force, written N.

**Newton's first law** — if the resultant force on an object is zero, a
stationary object stays stationary and a moving object keeps a constant
velocity.

**Newton's second law** — the acceleration of an object is proportional to the
resultant force and inversely proportional to the mass of the object, so force =
mass × acceleration.

**Newton's third law** — whenever two objects interact, the forces they exert on
each other are equal and opposite.

**Newtonmeter** — a spring balance with a scale, used to measure a force in
newtons.

**Non-contact force** — a force that acts between objects that are apart, such as
the gravitational force, the electrostatic force or the magnetic force.

**Normal contact force** — the push from a surface on an object, at right angles
to the surface.

**Pivot** — the fixed point about which an object turns.

**Pressure** — the force normal to a surface divided by the area of the surface,
measured in pascals. One pascal is one newton per square metre.

**Reaction time** — the time between noticing a hazard and starting to act. For
a driver, the time between seeing a hazard and braking.

**Resultant force** — a single force that has the same effect as all the forces
acting on an object together.

**Scalar** — a quantity that has a size but no direction, such as mass or speed.

**Speed** — how fast an object moves. Speed has no direction, so it is a scalar.

**Spring constant** — the force needed to stretch a spring by one metre, measured
in newtons per metre. A larger spring constant means a stiffer spring.

**Stopping distance** — the total distance a vehicle travels from the moment the
driver sees a hazard to the moment it stops; the thinking distance plus the
braking distance.

**Terminal velocity** — the steady velocity of an object falling through a fluid.
It is reached when the drag force equals the weight, so the resultant force is
zero.

**Thinking distance** — the distance a vehicle travels while the driver reacts.

**Upthrust** — the resultant upward force on an object in a fluid, caused by the
greater pressure on its lower surface.

**Vector** — a quantity that has both a size and a direction, such as force.

**Velocity** — speed in a given direction. Velocity is a vector.

**Weight** — the force of gravity on an object, measured in newtons. Weight is
mass multiplied by gravitational field strength.
