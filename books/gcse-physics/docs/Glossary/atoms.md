# Glossary — Atomic structure

**Activity** — the rate at which the unstable nuclei in a source decay, measured
in becquerels (Bq). One becquerel is one decay each second.

**Alpha particle** — a particle of two protons and two neutrons, emitted from the
nucleus of an unstable atom. It is the same as a helium nucleus.

**Atomic number** — the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. It decides
which element the atom is.

**Background radiation** — the ionising radiation that is present all the time,
from natural sources such as rocks and cosmic rays and from man-made sources.

**Beta particle** — a fast electron ejected from the nucleus when a neutron turns
into a proton.

**Chain reaction** — a reaction in which the neutrons released by one fission
cause further fissions, so the reaction keeps going.

**Contamination** — the unwanted presence of radioactive atoms on or in another
material.

**Energy level** — a distance from the nucleus at which electrons are arranged.
An electron moves to a higher or lower level by absorbing or emitting
electromagnetic radiation.

**Fission** — the splitting of a large and unstable nucleus into two smaller
nuclei, releasing energy.

**Fusion** — the joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus, releasing
energy. Some of the mass is converted into the energy of radiation.

**Gamma ray** — electromagnetic radiation emitted from the nucleus of an unstable
atom. It has no mass and no charge.

**Half-life** — the time it takes for the number of nuclei of a radioactive
isotope to halve, or for the count rate from a sample to fall to half its
starting level.

**Ion** — a charged particle formed when an atom loses or gains one or more
electrons.

**Irradiation** — exposing an object to nuclear radiation. An irradiated object
does not become radioactive.

**Isotopes** — atoms of the same element, with the same number of protons but
different numbers of neutrons.

**Mass number** — the total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an
atom.

**Neutron** — a particle in the nucleus with no electric charge.

**Nuclear radiation** — the particles or electromagnetic radiation emitted by an
unstable nucleus as it decays.

**Proton** — a particle in the nucleus with a relative charge of +1.

**Radiation dose** — a measure of the energy that ionising radiation transfers to
a person, measured in sieverts (Sv).

**Radioactive decay** — the random process in which an unstable nucleus gives out
radiation as it changes to become more stable.

**Tracer** — a small amount of a radioactive isotope taken into the body so that
its movement through an organ can be followed with a detector.
