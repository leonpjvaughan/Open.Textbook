# Glossary — Waves

**Absorption** — the taking in of electromagnetic radiation by a material. The
energy of the radiation is transferred to the thermal store of the material.

**Amplitude** — the maximum displacement of a point on a wave from its
undisturbed position.

**Angle of incidence** — the angle between an incident ray and the normal.

**Angle of reflection** — the angle between a reflected ray and the normal. It
equals the angle of incidence.

**Black body radiation** — the electromagnetic radiation emitted by an object
because of its temperature. A hotter object emits more radiation, with its most
intense wavelength at a shorter wavelength.

**Compression** — a region of a longitudinal wave where the particles are closer
together than in the undisturbed medium.

**Concave lens** — a lens that is thinner in the middle than at the edges. It
spreads parallel rays of light apart, so it is a diverging lens.

**Convex lens** — a lens that is thicker in the middle than at the edges. It
brings parallel rays of light together, so it is a converging lens.

**Dispersion** — the splitting of white light into the colours of the spectrum,
because each colour is refracted by a different amount.

**Echo** — a reflected sound wave, heard separately from the original sound.

**Echo sounding** — a method that uses high-frequency sound to detect objects in
deep water and to measure the depth of the water.

**Electromagnetic spectrum** — the seven groups of electromagnetic waves, from
radio waves at the longest wavelength to gamma rays at the shortest.

**Electromagnetic wave** — a transverse wave made of a vibrating electric field
and a vibrating magnetic field. It carries energy and does not need a medium.

**Filter** — a piece of transparent material that transmits some wavelengths of
light and absorbs the rest.

**Focal length** — the distance from the centre of a lens to its principal focus.

**Frequency** — the number of waves that pass a point each second, measured in
hertz.

**Hertz** — the unit of frequency; one hertz is one wave per second.

**Infrared** — electromagnetic radiation with a wavelength a little longer than
red light. Warm objects emit infrared radiation, and it is felt as heat.

**Infrasound** — sound with a frequency below the range of normal human hearing,
so below about 20 Hz.

**Ionising radiation** — radiation with enough energy to remove electrons from
atoms. Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are ionising.

**Longitudinal wave** — a wave in which the vibration is along the same line as
the direction in which the wave travels. A sound wave in air is longitudinal.

**Magnification** — the image height divided by the object height. It has no
unit.

**Material interface** — the boundary between two different materials.

**Medium** — the material that a wave passes through.

**Normal** — a line drawn at right angles to a surface at the point where a ray
meets it. Angles of incidence and reflection are measured from the normal.

**P-wave** — a longitudinal seismic wave produced by an earthquake. P-waves
travel through both solids and liquids.

**Period** — the time for one complete wave to pass a point, measured in
seconds. The period is the reciprocal of the frequency.

**Principal focus** — the point where a convex lens brings rays that are parallel
to the principal axis together.

**Pulse** — a single short disturbance that travels as a wave.

**Rarefaction** — a region of a longitudinal wave where the particles are spread
further apart than in the undisturbed medium.

**Real image** — an image formed where rays of light actually meet. It is
inverted and can be put on a screen.

**Reflection** — the bouncing of a wave off a surface.

**Refraction** — the change of direction of a wave when it passes from one
material into another and its speed changes.

**S-wave** — a transverse seismic wave produced by an earthquake. S-waves cannot
travel through a liquid.

**Seismic wave** — a wave produced by an earthquake that travels through the
Earth.

**Transmission** — the passing of radiation through a material.

**Transverse wave** — a wave in which the vibration is at right angles to the
direction in which the wave travels. A ripple on a water surface is transverse.

**Ultrasound** — sound with a frequency above the range of normal human hearing,
so above about 20 kHz.

**Ultraviolet** — electromagnetic radiation with a wavelength a little shorter
than violet light. Ultraviolet is ionising and can damage the skin and the eyes.

**Virtual image** — an image formed where rays of light only appear to come from.
It is upright and cannot be put on a screen.

**Wave** — a disturbance that transfers energy from one place to another without
transferring the medium.

**Wave speed** — the speed at which a wave transfers energy through a medium.
It equals the frequency multiplied by the wavelength.

**Wavelength** — the distance from a point on one wave to the equivalent point
on the next wave, measured in metres.
