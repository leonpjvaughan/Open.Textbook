# 7.1 Permanent and induced magnetism, magnetic forces and fields — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Sections 2.1 and 3.2.

1. Write down one contact force and one non-contact force.
2. A resultant force of 12 N acts on an object of mass 3.0 kg. Calculate the
   acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. A contact force acts when two objects touch, for example friction or a push.
   A non-contact force acts without touching, for example gravity or a magnetic
   force.
2. $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{12}{3.0} = 4.0\,\text{m/s}^2$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at Section 2.1. If question 2 went wrong,
rearrange $F = ma$ before you put the numbers in.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Two bar magnets are placed with their south poles facing each other.
Describe the force between them, and give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two poles are the same type. Decide whether two same-type poles push or
pull.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the two poles as like poles, then say what like poles do.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The magnets repel each other. A south pole and a south pole are like poles, and
two like poles repel. The force acts without contact, so the magnets push apart
before they touch. This is a model answer for you to compare with your own
wording.
</details>

**2.** A steel nail is held near a bar magnet. The nail is attracted, even though
it is not a permanent magnet. Explain why.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what the field of the bar magnet does to the magnetic parts inside
the steel.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the phrase induced magnet. Say what the field does to the nail, and what
kind of force induced magnetism causes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The steel nail becomes an induced magnet when it is placed in the field of the
bar magnet. The field lines up the magnetic parts inside the steel. Induced
magnetism always causes a force of attraction, so the nail is pulled towards the
magnet. When the magnet is taken away, the nail loses most of its magnetism.
</details>

**3.** An aluminium drinks can is placed near a strong magnet. Explain why the
can is not attracted.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask whether aluminium is a magnetic material.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the four magnetic materials, then say whether aluminium is one of them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Aluminium is not a magnetic material. The magnetic materials are iron, steel,
cobalt and nickel. A magnet attracts a magnetic material and also attracts
another magnet, but it does not attract aluminium, so the can is not pulled.
</details>

**4.** The north-seeking end of a compass needle points north. Explain what this
shows about the Earth.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The needle is a small bar magnet. A bar magnet lines up only when it is in a
magnetic field.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the Earth must have a magnetic field, then say what the field must come
from.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The compass needle is a small bar magnet, and it lines up only when it is in a
magnetic field. The needle points north wherever it is used on the Earth's
surface, so the Earth must have a magnetic field. The field must come from
magnetic material deep inside the Earth, which is evidence that the core of the
Earth is magnetic.
</details>

**5.** Describe how to use a plotting compass to plot one field line of a bar
magnet.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A field line is found one compass position at a time. The compass has to be
moved forwards along the line.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Start the compass near one pole. Mark where the needle points. Move the compass
so its south end sits at the mark.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Put the magnet on paper and mark its outline. Put the compass near one pole and
mark the two ends of the needle. Move the compass so that its south end sits
where the north end was, and mark the new position of the north end. Repeat until
the marks reach the other pole, then join the marks with a smooth curve. Start
from a different place to plot another line.
</details>

**6.** State where the magnetic field of a bar magnet is strongest, and where it
is weakest. Give a reason for your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at how the field is described around a magnet.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the parts of the magnet where the field lines are closest together, then
say what close lines mean.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The field is strongest at the poles, which are the two ends of the magnet. The
field lines are closest together there. The field gets weaker as the distance
from the magnet increases, so it is weakest far from the magnet.
</details>

**7.** A compass is placed 8.0 cm from a magnet. Write this distance in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One metre is 100 cm. Decide whether the number in metres should be larger or
smaller than 8.0.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide 8.0 by 100.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$8.0\,\text{cm} = \dfrac{8.0}{100}\,\text{m} = 0.080\,\text{m}$.
</details>

**8.** A magnetic force of 0.40 N acts on a steel ball of mass 0.10 kg.
Calculate the acceleration of the ball.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The magnetic force is the resultant force on the ball. Use Newton's second law.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $a = \dfrac{F}{m}$, then put in $F = 0.40$ N and $m = 0.10$ kg.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{F}{m} \\
  &= \frac{0.40}{0.10} \\
  &= 4.0\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
