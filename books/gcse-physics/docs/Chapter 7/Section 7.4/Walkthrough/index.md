# 7.4 Microphones and speakers — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 7.2, especially the motor effect and the force on a
current-carrying conductor; Section 7.3, especially the generator effect;
Section 4.1, especially sound as a pressure wave.

**By the end of this section you can:** explain how a moving-coil microphone
converts the pressure variations in a sound wave into variations in current; and
explain how a moving-coil loudspeaker and headphones do the reverse.

## 1. Two effects, two devices

A sound wave is a pressure wave. The air pushes on a surface, and the pressure
rises and falls as the wave passes. A microphone turns those pressure variations
into variations in an electric current. A loudspeaker does the opposite: it turns
variations in a current into pressure variations in the air.

The two devices use the two electromagnetic effects from this chapter.

- A **microphone** uses the generator effect. A change in a magnetic field
  induces a potential difference, and so drives a current.
- A **loudspeaker** uses the motor effect. A current in a magnetic field
  produces a force.

## 2. The moving-coil microphone

A moving-coil microphone has a diaphragm, a small coil of wire, and a magnet.

1. A sound wave arrives at the microphone. The pressure variations push the
   diaphragm in and out.
2. The diaphragm is attached to the coil, so the coil moves backwards and
   forwards in the magnetic field.
3. The coil is moving relative to the magnetic field, so a potential difference
   is induced across the ends of the coil. This is the generator effect.
4. The induced potential difference drives a current in the circuit. The current
   rises and falls as the coil moves faster or slower.

The result is a varying current that is a copy of the sound wave. The microphone
has converted sound into an electrical signal.

**Worked example 1.** Explain how a moving-coil microphone works.

A model answer is:

- Sound waves make the diaphragm, and with it the coil, move backwards and
  forwards.
- The coil moves relative to the magnetic field, so a potential difference is
  induced across the coil. This is the generator effect.
- The induced potential difference drives a current that varies as the coil
  moves. The microphone has converted sound into a varying current.

## 3. The moving-coil loudspeaker

A moving-coil loudspeaker has a magnet, a coil of wire, and a cone. The coil is
attached to the cone, and it sits in the magnetic field of the magnet.

1. A varying current from the amplifier flows through the coil.
2. The coil is in a magnetic field, so the current experiences a force. This is
   the motor effect.
3. As the current changes, the size and direction of the force change. The force
   moves the coil, and the coil pushes the cone.
4. The moving cone pushes the air, so it makes pressure variations. These
   pressure variations are a sound wave.

The current has been converted into sound. Headphones work in the same way, with
a small diaphragm in place of the cone.

**Explain.** The loudspeaker uses the motor effect, not the generator effect. Why
does a changing current make the cone vibrate, rather than move once and stay
still? Write your answer in two or three sentences.

**Worked example 2.** A loudspeaker coil has 0.050 m of wire inside the magnetic
field of the magnet. The flux density is 0.20 T, and the current in the coil is
0.50 A. Calculate the force on the coil.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= B I l \\
  &= 0.20 \times 0.50 \times 0.050 \\
  &= 0.0050\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

where $F$ is the force, $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $I$ is the current and
$l$ is the length of wire in the field.

The force changes size and direction as the current changes, so the cone vibrates.

> **Watch out.** A microphone uses the generator effect, and a loudspeaker uses
> the motor effect. It is a common mistake to swap them. Check by asking which way the energy
> is going. A microphone starts with movement and makes a current, so it is the
> generator effect. A loudspeaker starts with a current and makes movement, so it
> is the motor effect.

## 4. Comparing the two devices

| | Microphone | Loudspeaker |
|---|---|---|
| Input | pressure variations of a sound wave | variations in a current |
| Output | variations in a current | pressure variations of a sound wave |
| Effect | generator effect | motor effect |

Both devices turn one kind of variation into another. The microphone converts
sound into an electrical signal. The loudspeaker converts the electrical signal
back into sound.
