# 7.4 Microphones and speakers — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Section 7.3.

1. Describe the generator effect in one sentence.
2. A transformer changes 240 V to 12 V. Is it a step-up transformer or a
   step-down transformer?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. A moving conductor, or a changing magnetic field, induces a potential
   difference across the conductor, which drives a current in a complete
   circuit.
2. It is a step-down transformer, because the secondary potential difference is
   smaller than the primary potential difference.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at Section 7.3. If question 2 went wrong,
check which coil has the larger potential difference.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Explain how a moving-coil microphone converts a sound wave into a varying
current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Follow the energy: sound arrives, the diaphragm moves, the coil moves in a
field.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which effect is used when a coil moves in a magnetic field, then say what
that effect produces.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Sound waves make the diaphragm of the microphone vibrate. The diaphragm is
attached to a coil, so the coil moves backwards and forwards in the magnetic
field. The coil is moving relative to the field, so a potential difference is
induced across its ends. This is the generator effect. The induced potential
difference drives a current that varies as the coil moves, so the microphone
produces a varying current that copies the sound wave.
</details>

**2.** Explain how a moving-coil loudspeaker converts a varying current into
sound.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with the current in the coil. The coil sits in a magnetic field.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the motor effect, then say what the changing force does to the cone and to
the air.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A varying current flows through the coil of the loudspeaker. The coil sits in
the magnetic field of a magnet, so the current experiences a force. This is the
motor effect. As the current changes, the size and direction of the force change,
so the coil moves backwards and forwards and pushes the cone. The moving cone
makes pressure variations in the air, which are a sound wave.
</details>

**3.** Compare a moving-coil microphone with a moving-coil loudspeaker. State
the effect each device uses.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the input and the output of each device.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what goes into each device and what comes out, then name the effect for
each.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A microphone takes in the pressure variations of a sound wave and puts out
variations in a current. It uses the generator effect, because a coil moves in a
magnetic field.

A loudspeaker takes in variations in a current and puts out the pressure
variations of a sound wave. It uses the motor effect, because a current in a
magnetic field experiences a force.

Each device does the opposite of the other.
</details>

**4.** A loudspeaker coil has 0.10 m of wire inside the magnetic field. The flux
density is 0.30 T, and the current is 0.40 A. Calculate the force on the coil.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The coil is at right angles to the field. Use the force equation from the motor
effect.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $F = BIl$, then put in $B = 0.30$ T, $I = 0.40$ A and $l = 0.10$ m.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= B I l \\
  &= 0.30 \times 0.40 \times 0.10 \\
  &= 0.012\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>
