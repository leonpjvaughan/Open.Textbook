# 7.2 Magnetic effects of currents and the motor effect — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Sections 7.1 and 6.1.

1. A steel paper clip is placed in the field of a magnet and then taken away.
   Does it stay magnetic? Explain.
2. What is the unit of electric current, and what letter is used for current in
   an equation?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. It loses most or all of its magnetism. It became an induced magnet only
   while it was in the field.
2. The unit is the ampere, and the symbol is $I$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at induced magnets in Section 7.1. If
question 2 went wrong, look at current in Section 6.1.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Describe how to show that a current in a wire creates a magnetic field.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use a compass, because a compass needle turns when it sits in a magnetic
field.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Place the compass near a straight wire. Note where it points with the current
off, then switch the current on.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Place a long straight wire on a piece of card and put a compass near it. With the
current off, the compass points north. Switch the current on. The needle turns
away from north, so the current has made a magnetic field. Reverse the current,
and the needle turns the other way. This is a model answer for you to compare
with your own wording.
</details>

**2.** Describe the magnetic field around a long straight wire, and explain how
it changes as you move away from the wire.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Picture looking along the wire. The field lines form closed loops.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the field lines are circles around the wire, then say what happens to
the field strength as the distance grows.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The field lines are circles around the wire. The field is strongest close to the
wire. As you move further away, the field gets weaker. A larger current makes the
field stronger at every distance.
</details>

**3.** Explain how shaping a wire into a solenoid increases the magnetic effect
of the current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what happens to the field of each turn of the coil.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the field of each turn adds to the field of the other turns, then
describe the field inside the coil.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A solenoid is a coil of many turns. The magnetic field of each turn adds to the
field of the other turns, so the fields combine. Inside the solenoid the field is
strong and uniform, and outside it the field has the shape of the field of a bar
magnet. The magnetic effect is much larger than for one straight wire.
</details>

**4.** Explain why an iron core is added to a solenoid to make an electromagnet,
and how the magnet can be switched off.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Iron is a magnetic material. Think about what the field of the coil does to the
iron.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the iron becomes magnetised by the coil's field and adds to it. Then say
what happens to the field when the current stops.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The field of the coil magnetises the iron core. The magnetised core adds its own
field to the field of the coil, so the total field is much stronger. When the current is switched off, the coil's field goes and the
iron loses its magnetism, so the electromagnet can be switched off.
</details>

**5.** Describe the magnetic field inside a solenoid.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the field inside the coil with the field around a bar magnet.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say whether the field inside is strong or weak, and whether it is uniform.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Inside the solenoid the field is strong and uniform. The field lines are straight
and equally spaced, and they point along the axis of the coil. Outside the
solenoid the field spreads out and has the same shape as the field of a bar
magnet.
</details>

**6.** State Fleming's left-hand rule, and say what each part of the hand
represents.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Hold your left hand with the thumb and the first two fingers at right angles.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the field, the current and the force, and match each one to a finger or the
thumb.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Hold the left hand so that the thumb, the first finger and the second finger are
at right angles to each other. The first finger points in the direction of the
magnetic field, from north to south. The second finger points in the direction of
the current. The thumb points in the direction of the force on the conductor.
</details>

**7.** A wire of length 0.20 m carries a current of 3.0 A. It is at right angles
to a magnetic field of flux density 0.40 T. Calculate the force on the wire.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The three quantities you need are $B$, $I$ and $l$. Check that each is in its SI
unit before you multiply.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $F = BIl$, then put in $B = 0.40$ T, $I = 3.0$ A and $l = 0.20$ m.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= B I l \\
  &= 0.40 \times 3.0 \times 0.20 \\
  &= 0.24\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**8.** The force on a wire is 0.60 N. The wire is 0.50 m long and sits at right
angles to a magnetic field of flux density 0.30 T. Calculate the current in the
wire.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You want the current, so rearrange $F = BIl$ before you put the numbers in.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $I = \dfrac{F}{Bl}$, then work out $\dfrac{0.60}{0.30 \times 0.50}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= B I l \\
I &= \frac{F}{B l} \\
  &= \frac{0.60}{0.30 \times 0.50} \\
  &= 4.0\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>
