# Chapter 7: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The scrapyard crane lifts steel scrap with an electromagnet. The magnet is a coil
of 600 turns on an iron core, carrying a current of 4.0 A.

**1. Why an electromagnet, and not a permanent magnet.** An electromagnet can be
switched on and off. The crane switches the current on to pick up the steel
scrap, and switches it off to release the load. A permanent magnet would hold on
to the scrap and could not let go.

**2. Why the magnet attracts steel but not aluminium.** Steel is a magnetic
material. When the steel comes near the coil, it becomes an induced magnet, and
induced magnetism always causes a force of attraction. Aluminium is not a
magnetic material, so the magnet does not pull on it. Copper is not magnetic
either, so it is left behind with the aluminium.

**3. The field of the coil.** Inside the solenoid the field is strong and
uniform. The field lines are straight, equally spaced and parallel to the axis
of the coil. Outside the coil, the field has the same shape as the field of a bar
magnet, with a north pole at one end and a south pole at the other.

**4. The iron core.** The field of the coil magnetises the iron. The magnetised
core adds its own field to the field of the coil, so the total field is much
stronger. When the current is switched off, the coil's field goes and the
iron loses its magnetism, so the steel drops.

<!-- tier:h -->
Here $n_p$ and $n_s$ are the numbers of turns on the primary and secondary
coils. $V_p$ and $V_s$ are their potential differences, $P$ is power, $V$ is
potential difference and $I$ is current.

**5. The transformer.** The yard is supplied at 12 000 V and steps this down to
240 V. The primary coil has 4000 turns.

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_s &= n_p \times \frac{V_s}{V_p} \\
    &= 4000 \times \frac{240}{12\,000} \\
    &= 80
\end{aligned}
$$

The secondary coil has 80 turns. This is a step-down transformer.

**6. The current in the transmission line.** The yard uses 24 kW of power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= V I \\
I &= \frac{P}{V} \\
  &= \frac{24\,000}{12\,000} \\
  &= 2.0\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

The current in the 12 000 V line is 2.0 A.

**7. The microphone and the loudspeaker.** The microphone turns sound into a
varying current. Sound waves make the diaphragm and coil move in a magnetic
field, so a potential difference is induced. This is the generator effect. The
loudspeaker does the opposite. The varying current flows through a coil in a
magnetic field, so the coil feels a changing force. This is the motor effect. The
force makes the cone vibrate and send out sound.

The judgement that an electromagnet is the right choice for a scrapyard crane is
a reasoned answer. It follows from the facts above. Whether the choice is safe
and efficient is not the kind of claim a computer can check, so compare your
reasoning with the evidence in the working.
