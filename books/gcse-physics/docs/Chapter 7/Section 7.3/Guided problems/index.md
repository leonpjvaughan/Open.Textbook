# 7.3 Induced potential, transformers and the national grid — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Sections 7.2 and 6.4.

1. State the three factors that affect the size of the force on a
   current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.
2. A power supply has a potential difference of 12 V and drives a current of
   2.0 A. Calculate the power.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The size of the current, the magnetic flux density, and the length of the
   conductor in the field.
2. $P = VI = 12 \times 2.0 = 24\,\text{W}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at the motor effect in Section 7.2. If
question 2 went wrong, look at electrical power in Section 6.4.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Describe the generator effect.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The effect needs a change: either the conductor moves, or the field changes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what is induced across the ends of the conductor, and what happens if the
conductor is in a complete circuit.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

If an electrical conductor moves relative to a magnetic field, or the magnetic
field around it changes, a potential difference is induced across its ends. If
the conductor is part of a complete circuit, the induced potential difference
drives a current. The induced current makes a magnetic field that opposes the
original change. This is a model answer for you to compare with your own
wording.
</details>

**2.** State two ways to increase the size of an induced potential difference,
and one way to reverse its direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about how fast the change happens, and how strong the field is.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name a change to the movement and a change to the field for the size. For the
direction, name a change that turns the effect around.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

To increase the size: move the conductor faster through the field, or use a
stronger magnetic field, or use a coil with more turns.
To reverse the direction: reverse the direction of movement, or reverse the
direction of the magnetic field.
</details>

**3.** Explain how the generator effect is used in an alternator to generate
a.c., and in a dynamo to generate d.c.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The difference is what happens to the connections as the coil turns.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the coil rotates in a field in both cases. Then say what the alternator
does to the current each half turn, and what the commutator does in a dynamo.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

In both machines a coil rotates in a magnetic field, so a changing field passes
through the coil and a potential difference is induced.

In an alternator, the connections stay the same, so the current in the outside
circuit reverses every half turn. The output is alternating current.

In a dynamo, a commutator reverses the connections every half turn, so the
current in the outside circuit always flows the same way. The output is direct
current.
</details>

**4.** A transformer has 500 turns on the primary coil and 100 turns on the
secondary coil. The primary potential difference is 240 V. Calculate the
secondary potential difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The ratio of the potential differences equals the ratio of the numbers of turns.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $V_s = V_p \times \dfrac{n_s}{n_p}$, then put in the four numbers.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_s &= V_p \times \frac{n_s}{n_p} \\
    &= 240 \times \frac{100}{500} \\
    &= 48\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is a step-down transformer.
</details>

**5.** A step-up transformer changes 12 V to 240 V. The secondary coil has 2000
turns. Calculate the number of turns on the primary coil.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You want the primary turns, so rearrange the turns equation first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $n_p = n_s \times \dfrac{V_p}{V_s}$, then work out
$2000 \times \dfrac{12}{240}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
n_p &= n_s \times \frac{V_p}{V_s} \\
    &= 2000 \times \frac{12}{240} \\
    &= 100
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**6.** A transformer has a primary potential difference of 11 000 V and a
primary current of 0.50 A. The secondary potential difference is 250 V. Assume
the transformer is 100% efficient. Calculate the secondary current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

If the transformer is 100% efficient, the power input equals the power output.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $V_s I_s = V_p I_p$, then work out
$\dfrac{11\,000 \times 0.50}{250}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_s I_s &= V_p I_p \\
I_s &= \frac{V_p I_p}{V_s} \\
    &= \frac{11\,000 \times 0.50}{250} \\
    &= 22\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**7.** Explain why a transformer does not work when it is connected to a steady
direct current supply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A transformer needs a changing magnetic field in its core.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what a steady d.c. does to the field in the core, then say whether a
potential difference is induced in the secondary coil.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A transformer works because the alternating current in the primary coil makes a
changing magnetic field in the core. The changing field induces a potential
difference in the secondary coil. A steady direct current makes a steady field,
so the field in the core does not change and no potential difference is induced.
</details>

**8.** Explain why the national grid transmits electrical power at a very high
potential difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $P = VI$. For the same power, what happens to the current when the potential
difference is large?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the current is smaller, then link a smaller current to less heating in
the cables.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

For the same power, a higher potential difference means a smaller current,
because $P = VI$. A smaller current transfers less energy to the thermal store of
the transmission cables, so less energy is wasted as the cables heat up.
</details>
