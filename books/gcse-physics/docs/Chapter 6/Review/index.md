# Chapter 6: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The cabin runs from the 230 V mains supply and from a 12 V battery. Work through
the facts one at a time.

Here $P$ is power, $V$ is potential difference, $I$ is current and $R$ is
resistance. $E$ is energy, $Q$ is charge and $t$ is time.

**1. The heater.** The heater takes a current of 5.0 A from the 230 V supply.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= VI \\
  &= 230 \times 5.0 \\
  &= 1150\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \frac{V}{I} \\
  &= \frac{230}{5.0} \\
  &= 46\,\Omega
\end{aligned}
$$

The heater has a power of 1150 W and a resistance of 46 $\Omega$.

**2. The lamp.** The lamp takes a current of 0.20 A from the same supply.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= VI \\
  &= 230 \times 0.20 \\
  &= 46\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \frac{V}{I} \\
  &= \frac{230}{0.20} \\
  &= 1150\,\Omega
\end{aligned}
$$

The lamp has a power of 46 W and a resistance of 1150 $\Omega$.

**3. The total current.** The heater and the lamp are in parallel, so the
potential difference across each one is 230 V. The total current is the sum of
the branch currents.

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{total} &= 5.0 + 0.20 \\
          &= 5.2\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

The supply provides 5.2 A.

**4. The energy the heater transfers.** The heater runs for 30 minutes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= 30 \times 60 = 1800\,\text{s} \\
E &= Pt \\
  &= 1150 \times 1800 \\
  &= 2\,070\,000\,\text{J} \\
  &= 2.1 \times 10^6\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

To check, find the charge that flows through the heater and use $E = QV$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q &= It \\
  &= 5.0 \times 1800 \\
  &= 9000\,\text{C} \\
E &= QV \\
  &= 9000 \times 230 \\
  &= 2\,070\,000\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

Both methods give $2.1 \times 10^6\,\text{J}$ to two significant figures.

**5. The pump circuit.** The pump circuit has a 12 V battery and two resistors
of 6.0 $\Omega$ and 2.0 $\Omega$ in series.

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{total} &= 6.0 + 2.0 = 8.0\,\Omega \\
I &= \frac{V}{R_{total}} = \frac{12}{8.0} = 1.5\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

The current is 1.5 A everywhere in the series circuit. Find the potential
difference across each resistor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
V_{6.0} &= IR = 1.5 \times 6.0 = 9.0\,\text{V} \\
V_{2.0} &= IR = 1.5 \times 2.0 = 3.0\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

Check: $9.0\,\text{V} + 3.0\,\text{V} = 12\,\text{V}$, which is the battery
potential difference.

**6. Why the appliances are in parallel.** Each appliance is designed to work at
230 V. In parallel, the potential difference across each one is the full 230 V,
so each works at its correct power. In series they would share the 230 V, so
each would receive less than 230 V and would not work properly. In parallel the
two appliances can also be switched on and off separately.

**7. Safety.** The metal frame of the cabin is connected to the earth wire. If a
fault brought the live wire into contact with the frame, the current would flow
to earth along the low-resistance path instead of through a person. The frame
would not stay at 230 V, so it would be much safer to touch.

**8. The charged window.** Rubbing the plastic window with a dry cloth transfers
electrons from the cloth to the window. The window gains electrons, so it
becomes negatively charged. The charged window creates an electric field around
itself. The field exerts a force on the nearby dust, so the dust is pulled
towards the window and collects on it.

## What you can now do

- describe current as a rate of flow of charge, and use $Q = It$;
- use $V = IR$, and find a resistance from a $V$–$I$ graph;
- describe how the resistance of a lamp, a diode, a thermistor and an LDR
  changes;
- describe series and parallel circuits, and calculate currents and potential
  differences in each;
- explain why a series connection increases the total resistance and a parallel
  connection decreases it;
- recall the UK supply values and describe the three wires in a mains cable;
- explain the dangers of the live wire and the purpose of the earth wire;
- use $P = VI$, $P = I^2R$, $E = Pt$ and $E = QV$;
- explain why the National Grid transfers power at high potential difference;
- explain static charging, the forces between charges, and electric fields.
