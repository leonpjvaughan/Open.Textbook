# 6.1 Current, potential difference and resistance — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Chapter 1 and Chapter 5.

1. Use $v = f\lambda$ to find the frequency when $v = 340\,\text{m/s}$ and
   $\lambda = 0.50\,\text{m}$.
2. Name the energy store that increases when a resistor becomes hot.
3. Write a current of 0.25 A in milliamperes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda} = \dfrac{340}{0.50} = 680\,\text{Hz}$.
2. The thermal store of the resistor increases.
3. $0.25\,\text{A} = 250\,\text{mA}$, because milli means $10^{-3}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at rearranging the wave equation. If
question 2 went wrong, remember that a transfer by heating fills a thermal
store.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A charge of 90 C flows through a lamp in 30 s. Calculate the current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You are given the charge and the time, and you want the current. Choose the
equation that links those three quantities.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$, then put in $Q = 90\,\text{C}$ and $t = 30\,\text{s}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{Q}{t} \\
  &= \frac{90}{30} \\
  &= 3.0\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A current of 0.40 A flows through a heater for 5.0 minutes. Calculate the
charge that flows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The equation $Q = It$ uses time in seconds. The time in this question is in
minutes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Change 5.0 minutes into seconds by multiplying by 60. That gives 300 s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= 5.0 \times 60 = 300\,\text{s} \\
Q &= It \\
  &= 0.40 \times 300 \\
  &= 120\,\text{C}
\end{aligned}
$$

To two significant figures this is $1.2 \times 10^2\,\text{C}$.
</details>

**3.** The potential difference across a 15 $\Omega$ resistor is 6.0 V.
Calculate the current through it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You know the potential difference and the resistance, and you want the current.
Start from $V = IR$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rearrange the equation so that $I$ is on its own. Then substitute 6.0 V and
15 $\Omega$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{V}{R} \\
  &= \frac{6.0}{15} \\
  &= 0.40\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A current of 0.20 A flows through a 60 $\Omega$ resistor. Calculate the
potential difference across the resistor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both the current and the resistance are given. No rearranging is needed this
time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the current by the resistance, and keep the unit with the answer.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
V &= IR \\
  &= 0.20 \times 60 \\
  &= 12\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**5.** The potential difference across a thermistor is 9.0 V, and the current
through it is 0.050 A. Calculate its resistance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Take care with the current. The value 0.050 A is 50 milliamperes, so it is a
small current.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $R = \dfrac{V}{I}$ and divide 9.0 V by 0.050 A.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
R &= \frac{V}{I} \\
  &= \frac{9.0}{0.050} \\
  &= 180\,\Omega
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**6.** A filament lamp and an ohmic resistor are connected in turn to the same
supply. The potential difference is increased steadily from zero. Describe how
the current through each component changes, and explain the difference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One component keeps a constant resistance. The resistance of the other one
depends on its temperature.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what the graph of $V$ against $I$ looks like for the resistor. Then say what
happens to the filament as the current rises.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

For the ohmic resistor, the current is directly proportional to the potential
difference. The graph of $V$ against $I$ is a straight line through the origin,
because the resistance stays constant at a constant temperature.

For the filament lamp, the current is not directly proportional to the potential
difference. As the current rises, the filament gets hotter, so its resistance
increases. Each extra volt therefore produces a smaller increase in current, and
the graph curves so that it becomes less steep.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>
