# 6.5 Static electricity and electric fields — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Section 6.1 and Section 6.4.

1. Use $Q = It$ to find the charge when $I = 0.20\,\text{A}$ and
   $t = 30\,\text{s}$.
2. Name the two kinds of electric charge.
3. A device on the 230 V supply takes a current of 0.10 A. Calculate its power.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $Q = 0.20 \times 30 = 6.0\,\text{C}$.
2. Positive charge and negative charge.
3. $P = VI = 230 \times 0.10 = 23\,\text{W}$.

If question 1 went wrong, check that you multiplied the current by the time. If
question 3 went wrong, look again at $P = VI$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A plastic rod is rubbed with a dry cloth. Describe how the rod becomes
charged, and state the sign of the charge on the rod and on the cloth.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The charge comes from electrons moving from one material to the other. Decide
which material gains them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the electrons, then use that to decide the sign of the
charge on each material.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Rubbing the two insulators together transfers electrons from one material to the
other. Electrons are rubbed off the cloth and on to the rod.

The rod gains electrons, so it becomes negatively charged. The cloth loses
electrons, so it is left with an equal positive charge.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**2.** Two charged spheres are brought close together. Describe the force
between them when they carry the same type of charge, and the force when they
carry opposite types of charge.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the two rules for charged objects. Take the two cases one at a time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

State the force for the same charges first, then the force for the opposite
charges.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Two objects that carry the same type of charge repel each other. Two objects
that carry opposite types of charge attract each other.

In both cases the force acts without the objects touching, so it is a
non-contact force. The force gets stronger as the distance between the spheres
decreases.
</details>

**3.** A charged balloon is held near a small piece of paper. The paper is
attracted to the balloon, but the balloon does not touch it. Explain how the
balloon exerts a force on the paper.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The balloon creates something in the space around it. What is it called, and
what does it do to a charged object?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the field around the charged balloon, then say what a second charged object
feels when it is placed in that field.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The charged balloon creates an electric field around itself. The field is
strongest close to the balloon and weaker further away.

The paper is placed in the field, so it experiences a force. Because the paper
and the balloon carry opposite charges, the force is attractive, and the paper
moves towards the balloon without touching it. The force grows stronger as the
paper gets closer.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**4.** Explain how a strong electric field can cause a spark between a charged
object and a conductor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what a very strong field does to the air between the two objects.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the air molecules when the field is strong enough, then say
why that lets charge jump across the gap.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

When charge builds up on an object, the electric field near it becomes very
strong. If the field is strong enough, it ionises the air in the gap. Ionising
the air means removing electrons from the air molecules, so the air can conduct
a current.

The ionised air gives the charge a path, so the charge jumps across the gap as a
spark. The spark lasts for a very short time, so the current is large even when
the charge transferred is small.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**5.** Describe the electric field pattern around an isolated charged sphere.
Include the direction of the field lines for a positive sphere and for a
negative sphere.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Picture the lines starting at the surface of the sphere and spreading outwards.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which way the arrows point for each sign of charge, then say how the spacing
of the lines shows the strength of the field.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The field lines radiate outwards from the surface of the sphere, at right angles
to the surface.

For a positively charged sphere, the arrows point away from the sphere. For a
negatively charged sphere, the arrows point towards the sphere.

The lines are closest together near the surface and further apart further away.
This shows that the field is strongest close to the sphere and weaker at a
distance.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** A spark transfers a charge of 5.0 μC in a time of 2.0 μs. Say which
relationship you will use, then calculate the average current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The relationship links charge, current and time. Convert both values to coulombs
and seconds first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $I = \dfrac{Q}{t}$ and put in $5.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{C}$ and
$2.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{s}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{Q}{t} \\
  &= \frac{5.0 \times 10^{-6}}{2.0 \times 10^{-6}} \\
  &= 2.5\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

The average current is 2.5 A. The spark is brief, so the current is large even
though the charge is small.
</details>
