# 6.2 Series and parallel circuits — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Section 6.1 and Chapter 1.

1. Use $V = IR$ to find the potential difference when $I = 0.30\,\text{A}$ and
   $R = 20\,\Omega$.
2. A charge of 60 C flows past a point in 20 s. Calculate the current.
3. Name the instrument that is connected in series to measure current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $V = 0.30 \times 20 = 6.0\,\text{V}$.
2. $I = \dfrac{Q}{t} = \dfrac{60}{20} = 3.0\,\text{A}$.
3. An ammeter.

If question 1 went wrong, check that you multiplied the current by the
resistance. If question 3 went wrong, remember that a voltmeter goes in parallel
and an ammeter goes in series.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A 3.0 $\Omega$ resistor and a 5.0 $\Omega$ resistor are connected in
series with a 4.0 V supply. Calculate the total resistance, the current, and the
potential difference across each resistor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with the total resistance. When resistors are in series, their resistances
add.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find $R_{total} = 3.0 + 5.0 = 8.0\,\Omega$, then use $I = \dfrac{V}{R_{total}}$
to find the current.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{total} &= 3.0 + 5.0 = 8.0\,\Omega \\
I &= \frac{4.0}{8.0} = 0.50\,\text{A} \\
V_{3.0} &= 0.50 \times 3.0 = 1.5\,\text{V} \\
V_{5.0} &= 0.50 \times 5.0 = 2.5\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$

Check: $1.5\,\text{V} + 2.5\,\text{V} = 4.0\,\text{V}$, the supply potential
difference.
</details>

**2.** A 2.0 $\Omega$ resistor and a 4.0 $\Omega$ resistor are connected in
series with a 6.0 V battery. Find the current and the potential difference
across each resistor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The same current flows through both resistors. Find that current before you find
either potential difference.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add the resistances to get $6.0\,\Omega$, then divide the battery potential
difference by that total.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
R_{total} &= 2.0 + 4.0 = 6.0\,\Omega \\
I &= \frac{V}{R_{total}} = \frac{6.0}{6.0} = 1.0\,\text{A} \\
V_{2.0} &= 1.0 \times 2.0 = 2.0\,\text{V} \\
V_{4.0} &= 1.0 \times 4.0 = 4.0\,\text{V}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** An 8.0 $\Omega$ resistor and a 24 $\Omega$ resistor are connected in
parallel across a 6.0 V supply. Find the current through each resistor and the
total current from the supply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

In parallel, the potential difference across each resistor is the same as the
supply. Start with the 8.0 $\Omega$ branch.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $I = \dfrac{V}{R}$ twice. Both resistors have 6.0 V across them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{8.0} &= \frac{6.0}{8.0} = 0.75\,\text{A} \\
I_{24} &= \frac{6.0}{24} = 0.25\,\text{A} \\
I_{total} &= 0.75 + 0.25 = 1.0\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

The total resistance is smaller than 8.0 $\Omega$, the smaller of the two
resistors, because the total current is larger than either branch current.
</details>

**4.** Two lamps are connected in parallel across a 230 V supply. Each lamp has
a resistance of 1150 $\Omega$. Calculate the current through each lamp and the
total current from the supply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two lamps are identical, so the two branch currents are equal. Find one of
them first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide 230 V by 1150 $\Omega$ to find the current in one lamp, then double it for
the total.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I_{lamp} &= \frac{V}{R} = \frac{230}{1150} = 0.20\,\text{A} \\
I_{total} &= 0.20 + 0.20 = 0.40\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$

Each lamp takes 0.20 A, and the supply provides 0.40 A in total.
</details>

**5.** Explain why adding a resistor in series to a circuit decreases the
current from the supply.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what the extra resistor does to the total resistance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $I = \dfrac{V}{R_{total}}$. The supply potential difference does not change.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

In series, the resistances add, so adding a resistor increases the total
resistance of the circuit. The supply potential difference stays the same.
Because $I = \dfrac{V}{R_{total}}$, a larger total resistance gives a smaller
current through every component in the series circuit.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** A 9.0 V supply is connected in series with a 12 $\Omega$ resistor and an
unknown resistor. The potential difference across the 12 $\Omega$ resistor is
3.0 V. Find the current in the circuit and the resistance of the unknown
resistor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The current is the same everywhere in a series circuit, so find it from the
resistor you already know.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $I = \dfrac{V}{R}$ for the 12 $\Omega$ resistor. Then subtract its potential
difference from 9.0 V to find the potential difference across the unknown
resistor.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{3.0}{12} = 0.25\,\text{A} \\
V_{unknown} &= 9.0 - 3.0 = 6.0\,\text{V} \\
R_{unknown} &= \frac{6.0}{0.25} = 24\,\Omega
\end{aligned}
$$

The unknown resistor has a resistance of 24 $\Omega$.
</details>
