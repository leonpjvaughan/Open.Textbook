# 6.4 Energy transfers and the national grid — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from Chapter 1 and Section 6.1.

1. A motor transfers 600 J of energy in 5.0 s. Calculate its power.
2. Use $Q = It$ to find the charge when $I = 0.30\,\text{A}$ and
   $t = 60\,\text{s}$.
3. A 12 $\Omega$ resistor carries a current of 0.50 A. Calculate the potential
   difference across it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $P = \dfrac{E}{t} = \dfrac{600}{5.0} = 120\,\text{W}$.
2. $Q = 0.30 \times 60 = 18\,\text{C}$.
3. $V = IR = 0.50 \times 12 = 6.0\,\text{V}$.

If question 1 went wrong, remember that power is the rate of energy transfer.
If question 3 went wrong, check that you used $V = IR$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A lamp is connected to the 230 V mains supply and takes a current of
0.20 A. Calculate its power.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You know the potential difference and the current, and you want the power. Pick
the form of the power equation that uses both.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $P = VI$ and multiply 230 V by 0.20 A.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= VI \\
  &= 230 \times 0.20 \\
  &= 46\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A current of 3.0 A flows through an 8.0 $\Omega$ resistor. Calculate the
power transferred to the resistor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You know the current and the resistance, so use the form of the power equation
with those two quantities.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $P = I^2R$. Square the current first, then multiply by the resistance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= I^2R \\
  &= 3.0^2 \times 8.0 \\
  &= 9.0 \times 8.0 \\
  &= 72\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** The lamp of question 1 has a power of 46 W. It is switched on for 200 s.
Calculate the energy it transfers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the energy equation that links power and time. The time is already in
seconds.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E = Pt$ and substitute $P = 46\,\text{W}$ and $t = 200\,\text{s}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= Pt \\
  &= 46 \times 200 \\
  &= 9200\,\text{J} \\
  &= 9.2 \times 10^3\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A charge of 40 C flows through an appliance connected to the 230 V
supply. Calculate the energy transferred.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

This question gives you charge and potential difference, not current and time.
Use the energy equation with those two quantities.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E = QV$ and multiply 40 C by 230 V.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= QV \\
  &= 40 \times 230 \\
  &= 9200\,\text{J} \\
  &= 9.2 \times 10^3\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the same energy as in question 3, because the charge and the power
describe the same lamp.
</details>

**5.** A 460 W appliance is connected to the 230 V mains supply. Calculate the
current through it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start from $P = VI$ and make the current the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $I = \dfrac{P}{V}$ and divide 460 W by 230 V.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
I &= \frac{P}{V} \\
  &= \frac{460}{230} \\
  &= 2.0\,\text{A}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**6.** Explain why the National Grid transfers electrical power at a very high
potential difference, and state what the step-down transformers do.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the current that is needed for a given power, and then about the
energy wasted as heating in the cables.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $P = VI$ to link the potential difference to the current. Then use
$P = I^2R$ for the power wasted in the cables.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

For a given power, a higher potential difference means a lower current, because
$P = VI$. The power wasted as heating in the cables is $P = I^2R$, so a lower
current wastes much less energy. Transferring power at a high potential
difference therefore makes the National Grid efficient.

Step-down transformers reduce the potential difference from the transmission
cables to a much lower value, so that the supply is safe to use in homes and
businesses.

This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**7.** An appliance is connected to the 230 V mains supply. It takes a current
of 0.60 A for 5.0 minutes. Say which relationships you will use, then calculate
the power and the energy transferred.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the power first, from the potential difference and the current. Then use
that power with the time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $P = VI$, then change 5.0 minutes to seconds and use $E = Pt$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= VI = 230 \times 0.60 = 138\,\text{W} \\
t &= 5.0 \times 60 = 300\,\text{s} \\
E &= Pt = 138 \times 300 = 41\,400\,\text{J} \\
  &= 4.1 \times 10^4\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

To two significant figures the energy is $4.1 \times 10^4\,\text{J}$.
</details>
