# 6.4 Energy transfers and the national grid — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 6.1, especially $Q = It$ and $V = IR$; Chapter 1,
especially power, energy stores and energy transfers; Chapter 2, work done.

**By the end of this section you can:** use the equations $P = VI$, $P = I^2R$,
$E = Pt$ and $E = QV$; describe how domestic appliances transfer energy; and
explain why the National Grid transfers power at high potential difference.

## 1. Power in an electrical circuit

**Power** is the rate at which energy is transferred. It is measured in
**watts** (W). One watt is one joule each second.

When charge flows through a component, work is done on the component. The energy
is transferred from the electrical supply to another store. In a motor, the
energy goes to the kinetic store. In a heater, it goes to the thermal store.

The power transferred by a device depends on the potential difference across it
and the current through it.

$$ P = VI $$

where $P$ is the power in watts, $V$ is the potential difference in volts and
$I$ is the current in amperes.

The same power can also be found from the current and the resistance.

$$ P = I^2R $$

where $R$ is the resistance in ohms. This form is useful when you know the
current and the resistance, but not the potential difference.

## 2. Energy transferred

The total energy transferred depends on the power and on how long the device is
switched on.

$$ E = Pt $$

where $E$ is the energy transferred in joules, $P$ is the power in watts and $t$
is the time in seconds.

There is a second equation, which uses the charge that flows.

$$ E = QV $$

where $Q$ is the charge flow in coulombs and $V$ is the potential difference in
volts. The two energy equations give the same answer, because $Q = It$ and
$P = VI$.

## 3. Worked examples

**Worked example 1.** A heater is connected to the 230 V mains supply and takes a
current of 5.0 A. Calculate its power.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= VI \\
  &= 230 \times 5.0 \\
  &= 1150\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Worked example 2.** A current of 2.0 A flows through a 15 $\Omega$ resistor.
Calculate the power transferred to the resistor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= I^2R \\
  &= 2.0^2 \times 15 \\
  &= 4.0 \times 15 \\
  &= 60\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Worked example 3.** The heater of Worked example 1 is switched on for
30 minutes. Calculate the energy it transfers.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= 30 \times 60 = 1800\,\text{s} \\
E &= Pt \\
  &= 1150 \times 1800 \\
  &= 2\,070\,000\,\text{J} \\
  &= 2.1 \times 10^6\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Worked example 4.** A charge of 9000 C flows through the heater at a potential
difference of 230 V. Calculate the energy transferred.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E &= QV \\
  &= 9000 \times 230 \\
  &= 2\,070\,000\,\text{J} \\
  &= 2.1 \times 10^6\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

Both methods give the same energy, as they must.

## 4. Domestic appliances

Every appliance is designed to transfer energy usefully. The table shows two
examples.

| Appliance | Energy transfer | Power rating |
|---|---|---|
| Electric kettle | Electrical to thermal, in the heating element | About 3000 W |
| Electric motor | Electrical to kinetic, in the motor | About 500 W |

A higher power rating means more energy is transferred each second. A 3000 W
kettle transfers the same energy in one minute as a 500 W motor transfers in six
minutes. The energy transferred depends on the power **and** on the time.

## 5. The National Grid

The **National Grid** is a system of cables and transformers that links power
stations to consumers. Electrical power is transferred from the power stations
at a very high potential difference, and then transferred at a much lower
potential difference in each locality for domestic use.

Step-up transformers increase the potential difference from the power station to
the transmission cables. Step-down transformers then decrease it for homes and
businesses.

The National Grid is an efficient way to transfer energy because it uses a high
potential difference. For a given power, a higher potential difference means a
lower current, because $P = VI$. A lower current means less energy is
transferred to the thermal store of the cables, because the power wasted in the
cables is $P = I^2R$. Keeping the current low keeps the wasted power small.

**Explain.** The 0.10 kW lamp transfers more energy than the 2.0 kW heater,

> **Watch out.** Power and energy are not the same thing. Power is the rate of
> energy transfer, measured in watts. Energy is the total transferred, measured
> in joules. A low-power device left on for a long time can transfer more energy
> than a high-power device used briefly. Also check the unit of time: $E = Pt$
> needs seconds, and $P = I^2R$ squares the current before multiplying by the
> resistance. Re-check which equation a question needs before you substitute.
