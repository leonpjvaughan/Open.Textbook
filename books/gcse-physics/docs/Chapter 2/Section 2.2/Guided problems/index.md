# 2.2 Forces and elasticity — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Calculate the weight of a bag of mass 4.0 kg. Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.
2. Give one example of a contact force, and one example of a non-contact force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $W = mg = 4.0 \times 9.8 = 39.2\,\text{N}$, so $39\,\text{N}$ to 2 significant
   figures.
2. A contact force is friction, tension, air resistance or the normal contact
   force. A non-contact force is the gravitational force, the electrostatic force
   or the magnetic force.

If question 1 went wrong, return to Section 2.1 and the equation $W = mg$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A spring has a spring constant of 150 N/m. It is stretched by 0.24 m.
Calculate the force applied to the spring.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The force, the spring constant and the extension are linked by one equation.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $F = ke$, then put in $k = 150\,\text{N/m}$ and $e = 0.24\,\text{m}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= k e \\
  &= 150 \times 0.24 \\
  &= 36\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A force of 30 N stretches a spring by 0.12 m. Calculate the spring
constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need the spring constant on its own, so rearrange $F = ke$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the force by the extension: $k = \dfrac{F}{e}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
k &= \frac{F}{e} \\
  &= \frac{30}{0.12} \\
  &= 250\,\text{N/m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A spring has a spring constant of 350 N/m. It is stretched by 0.20 m.
Calculate the energy stored in the elastic potential store.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The extension is squared in the energy equation. Work out the square first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E_e = \tfrac{1}{2}ke^2$, then replace $e^2$ with $0.20^2 = 0.040$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_e &= \tfrac{1}{2} k e^2 \\
    &= 0.5 \times 350 \times 0.20^2 \\
    &= 0.5 \times 350 \times 0.040 \\
    &= 7.0\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A spring is stretched a little and springs back when it is released. A
second spring is stretched too far and stays longer. Describe the difference
between the two changes, and name each one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare what happens to each spring after the force is removed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which spring returns to its original length and which one does not.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The first spring returns to its original length when the force is removed, so
its change is elastic deformation. The second spring stays longer, so its change
is inelastic deformation. This answer is a model answer for you to compare with
your own wording.
</details>

**5.** A force is added to a spring and the extension is measured. The results
are shown in the table. The graph of force against extension is a straight line
through the origin. Calculate the spring constant from the gradient.

| Force (N) | Extension (m) |
|---|---|
| 0.0 | 0.00 |
| 20.0 | 0.10 |
| 40.0 | 0.20 |
| 60.0 | 0.30 |

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The gradient of a straight-line graph is the change in the vertical value divided
by the change in the horizontal value.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Choose the largest pair of values, such as 60.0 N and 0.30 m. Divide the force by
the extension.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ k = \frac{60.0}{0.30} = 200\,\text{N/m} $$

The gradient of a force-extension graph is the spring constant.
</details>
