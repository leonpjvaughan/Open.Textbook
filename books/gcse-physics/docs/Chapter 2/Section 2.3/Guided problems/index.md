# 2.3 Work done and energy transfer — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A spring of spring constant 200 N/m is stretched by 0.10 m. Calculate the
   force applied.
2. Name the store that fills when work is done against friction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $F = ke = 200 \times 0.10 = 20\,\text{N}$.
2. The thermal store of the surfaces.

If question 1 went wrong, return to Section 2.2 and the equation $F = ke$.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A force of 90 N pushes a crate 6.0 m along the line of the force.
Calculate the work done.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work done needs the force and the distance moved along the line of the force.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $W = Fs$, then multiply 90 N by 6.0 m.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= F s \\
  &= 90 \times 6.0 \\
  &= 540\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**2.** A machine does 1200 J of work over a distance of 25 m. Calculate the
force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need the force on its own, so rearrange $W = Fs$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the work done by the distance: $F = \dfrac{W}{s}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= \frac{W}{s} \\
  &= \frac{1200}{25} \\
  &= 48\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**3.** A bag of mass 6.0 kg is lifted 1.5 m onto a wall. Take
$g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$. Calculate the work done against gravity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The force that does the work is the weight of the bag, not the mass.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the weight first: $F = mg = 6.0 \times 9.8$. Then multiply by the
height.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= m g = 6.0 \times 9.8 = 58.8\,\text{N} \\
W &= F s = 58.8 \times 1.5 = 88.2\,\text{J} \\
  &= 88\,\text{J} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**4.** A box slides across a rough floor and slows to a stop. Describe the
energy transfer that takes place, and name the store that receives the energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the force between the box and the floor as the box slides.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the store that decreases and the store that increases, and say what causes
the transfer.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The kinetic store of the box decreases as the box slows. The friction force
between the box and the floor does work, and the energy is transferred to the
thermal store of the box and the floor. Their temperature rises. This answer is
a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**5.** A bag of mass 20 kg is lifted 1.8 m onto a shelf. Take
$g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$. Say which force does the work, then calculate the work
done.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lifting force must be at least as large as the weight, and it acts through
the height lifted.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the weight with $W = mg$, then use $W = Fs$ with the height as the distance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The lifting force does the work against gravity. Its size is

$$ F = m g = 20 \times 9.8 = 196\,\text{N} $$

Then

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= F s \\
  &= 196 \times 1.8 \\
  &= 352.8\,\text{J} \\
  &= 350\,\text{J} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
