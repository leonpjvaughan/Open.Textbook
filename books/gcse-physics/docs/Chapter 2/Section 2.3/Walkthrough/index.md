# 2.3 Work done and energy transfer — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 2.1, especially weight and resultant forces; Chapter 1,
especially energy stores and transfers.

**By the end of this section you can:** explain what work done means; calculate
the work done when a force moves an object; describe the energy transfer that
happens; and change between newton-metres and joules.

## 1. Work done by a force

When a force causes an object to move, **work is done** on the object. The force
must act along the line in which the object moves.

$$ W = F s $$

where $W$ is the work done in joules, $F$ is the force in newtons, and $s$ is the
distance moved along the line of the force in metres.

One joule of work is done when a force of one newton moves an object one metre.
Because a joule can be written as a newton metre, the two units are the same
size.

$$ 1\,\text{J} = 1\,\text{N m} $$

**Worked example 1.** A force of 75 N pushes a box 6.0 m along the line of the
force. Calculate the work done.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= F s \\
  &= 75 \times 6.0 \\
  &= 450\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Worked example 2.** A machine does 600 J of work over a distance of 8.0 m.
Calculate the force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= \frac{W}{s} \\
  &= \frac{600}{8.0} \\
  &= 75\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Worked example 3.** A force of 120 N does 480 J of work. Calculate the distance
moved.

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= \frac{W}{F} \\
  &= \frac{480}{120} \\
  &= 4.0\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

## 2. Work done and energy transfer

Doing work transfers energy from one store to another. The work done is the
amount of energy transferred.

- Lifting a box transfers energy from the chemical store of your muscles to the
  gravitational potential store of the box. The work done is $W = mgh$.
- Pushing a box against friction transfers energy from the kinetic store of the
  box to the thermal store of the box and the floor. The temperature of the
  surfaces rises.

**Worked example 4.** A box of mass 12 kg is lifted 2.5 m onto a shelf. Take
$g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$. Calculate the work done against gravity.

The force needed is the weight of the box.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= m g = 12 \times 9.8 = 117.6\,\text{N} \\
W &= F s = 117.6 \times 2.5 = 294\,\text{J} \\
  &= 290\,\text{J} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

The same energy is gained by the gravitational potential store of the box, so
$W = mgh$ gives the same answer.

**Worked example 5.** A friction force of 45 N acts on a crate that slides 12 m.
Calculate the work done against friction.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= F s \\
  &= 45 \times 12 \\
  &= 540\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

This 540 J is transferred to the thermal store of the crate and the floor.

> **Watch out.** Work is done only by the part of a force that acts **along the
> line of motion**. If you carry a heavy bag along a level corridor, your upward
> force keeps the bag at the same height, so it does no work on the bag. The
> distance moved is at right angles to the force. Re-check the direction of the
> force before you use $W = Fs$.

## 3. Power and work

Power is the rate at which energy is transferred, so it also tells you the rate
at which work is done.

$$ P = \frac{W}{t} $$

**Worked example 6.** A motor does 1800 J of work in 6.0 s. Calculate the power
of the motor.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= \frac{W}{t} \\
  &= \frac{1800}{6.0} \\
  &= 300\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

## You should now be able to

- explain what is meant by work done by a force;
- calculate work done, force or distance, using $W = Fs$;
- describe the energy transfer when work is done, including work done against
  friction;
- change between newton-metres and joules;
- calculate the power of a transfer from the work done and the time taken.
