# Chapter 2: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The bike-hire workshop checks the same numbers on every cargo bike. Take
$g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

**1. The weight of the bike and rider.** Their mass is 110 kg.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= m g \\
  &= 110 \times 9.8 \\
  &= 1078\,\text{N} \\
  &= 1.1 \times 10^3\,\text{N} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

**2. The spring constant of the front spring.** The spring compresses by 0.020 m
under a force of 600 N.

$$
\begin{aligned}
k &= \frac{F}{e} \\
  &= \frac{600}{0.020} \\
  &= 30000\,\text{N/m} \\
  &= 3.0 \times 10^4\,\text{N/m}
\end{aligned}
$$

**3. The energy stored in the front spring.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_e &= \tfrac{1}{2} k e^2 \\
    &= 0.5 \times 30000 \times 0.020^2 \\
    &= 6.0\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is also the work done in compressing the spring.

**4. The work done pushing the bike up the ramp.** The force is 150 N along a
ramp 8.0 m long.

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= F s \\
  &= 150 \times 8.0 \\
  &= 1200\,\text{J} \\
  &= 1.2 \times 10^3\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

**5. The pressure in the front tyre.** The contact area is
$12\,\text{cm}^2 = 0.0012\,\text{m}^2$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= \frac{F}{A} \\
  &= \frac{540}{0.0012} \\
  &= 450000\,\text{Pa} \\
  &= 4.5 \times 10^5\,\text{Pa}
\end{aligned}
$$

**6. The moment on the brake lever.** The hand pulls with 60 N at 0.090 m from
the pivot.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= F d \\
  &= 60 \times 0.090 \\
  &= 5.4\,\text{N m}
\end{aligned}
$$

**7. The force in the brake cable.** The cable is 0.030 m from the pivot, so it
must give the same moment.

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= \frac{M}{d} \\
  &= \frac{5.4}{0.030} \\
  &= 180\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

**8. Does the lever make the brakes work better?** Yes. The rider pulls the lever
with 60 N, and the cable pulls with 180 N, which is three times as large. The
lever is a force multiplier: the effort acts further from the pivot than the
cable, so the same moment gives a larger force at the shorter distance. A larger
force in the cable presses the brake blocks harder onto the wheel.

This final judgement is a reasoned answer. It uses the numbers above, and it
matches the rule for balanced moments. It is not the kind of claim a computer can
check, so compare your reasoning with the evidence in the working.

## What you can now do

- describe forces as interactions, and sort them into contact and non-contact
  forces;
- calculate weight from mass, and find the resultant of two forces in a straight
  line;
- resolve a force into two components, and use a free body diagram or a vector
  diagram (Higher tier);
- use the force-extension relationship and calculate the energy stored in a
  spring;
- calculate work done, force or distance, and describe the energy transfer;
- calculate pressure, force or area, and explain atmospheric pressure;
- calculate the pressure at a depth in a liquid and explain upthrust (Higher
  tier);
- calculate the moment of a force, use balanced moments, and explain how levers
  and gears work.
