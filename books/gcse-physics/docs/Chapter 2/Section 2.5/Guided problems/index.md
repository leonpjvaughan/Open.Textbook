# 2.5 Moments, levers and gears — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A force of 400 N acts on an area of 0.20 m². Calculate the pressure.
2. Change 25 cm into metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $p = \dfrac{F}{A} = \dfrac{400}{0.20} = 2000\,\text{Pa}$, which is
   $2.0 \times 10^3\,\text{Pa}$.
2. There are 100 cm in 1 m, so $25\,\text{cm} = 0.25\,\text{m}$.

If question 2 went wrong, remember to divide by 100, not multiply.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A force of 25 N acts at a perpendicular distance of 0.40 m from a pivot.
Calculate the moment of the force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The moment uses the force and the perpendicular distance from the pivot.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $M = Fd$, then multiply 25 N by 0.40 m. The distance is already in metres.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= F d \\
  &= 25 \times 0.40 \\
  &= 10\,\text{N m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A weight of 500 N sits 0.60 m from the pivot of a balanced beam. A second
weight sits 1.5 m from the pivot on the other side. Calculate the second weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Balance means the clockwise moment equals the anticlockwise moment.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the known moment, $500 \times 0.60$. Then divide it by the second
distance, 1.5 m.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
500 \times 0.60 &= F \times 1.5 \\
300 &= F \times 1.5 \\
F &= \frac{300}{1.5} \\
  &= 200\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A lever has an effort of 120 N at 0.50 m from the pivot and a load at
0.10 m from the pivot. Calculate the force on the load.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The lever is balanced, so the two moments are equal.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the effort moment, $120 \times 0.50$. Then divide it by 0.10 m.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
120 \times 0.50 &= F \times 0.10 \\
60 &= F \times 0.10 \\
F &= \frac{60}{0.10} \\
  &= 600\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A lever is used to lift a heavy stone. Explain how the lever transmits the
turning effect of the force, and why it makes the job easier.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the distance of the effort from the pivot with the distance of the load.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the moment when the same force acts further from the pivot.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A lever turns about a pivot. The effort makes a moment, $M = Fd$, which turns the
lever. The load is closer to the pivot than the effort, so the effort acts on a
longer lever arm. A small effort therefore makes a moment large enough to give a
large force on the load. The lever multiplies the force. This answer is a model
answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**5.** A gear with 20 teeth drives a gear with 40 teeth. The driving gear has a
moment of 12 N m. Calculate the moment of the driven gear.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The moments are in the same ratio as the numbers of teeth.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the ratio $\dfrac{20}{40}$, then multiply the driving moment by it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The ratio of the teeth is $\dfrac{20}{40} = 0.5$. The driven gear has a smaller
moment.

$$ M = 12 \times 0.5 = 6.0\,\text{N m} $$
</details>
