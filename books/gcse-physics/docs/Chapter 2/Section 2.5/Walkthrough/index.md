# 2.5 Moments, levers and gears — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Section 2.1, especially forces and vectors; multiplying and
dividing numbers; changing centimetres to metres.

**By the end of this section you can:** describe examples in which forces cause
rotation; calculate the moment of a force; use balanced moments to find a force
or a distance; and explain how levers and gears transmit the turning effect of a
force.

## 1. Forces that cause rotation

A force can make an object turn about a fixed point. The fixed point is called
the **pivot**. A door turns about its hinges, a spanner turns about a nut, and a
see-saw turns about its centre.

## 2. The moment of a force

The turning effect of a force is called the **moment** of the force.

$$ M = F d $$

where $M$ is the moment in newton-metres, $F$ is the force in newtons, and $d$ is
the **perpendicular distance** from the pivot to the line of action of the force,
in metres.

Perpendicular distance means the shortest distance from the pivot to the line
along which the force acts. A larger force, or the same force further from the
pivot, gives a larger moment.

**Worked example 1.** A force of 40 N acts at a perpendicular distance of 0.15 m
from a pivot. Calculate the moment of the force.

$$
\begin{aligned}
M &= F d \\
  &= 40 \times 0.15 \\
  &= 6.0\,\text{N m}
\end{aligned}
$$

## 3. Balanced moments

An object that is balanced does not turn. The total **clockwise** moment about
the pivot equals the total **anticlockwise** moment about the pivot.

**Worked example 2.** A weight of 300 N sits 2.0 m from the pivot of a balanced
beam. A second weight sits 2.5 m from the pivot on the other side. Calculate the
second weight.

The anticlockwise moment is $300 \times 2.0$. The clockwise moment is
$F \times 2.5$. Because the beam is balanced, the two moments are equal.

$$
\begin{aligned}
300 \times 2.0 &= F \times 2.5 \\
600 &= F \times 2.5 \\
F &= \frac{600}{2.5} \\
  &= 240\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

## 4. Levers

A **lever** is a bar that turns about a pivot. It transmits the turning effect of
a force. If the effort is applied further from the pivot than the load, the lever
multiplies the force: a small effort gives a large force on the load.

**Worked example 3.** A lever has an effort of 200 N at 0.80 m from the pivot and
a load at 0.20 m from the pivot. Calculate the force on the load.

$$
\begin{aligned}
200 \times 0.80 &= F \times 0.20 \\
160 &= F \times 0.20 \\
F &= \frac{160}{0.20} \\
  &= 800\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

The crowbar, the wheelbarrow and a spanner are all levers.

## 5. Gears

A **gear** is a wheel with teeth. When two gears mesh, the teeth of one turn the
other. The force on the teeth transmits the turning effect from one gear to the
other.

If a small gear drives a large gear, the large gear turns more slowly but with a
larger moment. If a large gear drives a small gear, the small gear turns faster
but with a smaller moment. The moments are in the same ratio as the numbers of
teeth on the gears.

**Worked example 4.** A gear with 30 teeth drives a gear with 15 teeth. The
driving gear has a moment of 8.0 N m. Calculate the moment of the driven gear.

The ratio of the teeth is $30 : 15$, which is $2 : 1$. The moment is multiplied
by 2.

$$ M = 8.0 \times 2 = 16\,\text{N m} $$

> **Watch out.** Use the **perpendicular** distance from the pivot to the line of
> action of the force, not the distance to the point where the force is applied.
> If you push a spanner at an angle, the perpendicular distance is smaller than
> the length of the spanner, so the moment is smaller. Keep every distance in
> metres: $15\,\text{cm}$ is $0.15\,\text{m}$. Re-check that your distances are
> less than about a metre for a hand tool.

## You should now be able to

- describe examples in which forces cause rotation;
- calculate the moment of a force, using $M = Fd$;
- use balanced moments to find a force or a perpendicular distance;
- explain how a lever transmits and can multiply the turning effect of a force;
- explain how gears transmit the turning effect of a force, using the ratio of
  the numbers of teeth.
