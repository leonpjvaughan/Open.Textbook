# Chapter 2: Forces and their interactions

## The chapter problem: the bike-hire workshop

A bike-hire workshop beside a river looks after a fleet of cargo bikes. The
mechanics check the same things on every bike: the suspension, the tyres, the
brakes and the gears.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- The bike and its rider together have a **mass of 110 kg**.
- The front suspension spring compresses by **0.020 m** when the front wheel
  pushes up with a **force of 600 N**.
- The front tyre carries a **force of 540 N**. Its contact patch with the road
  has an **area of 12 cm²**.
- Pushing the bike up the workshop ramp takes a **force of 150 N** along the
  ramp. The ramp is **8.0 m** long.
- The brake lever is **0.090 m** from its pivot. The brake cable is **0.030 m**
  from the pivot. A hand pulls the lever with a **force of 60 N**.

By the end of the chapter you will work out the weight of the loaded bike, the
spring constant of the front spring, the energy the spring stores, the work done
pushing the bike up the ramp, the pressure in the front tyre and the moment on
the brake lever. You will also decide whether the lever makes the brakes work
better.

> **First contact: no working yet.** Estimate. The bike and rider have a mass of
> 110 kg. Without a calculator, is their weight closer to 100 N, 1000 N or
> 10 000 N? Write your estimate down.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>If you are not sure where to start</summary>

Weight is a force. On the Earth, each kilogram has a weight of about 10 N. So
110 kg has a weight of about 1100 N, which is closer to 1000 N.
</details>

## By the end of this chapter

You will be able to:

- name contact and non-contact forces, and represent a force as a vector;
- calculate the weight of an object from its mass;
- find the resultant of two forces acting in a straight line;
- describe elastic and inelastic deformation;
- use the force-extension relationship, and calculate the energy stored in a
  spring;
- calculate the work done when a force moves an object;
- calculate pressure from force and area, and explain atmospheric pressure;
- calculate the moment of a force, and use balanced moments;
- explain how levers and gears transmit the turning effect of a force.

Two further skills are for Higher tier only: drawing free body diagrams and
resolving a force into two components, and using the pressure at a depth in a
fluid.

## Where it is used

Every tool you pick up uses these ideas. So does every bridge, crane, tyre and
tap. Engineers choose the shape and the size of a part so that the forces, the
pressure and the turning effects all stay safe.
