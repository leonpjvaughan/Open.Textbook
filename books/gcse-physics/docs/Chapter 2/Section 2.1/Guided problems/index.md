# 2.1 Forces and their interactions — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Calculate the energy in the kinetic store of an object of mass 2.0 kg moving
   at 3.0 m/s.
2. A spring is stretched. Name the store that increases.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.5 \times 2.0 \times 3.0^2 = 9.0\,\text{J}$.
2. The elastic potential store.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at Section 1.1 and the equation
$E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A climber has a mass of 65 kg. Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$. Calculate the
weight of the climber.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Weight is a force, so it is measured in newtons. The equation that links mass and
weight has $g$ in it.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $W = mg$, then put in $m = 65\,\text{kg}$ and $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
W &= m g \\
  &= 65 \times 9.8 \\
  &= 637\,\text{N} \\
  &= 640\,\text{N} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A force of 185 N acts to the right and a force of 120 N acts to the left.
Calculate the resultant force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two forces act along the same straight line, but in opposite directions.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract the smaller force from the larger force. The resultant points in the
direction of the larger force.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ 185 - 120 = 65\,\text{N} $$

The resultant force is 65 N to the right, the direction of the larger force.
</details>

**3.** A car travels along a straight road at a constant speed. Describe the
forces acting on the car, and explain why the resultant force is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Draw the car on its own and put an arrow on it for every force. Include the
weight and the force from the road.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A constant speed means the forces along the road balance. Name the forward force
from the engine and the backward force from air resistance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The car has its weight acting downwards, and a normal contact force from the road
acting upwards. These two forces are equal and opposite, so they balance. Along
the road, the forward force from the engine balances the backward force from air
resistance and friction. Every force is balanced, so the resultant force is
zero, and the car keeps a constant speed. This answer is a model answer for you
to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**4.** A force of 80 N acts at $25^\circ$ to the horizontal. Resolve the force
into a horizontal component and a vertical component.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The horizontal component uses the cosine of the angle. The vertical component
uses the sine.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $80 \cos 25^\circ$ and $80 \sin 25^\circ$. Check that the two components
are both smaller than 80 N.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F_{\text{horizontal}} &= 80 \cos 25^\circ = 72.5\,\text{N} \\
F_{\text{vertical}}   &= 80 \sin 25^\circ = 33.8\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**5.** Two forces of 25 N and 40 N act on a box in the same direction. Calculate
the resultant force, and state its direction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The forces point the same way, so one force adds to the other.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Add 25 N and 40 N.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ 25 + 40 = 65\,\text{N} $$

The resultant force is 65 N in the direction of the two forces.
</details>
