# 2.4 Pressure and pressure differences in fluids — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Calculate the work done when a force of 60 N moves an object 5.0 m along the
   line of the force.
2. Write $1\,\text{m}^2$ in square centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $W = Fs = 60 \times 5.0 = 300\,\text{J}$.
2. $1\,\text{m}^2 = 10000\,\text{cm}^2$.

If question 2 went wrong, remember that the factor of 100 applies to both
lengths, so it is squared: $100 \times 100 = 10000$.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A force of 240 N acts on an area of 0.020 m². Calculate the pressure.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Pressure is the force normal to a surface divided by the area. The area is
already in square metres.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $p = \dfrac{F}{A}$, then divide 240 N by 0.020 m².
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= \frac{F}{A} \\
  &= \frac{240}{0.020} \\
  &= 12000\,\text{Pa} \\
  &= 1.2 \times 10^4\,\text{Pa}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**2.** A pressure of 2500 Pa acts on an area of 0.48 m². Calculate the force
normal to the surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $p = \dfrac{F}{A}$ to make the force the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the pressure by the area: $F = pA$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= p A \\
  &= 2500 \times 0.48 \\
  &= 1200\,\text{N} \\
  &= 1.2 \times 10^3\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**3.** Calculate the pressure due to a column of water 0.20 m high. The density
of water is 1000 kg/m³ and $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The pressure at a depth uses the height of the column, the density and $g$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $p = h\rho g$, then put in $h = 0.20\,\text{m}$, $\rho = 1000\,\text{kg/m}^3$
and $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= h \rho g \\
  &= 0.20 \times 1000 \times 9.8 \\
  &= 1960\,\text{Pa} \\
  &= 2.0 \times 10^3\,\text{Pa}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**4.** Explain why atmospheric pressure decreases as the height above the ground
increases.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the air molecules above a surface, and about the weight of that air.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the number and the weight of the air molecules above the
surface as the surface goes higher.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The atmosphere is a thin layer of air around the Earth. At a high surface there
are fewer air molecules above it, so the weight of the air above is smaller.
Fewer molecules collide with the surface, so the pressure is smaller.
Atmospheric pressure therefore decreases as the height increases. This answer is
a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**5.** A stone is lowered into a bucket of water. Explain why the stone feels an
upward force, and state one factor that decides whether it floats.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the depth of the top and the bottom of the stone.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which surface has the greater pressure, and what that difference does to the
upward and downward forces.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The bottom of the stone is deeper than the top, so the pressure on the bottom is
greater. The upward force on the bottom is larger than the downward force on the
top, giving a resultant upward force called upthrust. The stone floats if its
average density is less than the density of water, and sinks if its average
density is greater. This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your
own wording.
</details>
