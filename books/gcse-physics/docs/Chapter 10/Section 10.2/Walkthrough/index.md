# 10.2 Red-shift and the Big Bang — Learn

**Prerequisites:** from Chapter 4, that a wave has a wavelength and a frequency;
from Chapter 5, that light is an electromagnetic wave and travels at
$3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$ in a vacuum; from Chapter 3, the equation
$\text{speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$; and writing very large
numbers in standard form.

**By the end of this section you can:** explain the red-shift of light from a
galaxy that is moving away; explain how red-shift is evidence that the universe
is expanding; explain how red-shift supports the Big Bang model; describe another
piece of evidence for the Big Bang; and calculate how long light takes to reach
us from a distant galaxy.

## 1. Light from a galaxy

A **galaxy** is a huge group of stars. The Sun is one star in the Milky Way
galaxy. Many other galaxies are so far away that their light takes millions or
billions of years to reach us.

The light from a galaxy contains a **spectrum**. The spectrum has a continuous
range of wavelengths, crossed by dark lines. Each dark line is at a fixed
wavelength, set by the atoms in the stars of the galaxy. The same atoms always
give the same pattern, so the pattern acts like a fingerprint.

When astronomers compare the pattern from a distant galaxy with the same pattern
made in a laboratory, the whole pattern is moved. It sits at longer wavelengths
than in the laboratory. The pattern has moved towards the red end of the
spectrum. This is called **red-shift**.

## 2. What red-shift shows

Light from a source that is moving away from you has a longer wavelength than
light from the same source at rest. The faster the source moves away, the bigger
the increase in wavelength. A source moving towards you has a shorter
wavelength.

Almost every galaxy shows red-shift. That shows that almost every galaxy is
moving away from us.

> **Watch out.** Red-shift does not mean that the galaxy looks red to the eye.
> The light from the galaxy still contains every wavelength. What moves is the
> pattern of dark lines. Re-check by comparing the position of one dark line in
> the laboratory spectrum with its position in the galaxy spectrum. The line
> moves, not the colour of the whole galaxy.

## 3. The further away, the faster

Astronomers measure the distance of a galaxy and the size of its red-shift. The
size of the red-shift gives the speed at which the galaxy is moving away. The
results follow a clear pattern.

| Galaxy | Distance from the Earth ($10^{23}\,\text{m}$) | Speed away from us (km/s) |
|---|---|---|
| A | 3 | 700 |
| B | 6 | 1400 |
| C | 9 | 2100 |
| D | 12 | 2800 |

The further away a galaxy is, the faster it is moving away. In this table, the
distance doubles and the speed doubles with it.

This pattern is evidence that the universe is expanding. The galaxies are not
spreading out through space. Space itself is expanding, and it carries the
galaxies apart.

## 5. The Big Bang model

The **Big Bang** model says that the universe began from a very small region that
was extremely hot and dense. The universe has been expanding ever since. This
happened about 13.8 billion years ago.

Red-shift is the main evidence for the model. The model predicts that distant
galaxies move away from us, and the red-shift shows that they do.

There is other evidence too. The **cosmic microwave background radiation** is a
faint microwave signal that comes from every direction in space. It is left over
from the hot, dense early universe. Its discovery in 1965 matched a prediction of
the Big Bang model.

Before that, some scientists supported a different model, called the Steady State
model. Both models could explain red-shift. The Steady State model does not
explain the cosmic microwave background radiation, so the Big Bang model became
the accepted one.

## 6. The expansion is speeding up

Since 1998, astronomers have studied very distant supernovae. These explosions
are further away than expected, which suggests that the expansion of the universe
is speeding up.

No one knows what causes this. The unknown cause is called **dark energy**.
**Dark matter** is another unknown. Scientists can measure its pull on galaxies,
but they do not know what it is. There is still much about the universe that is
not understood.

## 7. How scientists use observations

A model is a set of ideas that explains what we see. Scientists test a model by
using it to make predictions. Then they collect observations and check whether
the predictions are right.

A model is only kept while the evidence supports it. If new evidence does not
fit, the model is changed or replaced. This is how the model of the atom changed
from the plum pudding model to the nuclear model. It is also how the Big Bang
model replaced the Steady State model.

**Worked example 1.** Explain what the red-shift of a galaxy shows.

The wavelength of the light from the galaxy is longer than the wavelength of the
same light measured in a laboratory. This shows that the galaxy is moving away
from the Earth. The further away a galaxy is, the faster it is moving away. That
is evidence that the universe is expanding.

**Worked example 2.** Explain how red-shift supports the Big Bang model.

The Big Bang model says that the universe began from a very small, extremely hot
and dense region. The model predicts that the universe has been expanding ever
since. Red-shift shows that the galaxies are moving apart, so the universe is
expanding. This supports the Big Bang model.

**Worked example 3.** A distant galaxy is $2.4 \times 10^{25}\,\text{m}$ from the
Earth. Light travels at $3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$. Calculate how long the
light has taken to reach the Earth.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{d}{v} \\
  &= \frac{2.4 \times 10^{25}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}} \\
  &= 8.0 \times 10^{16}\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The time in seconds is very large. One year is about
$3.15 \times 10^{7}\,\text{s}$, so the time in years is

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{8.0 \times 10^{16}}{3.15 \times 10^{7}} \\
  &= 2.5 \times 10^{9}\,\text{years}
\end{aligned}
$$

The light has travelled for about 2.5 billion years. We see the galaxy as it was
2.5 billion years ago.
