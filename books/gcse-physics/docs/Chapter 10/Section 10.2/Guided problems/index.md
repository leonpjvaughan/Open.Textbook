# 10.2 Red-shift and the Big Bang — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from earlier chapters.

1. Which colour of visible light has the longer wavelength: red or blue?
2. A wave has a frequency of $5.0 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$ and a wavelength of
   $6.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$. Calculate its speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. Red light has the longer wavelength.
2. $v = f\lambda = 5.0 \times 10^{14} \times 6.0 \times 10^{-7} =
   3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$.

If question 1 went wrong, revisit the electromagnetic spectrum in Chapter 5. If
question 2 went wrong, revisit the wave equation in Chapter 4.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Explain what is meant by the red-shift of light from a galaxy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the light from the galaxy with the same light measured in a laboratory.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which way the wavelength changes, then say which part of the spectrum the
dark lines move towards.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The wavelength of the light from the galaxy is longer than the wavelength of the
same light measured in a laboratory. The dark lines in the spectrum of the galaxy
are moved towards the red end, where the wavelengths are longer. This happens
because the galaxy is moving away from the Earth.
</details>

**2.** A dark line in a laboratory spectrum has a wavelength of 500 nm. The same
line in the light from a galaxy has a wavelength of 550 nm. State whether the
galaxy is moving towards the Earth or away from it, and give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the two wavelengths. 550 nm is longer than 500 nm.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the wavelength has increased, so the light has been red-shifted. Then
link the red-shift to the direction of motion.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The wavelength in the galaxy light is 550 nm, which is longer than the laboratory
wavelength of 500 nm. The light is red-shifted. Red-shift is caused by a source
moving away, so the galaxy is moving away from the Earth.
</details>

**3.** Explain how red-shift provides evidence that the universe is expanding.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with the motion of one galaxy. Then add the pattern that appears when you
compare many galaxies at different distances.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the galaxies are moving away from us, and that the further away a galaxy
is, the faster it moves away. Then say what that pattern means for space itself.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The red-shift shows that most galaxies are moving away from us. The further away
a galaxy is, the faster it is moving away. This pattern is what we expect if
space itself is expanding. As space grows, it carries the galaxies apart, so the
universe is expanding.
</details>

**4.** Explain how red-shift supports the Big Bang model.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

State what the model says about the start of the universe.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what the model predicts about the universe since it began, then link the
prediction to what the red-shift shows.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The Big Bang model says that the universe began from a very small region that
was extremely hot and dense. The model predicts that the universe has been
expanding ever since. Red-shift shows that the galaxies are moving apart, so the
universe is expanding. This matches the prediction, so red-shift supports the
model.
</details>

**5.** Describe the cosmic microwave background radiation and explain what it
shows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

It is a signal, not a substance. Say where in space it comes from and which part
of the electromagnetic spectrum it belongs to.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that it comes from every direction. Then say what it is left over from, and
which model that supports.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The cosmic microwave background radiation is a faint microwave signal that comes
from every direction in space. It is left over from the hot, dense early
universe. This matches a prediction of the Big Bang model, so it is evidence that
supports the model.
</details>

**6.** The Andromeda galaxy is $2.4 \times 10^{22}\,\text{m}$ from the Earth.
Light travels at $3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$. Calculate how long light takes
to travel from Andromeda to the Earth. Give your answer in seconds, and then in
years. Take one year as $3.15 \times 10^{7}\,\text{s}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the same equation as for any other journey: time is distance divided by
speed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the time in seconds first. Then divide that time by the number of
seconds in a year.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{d}{v} \\
  &= \frac{2.4 \times 10^{22}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}} \\
  &= 8.0 \times 10^{13}\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{8.0 \times 10^{13}}{3.15 \times 10^{7}} \\
  &= 2.5 \times 10^{6}\,\text{years}
\end{aligned}
$$

The light has taken 2.5 million years to reach the Earth.
</details>

**7.** Explain why looking at a very distant galaxy is like looking back in time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the journey the light has made, and how long that journey took.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say when the light that we see now set out from the galaxy. Then say which
version of the galaxy we see.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Light takes a very long time to travel from a very distant galaxy, because the
distance is enormous. The light that we see now set out from the galaxy billions
of years ago. So we see the galaxy as it was then, not as it is now.
</details>

**8.** Two models of the universe were once considered. Explain why the Big Bang
model is the one accepted today.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Both models can explain red-shift. Look for the evidence that only one of them
can explain.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what both models explain, then say which piece of evidence the Steady State
model does not explain.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both the Steady State model and the Big Bang model can explain the red-shift of
the galaxies. The Steady State model does not explain the cosmic microwave
background radiation. That radiation fits a hot, dense beginning, so more
evidence supports the Big Bang model. It is the accepted model today.
</details>

**9.** Since 1998, observations of distant supernovae suggest that the expansion
of the universe is speeding up. Explain why scientists say that the universe is
still not fully understood.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about whether the cause of the speeding up is known.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Describe what the supernova observations show, then name the two things that are
not understood.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The distant supernovae are further away than expected, so the expansion of the
universe seems to be getting faster. The cause of the speeding up is not known.
It is called dark energy. Dark matter is also not understood, so there is still
much about the universe that is not known.
</details>
