# 10.1 The solar system and orbits — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory. They use ideas from earlier chapters.

1. State the speed of light in a vacuum.
2. A satellite travels $1.2 \times 10^{4}\,\text{m}$ in 4.0 s. Calculate its
   average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$.
2. $v = \dfrac{d}{t} = \dfrac{1.2 \times 10^{4}}{4.0} = 3.0 \times 10^{3}\,\text{m/s}$.

If question 1 went wrong, it is worth remembering: light and all other
electromagnetic waves travel at $3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$ in a vacuum. If
question 2 went wrong, revisit the speed equation in Chapter 3.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** State the main features of our solar system.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with the star at the centre. Then add the bodies that orbit the Sun, and
the bodies that orbit the planets.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write a list with one line for each kind of body: star, planet, dwarf planet,
moon, artificial satellite, asteroid and comet.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The solar system has one star, the Sun. Eight planets orbit the Sun, and dwarf
planets orbit it too. Moons, which are natural satellites, orbit the planets.
Artificial satellites built by people orbit the Earth and other bodies.
Asteroids and comets orbit the Sun. The whole solar system is a small part of the
Milky Way galaxy.
</details>

**2.** Give one similarity and one distinction between a planet and a moon.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask what each body orbits, and whether it was made by people or by nature.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write two short sentences. The first says what the two bodies have in common.
The second says one clear way in which they are different.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A planet orbits a star, and a moon orbits a planet. They are similar because
gravity keeps both of them in orbit. They are different because a planet is
large and orbits the Sun, while a moon is smaller and orbits a planet.
</details>

**3.** Explain what keeps a planet in a circular orbit around the Sun.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Name the force. Then say which way it points.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say that the force points towards the Sun, the centre of the orbit. Then say what
the force does to the direction of the planet.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Gravity provides a force of attraction between the planet and the Sun. The force
is directed towards the Sun, which is the centre of the orbit. This force changes
the direction of the planet, so its path curves into a circle instead of going in
a straight line.
</details>

**4.** The Moon is $3.84 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m}$ from the Earth. Light travels
at $3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$. Calculate how long light takes to travel from
the Moon to the Earth.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The light travels at a steady speed. Write down the equation that links time,
distance and speed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $t = \dfrac{d}{v}$. Put the distance over the speed, keeping the powers of
ten together.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{d}{v} \\
  &= \frac{3.84 \times 10^{8}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}} \\
  &= 1.28\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

Light takes 1.28 s to travel from the Moon to the Earth.
</details>

**5.** Jupiter is $7.8 \times 10^{11}\,\text{m}$ from the Sun. The Earth is
$1.5 \times 10^{11}\,\text{m}$ from the Sun. Work out how many times further from
the Sun Jupiter is than the Earth.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

To compare two distances, divide the larger by the smaller.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the distance of Jupiter by the distance of the Earth. The powers of ten
are the same, so they cancel.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$ \frac{7.8 \times 10^{11}}{1.5 \times 10^{11}} = 5.2 $$

Jupiter is 5.2 times further from the Sun than the Earth is.
</details>

**6.** Explain why the Sun stays the same size.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are two opposing effects. One comes from fusion and one comes from gravity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Describe what fusion does to the size of the Sun, then describe what gravity does
to it. Finish by comparing the sizes of the two effects.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Fusion reactions in the core release energy, which pushes outwards and tends to
make the Sun expand. Gravity pulls the material of the Sun inwards and tends to
make it collapse. The outward push and the inward pull are equal, so the Sun is
in equilibrium and stays the same size.
</details>

**7.** Describe the life cycle of a star that has about the same mass as the Sun.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with the cloud of dust and gas. Then follow the order of the stages.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name four stages in order: the cloud, the main-sequence star, the red giant and
the white dwarf.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The star forms from a cloud of dust and gas pulled together by gravity. Fusion
starts, and the star becomes a main-sequence star like the Sun. Later the star
expands into a red giant. It then cools and collapses into a white dwarf.
</details>

**8.** A satellite moves at a constant speed in a circular orbit, but its
velocity changes. Explain how this is possible.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Recall what the words speed and velocity each mean. One of them includes a
direction.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

State that velocity is speed in a given direction. Then describe what happens to
the direction as the satellite goes round the circle.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Velocity is speed in a given direction. As the satellite goes round its circular
orbit, the direction of its motion changes all the time. Its speed stays the
same, but the changing direction means that its velocity changes.
</details>

**9.** For a stable orbit, explain why the radius must change if the speed of the
satellite changes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A stable circular orbit is a match between two quantities: the speed and the
radius.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what a stable orbit needs. Then say what happens if the speed changes while
the radius stays the same.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A stable circular orbit needs a speed that matches its radius. If the speed
changes, the satellite can no longer stay in a stable orbit at the same radius.
The radius must change until the speed and the radius match again, so the orbit
becomes stable at the new radius.
</details>
