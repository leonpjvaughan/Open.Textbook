# Chapter 10: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The telescope orbits the Earth once every 95 minutes, about 540 km above the
ground. Radio signals from it travel at $3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$. It
photographs star-forming clouds and distant galaxies whose light is red-shifted.

**1. What keeps the telescope in orbit.** Gravity provides a force of attraction
between the telescope and the Earth. The force is directed towards the centre of
the Earth. This force changes the direction of the telescope all the time, so its
path curves into a circle. Without gravity, the telescope would travel in a
straight line away into space.

**2. Why the telescope does not fall.** The telescope is moving sideways as it
falls. The pull of gravity and its sideways motion together keep it falling
around the Earth instead of into it. It is never above gravity: gravity acts
between all masses, so the Earth pulls on the telescope even at that height. The
pull is weaker than at the ground, but it is not zero.

<!-- tier:h -->
**3. Velocity in the orbit.** The telescope moves at a constant speed, but its
direction changes all the time. Velocity is speed in a given direction, so the
velocity changes even though the speed does not. A stable orbit needs a speed
that matches its radius. If the speed changed, the radius would have to change
until the two matched again.
<!-- /tier:h -->

**4. The time for a radio signal to reach the ground.** The height is 540 km,
which is $5.4 \times 10^{5}\,\text{m}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{d}{v} \\
  &= \frac{5.4 \times 10^{5}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}} \\
  &= 1.8 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The signal takes $1.8 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{s}$, or 1.8 ms, to reach the ground.

**5. Why a star such as the Sun is stable.** Fusion reactions in the core release
energy, which pushes outwards and tends to make the star expand. Gravity pulls
the material inwards and tends to make it collapse. The outward push and the
inward pull are equal, so the star is in equilibrium. It keeps the same size and
shines at a steady rate.

**6. The life cycle of a star the size of the Sun.** It forms from a cloud of
dust and gas pulled together by gravity. Fusion starts, and the star becomes a
main-sequence star. Later it expands into a red giant, then cools and collapses
into a white dwarf. Fusion in stars makes the elements up to iron. Elements
heavier than iron are made in a supernova.

**7. The red-shift of the distant galaxy.** The light from the galaxy has a
longer wavelength than the same light made in a laboratory. The dark lines in its
spectrum are moved towards the red end. This shows that the galaxy is moving away
from us. The further away a galaxy is, the faster it is moving away, which is
evidence that the universe is expanding.

**8. How long the light has travelled.** The galaxy is
$2.4 \times 10^{25}\,\text{m}$ from the Earth.

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{d}{v} \\
  &= \frac{2.4 \times 10^{25}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}} \\
  &= 8.0 \times 10^{16}\,\text{s}
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{8.0 \times 10^{16}}{3.15 \times 10^{7}} \\
  &= 2.5 \times 10^{9}\,\text{years}
\end{aligned}
$$

The light has travelled for about 2.5 billion years. We see the galaxy as it was
2.5 billion years ago, not as it is now.

**9. How red-shift supports the Big Bang model.** The Big Bang model says the
universe began from a very small region that was extremely hot and dense, about
13.8 billion years ago, and has been expanding ever since. Red-shift shows that
the universe is expanding, so the observations match the model. The cosmic
microwave background radiation is further evidence: it is left over from the hot,
dense early universe.

**10. What is still not understood.** Since 1998, distant supernovae have
suggested that the expansion of the universe is speeding up. The cause is not
known. It is called dark energy, and dark matter is also not understood. This
final judgement is a reasoned answer. A computer cannot check it, so compare your
reasoning with the evidence above.
