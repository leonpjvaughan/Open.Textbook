# 3.2 Forces, acceleration and Newton's laws of motion — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A bus speeds up from 10 m/s to 16 m/s in 3.0 s. Calculate its acceleration.
2. A force of 30 N stretches a spring by 0.15 m. Calculate the spring constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{t} = \dfrac{16 - 10}{3.0} = \dfrac{6.0}{3.0} = 2.0\,\text{m/s}^2$.
2. $k = \dfrac{F}{e} = \dfrac{30}{0.15} = 200\,\text{N/m}$.

If question 1 went wrong, remember that acceleration uses the **change** in
velocity. If question 2 went wrong, look again at Section 2.2.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A car of mass 1500 kg accelerates at $3.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. Calculate the
resultant force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Newton's second law links the resultant force, the mass and the acceleration.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $F = ma$, then multiply 1500 kg by $3.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. The mass is already
in kilograms.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= m a \\
  &= 1500 \times 3.0 \\
  &= 4500\,\text{N} \\
  &= 4.5 \times 10^3\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A resultant force of 90 N gives an object an acceleration of
$6.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. Calculate the mass of the object.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $F = ma$ to make the mass the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the force by the acceleration: $m = \dfrac{F}{a}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
m &= \frac{F}{a} \\
  &= \frac{90}{6.0} \\
  &= 15\,\text{kg}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A resultant force of 200 N acts on a trolley of mass 50 kg. Calculate the
acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The same equation gives the acceleration when you divide the force by the mass.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $a = \dfrac{F}{m}$, then divide 200 N by 50 kg.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{F}{m} \\
  &= \frac{200}{50} \\
  &= 4.0\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A car travels along a straight road at a steady 15 m/s. Explain why the
resultant force on the car is zero, and what would happen if the driving force
were larger than the resistive forces.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Newton's first law. Think about the driving force and the resistive forces.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what the resultant force is when the two forces are equal, then say what a
non-zero resultant force does to the velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The engine gives a driving force forwards, and friction and air resistance give
resistive forces backwards. At a steady speed the two are equal and opposite, so
the resultant force is zero. Newton's first law says that an object with a zero
resultant force keeps a constant velocity. If the driving force were larger, the
resultant force would be forwards, so the car would accelerate. This answer is a
model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**5.** A swimmer pushes backwards against the water. Use Newton's third law to
explain why the swimmer moves forwards.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Newton's third law is about two objects interacting. Name both objects.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which force the water exerts on the swimmer, and compare its size with the
force from the swimmer.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The swimmer pushes backwards on the water, and the water pushes forwards on the
swimmer. By Newton's third law the two forces are equal in size and opposite in
direction, and they act on different objects. The forwards force from the water
is the force that moves the swimmer forwards. This answer is a model answer for
you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** Describe how to investigate the effect of the force on the acceleration of
a trolley of constant mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Name the equipment that measures the velocity, and state what you keep the same
and what you change.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say how you obtain the acceleration from the measurements, and say how you make
the force larger.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Set up a trolley on a runway with a string over a pulley to a hanging mass. The
weight of the hanging mass is the force on the trolley. Measure the velocity with
light gates or a motion sensor, and find the acceleration from a velocity-time
graph. Keep the mass of the trolley constant, increase the hanging mass to
increase the force, and record the acceleration each time. Plot a graph of
acceleration against force. This answer is a model answer for you to compare with
your own wording.
</details>
