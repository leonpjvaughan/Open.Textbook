# 3.4 Momentum — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A resultant force of 500 N acts on a mass of 200 kg. Calculate the
   acceleration.
2. A car slows from 20 m/s to 5.0 m/s in 3.0 s. Calculate its acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{500}{200} = 2.5\,\text{m/s}^2$.
2. $a = \dfrac{\Delta v}{t} = \dfrac{5.0 - 20}{3.0} = -5.0\,\text{m/s}^2$, so the
   deceleration is $5.0\,\text{m/s}^2$.

If question 2 went wrong, remember that a decrease in velocity gives a negative
acceleration.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A ball of mass 0.15 kg moves at 20 m/s. Calculate its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Momentum is the mass multiplied by the velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $p = mv$, then multiply 0.15 kg by 20 m/s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= m v \\
  &= 0.15 \times 20 \\
  &= 3.0\,\text{kg m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A lorry of mass 8000 kg travels at 25 m/s. Calculate its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The mass is already in kilograms and the velocity is in metres per second, so
you can multiply them.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $p = mv$, then work out $8000 \times 25$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
p &= m v \\
  &= 8000 \times 25 \\
  &= 200000\,\text{kg m/s} \\
  &= 2.0 \times 10^5\,\text{kg m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**3.** A trolley of mass 2.0 kg moves at 3.0 m/s and hits a stationary trolley of
mass 1.0 kg. The two trolleys stick together. Calculate their common velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after it. The
stationary trolley adds no momentum before the collision.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the momentum before, $2.0 \times 3.0$, then divide by the combined mass
of 3.0 kg.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
2.0 \times 3.0 &= (2.0 + 1.0) \times v \\
6.0 &= 3.0 v \\
v &= 2.0\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**4.** A car of mass 1000 kg travels at 20 m/s and stops in 0.25 s. Calculate the
force on the car.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The force equals the rate of change of momentum. The change in velocity is from
20 m/s to zero.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $F = \dfrac{m\Delta v}{\Delta t}$, then use $m = 1000$, $\Delta v = 20$ and
$\Delta t = 0.25$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= \frac{m \Delta v}{\Delta t} \\
  &= \frac{1000 \times 20}{0.25} \\
  &= \frac{20000}{0.25} \\
  &= 80000\,\text{N} \\
  &= 8.0 \times 10^4\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**5.** Explain how a seat belt reduces the chance of injury in a crash.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the change in momentum of the passenger, and about the time it takes.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the time taken for the passenger to stop, and what that does
to the force.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The passenger has a fixed change in momentum, set by the mass and the speed. A
seat belt stretches a little during the crash, so the passenger takes a longer
time to stop. A longer time means a smaller force, because
$F = \dfrac{m \Delta v}{\Delta t}$. The belt also spreads the force over a larger
area of the body. Both effects reduce the chance of injury. This answer is a
model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** Two skaters stand still and push against each other. The skater of mass
50 kg moves to the left at 1.2 m/s. Calculate the velocity of the skater of mass
75 kg.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Before the push, both skaters are stationary, so the total momentum is zero.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

After the push the total momentum is still zero, so the momenta of the two
skaters are equal in size and opposite in direction. Divide the momentum of the
first skater by 75 kg.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The momentum of the first skater is

$$ p = m v = 50 \times 1.2 = 60\,\text{kg m/s} \text{ to the left} $$

The total momentum must stay zero, so the second skater has $60\,\text{kg m/s}$
to the right.

$$
\begin{aligned}
75 \times v &= 60 \\
v &= \frac{60}{75} \\
  &= 0.80\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The skater of mass 75 kg moves to the right at 0.80 m/s.
</details>
