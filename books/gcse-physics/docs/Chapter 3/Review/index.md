# Chapter 3: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The bus and its passengers have a mass of 12000 kg. The bus travels at 12 m/s.
The driver's reaction time is 0.60 s. Take the dry braking distance as 9.0 m and
the wet braking distance as 14 m.

**1. The acceleration from rest to 12 m/s in 8.0 s.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{\Delta v}{t} \\
  &= \frac{12 - 0}{8.0} \\
  &= 1.5\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

**2. The resultant force that gives this acceleration.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= m a \\
  &= 12000 \times 1.5 \\
  &= 18000\,\text{N} \\
  &= 1.8 \times 10^4\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

**3. The thinking distance.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= v t \\
  &= 12 \times 0.60 \\
  &= 7.2\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

**4. The stopping distance on a dry road.**

$$ \text{stopping distance} = 7.2 + 9.0 = 16.2\,\text{m} $$

The hazard is 20 m ahead. The bus stops after 16.2 m, so it stops before the
hazard with 3.8 m to spare.

**5. The deceleration while braking on a dry road.** The bus slows from 12 m/s to
rest in 9.0 m.

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^2 - u^2 &= 2 a s \\
0^2 - 12^2 &= 2 \times a \times 9.0 \\
-144 &= 18 a \\
a &= -8.0\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

A deceleration of $8.0\,\text{m/s}^2$ is large. It is close to the acceleration of
a falling object, which is why the brakes become hot and why the passengers need
restraints.

**6. The stopping distance on a wet road.** The braking distance is 14 m.

$$ \text{stopping distance} = 7.2 + 14 = 21.2\,\text{m} $$

The hazard is 20 m ahead, so on a wet road the bus does **not** stop in time. It
is still moving when it reaches the hazard.

<!-- tier:h -->
**7. The braking force on a dry road.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
F &= m a \\
  &= 12000 \times 8.0 \\
  &= 96000\,\text{N} \\
  &= 9.6 \times 10^4\,\text{N}
\end{aligned}
$$

This is the force that produces the large deceleration of $8.0\,\text{m/s}^2$.

**8. The collision with the stationary van.** The bus has a momentum of
$12000 \times 12 = 144000\,\text{kg m/s}$. After the collision the bus and the
van move together.

$$
\begin{aligned}
144000 &= (12000 + 8000) \times v \\
144000 &= 20000 v \\
v &= 7.2\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

The two vehicles move off together at 7.2 m/s.
<!-- /tier:h -->

The judgement that the bus is safe on a dry road but not on a wet road is a
reasoned answer. It follows from the numbers above. It is not the kind of claim a
computer can check, so compare your reasoning with the evidence in the working.

## What you can now do

- tell distance from displacement, and speed from velocity, and recall typical
  speeds;
- calculate speed, distance, time and acceleration;
- read speed and acceleration from motion graphs, and find the distance from the
  area under a velocity-time graph (Higher tier);
- use $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$ and describe terminal velocity;
- apply Newton's three laws of motion and use $F = ma$;
- work out thinking distance, braking distance and stopping distance, and
  explain what affects each one;
- explain the dangers of large decelerations and estimate the force involved
  (Higher tier);
- calculate momentum, use conservation of momentum, and explain how safety
  features reduce the force in a crash (Higher tier).
