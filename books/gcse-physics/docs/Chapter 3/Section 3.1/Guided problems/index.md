# 3.1 Speed, velocity and motion graphs — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Two forces act on a trolley: 45 N forwards and 20 N backwards. Calculate the
   resultant force.
2. Calculate the energy in the kinetic store of a 3.0 kg object moving at
   4.0 m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. The forces act in opposite directions, so subtract:
   $45 - 20 = 25\,\text{N}$ forwards.
2. $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.5 \times 3.0 \times 4.0^2 = 24\,\text{J}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at Section 2.1: forces in opposite
directions subtract. If question 2 went wrong, remember to square the speed
before you multiply.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A train travels 450 m in 30 s. Calculate its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average speed is the total distance divided by the total time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $v = \dfrac{s}{t}$, then divide 450 m by 30 s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v &= \frac{s}{t} \\
  &= \frac{450}{30} \\
  &= 15\,\text{m/s}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**2.** A walker moves at a steady 1.4 m/s for 300 s. Calculate the distance she
travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The distance travelled is the speed multiplied by the time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $s = vt$, then multiply 1.4 m/s by 300 s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= v t \\
  &= 1.4 \times 300 \\
  &= 420\,\text{m} \\
  &= 4.2 \times 10^2\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**3.** A car speeds up from 6.0 m/s to 18 m/s in 4.0 s. Calculate its
acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Acceleration uses the **change** in velocity, not the final velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out the change first: $18 - 6.0$. Then divide by 4.0 s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{\Delta v}{t} \\
  &= \frac{18 - 6.0}{4.0} \\
  &= \frac{12}{4.0} \\
  &= 3.0\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**4.** A velocity-time graph is a straight line through $(0\,\text{s}, 0\,\text{m/s})$,
$(4.0\,\text{s}, 8.0\,\text{m/s})$ and $(8.0\,\text{s}, 16\,\text{m/s})$. Calculate
the acceleration from the gradient.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The acceleration is the gradient of a velocity-time graph.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the two end points: change in velocity is $16 - 0$, and change in time is
$8.0 - 0$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
a &= \frac{\text{change in velocity}}{\text{change in time}} \\
  &= \frac{16 - 0}{8.0 - 0} \\
  &= 2.0\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**5.** A van travels at 16 m/s. The driver brakes with a steady deceleration of
$4.0\,\text{m/s}^2$ until the van stops. Calculate the distance the van travels
while braking.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The van starts at 16 m/s and ends at rest, so the final velocity is zero. The
acceleration is negative, because the van slows down.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$, then put in $v = 0$, $u = 16$ and $a = -4.0$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^2 - u^2 &= 2 a s \\
0^2 - 16^2 &= 2 \times (-4.0) \times s \\
-256 &= -8.0 s \\
s &= \frac{256}{8.0} \\
  &= 32\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**6.** A skydiver jumps from a plane. Explain, in terms of forces, why the
skydiver reaches a terminal velocity, and why the velocity then stays constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One force pulls the skydiver down, and another force from the air pushes up. Say
what happens to the upward force as the skydiver gets faster.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what the resultant force is when the two forces become equal, and what a
zero resultant force does to the velocity.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At first the weight of the skydiver is larger than the drag force from the air,
so the resultant force is downwards and the skydiver accelerates. As the
skydiver gets faster, the drag force increases. When the drag force equals the
weight, the resultant force is zero. A zero resultant force means the velocity
stops changing, so the skydiver falls at a steady terminal velocity. This answer
is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>
