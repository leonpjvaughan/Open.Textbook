# 3.3 Safety in public transport — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A car of mass 800 kg accelerates at $2.5\,\text{m/s}^2$. Calculate the
   resultant force.
2. A cyclist travels 150 m in 20 s. Calculate her average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $F = ma = 800 \times 2.5 = 2000\,\text{N} = 2.0 \times 10^3\,\text{N}$.
2. $v = \dfrac{s}{t} = \dfrac{150}{20} = 7.5\,\text{m/s}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at Section 3.2: multiply the mass by the
acceleration. If question 2 went wrong, divide the distance by the time.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A car travels at 18 m/s. The driver has a reaction time of 0.50 s.
Calculate the thinking distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

During the reaction time the car keeps moving at its original speed.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply the speed by the reaction time: $s = vt$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
s &= v t \\
  &= 18 \times 0.50 \\
  &= 9.0\,\text{m}
\end{aligned}
$$
</details>

**2.** A car travels at 20 m/s. The driver's reaction time is 0.60 s and the
braking distance is 35 m. Calculate the thinking distance and the stopping
distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The stopping distance is the thinking distance plus the braking distance. Find
the thinking distance first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $20 \times 0.60$ for the thinking distance, then add the 35 m braking
distance.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Thinking distance:

$$ s = v t = 20 \times 0.60 = 12\,\text{m} $$

Stopping distance:

$$ \text{stopping distance} = 12 + 35 = 47\,\text{m} $$
</details>

**3.** A car travels at 12 m/s and stops in a distance of 18 m. Calculate the
deceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The car ends at rest, so the final velocity is zero.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$, then put in $u = 12$, $v = 0$ and $s = 18$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^2 - u^2 &= 2 a s \\
0^2 - 12^2 &= 2 \times a \times 18 \\
-144 &= 36 a \\
a &= \frac{-144}{36} \\
  &= -4.0\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

The deceleration is $4.0\,\text{m/s}^2$.
</details>

**4.** A car of mass 900 kg travels at 18 m/s and stops in a distance of 27 m.
Calculate the deceleration and the braking force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the deceleration first, then use $F = ma$ with the mass in kilograms.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v^2 - u^2 = 2as$ with $u = 18$, $v = 0$ and $s = 27$. Then multiply the
mass by the size of the deceleration.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
v^2 - u^2 &= 2 a s \\
0^2 - 18^2 &= 2 \times a \times 27 \\
-324 &= 54 a \\
a &= -6.0\,\text{m/s}^2
\end{aligned}
$$

The deceleration is $6.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. The braking force is

$$ F = m a = 900 \times 6.0 = 5400\,\text{N} = 5.4 \times 10^3\,\text{N} $$
</details>
<!-- /tier:h -->

**5.** Explain why a wet road increases the braking distance of a car.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the grip between the tyres and the road, and about the braking force
the tyres can transmit.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say what happens to the deceleration when the grip is reduced, and what a smaller
deceleration means for the distance travelled while stopping.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

On a wet road there is less grip between the tyres and the road, so the tyres can
transmit a smaller braking force. A smaller braking force gives a smaller
deceleration. The car takes longer to stop, and it travels further while it is
stopping, so the braking distance is longer. This answer is a model answer for
you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** Explain why a large deceleration is dangerous for the brakes and for the
control of a vehicle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about where the kinetic energy goes, and about what happens if the braking
force is larger than the grip of the tyres.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the store that receives the energy, and say what happens to the wheels if
they lock.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A large deceleration transfers a large amount of kinetic energy to the thermal
store of the brakes in a short time, so the brakes may overheat and work less
well. If the braking force is too large for the grip of the tyres, the wheels
lock and the vehicle skids. A skidding vehicle cannot be steered, so the driver
loses control. This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own
wording.
</details>
