# 1.2 Conservation, dissipation and energy resources — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. A lamp transfers 2000 J of energy in 50 s. Calculate its power.
2. Name the store that increases when a kettle heats water.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $P = \dfrac{E}{t} = \dfrac{2000}{50} = 40\,\text{W}$.
2. The thermal store of the water and the kettle.

If question 1 went wrong, go back to Section 1.1 and the equation $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$.
</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A ball has 30 J of energy in its gravitational potential store at the top
of a drop. Air resistance is ignored. Calculate the energy in its kinetic store
just before it lands.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The drop has no outside energy going in or out, so the total energy stored does
not change.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The gravitational potential store empties by exactly the amount the kinetic store
fills.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Energy is conserved, so the kinetic store gains the 30 J that the gravitational
potential store loses.

$$ E_k = 30\,\text{J} $$

</details>

**2.** In a real room, the ball from question 1 lands with 26 J in its kinetic
store. Calculate the energy dissipated, and name the store that receives it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The missing energy has not gone away. It has moved somewhere else.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Subtract the kinetic energy at the bottom from the energy at the top.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\text{dissipated} = 30 - 26 = 4\,\text{J}
$$

The 4 J is transferred to the thermal store of the ball and the air. Air
resistance is the cause.
</details>

**3.** A motor takes in 600 J of energy and transfers 450 J usefully. Calculate
its efficiency as a percentage.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Efficiency compares the useful output with the total input, so use 600 J, not
150 J.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\dfrac{450}{600}$, then multiply by 100 to turn the decimal into a
percentage.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{efficiency} &= \frac{450}{600} \\
                  &= 0.75 \\
                  &= 75\%
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**4.** A machine is 25% efficient and its useful output is 150 J. Calculate the
total input.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write 25% as 0.25 before you use the equation.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Rearrange $\text{efficiency} = \dfrac{\text{useful output}}{\text{total input}}$
to make the total input the subject.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
0.25 &= \frac{150}{\text{total input}} \\
\text{total input} &= \frac{150}{0.25} \\
                   &= 600\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**5.** A hot drink is poured into a thin plastic cup. The same drink in a thick
insulated cup cools more slowly. Explain why.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the two cups by the thickness and the thermal conductivity of the
material between the drink and the air.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Say which cup transfers energy to the surroundings faster, and why.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The thin plastic cup lets energy transfer from the hot drink to the cooler
surroundings quickly. The thick insulated cup has a layer of trapped air, which
has a low thermal conductivity. The thicker layer and the lower thermal
conductivity both slow the rate of energy transfer, so the drink cools more
slowly. This answer is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** A wind turbine removes $4.0 \times 10^6\,\text{J}$ of energy from the
kinetic store of the air every second. Its useful electrical output power is
1.6 MW. Calculate the efficiency of the turbine, and give one reason why the
efficiency is not 100%.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$4.0 \times 10^6\,\text{J}$ each second is a power of $4.0 \times 10^6\,\text{W}$,
which is 4.0 MW.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide the useful output power by the total input power.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{efficiency} &= \frac{1.6}{4.0} \\
                  &= 0.40 \\
                  &= 40\%
\end{aligned}
$$

The air still leaves the turbine with kinetic energy, and some energy is
transferred by friction in the moving parts to the thermal store of the
surroundings. That energy is dissipated, so it does not reach the generator as
useful electrical energy.
</details>
