# Chapter 1: Energy

## The chapter problem: the hillside field centre

A field centre stands beside a stream in the hills. The stream drops steeply and
turns a water wheel. The wheel drives a generator, and the generator makes
electricity for the centre.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- The stream brings **30 kg of water to the wheel each second**.
- The water falls **12 m** from the top of the drop to the wheel.
- The useful output power of the generator is **2.1 kW**.
- Each kettle holds **1.5 kg of water**, heated through **80 °C**.
- The specific heat capacity of water is **4200 J/kg°C**.

By the end of the chapter you will work out how much energy the falling water
gives up each second. You will find the greatest power the wheel could produce,
the efficiency of the generator, and the time the kettle needs. You will also
decide whether the stream is a sound energy resource for the centre.

> **First contact: no working yet.** Estimate. The water gives up about
> $30 \times 10 \times 12$ joules each second. The generator makes 2100 joules
> each second. Is the generator close to the energy in the falling water, or far
> below it?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>If you are not sure where to start</summary>

The falling water gives up energy from its gravitational potential store. Some of
that energy is transferred to the surroundings on the way. So the generator makes
less than the water gives up.
</details>

## By the end of this chapter

You will be able to:

- name the energy stores, and describe how energy is transferred between them;
- calculate the energy in an object's kinetic, gravitational potential and
  elastic potential stores;
- calculate the energy transferred when a substance is heated;
- calculate power, and use it to find an energy transfer or a time;
- state the principle of conservation of energy for a closed system;
- explain dissipation, and describe how to reduce unwanted energy transfer;
- calculate the efficiency of an energy transfer;
- compare renewable and non-renewable energy resources.

## Where it is used

Every energy bill, every choice of appliance and every decision about insulation
uses these ideas. So does the choice between a wind farm, a gas power station and
a hydro scheme. The same numbers decide how much a journey costs and how much
carbon dioxide it adds to the air.
