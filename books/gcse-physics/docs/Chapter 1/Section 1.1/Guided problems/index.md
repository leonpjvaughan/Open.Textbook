# 1.1 Energy stores and transfers — Practise with help

Work each problem first. Open a hint only if you are stuck, and the full working
only to check.

## Warm-up: remember

1. Work out $0.5 \times 1200 \times 15^2$.
2. Write 2500 in standard form.
3. Convert 500 g to kilograms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers</summary>

1. $0.5 \times 1200 \times 225 = 135000$.
2. $2.5 \times 10^3$.
3. $0.50\,\text{kg}$.

If question 1 went wrong, look again at the order of operations: work out the
power before you multiply. If question 3 went wrong, remember that there are
1000 g in 1 kg, so you divide by 1000.
</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A climber of mass 75 kg reaches a ledge 8.0 m above the ground. Take
$g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$. Calculate the energy gained by the gravitational
potential store.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The store that fills as the climber rises is the gravitational potential store.
Which equation has mass, gravitational field strength and height in it?
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E_p = m g h$ and put in $m = 75\,\text{kg}$, $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$ and
$h = 8.0\,\text{m}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_p &= m g h \\
    &= 75 \times 9.8 \times 8.0 \\
    &= 5880\,\text{J} \\
    &= 5900\,\text{J} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**2.** A ball of mass 0.16 kg is kicked at 20 m/s. Calculate the energy in its
kinetic store.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed enters the kinetic energy equation as a square. Work out the square
first, then multiply.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2$, then replace $v^2$ with $20^2 = 400$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_k &= \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2 \\
    &= 0.5 \times 0.16 \times 20^2 \\
    &= 0.5 \times 0.16 \times 400 \\
    &= 32\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**3.** A block of copper of mass 1.2 kg is heated from 20 °C to 70 °C. The
specific heat capacity of copper is 390 J/kg°C. Calculate the energy transferred
to the block.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The symbol $\Delta\theta$ means the change in temperature, not the final
temperature. Find the change first.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $\Delta\theta = 70 - 20 = 50\,^\circ\text{C}$, then substitute into
$\Delta E = m c \Delta\theta$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E &= m c \Delta\theta \\
         &= 1.2 \times 390 \times 50 \\
         &= 23400\,\text{J} \\
         &= 2.3 \times 10^4\,\text{J} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**4.** A motor transfers 18000 J in 12 s. Calculate its power.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Power is the energy transferred each second, so it needs the energy and the time.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $P = \dfrac{E}{t}$ and divide 18000 J by 12 s.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$
\begin{aligned}
P &= \frac{E}{t} \\
  &= \frac{18000}{12} \\
  &= 1500\,\text{W}
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>

**5.** A ball is dropped and bounces back to a lower height each time. Explain,
in terms of energy stores, why it never returns to its starting height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what happens at the moment the ball hits the floor, and while it is
moving through the air.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the store that receives the energy that the ball loses on each bounce.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

As the ball falls, energy moves from the gravitational potential store to the
kinetic store. When the ball hits the floor, some of the kinetic energy is
transferred to the thermal store of the ball, the floor and the air. Air
resistance transfers more energy to the thermal store of the air on every trip.
The energy in the gravitational potential store at the top of each bounce is
therefore smaller than before, so the ball reaches a lower height. This answer
is a model answer for you to compare with your own wording.
</details>

**6.** A car of mass 1000 kg is travelling at 12 m/s. The driver brakes until the
car stops. Calculate the energy transferred to the thermal store of the brakes,
and name the store that decreased.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The car loses one store of energy as it slows down. Identify that store before
you calculate.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use the kinetic energy equation with $m = 1000\,\text{kg}$ and $v = 12\,\text{m/s}$.
</details>

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The kinetic store decreases. Its energy is transferred to the thermal store of
the brakes.

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_k &= \tfrac{1}{2} m v^2 \\
    &= 0.5 \times 1000 \times 12^2 \\
    &= 0.5 \times 1000 \times 144 \\
    &= 72000\,\text{J} \\
    &= 7.2 \times 10^4\,\text{J} \quad (2\ \text{significant figures})
\end{aligned}
$$

</details>
