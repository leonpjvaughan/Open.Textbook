# Chapter 1: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

The field centre stream brings 30 kg of water to the wheel each second, and the
water falls 12 m. Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

**1. The energy the water gives up each second.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
E_p &= m g h \\
    &= 30 \times 9.8 \times 12 \\
    &= 3528\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

The water gives up 3528 J each second, so its gravitational potential store
empties at a rate of 3528 J/s.

**2. The greatest power the wheel could produce.** Power is the rate of energy
transfer, so if every joule reached the generator,

$$ P = 3528\,\text{W} \approx 3.5\,\text{kW} $$

Real machines cannot reach this value, because some energy is always dissipated.

**3. The efficiency of the generator.** The useful output is 2.1 kW, which is
2100 W.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{efficiency} &= \frac{2100}{3528} \\
                  &= 0.60 \\
                  &= 60\%
\end{aligned}
$$

**4. The energy the kettle needs.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta E &= m c \Delta\theta \\
         &= 1.5 \times 4200 \times 80 \\
         &= 504000\,\text{J} \\
         &= 5.0 \times 10^5\,\text{J}
\end{aligned}
$$

**5. The time the kettle takes on the generator's output.**

$$
\begin{aligned}
t &= \frac{E}{P} \\
  &= \frac{504000}{2100} \\
  &= 240\,\text{s} \\
  &= 4.0\,\text{minutes}
\end{aligned}
$$

**6. If the stream runs low.** Suppose a dry spell halves the flow to 15 kg per
second. The energy given up each second also halves, so the useful output power
falls as well, from 2100 W to about 1060 W. The kettle then takes about 480 s,
or 8.0 minutes, to transfer the same 504000 J.

**7. Is the stream a sound energy resource?** The stream is **renewable**: rain
refills it, so it will not run out. It produces no carbon dioxide while it
generates. Its weakness is **reliability**: the flow depends on the weather, so
in a dry spell it may not deliver enough power. A centre that depended on the
stream alone would need a way to store energy or a backup generator. This final
judgement is a reasoned answer. It is not the kind of claim a computer can check,
so compare your reasoning with the evidence above.

## What you can now do

- name the energy stores and describe transfers between them;
- calculate gravitational potential, kinetic and elastic potential energy, and
  the energy transferred by heating;
- calculate power, the work done by a force, and the time or energy from a power;
- use conservation of energy in a closed system;
- explain dissipation and describe how to reduce unwanted transfer;
- calculate efficiency as a decimal or a percentage;
- compare renewable and non-renewable energy resources and explain trends in
  their use.
