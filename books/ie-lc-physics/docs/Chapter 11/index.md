# Chapter 11: Sound

An ambulance comes towards you with its siren sounding. The note is high. As it passes,
the note drops, even though the driver hears one steady note. In this chapter you find
out what carries the sound to your ear, how fast it travels, why some notes are high and
some are loud, and why a moving source changes the note you hear.

## The chapter problem

**The siren that changes note.** An ambulance siren makes a steady note of 700 Hz. The
ambulance travels at 30 m/s along a straight road past you, and you stand at the
roadside. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

By the end of the chapter you can say what kind of wave carries the sound to your ear,
work out how far away the ambulance is from the time the sound takes, explain why the
note sounds louder when the ambulance is near, and say why its pitch rises as it
approaches and falls as it passes. You can also calculate the two frequencies you hear.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- explain that sound is a longitudinal wave that needs a medium, and describe the
  compressions and rarefactions it makes;
- state a value for the speed of sound in air, and say how the speed depends on the
  medium;
- measure the speed of sound in air, using an echo or a resonance tube;
- relate the pitch, the loudness and the quality of a note to its frequency, its
  amplitude and its overtones;
- describe the Doppler effect for a moving source, and explain the change in pitch as it
  passes;
- use $f' = \dfrac{fc}{c \pm u}$ to calculate the frequency heard from a moving source.

## Where it is used

Sirens and speed traps, ultrasound scans and sonar, hearing protection in industry, and
the design of concert halls. The Doppler effect also explains the red shift of distant
galaxies, which is evidence that the universe is expanding.

## Your turn to connect it

Think of a sound you hear often that changes as it moves: a passing siren, a car on a wet
road, a train, a motorbike, or an aircraft overhead.

Choose one. Write down what happens to the note as it comes towards you and as it goes
away. Keep your answer. The Review returns to it, and by then you can explain it with the
physics of this chapter.
