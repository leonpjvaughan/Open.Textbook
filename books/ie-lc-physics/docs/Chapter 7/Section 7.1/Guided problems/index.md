# 7.1 Angular velocity and period — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A car travels 120 m in 6.0 s. Calculate its average speed.
2. How many seconds are there in 2.5 minutes?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $v = \dfrac{s}{t} = \dfrac{120}{6.0} = 20\,\text{m/s}$.
2. $2.5 \times 60 = 150\,\text{s}$.

Both ideas return here. The speed of a point on a rim comes from a distance divided by a
time, and revolutions per minute must be converted into seconds.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A wheel turns 450 times in 90 s. Calculate its frequency and its period.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The frequency is the number of turns in **one** second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$f = \dfrac{450}{90}$, then $T = \dfrac{1}{f}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$f = \frac{450}{90} = 5.0\,\text{Hz}$$

$$T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{5.0} = 0.20\,\text{s}$$

A frequency of 5.0 Hz means five turns every second, so one turn takes a fifth of a
second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A fan turns at 1200 revolutions per minute. Calculate its frequency in hertz.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the rate in revolutions per minute by 60.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$f = \dfrac{1200}{60}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$f = \frac{1200}{60} = 20\,\text{Hz}$$

The fan turns 20 times every second. An answer of 1200 Hz would come from using the
number of revolutions per minute without converting.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Calculate the period of the fan in question 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The period is the reciprocal of the frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$T = \dfrac{1}{20}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{20} = 0.050\,\text{s}$$

The fan completes one turn in 0.050 s, which is fifty milliseconds.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Calculate the angular velocity of the fan in question 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\omega = 2\pi f$. One complete turn is $2\pi$ radians.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\omega = 2\pi \times 20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 20 = 126\,\text{rad/s}$$

To two significant figures that is $1.3 \times 10^{2}\,\text{rad/s}$. The angle turned
through grows by 126 radians every second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A wheel of radius 0.40 m turns at 3.0 revolutions per second. Calculate the speed
of a point on the rim.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the angular velocity first, then use $v = r\omega$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\omega = 2\pi \times 3.0$, then $v = 0.40 \times \omega$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\omega = 2\pi f = 2\pi \times 3.0 = 18.8\,\text{rad/s}$$

$$v = r\omega = 0.40 \times 18.8 = 7.5\,\text{m/s}$$

A point on the rim travels 7.5 m every second, even though the wheel does not move
forwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A point on the rim of a wheel of radius 0.25 m moves at 5.0 m/s. Calculate the
angular velocity of the wheel.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $v = r\omega$ for $\omega$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\omega = \dfrac{v}{r} = \dfrac{5.0}{0.25}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\omega = \frac{v}{r} = \frac{5.0}{0.25} = 20\,\text{rad/s}$$

The wheel turns through 20 radians every second. A smaller wheel needs a larger angular
velocity to give the same rim speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Two points lie on the same wheel, one close to the axis and one on the rim.
Explain why they have the same angular velocity but different speeds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the angle each point turns through in one second, then about the distance
each point travels in that second.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both points are fixed to the same wheel, so they turn through the same angle. The rim
point is farther from the axis, so it travels a longer arc.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The two points are part of the same rigid wheel, so they complete one turn in the same
time. Their angular velocities are equal, because the angle turned through in one second
is the same.

Their speeds differ because speed is distance divided by time. In one turn the rim point
travels the full circumference, $2\pi r$, while the point near the axis travels a much
smaller circle. The same time covers a longer distance for the rim point, so its speed
is larger.

The equation $v = r\omega$ shows this directly: $\omega$ is the same for both, so $v$ is
larger for the larger $r$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**8.** A car speeds up from 12 m/s to 24 m/s in 6.0 s. Calculate its acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{24 - 12}{6.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{24 - 12}{6.0} = \frac{12}{6.0} = 2.0\,\text{m/s}^{2}$$

A car that turns a corner at a steady speed also has an acceleration, even though this
number would be zero. The next section explains why.

</details>
</details>
</details>
