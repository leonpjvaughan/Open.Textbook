# 7.4 Circular motion problems — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the equation for the centripetal force in terms of the mass $m$, the
   speed $v$ and the radius $r$.
2. A 0.40 kg mass hangs on a string. Calculate its weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$, directed towards the centre of the circle.
2. $W = mg = 0.40 \times 9.8 = 3.9\,\text{N}$, where $W$ is the weight and $g$ the
   gravitational field strength.

Both ideas return here. Every problem in this section ends in $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$, and
the weight decides the sign of each term in the equation.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A bucket of water is swung in a vertical circle of radius 1.0 m. Calculate the
minimum speed at the top so that the water does not fall out.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the minimum speed the weight alone supplies the centripetal force, so $mg =
\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The mass cancels, leaving $v = \sqrt{gr} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 1.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{gr} = \sqrt{9.8 \times 1.0} = 3.1\,\text{m/s}$$

At 3.1 m/s the water is on the point of leaving the bucket. At a higher speed the
bucket pushes on the water as well.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A 0.50 kg ball on a string of length 0.80 m moves at 4.0 m/s at the top of a
vertical circle. Calculate the tension in the string at the top.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the top the tension and the weight both point towards the centre, so
$T + mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$T = \dfrac{0.50 \times 16}{0.80} - 0.50 \times 9.8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{0.50 \times 16}{0.80} = 10\,\text{N}$$

$$mg = 0.50 \times 9.8 = 4.9\,\text{N}$$

$$T = 10 - 4.9 = 5.1\,\text{N}$$

The weight supplies 4.9 N of the 10 N needed, so the string carries the rest.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The same ball of mass 0.50 kg moves at 4.0 m/s at the bottom of the same circle.
Calculate the tension in the string at the bottom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the bottom the weight points away from the centre, so $T - mg = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$T = \dfrac{0.50 \times 16}{0.80} + 0.50 \times 9.8$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{mv^2}{r} + mg = 10 + 4.9 = 15\,\text{N}$$

The tension is three times larger at the bottom than at the top, because it must
support the weight as well as turn the ball.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A bend of radius 80 m is banked so that no friction is needed at 25 m/s.
Calculate the banking angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\tan\theta = \dfrac{v^2}{rg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\tan\theta = \dfrac{25^2}{80 \times 9.8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg} = \frac{625}{784} = 0.797$$

$$\theta = 39^\circ$$

At this angle the normal force alone supplies the centripetal force, so the bend works
even when the road is icy.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A car of mass 1200 kg goes round a flat bend of radius 60 m. The maximum friction
force is 9000 N. Calculate the maximum speed of the car.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Friction supplies the centripetal force. Rearrange $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ for $v$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{9000 \times 60}{1200}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{Fr}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{9000 \times 60}{1200}} = \sqrt{450} = 21\,\text{m/s}$$

At 21 m/s the friction is exactly enough. The mass of the car cancels in the last step,
so the answer does not depend on how heavy the car is.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A satellite orbits at $6.8 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}$ from the centre of the Earth.
Gravity provides the centripetal force. Calculate its speed. Take
$M = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Set the gravitational force equal to the centripetal force, then cancel the mass of the
satellite.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\dfrac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.97 \times
10^{24}}{6.8 \times 10^{6}}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}} = \sqrt{\frac{3.98 \times 10^{14}}{6.8 \times 10^{6}}} =
\sqrt{5.9 \times 10^{7}} = 7.7 \times 10^{3}\,\text{m/s}$$

That is 7.7 km/s. The satellite is in a low orbit, just above the atmosphere, and it
circles the Earth about 16 times a day.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why the tension in a string is larger at the bottom of a vertical circle
than at the top when the speed is the same.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At which point does the weight point towards the centre, and at which point does it
point away?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

At the top the weight helps the tension. At the bottom the tension must overcome the
weight and still supply the centripetal force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At the top of the circle the weight points towards the centre, the same way as the
tension. The two forces add to give the centripetal force, so
$T_{\text{top}} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} - mg$.

At the bottom the weight points away from the centre. The tension must supply the
centripetal force and also support the weight, so
$T_{\text{bottom}} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r} + mg$.

The centripetal force $\dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ is the same at both points when the speed is the
same, so the tension at the bottom is larger by twice the weight.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 7.3

**8.** A 0.25 kg stone on a string of length 0.50 m moves at 4.0 m/s in a horizontal
circle. Calculate the tension in the string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The tension supplies the centripetal force, so use $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = \dfrac{0.25 \times 4.0^2}{0.50}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{0.25 \times 16}{0.50} = 8.0\,\text{N}$$

In this horizontal circle the weight does not help or hinder the turning, so the tension
supplies the whole centripetal force. In a vertical circle the weight changes the
tension from one point to the next.

</details>
</details>
</details>
