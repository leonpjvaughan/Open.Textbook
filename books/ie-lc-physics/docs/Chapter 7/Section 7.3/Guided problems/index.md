# 7.3 Centripetal force and its sources — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State Newton's second law as an equation.
2. A 0.25 kg mass has a weight of 2.45 N on the Earth. Calculate the gravitational field
   strength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $F = ma$. A resultant force $F$ on a mass $m$ gives an acceleration $a$.
2. $g = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{2.45}{0.25} = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

Both ideas return here. Newton's second law turns the centripetal acceleration into a
force, and the weight is the force that competes with it in the next section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A 0.60 kg stone on a string of length 0.75 m moves at 4.0 m/s in a horizontal
circle. Calculate the tension in the string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The tension supplies the centripetal force, so use $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = \dfrac{0.60 \times 4.0^2}{0.75}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{0.60 \times 16}{0.75} = \frac{9.6}{0.75} = 12.8\,\text{N}$$

To two significant figures that is 13 N. The force points along the string, towards the
hand.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A car of mass 900 kg goes round a bend of radius 60 m at 18 m/s. Calculate the
friction force needed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Friction is the only horizontal force, so it supplies the whole centripetal force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = \dfrac{900 \times 18^2}{60}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{900 \times 324}{60} = \frac{291600}{60} = 4.9 \times 10^{3}\,\text{N}$$

That is nearly 5000 N of friction from the tyres, which is why a car needs good tyres
to take a bend at speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A 0.50 kg mass moves in a circle of radius 0.40 m with an angular velocity of
6.0 rad/s. Calculate the centripetal force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You are given the angular velocity, so use $F = m\omega^2 r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = 0.50 \times 6.0^2 \times 0.40$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = m\omega^2 r = 0.50 \times 36 \times 0.40 = 7.2\,\text{N}$$

The same answer comes from $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$, using
$v = r\omega = 0.40 \times 6.0 = 2.4\,\text{m/s}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A 0.20 kg ball on a string of length 0.90 m moves at 6.0 m/s. Calculate the
tension in the string.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ with $r = 0.90\,\text{m}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = \dfrac{0.20 \times 6.0^2}{0.90}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{mv^2}{r} = \frac{0.20 \times 36}{0.90} = \frac{7.2}{0.90} = 8.0\,\text{N}$$

The ball is lighter than the stone in question 1, but it moves faster, so the tension is
of a similar size.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A car of mass 1000 kg needs a friction force of 5000 N to go round a bend of
radius 45 m. Calculate the maximum speed of the car.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $F = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ for $v$. The speed is the square root of $\dfrac{Fr}{m}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{5000 \times 45}{1000}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{Fr}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{5000 \times 45}{1000}} = \sqrt{225} = 15\,\text{m/s}$$

At 15 m/s the friction is exactly enough. At a higher speed the car cannot turn tightly
enough and it slides outwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Name the force that supplies the centripetal force for a satellite in orbit, and
for a car on a bend.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what is already pulling or pushing each object towards the centre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A satellite is in a gravitational field, and a car's tyres touch the road.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

For a satellite the centripetal force is gravity, the pull of the planet towards its
centre. For a car on a bend the centripetal force is friction between the tyres and the
road.

Neither force is a new force invented for circular motion. Each one already acts, and
each one happens to point towards the centre.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why there is no outward force on a passenger in a car that turns a
corner.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which way does the resultant force on the passenger point? What would happen if the
inward force stopped?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The door or the seat pushes the passenger inwards. If that push stopped, the passenger
would continue in a straight line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The only resultant force on the passenger points towards the centre of the turn. It is
supplied by the door or the seat, and it is the centripetal force. There is no force
pushing the passenger outwards.

If the inward push stopped, the passenger would continue in a straight line along the
tangent, because a body keeps its velocity unless a resultant force acts on it. The
sensation of being pushed outwards is the passenger's inertia resisting the inward push,
not a real outward force.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 7.2

**8.** A stone on a string of length 0.50 m moves at 5.0 m/s. Calculate its centripetal
acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $a = \dfrac{v^2}{r}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{5.0^2}{0.50}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{v^2}{r} = \frac{25}{0.50} = 50\,\text{m/s}^{2}$$

That is about five times $g$. A 0.20 kg stone would then need a force of
$F = ma = 0.20 \times 50 = 10\,\text{N}$, which is why a thin thread would break.

</details>
</details>
</details>
