# 15.5 The electric motor — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory, then check.

1. State the principle of conservation of energy.
2. A lamp has a power of 60 W. Calculate the energy it transfers in 5.0 minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one store to another.
   A motor transfers electrical energy to kinetic energy and to heat.
2. $E = Pt = 60 \times 300 = 1.8 \times 10^{4}$ J. The time must be in seconds: 5.0
   minutes is 300 s.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A motor coil has 20 turns. The side of the coil in the field is 5.0 cm long, the
flux density is 0.40 T, and the current is 0.30 A. Calculate the force on one side of
one turn, and the total force on that side of the coil.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First use $F = BIl$ for one turn, then multiply by the number of turns.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = 0.40 \times 0.30 \times 0.050$, then multiply by 20.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = BIl = 0.40 \times 0.30 \times 0.050 = 6.0 \times 10^{-3}\,\text{N}$$

$$F_{\text{total}} = 20 \times 6.0 \times 10^{-3} = 0.12\,\text{N}$$

The length must be in metres: 5.0 cm is 0.050 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** State the function of the split-ring commutator in a direct-current motor.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what would happen to the coil after half a turn without it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The commutator changes the connections to the coil every half turn.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full answer</summary>

The commutator reverses the direction of the current in the coil every half turn.
Reversing the current reverses the force on each side of the coil, so the coil keeps
turning the same way instead of stopping or turning back.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Explain why a coil in a magnetic field would stop after half a turn if there
were no commutator.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the position of the coil when its plane is at right angles to the field.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

At that position the forces act along the same line through the axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full answer</summary>

As the coil turns, the forces on the two sides stay the same size and the same
direction in space. After half a turn the coil is in the position where its plane is at
right angles to the field, and the forces on the two sides act along the same line
through the axis. They no longer produce a turning effect, so the coil stops there.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** State two changes that would increase the turning effect on a motor coil, and
explain each one.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the quantities in $F = BIl$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A larger force on each side gives a larger turning effect.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full answer</summary>

Increase the current, because the force on each side is proportional to the current.
Use a stronger magnet, because the force is proportional to the flux density. A third
change is to increase the number of turns on the coil, because every turn adds its own
force.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A motor draws a current of 0.50 A from a 6.0 V supply. Calculate the power it
uses and the energy it transfers in 30 s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $P = VI$, then $E = Pt$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$P = 6.0 \times 0.50 = 3.0$ W, then $E = 3.0 \times 30$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P = VI = 6.0 \times 0.50 = 3.0\,\text{W}$$

$$E = Pt = 3.0 \times 30 = 90\,\text{J}$$

Some of the energy turns the motor and the rest warms the coil.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**6.** A toy car of mass 0.50 kg moves at 2.0 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy, and
compare it with the energy the motor transfers in 30 s in question 5.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E_k = \dfrac{1}{2}mv^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E_k = 0.5 \times 0.50 \times 2.0^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.5 \times 0.50 \times 2.0^2 = 1.0\,\text{J}$$

The motor transfers 90 J in 30 s, which is far more than the 1.0 J the car has as
kinetic energy. Most of the motor's energy goes into overcoming friction and into heat,
which is why a motor gets warm.

</details>
</details>
</details>
