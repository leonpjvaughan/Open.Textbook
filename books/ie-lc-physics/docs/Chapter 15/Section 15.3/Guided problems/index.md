# 15.3 The force on a current-carrying conductor — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

Answer these from memory, then check.

1. A resistor of resistance 12 Ω has a potential difference of 6.0 V across it.
   Calculate the current through it.
2. State what a force is, and give its unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $I = \dfrac{V}{R} = \dfrac{6.0}{12} = 0.50$ A. A current is a flow of charge, and in
   this section that flow is what the magnetic field pushes on.
2. A force is a push or a pull, and it is measured in newtons, N. The force in this
   section is a push on a wire, at right angles to both the current and the field.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A wire of length 0.10 m carries a current of 2.0 A at right angles to a field of
flux density 0.50 T. Calculate the force on the wire.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write down which quantity you are given and which one you want, then choose the form of
$F = BIl$ that fits.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

You are given $B$, $I$ and $l$, and you want $F$, so multiply them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = BIl = 0.50 \times 2.0 \times 0.10 = 0.10\,\text{N}$$

Check the unit: tesla multiplied by ampere multiplied by metre gives a newton.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A wire in a field of 0.40 T carries a current of 3.0 A and experiences a force
of 0.24 N. Calculate the length of wire that is in the field.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $F = BIl$ for $l$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$l = \dfrac{F}{BI} = \dfrac{0.24}{0.40 \times 3.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$l = \frac{F}{BI} = \frac{0.24}{0.40 \times 3.0} = 0.20\,\text{m}$$

If you wrote 0.80 m, you divided by $B$ only. The current has to be in the
denominator as well.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A wire of length 0.10 m carries a current of 5.0 A at right angles to a magnetic
field and experiences a force of 0.15 N. Calculate the magnetic flux density.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $F = BIl$ for $B$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$B = \dfrac{F}{Il} = \dfrac{0.15}{5.0 \times 0.10}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$B = \frac{F}{Il} = \frac{0.15}{5.0 \times 0.10} = 0.30\,\text{T}$$

A flux density of 0.30 T is a strong laboratory field, so the answer is reasonable.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A wire in a magnetic field carries a current at right angles to the field. The
length of wire in the field is doubled and the flux density is halved. State what
happens to the force, and explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The force is proportional to both $B$ and $l$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Doubling $l$ doubles the force. Halving $B$ halves it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The force is unchanged. $F = BIl$, so the force is proportional to both $B$ and $l$.
Doubling $l$ doubles the force and halving $B$ halves it, so the two changes cancel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A wire carries a current into the page and lies in a magnetic field that points
from left to right. State the direction of the force on the wire.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Fleming's left-hand rule. First finger along the field, second finger along the
current.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

First finger points to the right. Second finger points into the page.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

First finger points to the right, along the field. Second finger points into the page,
along the current. The thumb points **downwards**, so the force on the wire is
downwards. Check it: the force is at right angles to the field and to the current, and
downwards is at right angles to both.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 14

**6.** A motor draws a current of 0.25 A from a 3.0 V supply. Calculate the power it
uses, and the energy it transfers in 60 s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $P = VI$, then $E = Pt$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$P = 3.0 \times 0.25 = 0.75$ W, then $E = 0.75 \times 60$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P = VI = 3.0 \times 0.25 = 0.75\,\text{W}$$

$$E = Pt = 0.75 \times 60 = 45\,\text{J}$$

Some of that energy turns the fan, and the rest is transferred as heat in the coil,
which is why a motor gets warm.

</details>
</details>
</details>
