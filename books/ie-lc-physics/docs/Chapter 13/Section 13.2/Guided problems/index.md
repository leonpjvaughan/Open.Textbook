# 13.2 Coulomb's law — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. What is the unit of charge, and what is the charge on one electron?
2. State Newton's third law in your own words.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The unit of charge is the coulomb, C. One electron carries
   $-1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C.
2. If object A pushes or pulls object B, then B pushes or pulls A with a force of the
   same size, in the opposite direction.

The second answer matters here. The two charges in Coulomb's law pull or push each
other with equal forces, even when their charges are different.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Two point charges of $+2.0\ \mu\text{C}$ and $+5.0\ \mu\text{C}$ are $0.10$ m
apart. Calculate the force between them. Take $k = 9.0 \times 10^{9}$ N m$^{2}$
C$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Convert the charges to coulombs, then use $F = \dfrac{kq_1q_2}{d^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = \dfrac{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 5.0 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.10)^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 5.0 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.10)^2} = \frac{9.0 \times 10^{-2}}{1.0 \times 10^{-2}} = 9.0\,\text{N}$$

Both charges are positive, so the force is repulsive.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A charge of $+6.0\ \mu\text{C}$ and a charge of $-2.0\ \mu\text{C}$ are
$0.30$ m apart. Calculate the size of the force between them, and state its
direction. Take $k = 9.0 \times 10^{9}$ N m$^{2}$ C$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the magnitudes in the formula, then decide the direction from the signs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = \dfrac{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 6.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.30)^2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 6.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 2.0 \times 10^{-6}}{(0.30)^2} = \frac{1.08 \times 10^{-1}}{9.0 \times 10^{-2}} = 1.2\,\text{N}$$

The charges have opposite signs, so the force is attractive. Each charge is pulled
towards the other.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Two charges attract with a force of $0.80$ N when they are $0.10$ m apart.
Calculate the force between them when the distance is doubled to $0.20$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The force follows an inverse-square law.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Doubling the distance divides the force by $2^2 = 4$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{0.80}{4} = 0.20\,\text{N}$$

The force is one quarter of its original value. If the distance were tripled, the
force would fall to one ninth.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Two equal charges of $+4.0\ \mu\text{C}$ each repel with a force of $1.6$ N.
Calculate the distance between them. Take $k = 9.0 \times 10^{9}$ N m$^{2}$
C$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $F = \dfrac{kq_1q_2}{d^2}$ for $d$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$d = \sqrt{\dfrac{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 4.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 4.0 \times 10^{-6}}{1.6}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \sqrt{\frac{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 4.0 \times 10^{-6} \times 4.0 \times 10^{-6}}{1.6}} = \sqrt{9.0 \times 10^{-2}} = 0.30\,\text{m}$$

Check the sense of it: a large force at a small distance is what the inverse-square
law predicts, and $0.30$ m is the larger of the two distances in this set.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A charge of $+3.0\ \mu\text{C}$ is $0.10$ m from a second point charge. The
force between them is $2.7$ N. Calculate the size of the second charge. Take
$k = 9.0 \times 10^{9}$ N m$^{2}$ C$^{-2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange the law for $q_2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$q_2 = \dfrac{Fd^2}{kq_1} = \dfrac{2.7 \times (0.10)^2}{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 3.0 \times 10^{-6}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$q_2 = \frac{Fd^2}{kq_1} = \frac{2.7 \times (0.10)^2}{9.0 \times 10^{9} \times 3.0 \times 10^{-6}} = \frac{2.7 \times 10^{-2}}{2.7 \times 10^{4}} = 1.0 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{C}$$

That is $1.0\ \mu\text{C}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain what an inverse-square law means. Then state what happens to the force
between two charges when the distance is halved and one of the charges is doubled.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Deal with the distance and the charge one at a time, then combine them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Halving the distance multiplies the force by four. Doubling one charge doubles it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An inverse-square law means the force is proportional to one divided by the square of
the distance, $F \propto 1/d^2$.

Halving the distance multiplies the force by $2^2 = 4$. Doubling one charge doubles
the force. Together, the force becomes $4 \times 2 = 8$ times larger.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 6

**7.** Two masses attract each other with a gravitational force of $36$ N when their
centres are $2.0$ m apart. Calculate the force between them when their centres are
$6.0$ m apart.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Newton's law of gravitation is also an inverse-square law.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The distance is tripled, so divide by $3^2 = 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = \frac{36}{9} = 4.0\,\text{N}$$

Coulomb's law and Newton's law of gravitation have the same inverse-square shape. The
difference is that gravity only attracts, while the electric force can attract or
repel.

</details>
</details>
</details>
