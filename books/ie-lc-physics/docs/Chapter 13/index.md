# Chapter 13: Charge and electric fields

Rub a balloon on your sleeve and hold it near a wall. The balloon sticks. Nothing
touches it, and no engine holds it there. In this chapter you find the force behind
that trick, and you learn how to calculate it.

## The chapter problem

**The drop that lands in the right place.** An inkjet print head makes a tiny drop of
ink and gives it an electric charge. Two charged plates then steer the drop to the
exact spot on the page. Without the charge the drop would fly straight on, and the
page would be a smear.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- the charge on a drop is $-2.0 \times 10^{-12}$ C;
- two drops may pass within $1.0$ mm of each other;
- the steering plates are $2.0$ mm apart;
- the potential difference across the plates is $1200$ V;
- the Coulomb constant is $k = 9.0 \times 10^{9}$ N m$^{2}$ C$^{-2}$;
- the charge on an electron is $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C.

By the end of the chapter you can find the number of electrons on a drop, the force
between two drops, the field between the plates, and the force that steers one drop.
You can also say which plate the drop moves towards, and why the printer must charge
the drop before it can aim it.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- describe the two kinds of electric charge, and charge an object by friction or by
  induction;
- use $q = ne$ to find the charge on an object or the number of electrons it has
  gained or lost;
- classify materials as conductors or insulators, and explain the difference using
  free and bound electrons;
- state Coulomb's law and use $F = \dfrac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}\dfrac{q_1q_2}{d^2}$ to
  find a force, a charge or a distance;
- describe electric fields with field lines, and use $E = \dfrac{F}{q}$ and $F = qE$;
- find the field strength between parallel plates with $E = \dfrac{V}{d}$, and the
  field and potential of a point charge.

## Where it is used

Inkjet and laser printers, photocopiers and electrostatic paint sprayers all steer
charged particles with electric fields. Lightning, static shocks and the dust that
clings to a screen are the same physics on a larger or smaller scale.

## Your turn to connect it

Where have you met a charge that acted without touching? A balloon stuck to a wall,
a shock from a car door, a crackle when you pull off a fleece, dust on a screen, a

Choose one. Write down what is charged, what it moves towards or away from, and what
