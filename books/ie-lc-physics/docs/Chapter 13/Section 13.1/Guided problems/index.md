# 13.1 Electric charge and charging — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. What is the unit of force, and what does a force do to an object?
2. An atom is neutral. What does that tell you about its numbers of protons and
   electrons?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The unit of force is the newton, N. A force changes an object's motion, or
   changes its shape.
2. The atom has the same number of protons as electrons. The positive and negative
   charges cancel, so the atom has no net charge.

The second answer is the starting point of this section. An object becomes charged
only when its electrons and protons no longer balance.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A plastic rod is rubbed and gains $5.0 \times 10^{14}$ electrons. Calculate
the charge on the rod. Take $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $q = ne$. The rod gained electrons, so the charge is negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$q = -ne = -5.0 \times 10^{14} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$q = -ne = -5.0 \times 10^{14} \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = -8.0 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{C}$$

That is $-80\ \mu\text{C}$. The minus sign is there because the rod gained electrons,
so it is negative.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A metal sphere carries a charge of $+6.4 \times 10^{-17}$ C. Calculate the
number of electrons it has lost. Take $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ C.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $q = ne$ for $n$. The charge is positive, so the sphere has lost electrons.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$n = \dfrac{q}{e} = \dfrac{6.4 \times 10^{-17}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$n = \frac{q}{e} = \frac{6.4 \times 10^{-17}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 400$$

The sphere has lost 400 electrons. A positive charge means a shortage of electrons,
not an excess.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Two identical metal spheres carry charges of $+5.0\ \mu\text{C}$ and
$+1.0\ \mu\text{C}$. They are touched together and then separated. Calculate the
charge on each sphere.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Charge is conserved, so the total charge is shared equally between the identical
spheres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Total $= +5.0\ \mu\text{C} + 1.0\ \mu\text{C} = +6.0\ \mu\text{C}$. Divide by two.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{Total} = +5.0\ \mu\text{C} + 1.0\ \mu\text{C} = +6.0\ \mu\text{C}$$

$$\text{Each sphere} = \frac{+6.0\ \mu\text{C}}{2} = +3.0\ \mu\text{C}$$

The total charge before and after is the same. That is the conservation of charge.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A polythene rod becomes negative when it is rubbed with wool. Explain why
the wool becomes positive, and why the two charges are equal in size.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rubbing does not create charge. It moves electrons.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The polythene gains the electrons that the wool loses.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Rubbing transfers electrons from the wool to the polythene. The polythene gains
electrons, so it becomes negative. The wool loses the same electrons, so it becomes
positive.

Charge is conserved, so no charge was created or destroyed. Every electron that left
the wool arrived on the rod, which is why the two charges are equal in size and
opposite in sign.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Classify each of these as a conductor or an insulator: copper, glass, rubber,
aluminium. Explain your choice using electrons.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which materials have free electrons that can move through them?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Metals have free electrons. Glass and rubber hold their electrons bound to atoms.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Copper and aluminium are conductors. They are metals, and some of their electrons are
free to move through the material, so charge can travel through them.

Glass and rubber are insulators. Their electrons are bound to their atoms, so charge
cannot move through them. A charge placed on an insulator stays where it is placed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Describe how a negatively charged rod can be used to give a metal sphere a
positive charge by induction. The rod must not touch the sphere.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the free electrons in the sphere and what grounding does.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Bring the rod near, ground the far side, remove the wire, then remove the rod.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Bring the negatively charged rod near the sphere. The free electrons in the sphere
are pushed to the far side, so the near side is left positive.

Now connect the far side to the Earth. The pushed-away electrons flow into the Earth.
Remove the wire while the rod is still in place, then remove the rod. The sphere is
left with a positive charge.

The rod never touched the sphere, which is why the method is called charging by
induction.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 6

**7.** A 3.0 kg mass has a weight of 29.4 N on the surface of a planet. Calculate the
gravitational field strength at the surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Field strength is force per unit mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$g = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{29.4}{3.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$g = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{29.4}{3.0} = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$$

This is the same idea as the electric field strength you meet in Section 13.3. There,
the field strength is the force per unit **charge**, just as here it is the force per
unit **mass**.

</details>
</details>
</details>
