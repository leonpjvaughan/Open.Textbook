# 3.3 Newton's third law and momentum — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State Newton's third law in your own words.
2. A resultant force of 20 N acts on a mass of 4.0 kg. Calculate the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. If A pushes or pulls B, then B pushes or pulls A with an equal force in the
   opposite direction. The two forces act on different objects.
2. $a = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{20}{4.0} = 5.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. If you wrote 80, you
   multiplied instead of dividing.

Both return here: a change in momentum takes a force, and the force obeys $F = ma$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A car of mass 900 kg travels at 12 m/s. Calculate its momentum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Momentum is mass times velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$p = mv = 900 \times 12$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$p = mv = 900 \times 12 = 10800\,\text{kg m/s}$$

That is $1.1 \times 10^{4}\,\text{kg m/s}$ to two significant figures. Momentum is a
vector, so it points along the velocity.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A force of 60 N acts on a trolley for 0.50 s. Calculate the impulse.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Impulse is force times time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$Ft = 60 \times 0.50$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$Ft = 60 \times 0.50 = 30\,\text{N s}$$

An impulse of $30\,\text{N s}$ changes the momentum of the trolley by
$30\,\text{kg m/s}$. The two units are the same size.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A ball of mass 0.20 kg moves at 6.0 m/s and is stopped in 0.040 s. Calculate
the change in momentum of the ball and the force on it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The ball finishes at rest, so its final velocity is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\Delta p = m(v - u) = 0.20 \times (0 - 6.0)$. Then $F = \dfrac{\Delta p}{t}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\Delta p = m(v - u) = 0.20 \times (0 - 6.0) = -1.2\,\text{kg m/s}$$

$$F = \frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{-1.2}{0.040} = -30\,\text{N}$$

The minus sign says the change and the force point the way the ball came from. The
force is 30 N, and it stops the ball.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A passenger of mass 70 kg speeds up from rest to 3.0 m/s in 1.5 s. Calculate
her change in momentum and the resultant force on her.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

She starts from rest, so her initial momentum is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\Delta p = mv = 70 \times 3.0$. Then divide by 1.5 s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\Delta p = mv = 70 \times 3.0 = 210\,\text{kg m/s}$$

$$F = \frac{\Delta p}{t} = \frac{210}{1.5} = 140\,\text{N}$$

The resultant force on her is 140 N in the direction of travel. That is the contact
force from the floor minus her weight, so the floor pushes harder than her weight
while she speeds up.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A swimmer pushes the water backwards with her hands. Explain how this makes her
move forwards, and name the two forces in the pair.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Newton's third law pairs a force with its partner, acting on the other object.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The swimmer pushes the water. The water pushes back.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The two forces are the push of the swimmer's hands on the water, backwards, and the
push of the water on the swimmer's hands, forwards.

They are equal in size and opposite in direction, and they act on different objects:
one acts on the water and one acts on the swimmer. The force on the swimmer points
forwards, so it is the force that moves her. The force on the water pushes the water
backwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A book rests on a table. A student says the weight of the book and the contact
force from the table are a Newton's third law pair. Explain why the student is wrong,
and name the correct partner for the weight.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which object does each force act on?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both of the student's forces act on the book.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The weight of the book and the contact force from the table both act on the book, so
they act on the same object. A third-law pair must act on two different objects, so
these two forces are not a pair.

They are equal and opposite, which is why the book stays at rest, but that makes them
a balanced pair rather than a third-law pair.

The correct partner for the weight of the book is the pull of the book on the Earth.
That force acts on the Earth, and it is equal in size to the weight of the book.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**7.** A ball is thrown straight up at 15 m/s. Calculate the time it takes to reach
its highest point. Take $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the highest point the velocity is momentarily zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = u - gt$ with $v = 0$, so $t = \dfrac{u}{g}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = \frac{u}{g} = \frac{15}{9.8} = 1.5\,\text{s}$$

This matters here: while the ball is in the air the only force on it is its weight, so
its momentum changes at a steady rate. The force is the rate of change of momentum.

</details>
</details>
</details>
