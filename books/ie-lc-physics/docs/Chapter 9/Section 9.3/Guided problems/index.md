# 9.3 Transverse and longitudinal waves — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State one difference between a mechanical wave and an electromagnetic wave.
2. A wave has a wavelength of 0.50 m and a frequency of 680 Hz. Calculate its speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. A mechanical wave needs a medium, and an electromagnetic wave does not. Sound is
   mechanical and light is electromagnetic.
2. $v = f\lambda = 680 \times 0.50 = 340\,\text{m/s}$.

The second answer is a useful number: 340 m/s is the speed of sound in air. The first
one is the idea this section builds on, because the medium decides what kind of wave it
is and how the particles move.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A wave travels along a rope. The particles of the rope move up and down while the
wave travels along the rope. State whether the wave is transverse or longitudinal, and
explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the direction the particles move with the direction the wave travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Up and down is at right angles to along the rope.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The wave is transverse. The particles of the rope vibrate up and down, which is at right
angles to the direction the wave travels along the rope. A transverse wave is one whose
particles vibrate at right angles to the direction of travel.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** State what a compression is, and name the kind of wave it belongs to.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the spacing of the particles in the region.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A compression is a region of the medium, not a single particle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A compression is a region of a medium where the particles are closer together than
usual. It belongs to a longitudinal wave, and it travels along the wave. A rarefaction
is the matching region where the particles are farther apart than usual.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Explain why a sound wave cannot be polarised.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which kind of wave can be polarised?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Polarisation selects one direction of vibration at right angles to the wave.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Only a transverse wave can be polarised, because its vibration has a direction at right
angles to the direction of travel that a filter can select. Sound is a longitudinal
wave, so its particles vibrate along the direction of travel. There is no sideways
direction to select, so a sound wave cannot be polarised.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Light is an electromagnetic wave. State whether it is transverse or longitudinal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Electromagnetic waves can be polarised.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Only one kind of wave can be polarised.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Light is a transverse wave. Every electromagnetic wave is transverse, which is why light
can be polarised by a filter. Its electric and magnetic vibrations point at right angles
to the direction in which the light travels.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Describe the direction in which the particles of the medium vibrate in a
longitudinal wave.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The word *longitudinal* refers to the length of the wave.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Think about the spring: the coils move backwards and forwards along it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

In a longitudinal wave the particles vibrate back and forth along the direction in which
the wave travels. They bunch together into compressions and spread apart into
rarefactions, and these regions move along the wave. The particles themselves stay near
their own positions.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A wave on a string and a sound wave in air are both travelling towards you. Compare
the direction of vibration in the two waves, and state which kind each one is.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One vibration is across the wave and one is along it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the kind of wave for each direction of vibration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

On the string the particles vibrate at right angles to the direction the wave travels,
so the wave on the string is transverse. In the air the particles vibrate along the
direction the sound travels, so the sound wave is longitudinal. Both waves carry energy
towards you, and the difference is the direction of the vibration.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A student draws a sound wave as a wavy line with crests and troughs. Explain what
is wrong with the drawing, and describe the correct picture.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A wavy line shows a displacement at right angles to the wave.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Sound is longitudinal, so the displacement is along the wave.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A wavy line with crests and troughs is a picture of a transverse wave, because the
displacement is drawn at right angles to the direction of travel. Sound is a
longitudinal wave, so its displacement is along the direction of travel. The correct
picture shows bands of compressed air and spread-out air travelling away from the
source, one after another.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**8.** A car slows from 30 m/s to 10 m/s in 5.0 s. Calculate its acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time taken.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{10 - 30}{5.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{10 - 30}{5.0} = \frac{-20}{5.0} =
-4.0\,\text{m/s}^{2}$$

The acceleration is $-4.0\,\text{m/s}^{2}$, so the car decelerates at
$4.0\,\text{m/s}^{2}$. A wave in a given medium has a steady speed, so its acceleration
is zero, even though the particles of the medium are vibrating.

</details>
</details>
</details>
