# 9.2 Frequency, wavelength and wave speed — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the parts of a wave from Section 9.1; speed as distance divided by
time from Chapter 2.

**Objectives:** define the frequency, the period and the wavelength of a wave; use
$f = \dfrac{1}{T}$ and $v = f\lambda$; explain why the frequency stays the same when a
wave enters a new medium.

By the end of this section, you can use one equation to link the frequency, the
wavelength and the speed of any wave.

## A wave repeats

A wave on a rope is a bump that repeats. The same shape arrives again and again, and it
is worth measuring two things: how often the wave repeats, and how long one repeat is.

- The **frequency** $f$ is the number of complete waves produced each second. It is
  measured in hertz (Hz).
- The **period** $T$ is the time for one complete wave. It is measured in seconds (s).
- The **wavelength** $\lambda$ is the distance between two successive crests, or between
  any two successive points in step. It is measured in metres (m).

The frequency and the period are reciprocals of each other:

$$f = \frac{1}{T} \qquad T = \frac{1}{f}$$

A wave with a frequency of 50 Hz repeats fifty times every second, so one complete wave
takes $\dfrac{1}{50} = 0.020\,\text{s}$.

## The wave equation

A wave travels at a steady speed through a given medium. In one period it moves forward
by exactly one wavelength. That gives the most useful equation in this chapter.

> **The wave equation.**
>
>
> $$v = f\lambda$$
>
>
>
> - $v$ is the wave speed, in metres per second (m/s);
> - $f$ is the frequency, in hertz (Hz);
> - $\lambda$ is the wavelength, in metres (m).

The equation works for every wave. Use it in the form that suits the quantity you want:

$$v = f\lambda \qquad f = \frac{v}{\lambda} \qquad \lambda = \frac{v}{f}$$

**Example 1.** A wave has a frequency of 50 Hz. Calculate its period.

$$T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{50} = 0.020\,\text{s}$$

**Example 2.** A sound wave has a frequency of 196 Hz and travels through air at
340 m/s. Calculate its wavelength.

$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{340}{196} = 1.7\,\text{m}$$

**Example 3.** Waves travel along a guitar string at 255 m/s. The string sounds a note
of frequency 196 Hz. Calculate the wavelength of the wave on the string.

$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{255}{196} = 1.30\,\text{m}$$

**Example 4.** A wave travels at 340 m/s and has a wavelength of 0.50 m. Calculate its
frequency.

$$f = \frac{v}{\lambda} = \frac{340}{0.50} = 6.8 \times 10^{2}\,\text{Hz}$$

## Frequency comes from the source

When a wave enters a new medium, its frequency does not change. The frequency is set by
the source, and the source keeps vibrating at the same rate. The speed does change,
because the speed depends on the medium. The wavelength changes too, so that $v = f\lambda$
still holds with the new speed and the unchanged frequency.

Sound leaves the string at 196 Hz and reaches your ear at 196 Hz. The wave on the string
travels at 255 m/s, and the sound in the air travels at 340 m/s. The two waves have the
same frequency and different wavelengths.

> **Watch out.** The frequency is not the same as the period. A wave with a frequency of
> 196 Hz has a period of 0.0051 s, and the two numbers are reciprocals. Check the unit of
> every quantity before you substitute: a wavelength in centimetres and a frequency in
> kilohertz both need converting first, and $v = f\lambda$ only works in metres, hertz
> and metres per second.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The string sounds 196 Hz. Waves travel along the string at
255 m/s, and sound travels through the air at 340 m/s.

1. Calculate the wavelength of the wave on the string.
2. Calculate the wavelength of the sound wave in the air.
3. The two waves have the same frequency. Explain why their wavelengths are different.

Keep your answers. Section 9.5 shows that the wavelength on the string is exactly twice
the length of the string, which is how a standing wave fits on it.

## You should now be able to

- define the frequency, the period and the wavelength of a wave, with the unit of each;
- use $f = \dfrac{1}{T}$ to move between a frequency and a period;
- use $v = f\lambda$ in any of its three forms;
- explain why the frequency of a wave does not change when it enters a new medium, while
  its speed and its wavelength do;
- keep a time in seconds and a frequency in hertz apart.
