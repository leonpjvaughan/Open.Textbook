# 9.2 Frequency, wavelength and wave speed — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wave has a crest 8.0 cm above its rest position. State the amplitude of the wave.
2. A car travels 150 m in 6.0 s. Calculate its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The amplitude is 8.0 cm, because the amplitude is the maximum displacement from the
   rest position.
2. $v = \dfrac{s}{t} = \dfrac{150}{6.0} = 25\,\text{m/s}$.

The second one is the shape of the wave equation: a distance divided by a time. In
$v = f\lambda$ the frequency counts the waves and the wavelength is the distance of one
of them.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A wave has a frequency of 25 Hz. Calculate its period.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The period is the reciprocal of the frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$T = \dfrac{1}{25}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{25} = 0.040\,\text{s}$$

A frequency of 25 Hz means 25 complete waves every second, so one wave takes a
twenty-fifth of a second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A wave has a period of 0.20 s. Calculate its frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The frequency is the reciprocal of the period.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$f = \dfrac{1}{0.20}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$f = \frac{1}{T} = \frac{1}{0.20} = 5.0\,\text{Hz}$$

The wave completes five full vibrations every second. Questions 1 and 2 are the same
relationship used in opposite directions.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A wave travels at 8.0 m/s and has a frequency of 4.0 Hz. Calculate its wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $v = f\lambda$ for the wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f} = \dfrac{8.0}{4.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{8.0}{4.0} = 2.0\,\text{m}$$

The unit check: $\dfrac{\text{m/s}}{\text{Hz}} = \dfrac{\text{m/s}}{\text{s}^{-1}} =
\text{m}$, which is a length. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A wave has a wavelength of 0.25 m and a frequency of 12 Hz. Calculate its speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the frequency by the wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = 12 \times 0.25$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = f\lambda = 12 \times 0.25 = 3.0\,\text{m/s}$$

The unit check: $\text{Hz} \times \text{m} = \text{s}^{-1} \times \text{m} =
\text{m/s}$, which is a speed. Correct.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A sound wave has a frequency of 340 Hz and travels through air at 340 m/s.
Calculate its wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\lambda = \dfrac{v}{f}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\lambda = \dfrac{340}{340}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{340}{340} = 1.0\,\text{m}$$

The two numbers happen to be equal, and the wavelength is 1.0 m. Keep the units: the
speed is in m/s and the frequency is in Hz, and the answer is in metres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A wave has a frequency of 4.0 Hz and travels at 1.0 m/s. Calculate its wavelength
in centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the wavelength in metres first, then convert.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\lambda = \dfrac{1.0}{4.0} = 0.25\,\text{m}$, then multiply by 100 to get centimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{1.0}{4.0} = 0.25\,\text{m}$$

$$0.25\,\text{m} = 25\,\text{cm}$$

Working in metres and converting at the end is safer than converting the speed and the
frequency first.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A sound wave passes from air into water. Explain why its frequency does not
change, and why its wavelength does change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which quantity is set by the source, and which is set by the medium?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The source keeps vibrating at the same rate. The speed of the wave depends on the
medium. Use $v = f\lambda$ to link the two.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The frequency is set by the source of the wave. The source keeps vibrating at the same
rate, so the frequency stays the same. The speed of a wave depends on the medium, and
sound travels faster in water than in air, so the speed changes. Since $v = f\lambda$
must still hold with a changed $v$ and an unchanged $f$, the wavelength must change too.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 1

**8.** Convert 1500 ms to seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The prefix milli means $10^{-3}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply by $10^{-3}$, which is the same as dividing by 1000.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$1500\,\text{ms} = 1500 \times 10^{-3} = 1.5\,\text{s}$$

A period is often quoted in milliseconds, and $v = f\lambda$ needs seconds. This
conversion is the step that catches people out when a wave repeats very quickly.

</details>
</details>
</details>
