# 9.1 What a wave is — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Convert 250 g to kilograms.
2. A car travels 240 m in 12 s. Calculate its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $250\,\text{g} = 250 \div 1000 = 0.25\,\text{kg}$.
2. $v = \dfrac{s}{t} = \dfrac{240}{12} = 20\,\text{m/s}$.

Both ideas return in this section. A wave question often hides a unit conversion, and
the speed of a wave is a distance divided by a time.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A wave on a rope has an amplitude of 0.20 m. State the vertical distance from a
crest to the next trough.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The crest is one amplitude above the rest position and the trough is one amplitude
below it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The distance is $2 \times 0.20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2 \times 0.20 = 0.40\,\text{m}$$

The crest is 0.20 m above the rest position and the trough is 0.20 m below it, so the
distance between them is twice the amplitude.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A wave has an amplitude of 0.05 m. State its maximum displacement from the rest
position.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The amplitude **is** the maximum displacement from the rest position.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The maximum displacement is the height of a crest above the rest position.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The maximum displacement is $0.05\,\text{m}$, which is the amplitude. The amplitude is
always written as a positive number, even though the trough has a displacement of
$-0.05\,\text{m}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** One wave has three times the amplitude of another. How many times more energy does
it carry?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The energy depends on the square of the amplitude.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Work out $3^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The energy is proportional to $A^2$, so trebling the amplitude multiplies the energy by

$$3^2 = 9$$

The larger wave carries nine times the energy.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The maximum displacement of a wave from its rest position is 35 cm. State the
amplitude in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are 100 cm in a metre, so divide by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$35 \div 100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$35\,\text{cm} = 0.35\,\text{m}$$

The amplitude is the maximum displacement from the rest position, so it is 0.35 m. Leaving
the answer in centimetres would mix units with the metre in $v = f\lambda$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A wave has an amplitude of 2.0 cm. State the amplitude in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2.0 \div 100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$2.0\,\text{cm} = 0.020\,\text{m}$$

The leading zero matters: 0.020 m is two centimetres, and 0.20 m would be twenty
centimetres, ten times larger.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain what a wave transfers from one place to another, and what it does not
transfer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the rope at the door: what arrived at the far end, and what stayed where it
was?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Name the two quantities, then say what the particles of the medium do.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A wave transfers energy from one place to another. It does not transfer the matter of
the medium. The particles of the medium vibrate about fixed positions, so they pass the
disturbance on to their neighbours and then settle back. On a rope, the bump travels to
the far end while the rope itself stays in place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A sound wave travels through air. State the medium, and state whether the wave is
mechanical or electromagnetic.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which material does the sound travel through?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The medium is the material the wave travels through.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The medium is the air. Sound is a mechanical wave, because it travels by making the
particles of a medium vibrate. That is why sound cannot travel through a vacuum: a
vacuum has no particles to pass the disturbance on.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 2

**8.** A car speeds up from 10 m/s to 22 m/s in 4.0 s. Calculate its acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time taken.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{22 - 10}{4.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{22 - 10}{4.0} = \frac{12}{4.0} = 3.0\,\text{m/s}^{2}$$

A wave speed is steady in a given medium, so a wave does not accelerate in the way this
car does. The wave equation in the next section describes a steady speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>
