# 9.1 What a wave is — Learn

**Prerequisites:** units and prefixes from Chapter 1; speed as distance divided by time
from Chapter 2.

**Objectives:** describe a wave as a disturbance that transfers energy without
transferring matter; tell a mechanical wave from an electromagnetic wave; name the rest
position, displacement, crest, trough and amplitude of a wave; describe how the energy
carried depends on the amplitude.

By the end of this section, you can describe what a wave does and name the parts of one.

## A travelling disturbance

Stretch a rope across the floor and flick one end. A bump runs along the rope to the far
end. The rope itself stays where it is. Something has travelled from your hand to the far
end, and it is not the rope.

That travelling bump is a **wave**. A wave is a disturbance that travels through a
medium or through space and carries **energy** from one place to another. The particles
of the medium vibrate about their own positions. They pass the disturbance on to their
neighbours, and then they settle back.

So a wave transfers energy without transferring matter. That single sentence is the
whole of this section.

## Try it: the rope

**Predict.** Tie a rope to a door handle and hold the other end. Flick your end once, so
that a single bump runs along the rope. Before you flick, write down what you expect the
far end of the rope to do when the bump arrives.

**Test.** Flick the rope and watch the far end. Then flick it twice, in quick
succession, and watch again.

**Explain.** Write two or three sentences. Say what travelled along the rope, what
happened at the far end, and what happened to the rope itself. Your explanation is the
answer to the question *what is a wave?*

## Mechanical and electromagnetic waves

Waves come in two families.

- A **mechanical wave** travels by making the particles of a medium vibrate. It needs a
  medium, so it cannot cross a vacuum. Sound is a mechanical wave. So is a wave on a
  rope, a wave on water, and an ultrasound pulse in a body.
- An **electromagnetic wave** needs no medium. It can travel through a vacuum. Light,
  radio waves, microwaves, infrared, ultraviolet and X-rays are all electromagnetic
  waves.

A **medium** is the material a wave travels through. The air is the medium for sound.
The rope is the medium for the wave you made at the door.

## The parts of a wave

A wave on a rope is straightforward to draw. Freeze the rope at one instant and mark
the features.

- The **rest position** is where the rope sits when no wave passes. It is the straight
  line down the middle.
- The **displacement** of a point is its distance from the rest position at that instant.
  Displacement can be positive or negative.
- A **crest** is a point of maximum positive displacement.
- A **trough** is a point of maximum negative displacement.
- The **amplitude** $A$ is the maximum displacement from the rest position. It is the
  height of a crest above the rest position, and it is always a positive number.

The distance from a crest to the next trough is twice the amplitude, because the crest
is $A$ above the rest position and the trough is $A$ below it.

## Amplitude and energy

A bigger flick puts more energy into the rope, and the crests are taller. The amplitude
measures how much energy the wave carries.

> **Amplitude and energy.** The energy carried by a wave is proportional to the square of
> its amplitude.

Doubling the amplitude gives four times the energy. Trebling the amplitude gives nine
times the energy. A sound wave with a larger amplitude is louder, and a light wave with
a larger amplitude is brighter.

**Example 1.** A wave on a rope has an amplitude of 0.12 m. Calculate the vertical
distance from a crest to the next trough.

The crest sits 0.12 m above the rest position and the trough sits 0.12 m below it, so

$$2 \times 0.12 = 0.24\,\text{m}$$

**Example 2.** One wave has twice the amplitude of another. How many times more energy
does it carry?

The energy depends on the square of the amplitude, so doubling the amplitude multiplies
the energy by $2^2 = 4$. The larger wave carries four times the energy.

**Example 3.** The maximum displacement of a wave from its rest position is 15 cm. State
the amplitude in metres.

The amplitude is the maximum displacement from the rest position, so it is 15 cm. There
are 100 cm in a metre:

$$15\,\text{cm} = 0.15\,\text{m}$$

> **Watch out.** A wave does not carry the medium with it. The far end of the rope does
> not travel to your hand, and the air does not travel from a speaker to your ear. If you
> think the medium moves along with the wave, every later idea about superposition and
> stationary waves becomes hard to follow. Only the energy and the disturbance travel.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. You pluck the guitar string, and the note reaches your ear.

1. State what travels along the string and through the air to your ear.
2. State what stays in place on the string.
3. The note is louder when you pluck the string harder. State what happens to the
   amplitude of the wave on the string.

Keep your answers. Section 9.2 uses the wave on the string to find its wavelength and its
speed.

## You should now be able to

- describe a wave as a travelling disturbance that transfers energy without transferring
  matter;
- tell a mechanical wave from an electromagnetic wave, and give an example of each;
- name the rest position, displacement, crest, trough and amplitude of a wave;
- explain why the distance from a crest to the next trough is twice the amplitude;
- state that the energy carried by a wave is proportional to the square of its
  amplitude.
