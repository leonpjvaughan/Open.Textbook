# Chapter 22: The Physics In Practice Investigation

Two fifths of your Leaving Certificate Physics mark comes from one piece of work.
It is not an examination paper. It is an investigation that you choose, carry out
and report on, and the State Examinations Commission sets the brief.

The work has two phases. First you research an issue from secondary sources, which
are readings and reports that other people have written. Then you run an experiment
of your own and gather primary data, which are readings you take yourself. Finally
you write a report that gives an honest account of what happened, including what
you would change.

## The chapter problem

**Your investigation.** You will choose an issue, research it, gather your own data
on it, and write an honest account of what happened.

This is the largest single piece of work in the course, and it is worth more than
any other. By the end of the chapter you can turn an interest into a testable
question, find and judge the sources that bear on it, design a fair experiment,
process the readings with their uncertainties, and write a report that a reader can
check. The investigation is yours, so the chapter gives you the method rather than
the answer.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- read the SEC brief and turn an interest into a testable question;
- write a hypothesis that physics supports, and say how the readings will test it;
- judge a secondary source for reliability and bias, and record it so you can cite
  it;
- tell primary data from secondary data, and explain why each matters;
- design a fair investigation, with the variables chosen and the errors controlled;
- gather primary data, with repeats, and record it in a clear table;
- process the data, with uncertainties, a graph and a gradient, and test the
  hypothesis against the evidence;
- evaluate the method, say what limited the result, and say what you would change;
- write the report, relate the result to society, and give an honest reflection.

## Where it is used

The **Physics In Practice Investigation** is 40% of the mark at Ordinary and Higher
level, and the written examination is the other 60%. The SEC publishes a common
brief each year, and you submit the report in Year 2 in the format the SEC
prescribes. The work is expected to take up to 20 hours.

The same habits carry into the written paper, where Section A asks you to describe
experiments and their errors. Every experiment in this course, from a falling ball
to a filament lamp, uses the method in this chapter.

## Your turn to connect it

What have you wondered about in physics this year that nobody has given you a
number for?

Think of something you have noticed and wanted to measure: how warm a room stays,
how far a phone signal reaches, how much a bicycle slows on a hill, how bright a
lamp really is. Write down the thing you wondered about, in one sentence, and keep
it. Section 22.1 turns it into a question, and the Review returns to it.
