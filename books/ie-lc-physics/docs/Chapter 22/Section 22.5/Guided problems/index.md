# 22.5 Writing the report — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Name the four qualities the SEC uses to judge the investigation.
2. Convert 0.125 m to millimetres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. Knowledge and understanding; investigating; communicating; relating to society.
   A report that shows all four earns more than one that shows only the first.
2. $0.125\,\text{m} = 0.125 \times 1000 = 125\,\text{mm}$. There are 1000 mm in a
   metre.

Both return here. The qualities decide what the report must contain, and a
measurement is written with its unit and the figures the data support.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** Name three sections of an investigation report, and say what each one
contains.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the purpose, the method and the conclusion.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The purpose states the question. The method gives the steps. The conclusion states
what the evidence shows.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The purpose states the question and the physics being investigated. The method
gives the steps another person would follow to repeat the readings. The conclusion
states what the evidence shows, and whether it supports the hypothesis.

Other sections carry the background research, the results, the analysis, the
evaluation and the references. Together they let a reader follow the work and check
it.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A graph of voltage against angle has its largest mean value at 0°, and the
voltage falls to half by 60°. The uncertainty in each mean is 0.05 V. Write a
conclusion for the investigation.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

State the pattern, give the numbers, and compare the effect with the uncertainty.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The voltage is largest at 0° and falls as the angle grows. The fall is much larger
than 0.05 V.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The evidence supports the hypothesis. The mean voltage is largest when the cell
faces the lamp, at 0°, and it falls as the cell turns away. It has fallen to half
its value by 60°.

The uncertainty in each mean is about 0.05 V, so the fall is much larger than the
uncertainty. The conclusion therefore rests on a real effect, not on the scatter of
the readings.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Explain why an honest account of what did not work is worth more than a
tidy report that hides the problems.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what a reader can judge, and about what the SEC descriptors reward.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

If the problems are hidden, the reader cannot tell how much the result can be
trusted.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An honest account says what limited the readings and how large each problem was. A
report that hides the problems cannot be judged, because the reader cannot see how
much the result can be trusted.

Saying what you would change also shows that you understand the method. The SEC
descriptors reward exactly that: a reflection on what worked and what did not.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Describe how you would relate an investigation into home insulation to
society.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Name what the result means for a household, then weigh the factors.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Consider the money saved, the cost of the work, the carbon dioxide avoided, and who
pays.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

State what the result means for a household, such as the money saved on heating
each year and the warmer room. Then weigh the factors: the cost of the insulation,
the carbon dioxide avoided, and who pays for the work.

Finish with a judgement: what you would recommend, and for whom. A recommendation
that names the payback time and the people who benefit is stronger than a general
statement that insulation is good.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A report has a word limit of 1500 words. A student writes 450 words of
background research and 300 words of method. Calculate the percentage of the limit
used so far.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Add the two sections first, then divide by the limit and multiply by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$450 + 300 = 750$ words. Then $\dfrac{750}{1500} \times 100\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The student has written $450 + 300 = 750$ words. As a percentage of the limit,

$$\frac{750}{1500} \times 100\% = 50\%$$

Half the limit is used by the background and the method, which leaves half for the
results, the analysis, the conclusion and the evaluation. The sections that carry
the marks for investigating and communicating need their share.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain how you would use a source in your report without committing
plagiarism.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are three habits: your own words, quotation marks, and a reference.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write the idea yourself, quote only when the exact words matter, and cite every
source.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Write the idea in your own words, then check that the meaning has not changed. If
you use the exact words, put them in quotation marks and keep the quotation short.
Give the source for every idea or figure you take.

The reference list lets a reader find each source and check it. That is part of
communicating the work, which is one of the four qualities the SEC judges.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 21

**7.** One fission of a uranium-235 nucleus releases about 200 MeV. Convert this
energy to joules, taking $1\,\text{MeV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-13}$ J.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Multiply the energy in MeV by the number of joules in one MeV.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$200 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-13}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = 200 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-13} = 3.2 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{J}$$

The energy of one fission is $3.2 \times 10^{-11}$ J. In a report, the unit is
part of the answer, so the conversion is written out rather than left to the
reader.

</details>
</details>
</details>
