# 10.3 Total internal reflection and the critical angle — Learn

**Prerequisites:** Snell's law and the refractive index from Section 10.2.

**Objectives:** explain what happens when light tries to leave a dense medium at a
large angle; define the critical angle; use $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$; state the two
conditions for total internal reflection; describe one use of it.

By the end of this section, you can find the critical angle of a material and decide
whether light escapes from a surface or is trapped inside.

## Leaving a dense medium

In Section 10.2, light entered glass from air and bent towards the normal. Reverse
the ray and the light leaves the glass and bends away from the normal. The ray inside
the glass is now the one that matters, so the angle of incidence is measured inside
the glass.

As the angle inside the glass increases, the angle of refraction in the air
increases faster. At one special angle the refracted ray leaves along the surface
itself, at 90° to the normal. That angle inside the glass is the **critical angle**
$C$.

Turn the ray a little further and there is no refracted ray at all. All the light is
reflected back inside the glass, obeying the law of reflection. This is **total
internal reflection**.

## The critical angle

At the critical angle the angle of refraction is 90°, so $\sin r = 1$. Putting this
into Snell's law gives a very short relationship.

> **The critical angle.**
> $$\sin C = \frac{1}{n} \qquad \text{or} \qquad n = \frac{1}{\sin C}$$
> $C$ is measured inside the denser medium, from the normal.

The critical angle depends only on the refractive index. A large refractive index
gives a small critical angle.

| Medium | Refractive index | Critical angle |
|---|---|---|
| water | 1.33 | 48.8° |
| glass | 1.50 | 41.8° |
| diamond | 2.42 | 24.4° |

**Example 1.** Calculate the critical angle for a glass-air boundary. The refractive
index of the glass is 1.50.

$$\sin C = \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{1.50} = 0.6667$$

$$C = 41.8^\circ$$

**Example 2.** Calculate the critical angle for a water-air boundary. The refractive
index of water is 1.33.

$$\sin C = \frac{1}{1.33} = 0.7519$$

$$C = 48.8^\circ$$

**Example 3.** The critical angle for a boundary between a transparent material and
air is 45.0°. Calculate the refractive index of the material.

$$n = \frac{1}{\sin C} = \frac{1}{\sin 45.0^\circ} = \frac{1}{0.7071} = 1.41$$

## The two conditions

Total internal reflection happens only when both of these are true.

1. The light is travelling from a **denser** medium into a **less dense** one, such
   as from glass into air. There is no critical angle for light going the other way.
2. The angle of incidence inside the denser medium is **greater than** the critical
   angle.

When light goes from air into glass, the second condition can never be met. The
light can only bend towards the normal, and some of it always enters the glass.

## Uses of total internal reflection

An **optical fibre** carries a light signal along a thin glass core. The core has a
higher refractive index than the cladding around it. Light travelling along the core
meets the boundary at an angle greater than the critical angle, so it is totally
internally reflected and stays inside. A signal can travel for kilometres this way
with very little loss. Broadband internet and medical endoscopes both use optical
fibres.

A **prism** in binoculars or a periscope uses total internal reflection instead of a
silvered mirror. The light meets the inside of the glass face at 45°, which is
greater than the critical angle for glass, so it reflects with no loss at all.

A **diamond** has a very small critical angle of 24.4°. Light that enters a cut
diamond is reflected many times inside it before it leaves, which is why a diamond
sparkles.

> **Watch out.** Total internal reflection is not the same as ordinary reflection,
> and it cannot happen at every boundary. Check the direction of travel first. Light
> going from air into glass always enters the glass, however large the angle of
> incidence. Also compare the angle inside the denser medium with $C$, not the angle
> outside.
> **Re-check:** name the two media and the direction of travel before you use
> $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The laser beam has entered the glass block of
refractive index 1.50 at 40.0°, and inside the glass it makes an angle of 25.4° with
the normal. The ray travels down through the block and meets the vertical side face.

1. Calculate the critical angle for the glass-air boundary.
2. The normal at the side face is horizontal. Show that the angle of incidence at the
   side face is 64.6°.
3. Compare 64.6° with the critical angle, and state whether the light leaves through
   the side face. Explain your answer.

block.

- describe what happens as the angle of incidence inside a dense medium increases;
- define the critical angle and state where it is measured;
- use $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$ to find a critical angle or a refractive index;
- state the two conditions needed for total internal reflection;
- explain how an optical fibre and a prism use total internal reflection.
