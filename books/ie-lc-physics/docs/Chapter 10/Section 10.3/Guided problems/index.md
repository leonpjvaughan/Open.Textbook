# 10.3 Total internal reflection and the critical angle — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A ray of light passes from air into glass at an angle of incidence of 50.0°. State
   whether it bends towards or away from the normal, and why.
2. Calculate the speed of light in a glass of refractive index 1.50. Take the speed
   of light in air as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. It bends towards the normal, because glass is a slower medium than air and the
   light slows down.
2. $c_2 = \dfrac{c_1}{n} = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.50} =
   2.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$.

Refraction is the starting point for this section. Light that bends towards the
normal on the way into a medium bends away from the normal on the way out, and that
is what makes a critical angle possible.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Calculate the critical angle for a glass-air boundary. The refractive index of
the glass is 1.60.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin C = \dfrac{1}{1.60} = 0.625$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin C = \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{1.60} = 0.625$$

$$C = 38.7^\circ$$

A larger refractive index gives a smaller critical angle. This glass traps light
more strongly than glass of refractive index 1.50.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Calculate the critical angle for a water-air boundary. The refractive index of
water is 1.33.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin C = \dfrac{1}{1.33} = 0.7519$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin C = \frac{1}{1.33} = 0.7519$$

$$C = 48.8^\circ$$

A swimmer under the water sees the whole surface above them as a bright circle,
because light from beyond the circle is totally internally reflected.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The critical angle for a boundary between a transparent material and air is
30.0°. Calculate the refractive index of the material.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$ for $n$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$n = \dfrac{1}{\sin 30.0^\circ} = \dfrac{1}{0.5000}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$n = \frac{1}{\sin C} = \frac{1}{\sin 30.0^\circ} = \frac{1}{0.5000} = 2.0$$

A refractive index of 2.0 means the light travels at half the speed it has in air.
The material traps light at any angle above 30°.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Light inside a glass block of refractive index 1.50 meets the glass-air
boundary at an angle of incidence of 40.0°. The critical angle for the glass is
41.8°. State whether the light leaves the glass, and calculate the angle of
refraction if it does.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the angle of incidence with the critical angle first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

40.0° is less than 41.8°, so the light refracts out. Use $\sin r = n\sin i$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angle of incidence, 40.0°, is less than the critical angle, so the light leaves
the glass.

$$\sin r = n\sin i = 1.50 \times \sin 40.0^\circ = 1.50 \times 0.6428 = 0.9642$$

$$r = 74.6^\circ$$

The refracted ray is close to the surface. A slightly larger angle of incidence
would take the light past the critical angle, and no light would leave at all.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Light inside the same glass block meets the glass-air boundary at an angle of
incidence of 50.0°. State what happens to the light, and explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare 50.0° with the critical angle of 41.8°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

50.0° is greater than 41.8°, so no refracted ray leaves the glass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The angle of incidence, 50.0°, is greater than the critical angle of 41.8°, so total
internal reflection occurs. All the light is reflected back inside the glass. The
reflected ray obeys the law of reflection, so its angle of reflection is also 50.0°.
No light leaves through that surface.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain how an optical fibre keeps a light signal inside the fibre.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Name the two parts of the fibre and compare their refractive indices.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The core has the higher refractive index. Compare the angle of incidence at the
boundary with the critical angle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The core of the fibre has a higher refractive index than the cladding around it.
Light in the core meets a boundary with a material of lower refractive index. The
light strikes the boundary at an angle greater than the critical angle, so it is
totally internally reflected back into the core. The signal travels along the fibre
with very little loss, because no light escapes at the walls.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why a cut diamond sparkles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The refractive index of diamond is 2.42.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the critical angle, and think about how many times light can reflect inside the
stone.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The critical angle for diamond is small, about 24.4°, because its refractive index is
large. Light that enters the diamond meets the inside faces at angles greater than
24.4°, so it is totally internally reflected many times. The light finally leaves
from the top face, and the many reflections make the stone sparkle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 10.2

**8.** A ray of light in air enters a glass of refractive index 1.50 at an angle of
incidence of 30.0°. Calculate the angle of refraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Snell's law and rearrange it for $\sin r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin r = \dfrac{\sin 30.0^\circ}{1.50}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin r = \frac{\sin 30.0^\circ}{1.50} = \frac{0.5000}{1.50} = 0.3333$$

$$r = 19.5^\circ$$

The light bends towards the normal, because it has entered a slower medium. This is
the refraction that makes the critical angle possible when the light tries to leave
again.

</details>
</details>
</details>
