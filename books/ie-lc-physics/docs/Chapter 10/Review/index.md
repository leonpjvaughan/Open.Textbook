# Chapter 10: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The laser investigation.** A laser emits red light of wavelength 650 nm. A glass
block has a refractive index of 1.50. The beam meets the top face of the block at an
angle of incidence of 40.0°. The speed of light in air is $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

In these formulas $i$ and $r$ are the angles of incidence and refraction, $n$ the
refractive index, $c_1$ and $c_2$ the speeds of light in air and in the glass, and
$\lambda_1$ and $\lambda_2$ the wavelengths in air and in the glass. $f$ is the
frequency, $C$ the critical angle, $d$ the slit spacing and $\theta$ the angle of a
maximum from the normal.

**Step 1: the reflected ray.** The law of reflection gives the angle of reflection
straight from the angle of incidence, both measured from the normal:

$$r = i = 40.0^\circ$$

The reflected ray leaves the top face at 40.0° on the other side of the normal.

**Step 2: the refracted ray.** Snell's law links the two angles:

$$\sin r = \frac{\sin i}{n} = \frac{\sin 40.0^\circ}{1.50} = \frac{0.6428}{1.50}
= 0.4285$$

$$r = 25.4^\circ$$

The ray inside the glass makes 25.4° with the normal. It bends towards the normal
because the glass is a slower medium.

**Step 3: the speed in the glass.** The refractive index compares the two speeds:

$$c_2 = \frac{c_1}{n} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.50} = 2.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$$

**Step 4: the wavelength and the frequency.** The frequency is set by the laser, so
it does not change. The wavelength shortens in the same ratio as the speed:

$$\lambda_2 = \frac{\lambda_1}{n} = \frac{6.50 \times 10^{-7}}{1.50}
= 4.33 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$$

$$f = \frac{c_1}{\lambda_1} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{6.50 \times 10^{-7}}
= 4.6 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$$

The light is still red inside the glass, because the frequency is unchanged.

**Step 5: the side face.** The normal at the side face is horizontal, so the angle of
incidence there is

$$90^\circ - 25.4^\circ = 64.6^\circ$$

The critical angle for the glass is

$$\sin C = \frac{1}{n} = \frac{1}{1.50} = 0.6667 \qquad C = 41.8^\circ$$

The angle of incidence, 64.6°, is greater than the critical angle, so the light is
totally internally reflected at the side face and stays inside the block.

**Step 6: the narrow gap.** When the student sends the laser through a single narrow
slit, the light spreads out by diffraction. The spreading is large because the slit
width is comparable to the wavelength. With two slits, the two diffracted beams
overlap and interfere. The waves arrive in step at some points, giving bright
fringes, and out of step at others, giving dark fringes.

<!-- tier:h -->
**Step 7: the grating.** The grating has 300 lines per millimetre, so the slit
spacing is

$$d = \frac{0.001}{300} = 3.33 \times 10^{-6}\,\text{m}$$

For the first order, $n = 1$:

$$\sin\theta = \frac{\lambda}{d} = \frac{6.50 \times 10^{-7}}
{3.33 \times 10^{-6}} = 0.195 \qquad \theta = 11.2^\circ$$

For the second order, $n = 2$:

$$\sin\theta = \frac{2\lambda}{d} = 0.390 \qquad \theta = 23.0^\circ$$

The largest order is found from $\dfrac{d}{\lambda} = \dfrac{3.33 \times 10^{-6}}
{6.50 \times 10^{-7}} = 5.13$. The largest whole number below 5.13 is 5, so five
orders are visible on each side of the centre. A sixth order would need
$\sin\theta$ to be greater than 1, which is impossible.
<!-- /tier:h -->

**The answer.** The reflected ray leaves at 40.0°. The ray inside the glass makes
25.4° with the normal, travels at $2.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s, has a wavelength of
433 nm and keeps the frequency $4.6 \times 10^{14}$ Hz. It meets the side face at
64.6°, which is greater than the critical angle of 41.8°, so it is totally
internally reflected. At a narrow slit the light diffracts, and two slits give
bright and dark fringes.
<!-- tier:h -->
A grating with 300 lines per millimetre gives first-order maxima at 11.2°,
second-order maxima at 23.0°, and five visible orders on each side.

and say why. Two of them ask you to choose between Snell's law and the critical

## What you can now do

- state the law of reflection and find an angle from it;
- explain the difference between specular reflection and diffuse reflection;
- explain why light bends when it enters a new medium, and say which way it bends;
- use $n = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$ and Snell's law, $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$;
- find the speed, the wavelength or the frequency of light in a medium, and explain
  why the frequency does not change;
- explain total internal reflection, state its two conditions, and use
  $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$;
- describe diffraction and say when it is most noticeable;
- explain how two coherent waves give bright and dark fringes, using the path
  difference;
- describe what white light does to an interference pattern;
<!-- tier:h -->
- find the slit spacing of a grating and use $n\lambda = d\sin\theta$;
- find the largest visible order, and explain why a grating gives sharp maxima.

## Thinking back

Return to the moment you chose at the start of the chapter — the rainbow, the bent
straw, your face in a window, or the colours on a CD.

You can now name the effect and explain it with the wave model. A bent straw is
refraction, because the light changes speed at the surface and bends. A rainbow and
the colours on a CD come from the way the direction of the light depends on its
wavelength. Your face in a window is reflection, and the window is smooth enough to
give a clear image.

Write down which of the three behaviours your example uses, and one sentence that
explains why. If your example uses more than one, say so. Naming the effect, and
saying what the wave does, is exactly what the Physics In Practice Investigation asks
you to do with your own measurements.
