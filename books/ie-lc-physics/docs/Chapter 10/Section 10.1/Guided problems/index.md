# 10.1 Reflection of waves — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wave has a crest 5.0 cm above its rest position. State the amplitude of the
   wave.
2. A wave has a frequency of 50 Hz and a wavelength of 2.0 m. Calculate its speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The amplitude is 5.0 cm, because the amplitude is the maximum displacement from
   the rest position.
2. $v = f\lambda = 50 \times 2.0 = 100\,\text{m/s}$.

A wave carries energy away from its source, and reflection sends that energy back
towards the source. The wave equation returns at the end of this section, when an
echo is timed.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A ray of light makes an angle of 35° with the surface of a plane mirror.
Calculate the angle of incidence.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The angle of incidence is measured from the normal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The normal is at 90° to the surface, so subtract 35° from 90°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$i = 90^\circ - 35^\circ = 55^\circ$$

The ray is close to the surface, so it is far from the normal. A small angle to the
surface means a large angle of incidence.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A ray of light has an angle of incidence of 30°. State the angle of reflection,
and calculate the angle between the incident ray and the reflected ray.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The law of reflection gives the first angle. The two rays lie on opposite sides of
the normal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle of reflection is 30°. Add the two angles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$r = i = 30^\circ$$

$$i + r = 30^\circ + 30^\circ = 60^\circ$$

The angle between the rays is 60°. The normal sits halfway between them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A ray of light reflects from a plane mirror so that the incident ray and the
reflected ray are at right angles to each other. Calculate the angle of incidence.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The two angles are equal and they add to 90°.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Divide 90° by 2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$i = \frac{90^\circ}{2} = 45^\circ$$

The normal is at 45° to both rays. This is the arrangement used in a periscope,
where a mirror turns a beam through a right angle.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A student claps and hears the echo from a wall 1.2 s later. The speed of sound
in air is 340 m/s. Calculate the distance from the student to the wall.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sound travels to the wall and back, so it covers twice the distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Find the total distance with $s = vt$, then halve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s_{\text{total}} = vt = 340 \times 1.2 = 408\,\text{m}$$

$$s = \frac{408}{2} = 204\,\text{m}$$

The distance to the wall is 204 m.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** An ultrasound pulse is sent into water. The echo returns after 0.040 s. The
speed of ultrasound in water is 1500 m/s. Calculate the depth of the water.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The pulse travels down and back, so the depth is half the total distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$$d = \frac{vt}{2} = \frac{1500 \times 0.040}{2}$$

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = \frac{1500 \times 0.040}{2} = 30\,\text{m}$$

The same method is used in sonar, in radar and in medical ultrasound imaging. The
echo time is measured, and the speed is known.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why a plane mirror gives a clear image of your face, but a white wall
does not.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the surface of the mirror with the surface of the wall.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Think about the direction of the normal at each point, and what happens to rays that
arrive parallel.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A mirror is smooth, so the normal is the same direction at every point. Rays that
arrive parallel reflect in the same direction, and the pattern of the image is
preserved. A wall is rough on the scale of the wavelength of light, so the normal
points in a different direction at each point. Parallel rays leave in many
directions, the light is scattered, and no image forms.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A ray of light reflects from a mirror. The angle between the reflected ray and
the surface of the mirror is 65°. Calculate the angle of incidence.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the angle of reflection first, then use the law of reflection.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle of reflection is $90^\circ - 65^\circ = 25^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$r = 90^\circ - 65^\circ = 25^\circ$$

$$i = r = 25^\circ$$

The angle of incidence is 25°. Check that the reflected ray is close to the surface
and the angle of reflection is small, which agrees with the diagram.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 9.2

**8.** A sound wave has a frequency of 250 Hz and a wavelength of 1.2 m. Calculate
its speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the wave equation from Section 9.2.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = f\lambda = 250 \times 1.2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = f\lambda = 250 \times 1.2 = 300\,\text{m/s}$$

The speed of a sound wave in air is set by the air, not by the frequency. A higher
frequency gives a shorter wavelength, and the product stays the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>
