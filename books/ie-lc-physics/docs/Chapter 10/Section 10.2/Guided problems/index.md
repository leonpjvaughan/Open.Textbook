# 10.2 Refraction and Snell's law — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State what happens to the frequency of a wave when it passes from one medium
   into another.
2. A wave travels at 340 m/s and has a wavelength of 0.50 m. Calculate its
   frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The frequency stays the same, because it is set by the source.
2. $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda} = \dfrac{340}{0.50} = 680\,\text{Hz}$.

Both ideas are used here. The frequency does not change when light enters glass, and
the wave equation still links the speed, the frequency and the wavelength. Only the
speed and the wavelength change.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A ray of light in air meets a water surface at an angle of incidence of 30.0°.
The refractive index of water is 1.33. Calculate the angle of refraction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use Snell's law, $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$, and rearrange it for $\sin r$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin r = \dfrac{\sin 30.0^\circ}{1.33} = \dfrac{0.5000}{1.33}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin r = \frac{\sin 30.0^\circ}{1.33} = \frac{0.5000}{1.33} = 0.3759$$

$$r = 22.1^\circ$$

The ray bends towards the normal, because water is a slower medium than air. The
angle of refraction is smaller than the angle of incidence.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Light travels through air at $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. Calculate its speed in
water of refractive index 1.33.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $n = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$ and rearrange it for $c_2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c_2 = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.33}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c_2 = \frac{c_1}{n} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.33}
= 2.3 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$$

To two significant figures the speed in water is $2.3 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. The water
slows the light to about three quarters of its speed in air.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Light travels at $2.5 \times 10^{8}$ m/s in a sample of glass. Calculate the
refractive index of the glass. Take the speed of light in air as
$3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the speed in air by the speed in the glass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$n = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{2.5 \times 10^{8}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$n = \frac{c_1}{c_2} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{2.5 \times 10^{8}} = 1.2$$

The refractive index is 1.2 and it has no unit. A ratio of two speeds cannot carry
a unit.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A laser emits light of wavelength 600 nm in air. The light enters water of
refractive index 1.33. The frequency does not change. Calculate the wavelength of
the light in the water.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wavelength changes in the same ratio as the speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\lambda_2 = \dfrac{6.00 \times 10^{-7}}{1.33}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda_2 = \frac{\lambda_1}{n} = \frac{6.00 \times 10^{-7}}{1.33}
= 4.51 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$$

That is 451 nm. The light is red in the air and stays red in the water, because the
frequency is unchanged. Only the wavelength and the speed change.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ray of light inside a glass of refractive index 1.50 meets the glass-air
boundary at an angle of incidence of 15.0°. Calculate the angle of refraction in the
air.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The light is going into a faster medium, so it bends away from the normal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\sin r = n\sin i = 1.50 \times \sin 15.0^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin r = 1.50 \times \sin 15.0^\circ = 1.50 \times 0.2588 = 0.3882$$

$$r = 22.8^\circ$$

The angle of refraction is larger than the angle of incidence, because the light
bends away from the normal on leaving the glass.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why a straw looks bent at the surface when it stands in a glass of
water.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Follow the light from the part of the straw that is under the water to your eye.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The light leaves the water and enters the air. Which way does it bend, and where does
your eye think it came from?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Light from the part of the straw under the water travels through the water and then
out into the air. At the surface the light speeds up and bends away from the normal.
Your eye follows the rays back in straight lines, so it places the image of the straw
at a position that is not where the straw really is. The part under the water appears
displaced, so the straw looks bent at the surface.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A ray of light enters a glass block along the normal. State the angle of
refraction, and explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What is the angle of incidence for a ray along the normal?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The angle of incidence is 0°. Put this into Snell's law.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\sin r = \frac{\sin 0^\circ}{n} = 0$$

$$r = 0^\circ$$

The ray is not bent. The whole wavefront arrives at the surface at the same moment,
so every part of it slows at the same time and the direction does not change. The
speed and the wavelength still change.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 10.1

**8.** A ray of light makes an angle of 30° with the surface of a plane mirror.
Calculate the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Measure both angles from the normal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$i = 90^\circ - 30^\circ$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$i = 90^\circ - 30^\circ = 60^\circ$$

$$r = i = 60^\circ$$

Both angles are 60°, measured from the normal. The reflection law from Section 10.1
sets the second angle once the first is known.

</details>
</details>
</details>
