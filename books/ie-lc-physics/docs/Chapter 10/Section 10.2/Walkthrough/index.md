# 10.2 Refraction and Snell's law — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the law of reflection from Section 10.1; the wave equation
$v = f\lambda$ from Section 9.2.

**Objectives:** explain why a wave changes direction when it enters a new medium;
define the refractive index; use $n = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$ and Snell's law,
$n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$; explain why the frequency does not change.

By the end of this section, you can find the angle of refraction and the speed,
wavelength and frequency of light in a new medium.

## Why light changes direction

Put a pencil in a glass of water and look from the side. The pencil appears to bend
at the surface. The light from the pencil does bend there, and the effect is
**refraction**.

Light travels at different speeds in different materials. It travels fastest in a
vacuum, almost as fast in air, and more slowly in water, glass or plastic. When a
ray crosses from one material into another, its speed changes. The change of speed
changes the direction of the ray, unless the ray arrives along the normal.

- Going into a **slower** medium, the ray bends **towards** the normal.
- Going into a **faster** medium, the ray bends **away from** the normal.
- A ray along the normal is **not** bent, because the whole wavefront slows at the
  same time.

The angle of incidence $i$ and the angle of refraction $r$ are both measured from the
normal.

## The refractive index

The **refractive index** of a medium compares the speed of light in a vacuum or air
with its speed in the medium.

> **Refractive index.**
> $$n = \frac{c_1}{c_2}$$
> - $c_1$ is the speed of light in air or a vacuum, in metres per second (m/s);
> - $c_2$ is the speed of light in the medium, in metres per second (m/s).

The refractive index has no unit, because it is a ratio of two speeds. A larger
refractive index means the light is slowed more.

| Medium | Refractive index |
|---|---|
| air | 1.00 |
| water | 1.33 |
| glass | about 1.50 |
| diamond | 2.42 |

**Example 1.** Light travels through air at $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. Calculate its
speed in a glass of refractive index 1.50.

$$c_2 = \frac{c_1}{n} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.50} = 2.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$$

## Snell's law

The angles are linked to the refractive index by **Snell's law**.

> **Snell's law.**
> $$n = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r}$$
> - $i$ is the angle of incidence, measured from the normal;
> - $r$ is the angle of refraction, measured from the normal.

This is the most useful equation in the chapter. It works for light passing in
either direction across a boundary.

**Example 2.** A ray of light in air meets a glass surface at an angle of incidence
of 40.0°. The refractive index of the glass is 1.50. Calculate the angle of
refraction.

Rearranging Snell's law for $\sin r$:

$$\sin r = \frac{\sin i}{n} = \frac{\sin 40.0^\circ}{1.50} = \frac{0.6428}{1.50}
= 0.4285$$

$$r = 25.4^\circ$$

The light bends towards the normal, because it has entered a slower medium.

**Example 3.** A ray of light inside a glass of refractive index 1.50 meets the
glass-air boundary at an angle of incidence of 20.0°. Calculate the angle of
refraction in the air.

$$\sin r = n\sin i = 1.50 \times \sin 20.0^\circ = 1.50 \times 0.3420 = 0.5130$$

$$r = 30.9^\circ$$

The light bends away from the normal, because it has entered a faster medium.

**Example 4.** A ray of light passes from air into a transparent material. The angle
of incidence is 40.0° and the angle of refraction is 25.4°. Calculate the refractive
index of the material.

$$n = \frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \frac{\sin 40.0^\circ}{\sin 25.4^\circ}
= \frac{0.6428}{0.4290} = 1.50$$

## The frequency does not change

When light crosses into a new medium, its **frequency** stays the same. The frequency
is set by the source, and the source keeps vibrating at the same rate. The speed
changes, because the speed depends on the medium. Since $v = f\lambda$ must still
hold, the **wavelength** changes in the same ratio as the speed.

**Example 5.** A laser emits light of wavelength 650 nm in air. The light enters a
glass of refractive index 1.50. Calculate its wavelength in the glass.

$$\lambda_2 = \frac{\lambda_1}{n} = \frac{6.50 \times 10^{-7}}{1.50}
= 4.33 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$$

where $\lambda_1$ is the wavelength in air and $\lambda_2$ the wavelength in the glass.

That is 433 nm. The frequency is unchanged, the speed is smaller and the wavelength
is shorter.

> **Watch out.** Snell's law uses the **sine** of each angle, not the angle itself.
> $\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$ is not the same as $\dfrac{i}{r}$, and using the ratio of
> the angles gives a wrong refractive index. The refractive index also has no unit,
> so an answer in metres or in metres per second is a sign that a step has gone
> wrong.
> **Re-check:** take the sine of both angles before you divide, and confirm that
> $n$ comes out close to 1.5 for glass.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The laser beam enters the glass block of refractive
index 1.50 at an angle of incidence of 40.0°. The wavelength in air is 650 nm, and
the speed of light in air is $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

1. Calculate the angle of refraction inside the glass.
2. Calculate the speed of the light in the glass.
3. Calculate the wavelength of the light in the glass.
4. Calculate the frequency of the light, and explain why this value is the same in
   the glass as in the air.

Keep your answers. Section 10.3 follows the ray inside the block to the side face.

## You should now be able to

- explain why a wave changes direction when its speed changes;
- say whether a ray bends towards or away from the normal at a boundary;
- use $n = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$ to find a refractive index or a speed;
- use Snell's law, $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$, in either direction;
- explain why the frequency stays the same and the wavelength changes when light
  enters a new medium.
