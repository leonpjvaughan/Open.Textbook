# Chapter 10: Reflection, refraction and diffraction

Light travels in straight lines until it meets something. Then it can bounce back,
bend, or spread out. The same three behaviours belong to sound, water waves and
radio signals, so what you learn about light in this chapter describes every wave.

## The chapter problem: the laser investigation

A student sets out three pieces of apparatus on a bench. A laser emits red light of
wavelength 650 nm. A glass block has a refractive index of 1.50. A diffraction
grating has 300 lines per millimetre.

The student shines the laser at the top face of the glass block. The beam meets the
surface at an angle of incidence of 40.0°. Part of the light reflects from the
surface, and the rest enters the glass and travels down through the block.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- the wavelength of the laser light in air is **650 nm**, which is
  **6.50 × 10⁻⁷ m**;
- the refractive index of the glass is **1.50**;
- the speed of light in air is **3.0 × 10⁸ m/s**;
- the angle of incidence at the top face is **40.0°**;
- the grating has **300 lines per millimetre**.

By the end of the chapter you can say where the reflected ray goes, find the angle
of refraction inside the glass and the speed, wavelength and frequency of the light
there, say whether the light escapes through the side face, and explain the pattern
the light makes at a narrow gap.

You can also use the grating to find the angles of the first and second order
maxima.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- state the law of reflection and use it to find an angle;
- explain the difference between a clear reflection from a smooth surface and a
  scattered reflection from a rough one;
- explain why light changes direction when it enters a new medium;
- use $n = \dfrac{c_1}{c_2}$ and Snell's law, $n = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$;
- find the speed, the wavelength or the frequency of light in a medium;
- explain total internal reflection and use $\sin C = \dfrac{1}{n}$;
- list the two conditions needed for total internal reflection, and give a use of it;
- describe diffraction and say when it is most noticeable;
- explain how two coherent waves produce a pattern of bright and dark fringes;

- use $n\lambda = d\sin\theta$ for a diffraction grating, and find the order of a
  maximum.

## Where it is used

A mirror, a camera lens and a periscope all use reflection. Spectacles, a swimming
pool that looks shallower than it is, and a straw that looks bent in a glass of water
all use refraction. Rainbows and prisms split white light because the refractive
index depends on the wavelength. Fibre-optic broadband, medical endoscopes and the
sparkle of a diamond all use total internal reflection. Radio reception behind a hill
and the coloured tracks on a CD both come from diffraction.
