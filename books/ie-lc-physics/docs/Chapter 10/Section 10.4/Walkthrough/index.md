# 10.4 Diffraction and interference — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the principle of superposition from Section 9.4; the wave
equation from Section 9.2.

**Objectives:** describe diffraction and say when it is most noticeable; explain what
coherent sources are; explain how two waves produce bright and dark fringes; describe
what white light does to the pattern.

By the end of this section, you can explain why a wave spreads at a gap and how two
waves combine to give a pattern of bright and dark bands.

## Waves spread at a gap

Stand outside a room and listen to a conversation through an open doorway. The sound
reaches you even when you are not in line with the gap. The sound wave has spread out
as it passed through the doorway. This spreading is **diffraction**.

Diffraction happens when a wave passes through a gap or past an obstacle. The wave
bends around the edges and spreads into the region behind them.

- The spreading is greatest when the width of the gap is about the same as the
  wavelength.
- A gap much wider than the wavelength lets most of the wave travel straight on, with
  only a little spreading at the edges.
- A long wavelength diffracts more than a short wavelength through the same gap.

This is why you can hear a radio station behind a hill, but the television signal
from the same transmitter is much weaker there. Radio waves have wavelengths of
metres, and television waves have wavelengths of centimetres. The radio wave bends
around the hill, and the television wave does not.

## Two waves meet

In Section 9.4 you met the **principle of superposition**. Where two waves meet, the
resultant displacement is the sum of the two separate displacements.

- Where a crest meets a crest, the displacements add and the resultant is large.
  This is **constructive interference**.
- Where a crest meets a trough, the displacements partly or completely cancel. This
  is **destructive interference**.

Two sources are **coherent** when they have the same frequency and a constant phase
difference. Only coherent sources give a steady interference pattern. If the phase
difference changed at random, the bright and dark positions would move and the
pattern would blur.

In practice, one source is split into two. A laser beam passing through two narrow
slits gives two coherent sources, because both slits are lit by the same wave.

## Bright and dark fringes

Light from the two slits travels different distances to reach a point on the screen.
The difference between the two distances is the **path difference**.

- If the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths, the waves arrive in step.
  A bright fringe appears.
- If the path difference is a half number of wavelengths, the waves arrive out of
  step. A dark fringe appears.

In symbols, with $n = 0, 1, 2, 3, \ldots$:

$$\text{bright: } \text{path difference} = n\lambda$$

$$\text{dark: } \text{path difference} = \left(n + \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\lambda$$

where $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the light.

The pattern is a row of bright and dark fringes. The centre is bright, because the
path difference there is zero.

**Example 1.** In a two-slit experiment the path difference to a point on the screen
is $1.3\,\mu\text{m}$. The wavelength of the light is 650 nm. State whether the point
is bright or dark.

$$\frac{\text{path difference}}{\lambda} = \frac{1.3 \times 10^{-6}}
{6.5 \times 10^{-7}} = 2$$

The path difference is 2 wavelengths, which is a whole number, so the point is a
bright fringe.

**Example 2.** In the same experiment the path difference to another point is
$1.625\,\mu\text{m}$. State whether the point is bright or dark.

$$\frac{\text{path difference}}{\lambda} = \frac{1.625 \times 10^{-6}}
{6.5 \times 10^{-7}} = 2.5$$

The path difference is 2.5 wavelengths, which is a half number, so the point is a
dark fringe.

## White light and the pattern

White light contains a range of wavelengths. The position of a bright fringe depends
on the wavelength, so each colour has its maximum at a different angle. Away from the
centre the colours separate into a series of coloured fringes. The centre fringe
stays white, because every wavelength has a maximum there.

This is the same idea that makes a rainbow. The refractive index of water depends
slightly on the wavelength, so different colours bend by different amounts.

> **Watch out.** Diffraction and interference are not the same thing. Diffraction is
> one wave spreading at a gap. Interference is two or more waves combining. A
> two-slit pattern needs both: the slits must be narrow enough to diffract the light
> into overlapping beams, and the two beams must then interfere.
> **Re-check:** ask whether you are describing one wave at a gap or two waves
> meeting.
