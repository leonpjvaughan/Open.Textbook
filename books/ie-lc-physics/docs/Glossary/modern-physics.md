# Glossary — Modern Physics

**Absorption line spectrum** — a spectrum with dark lines where light of certain
wavelengths has been absorbed.

**Activity** — the number of nuclear decays per second; measured in becquerels.

**Alpha particle** — a helium nucleus emitted by a radioactive nucleus.

**Anode** — the positive electrode.

**Antineutrino** — a very small neutral particle emitted with a beta particle.

**Antiparticle** — a particle with the same mass as another but the opposite
charge.

**Atomic mass unit** — a unit of mass equal to one twelfth of the mass of a
carbon-12 atom.

**Atomic number** — the number of protons in a nucleus.

**Background radiation** — ionising radiation from the environment, present at
all times.

**Becquerel** — the unit of activity, equal to one decay per second.

**Beta decay** — the emission of a beta particle from a nucleus, in which a
neutron turns into a proton.

**Binding energy** — the energy needed to separate a nucleus into its individual
nucleons.

**Binding energy per nucleon** — the binding energy divided by the number of
nucleons.

**Boson** — a particle that carries one of the fundamental forces.

**Cathode** — the negative electrode.

**Cathode rays** — streams of electrons emitted from a hot cathode.

**Chain reaction** — a fission reaction in which each fission causes further
fissions.

**Characteristic lines** — sharp X-ray wavelengths produced when an electron
drops to an inner energy level.

**Continuous spectrum** — a spectrum containing every wavelength in a range.

**Control rod** — a rod that absorbs neutrons to slow or stop a chain reaction.

**Corrected count rate** — a count rate with the background count rate
subtracted.

**Critical** — describing a chain reaction that continues at a steady rate.

**Crossed fields** — electric and magnetic fields at right angles to each other.

**Decay constant** — the probability that a given nucleus decays in one second.

**Einstein's photoelectric equation** — the equation that links photon energy,
work function and maximum kinetic energy.

**Electron gun** — a device that produces a beam of electrons.

**Emission line spectrum** — a spectrum containing bright lines at particular
wavelengths.

**Energy level** — a fixed amount of energy that an electron in an atom may
have.

**Excited state** — an energy level above the ground state.

**Fermion** — a particle of matter, such as an electron or a quark.

**Fission products** — the smaller nuclei left after a nucleus splits.

**Ground state** — the lowest energy level of an electron in an atom.

**Half-life** — the time for half the nuclei in a sample to decay.

**Ionisation energy** — the energy needed to remove an electron from an atom.

**Ionising radiation** — radiation with enough energy to remove electrons from
atoms.

**Lepton** — a fundamental particle that does not feel the strong nuclear force,
such as an electron.

**Mass defect** — the difference between the mass of a nucleus and the total
mass of its separate nucleons.

**Mass number** — the total number of protons and neutrons in a nucleus.

**Moderator** — a material that slows neutrons so they can cause further
fissions.

**Nuclear fission** — the splitting of a large nucleus into smaller nuclei, with
the release of energy.

**Nuclear fusion** — the joining of small nuclei to form a larger one, with the
release of energy.

**Nuclear model** — the model of the atom with a small, dense, positive nucleus.

**Pair production** — the creation of a particle and its antiparticle from a
photon.

**Photoelectric effect** — the emission of electrons from a metal surface when
light of a high enough frequency shines on it.

**Photoelectrons** — the electrons emitted in the photoelectric effect.

**Photon** — a packet of electromagnetic energy.

**Planck constant** — the constant $h$ that links the energy of a photon to its
frequency.

**Positron** — the antiparticle of the electron.

**Quantum leap** — a jump of an electron between energy levels.

**Quark** — a fundamental particle from which protons and neutrons are built.

**Radioactive decay** — the spontaneous change of an unstable nucleus, with the
emission of radiation.

**Random** — describing decay in which it is impossible to predict which nucleus
decays next.

**Secondary source** — a source that reports research done by someone else.

**Spontaneous** — happening without any outside cause.

**Stopping voltage** — the potential difference that brings the most energetic
photoelectrons to rest.

**Strong nuclear force** — the force that holds protons and neutrons together in
a nucleus.

**Thermionic emission** — the release of electrons from a hot metal surface.

**Thomson's model** — the model of the atom as a ball of positive charge with
electrons embedded in it.

**Threshold frequency** — the lowest frequency of light that causes the
photoelectric effect.

**Threshold wavelength** — the longest wavelength of light that causes the
photoelectric effect.

**Tube voltage** — the potential difference across an X-ray tube, which sets the
energy of the electrons.

**Weak nuclear force** — the force responsible for beta decay.

**Work function** — the least energy needed to remove an electron from a metal
surface.
