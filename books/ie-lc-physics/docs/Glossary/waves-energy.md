# Glossary — Wave Motion and Energy Transfer

**Absolute zero** — the lowest possible temperature, at which particles have the
least kinetic energy.

**Amplitude** — the greatest distance a particle moves from its rest position.

**Angle of incidence** — the angle between an incoming ray and the normal.

**Angle of reflection** — the angle between a reflected ray and the normal.

**Antinode** — a point on a stationary wave where the amplitude is greatest.

**Celsius** — a temperature scale on which water freezes at $0^\circ$ and boils
at $100^\circ$.

**Change of state** — the change of a substance between solid, liquid and gas.

**Coherent** — having a constant phase difference, so the waves can interfere
steadily.

**Compression** — a region of a longitudinal wave where the particles are closer
together.

**Conduction** — the transfer of heat through a material by particles passing
energy along.

**Constructive interference** — interference in which two waves add to give a
larger amplitude.

**Convection** — the transfer of heat by the movement of a fluid.

**Convection current** — a flow of fluid caused by hot fluid rising and cool
fluid sinking.

**Converging lens** — a lens that brings parallel rays to a focus.

**Crest** — the highest point of a transverse wave.

**Critical angle** — the angle of incidence above which total internal
reflection happens.

**Destructive interference** — interference in which two waves cancel to give a
smaller amplitude.

**Diffraction** — the spreading of a wave as it passes an edge or a gap.

**Diffraction grating** — a surface with many parallel slits that splits light
into orders.

**Diffuse reflection** — reflection from a rough surface, which scatters the
rays.

**Dispersion** — the splitting of white light into its colours by refraction.

**Diverging lens** — a lens that spreads parallel rays apart.

**Doppler effect** — the change in the observed frequency of a wave when the
source and the observer move relative to each other.

**Echo** — a reflected sound heard after the original sound.

**Electromagnetic spectrum** — the family of electromagnetic waves, from radio
waves to gamma rays.

**Electromagnetic wave** — a wave made of oscillating electric and magnetic
fields, which travels through a vacuum.

**First overtone** — the next harmonic above the fundamental.

**Focal length** — the distance from a lens to its principal focus.

**Fundamental** — the lowest frequency at which a body vibrates.

**Grating equation** — the formula $n\lambda=d\sin\theta$ that links order,
wavelength and slit spacing.

**Harmonic** — a frequency that is a whole-number multiple of the fundamental.

**Heat** — energy transferred from a hotter body to a colder one.

**Heating curve** — a graph of temperature against time as a substance is
heated.

**Infrasound** — sound with a frequency below $20$ Hz.

**Internal energy** — the total kinetic and potential energy of the particles in
a body.

**Irradiance** — the power of radiation arriving per unit area.

**Kelvin** — the SI unit of temperature, starting at absolute zero.

**Law of reflection** — the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.

**Lens** — a shaped piece of transparent material that refracts light to form an
image.

**Lens formula** — the formula that links object distance, image distance and
focal length.

**Light year** — the distance light travels in one year.

**Longitudinal wave** — a wave in which the particles vibrate along the
direction of travel.

**Loudness** — how loud a sound seems, which depends on its amplitude.

**Magnification** — the ratio of the image height to the object height.

**Mechanical wave** — a wave that needs a material medium to travel.

**Medium** — the material a wave travels through.

**Node** — a point on a stationary wave that does not move.

**Normal** — the line drawn at right angles to a surface at the point where a
ray meets it.

**Octave** — the interval between a note and another with twice its frequency.

**Optical fibre** — a thin glass fibre that carries light by total internal
reflection.

**Order** — the number of wavelengths of path difference in a diffraction
pattern.

**Overtones** — the harmonics above the fundamental.

**Path difference** — the difference in the distances two waves travel to the
same point.

**Pitch** — how high or low a sound seems, which depends on its frequency.

**Polarised** — having vibrations in one plane only.

**Principal focus** — the point at which rays parallel to the axis meet after
passing through a lens.

**Principle of superposition** — the rule that overlapping waves add to give a
resultant displacement.

**Prism** — a block of glass that disperses light into a spectrum.

**Quality** — the character of a sound, set by the overtones present.

**Radiation** — the transfer of heat by electromagnetic waves.

**Rarefaction** — a region of a longitudinal wave where the particles are
farther apart.

**Real image** — an image that can be caught on a screen.

**Reflection** — the bouncing of a wave off a surface.

**Refraction** — the change of direction of a wave as it passes from one medium
to another.

**Refractive index** — the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to its speed
in a medium.

**Resonance** — the large amplitude that results when a body is driven at its
natural frequency.

**Rest position** — the position a particle sits in when no wave passes.

**Sine** — the trigonometric ratio of the opposite side to the hypotenuse.

**Slit spacing** — the distance between the centres of two neighbouring slits in
a grating.

**Snell's law** — the rule that links the angles of incidence and refraction to
the refractive indices.

**Solar constant** — the irradiance of the Sun's radiation at the Earth.

**Specific heat capacity** — the energy needed to raise the temperature of one
kilogram of a substance by one kelvin.

**Specific latent heat** — the energy needed to change the state of one kilogram
of a substance without changing its temperature.

**Specific latent heat of fusion** — the energy needed to melt one kilogram of a
substance.

**Specific latent heat of vaporisation** — the energy needed to boil one
kilogram of a substance.

**Spectrometer** — an instrument that measures the wavelengths in light.

**Specular reflection** — reflection from a smooth surface, which keeps the rays
in order.

**Speed of light** — the speed at which light travels in a vacuum, about
$3\times10^8$ m/s.

**Stationary wave** — a wave pattern formed when two waves of the same frequency
travel in opposite directions.

**Thermal equilibrium** — the state in which two bodies are at the same
temperature and no heat flows.

**Total internal reflection** — the complete reflection of a wave inside a
medium when it meets a boundary at more than the critical angle.

**Transverse wave** — a wave in which the particles vibrate at right angles to
the direction of travel.

**Trough** — the lowest point of a transverse wave.

**Ultrasound** — sound with a frequency above $20$ kHz.

**Vacuum** — a space with no matter in it.

**Vacuum flask** — a container that reduces all three methods of heat transfer.

**Virtual image** — an image that cannot be caught on a screen.

**Wave** — a disturbance that transfers energy without transferring matter.

**Wave equation** — the formula $v=f\lambda$ that links wave speed, frequency
and wavelength.

**Wavefront** — a line joining points on a wave that are in step.

**Wavelength** — the distance between two neighbouring crests.
