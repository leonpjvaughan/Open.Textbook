# Glossary — Forces and Motion

**Absolute uncertainty** — the size of the range within which a measured value
lies, in the unit of the measurement.

**Acceleration** — the rate of change of velocity.

**Accurate** — close to the true value.

**Air resistance** — the drag force that opposes motion through the air.

**Angular velocity** — the rate at which an angle changes, measured in radians
per second.

**Anomalous** — a reading that does not fit the pattern of the others.

**Assumption** — something taken as true to build a model, without proving it.

**Base quantity** — one of the quantities from which all others are built, such
as length, mass or time.

**Base unit** — the unit of a base quantity, such as the metre or the kilogram.

**Centripetal acceleration** — the acceleration of a body moving in a circle,
directed towards the centre.

**Centripetal force** — the resultant force that keeps a body moving in a
circle.

**Coefficient of friction** — the constant $\mu$ that links the limiting
friction to the normal reaction.

**Contact force** — a force that acts where two surfaces touch.

**Control variable** — a quantity kept the same in an investigation so the test
is fair.

**Deceleration** — acceleration that reduces the speed.

**Dependent variable** — the quantity you measure in an investigation.

**Derived unit** — a unit built from base units, such as the newton.

**Displacement** — the distance moved in a stated direction; a vector.

**Distance** — the total length of the path travelled; a scalar.

**Distance–time graph** — a graph of distance against time.

**Drag** — a resistive force that opposes motion through a fluid.

**Efficiency** — the useful energy or power output written as a fraction of the
input.

**Elastic limit** — the point beyond which a stretched spring does not return to
its natural length.

**Elastic potential energy** — the energy stored in a stretched or compressed
spring.

**Equations of motion** — the formulae that link displacement, velocity,
acceleration and time for constant acceleration.

**Escape velocity** — the least speed a body needs to leave a planet's
gravitational field.

**Evidence** — observations or measurements that support or question an idea.

**Fair test** — an investigation in which only the independent variable is
changed.

**Free-body diagram** — a diagram showing one body alone, with an arrow for
every force acting on it.

**Free fall** — motion under gravity alone, with no air resistance.

**Frequency** — the number of complete cycles each second.

**Friction** — a contact force that opposes sliding between two surfaces.

**Geostationary orbit** — an orbit above the equator with a period of one day,
so the satellite stays above one point.

**Gravitational constant** — the constant $G$ in Newton's law of universal
gravitation.

**Gravitational field strength** — the force of gravity per unit mass at a
point, measured in newtons per kilogram.

**Gravitational potential** — the work done per unit mass in bringing a body
from infinity to a point.

**Gravitational potential energy** — the energy a body has because of its
position in a gravitational field.

**Hooke's law** — the extension of a spring is proportional to the force
applied, up to the elastic limit.

**Impulse** — the product of a force and the time for which it acts.

**Independent variable** — the quantity you choose to change in an
investigation.

**Inverse-square law** — a rule in which one quantity is inversely proportional
to the square of another.

**Kepler's third law** — the result that the square of a planet's period is
proportional to the cube of its orbital radius.

**Kinetic energy** — the energy a body has because of its motion.

**Kinetic friction** — friction acting on a body that is already sliding.

**Limit of proportionality** — the point beyond which extension is no longer
proportional to force.

**Mass** — the quantity of matter in a body, measured in kilograms.

**Momentum** — the product of a body's mass and velocity.

**Natural length** — the length of a spring when no force acts on it.

**Net force** — another name for the resultant force.

**Newton's first law** — a body stays at rest or moves at a constant velocity
unless a resultant force acts on it.

**Newton's law of universal gravitation** — the force between two masses is
proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the
square of the distance between them.

**Newton's second law** — the resultant force on a body equals its mass times
its acceleration.

**Newton's third law** — if one body pushes another, the second pushes back with
an equal force in the opposite direction.

**Non-contact force** — a force that acts without the bodies touching, such as
gravity.

**Orbit** — the path of a body that moves around another under gravity.

**Peer review** — checking scientific work by other experts before it is
accepted.

**Percentage uncertainty** — the absolute uncertainty written as a percentage of
the measured value.

**Period** — the time for one complete cycle.

**Physical quantity** — something that can be measured and given a number and a
unit.

**Power** — the rate at which energy is transferred.

**Precise** — giving readings that are close to one another when repeated.

**Principle of conservation of energy** — energy cannot be created or destroyed,
only transferred from one store to another.

**Random error** — a scatter in readings caused by small, unpredictable changes.

**Reliable** — giving consistent results when the measurement is repeated.

**Resultant force** — the single force that has the same effect as all the
forces acting together.

**Scalar** — a quantity that has a size but no direction.

**Scientific model** — a simplified description of a real system, used to
explain and predict.

**Significant figures** — the digits that carry meaning in a measured value.

**SI system** — the international system of units used in science.

**Speed** — the rate of change of distance, without a direction.

**Spring constant** — the force needed to stretch a spring by one metre.

**Static friction** — friction acting on a body that is not yet sliding.

**Systematic error** — an error that shifts every reading in the same direction.

**Tension** — the pulling force in a string or spring.

**Terminal velocity** — the constant speed reached when the drag equals the
driving force.

**Thermal energy** — energy transferred because of a temperature difference.

**Uncertainty** — the range within which the true value of a measurement is
expected to lie.

**Unit** — the standard amount a quantity is measured in.

**Velocity** — the rate of change of displacement; a vector.

**Velocity–time graph** — a graph of velocity against time.

**Weight** — the force of gravity on a body, equal to its mass times the
gravitational field strength.

**Work** — the energy transferred when a force moves a body through a distance.

**Work-energy model** — a way to solve motion problems by tracking energy
transfers instead of forces.
