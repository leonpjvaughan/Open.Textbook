# Glossary — Electric and Magnetic Fields

**Alternating current** — a current that reverses direction at a steady
frequency.

**Ammeter** — an instrument that measures current, connected in series.

**Circuit breaker** — a switch that opens a circuit when the current is too
large.

**Conductor** — a material in which charge can move freely.

**Conservation of charge** — charge cannot be created or destroyed, only moved.

**Coulomb's law** — the force between two point charges is proportional to the
product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance
between them.

**Couple** — two equal and opposite forces that turn a body without moving it.

**Direct current** — a current that flows in one direction only.

**Eddy currents** — circulating currents induced in a piece of metal by a
changing magnetic field.

**Electric charge** — a property of matter that makes bodies attract or repel;
measured in coulombs.

**Electric current** — the rate of flow of electric charge.

**Electric field** — a region in which a charge experiences a force.

**Electric field strength** — the force per unit positive charge at a point.

**Electric potential** — the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a
charge from infinity to a point.

**Electromagnet** — a magnet made from a coil of wire carrying a current, often
with an iron core.

**Electromotive force** — the energy supplied by a source per unit charge that
passes through it.

**Electron** — a negatively charged particle found outside the nucleus of an
atom.

**Elementary charge** — the smallest amount of charge, the charge on one
electron.

**Equipotential surfaces** — surfaces on which the electric potential is the
same everywhere.

**Faraday's law of induction** — the induced emf is proportional to the rate of
change of magnetic flux linkage.

**Fleming's left-hand rule** — a rule that gives the direction of the force on a
current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.

**Flux linkage** — the product of the magnetic flux and the number of turns of a
coil.

**Free electron** — an electron that can move through a conductor.

**Fuse** — a thin wire that melts and breaks a circuit when the current is too
large.

**Grounding** — connecting a body to the Earth so that charge can flow away.

**Induced charge** — charge separated in a conductor by a nearby charged body.

**Insulator** — a material in which charge cannot move freely.

**Internal resistance** — the resistance inside a source of emf.

**Kilowatt-hour** — a unit of energy equal to the energy used by a one-kilowatt
appliance in one hour.

**Lenz's law** — the induced current opposes the change that produced it.

**Light-dependent resistor** — a resistor whose resistance falls as the light
falling on it increases.

**Lost volts** — the potential difference dropped across the internal resistance
of a source.

**Magnet** — a body that attracts iron and produces a magnetic field.

**Magnetic field** — a region in which a magnetic pole or a moving charge
experiences a force.

**Magnetic flux** — the amount of magnetic field passing through an area;
measured in webers.

**Magnetic flux density** — the strength of a magnetic field, measured in
teslas.

**Motor effect** — the force on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic
field.

**Mutual induction** — the production of an emf in one coil by a changing
current in another.

**Neutral** — having equal positive and negative charge.

**Neutron** — an uncharged particle in the nucleus of an atom.

**Ohmic conductor** — a conductor that obeys Ohm's law.

**Ohm's law** — the current through a conductor is proportional to the potential
difference across it, at constant temperature.

**Open circuit** — a circuit with a break, so no current flows.

**Peak value** — the greatest value reached by an alternating quantity.

**Permittivity of free space** — the constant $\varepsilon_0$ in Coulomb's law.

**Point charge** — a charge treated as if it were all at one point.

**Potential difference** — the energy transferred per unit charge between two
points.

**Primary coil** — the input coil of a transformer.

**Proton** — a positively charged particle in the nucleus of an atom.

**Quantised** — existing only in whole-number multiples of a smallest amount.

**Radial field** — a field whose lines point directly towards or away from a
single point.

**Resistance** — the opposition a component offers to the flow of current.

**Right-hand grip rule** — a rule that gives the direction of the magnetic field
around a current-carrying wire or coil.

**r.m.s. value** — another name for the root mean square value.

**Root mean square** — the value of an alternating current that gives the same
heating effect as a steady direct current.

**Secondary coil** — the output coil of a transformer.

**Slip rings** — rings that carry current from a rotating coil without twisting
the wires.

**Solenoid** — a long coil of wire that acts as a magnet when a current flows.

**Step-down transformer** — a transformer that lowers an alternating voltage.

**Step-up transformer** — a transformer that raises an alternating voltage.

**Terminal potential difference** — the potential difference across the
terminals of a source when a current flows.

**Tesla** — the unit of magnetic flux density.

**Thermistor** — a resistor whose resistance changes with temperature.

**Uniform field** — a field whose strength and direction are the same
everywhere.

**Variable resistor** — a resistor whose resistance can be changed.

**Voltmeter** — an instrument that measures potential difference, connected in
parallel.

**Weber** — the unit of magnetic flux.
