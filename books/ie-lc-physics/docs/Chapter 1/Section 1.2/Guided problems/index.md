# 1.2 Measuring, accuracy and uncertainty — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Convert 0.25 kg to grams.
2. How many significant figures are in the value 0.040 m?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $0.25 \times 1000 = 250\,\text{g}$.
2. Two. The leading zeros do not count, and the trailing zero after the decimal point
   does count.

Both ideas return here: a measurement carries a unit, and its digits carry
information. The uncertainty is the third thing a measurement needs.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A ruler is marked in millimetres. State the reading uncertainty of a single
reading, in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The reading uncertainty is half the smallest division.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The smallest division is $0.001\,\text{m}$. Halve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{0.001}{2} = 0.0005\,\text{m}$$

So the reading uncertainty is $\pm 0.0005\,\text{m}$, which is $\pm 0.5\,\text{mm}$.
Halving the smallest division is the convention used in this course for an analogue
scale.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A length is measured as 0.080 m, with a reading uncertainty of $\pm 0.001$ m.
Calculate the percentage uncertainty.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Divide the uncertainty by the reading, then multiply by 100.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{0.001}{0.080} \times 100\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{0.001}{0.080} \times 100\% = 1.25\%$$

So the length is $0.080\,\text{m} \pm 1.25\%$. Notice that this is smaller than the
$2.5\%$ for a 0.040 m reading: the same uncertainty matters less on a longer reading.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Two lengths are added: 0.250 m $\pm$ 0.002 m and 0.140 m $\pm$ 0.002 m. Find
the total and its absolute uncertainty.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For a sum, add the absolute uncertainties.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Total $= 0.250 + 0.140$. Uncertainty $= 0.002 + 0.002$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{total} = 0.250 + 0.140 = 0.390\,\text{m}$$

$$\text{uncertainty} = 0.002 + 0.002 = 0.004\,\text{m}$$

The total is $0.390\,\text{m} \pm 0.004\,\text{m}$. When quantities are added, their
absolute uncertainties add.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A mass of 2.0 kg $\pm$ 1% is moving at 3.0 m/s $\pm$ 4%. The kinetic energy is
$\frac{1}{2}mv^2$. Find the percentage uncertainty in the kinetic energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed is squared, so its percentage uncertainty is doubled before the
percentages are added.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$1\% + 2 \times 4\%$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The percentage uncertainty from the speed is $2 \times 4\% = 8\%$, because $v$ is
squared. The percentage uncertainty from the mass is $1\%$.

$$\text{total percentage uncertainty} = 1\% + 8\% = 9\%$$

For a product or a quotient, the percentage uncertainties add, and a power multiplies
its own percentage uncertainty.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A student times a fall five times and records 0.52 s, 0.50 s, 0.54 s, 0.51 s
and 0.53 s. Find the mean and the uncertainty as half the range.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the mean first, then the range, then halve it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Mean $= \dfrac{0.52 + 0.50 + 0.54 + 0.51 + 0.53}{5}$. Range $= 0.54 - 0.50$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{mean} = \frac{2.60}{5} = 0.52\,\text{s}$$

$$\text{range} = 0.54 - 0.50 = 0.04\,\text{s} \qquad \text{uncertainty} = \frac{0.04}{2} = 0.02\,\text{s}$$

The result is $0.52\,\text{s} \pm 0.02\,\text{s}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A balance reads the mass of a block as 0.480 kg. The true mass is 0.500 kg.
State whether the reading is accurate, and describe the kind of error that is
present.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the reading with the true value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The reading is 0.020 kg below the true value.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The reading is not accurate, because it is 0.020 kg below the true mass. A single
reading cannot tell you whether the error is random or systematic, but a reading that
sits consistently to one side of the true value points to a systematic error.

A zero error on the balance would do this. Repeating the measurement would give the
same low value, so a mean would not help. The balance needs to be checked and set to
zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 1.1

**7.** A ball falls 1.50 m in 0.55 s. Calculate its average speed. State the unit of
the answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average speed is distance divided by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \dfrac{1.50}{0.55}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \frac{d}{t} = \frac{1.50}{0.55} = 2.7\,\text{m/s}$$

where $v$ is the average speed, $d$ the distance fallen and $t$ the time taken. Both readings carry two significant figures, so the answer does too. The unit is
metres per second, m/s.

This is an average speed for the whole fall. The ball starts from rest and speeds up,
so it is moving faster than 2.7 m/s at the end.

</details>
</details>
</details>
