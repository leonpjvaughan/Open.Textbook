# Chapter 1: Measuring, modelling and investigating

Physics is a set of measurements and a set of models. A measurement is a number with a
unit and an uncertainty. A model is a simplified picture of what is happening. This
chapter shows you how to take a measurement honestly, and how to judge a model against
it.

## The chapter problem

**How fast does a dropped ball actually fall?** You drop a ball and time its fall.
Then you use the simplest model of free fall to predict the time and the speed. The two
numbers will not agree exactly.

By the end of the chapter you can say which part of the difference comes from the model
and which part comes from your measurement. That is the whole of experimental physics
in one question.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- state the seven SI base quantities and their units, and convert between prefixes;
- write a measured value to the number of significant figures the instrument supports;
- explain the difference between accuracy and precision, and between random and
  systematic error;
- calculate the absolute and percentage uncertainty in a single reading and in a
  result;
- explain what a scientific model is, and say how a model is tested and changed;
- plan an investigation with independent, dependent and controlled variables;
- read a gradient, an intercept and an area from a graph, with their units.

## Where it is used

Every experiment in the course, and the **Physics In Practice Investigation**, which
is 40% of your Leaving Certificate mark. The habits in this chapter are the ones that
investigation asks you to show.
