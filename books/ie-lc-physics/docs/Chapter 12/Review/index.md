# Chapter 12: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**Sunlight on the school roof.** A school has a solar panel 2.0 m long and 1.0 m
wide. The irradiance of the sunlight at the panel is 1000 W/m$^2$. The Sun is
$1.5 \times 10^{11}$ m from the Earth, and light travels at
$3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. A student spreads the light with a prism and focuses it
with a converging lens of focal length 12.0 cm.

In these formulas $t$ is a time, $d$ a distance, $c$ the speed of light, $f$ a
frequency, $\lambda$ a wavelength and $A$ an area. Also $P$ is a power, $I$ an
irradiance, $E$ an energy, $m$ a magnification, and $u$ and $v$ the object and
image distances.

**Step 1: the spectrum.** The seven families, from the longest wavelength to the
shortest, are radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray and gamma.
The frequency runs the other way, from the lowest for radio to the highest for
gamma, because $c = f\lambda$ holds for all of them with the same speed $c$.

**Step 2: the spectrum from the prism.** White light is a mixture of all the
visible wavelengths. In glass, violet travels more slowly than red, so the glass
has a larger refractive index for violet and bends it most. The prism bends violet
most and red least, so the colours leave in different directions and form a
spectrum. X-rays and gamma rays are strongly ionising, and ultraviolet is ionising
but less strongly.

**Step 3: how long the sunlight took.** The light crosses the distance from the
Sun at the speed of light:

$$t = \frac{d}{c} = \frac{1.5 \times 10^{11}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}} = 500\,\text{s}$$

That is 8.3 minutes. The sunlight that reaches the roof left the Sun about eight
minutes earlier.

**Step 4: the frequency of the red light.** Red light has a wavelength of 700 nm,
which is $7.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m:

$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{7.0 \times 10^{-7}}
= 4.3 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$$

The frequency does not change when the light enters the glass. Only the speed and
the wavelength change.

**Step 5: the power on the panel.** The area is

$$A = 2.0 \times 1.0 = 2.0\,\text{m}^2$$

The irradiance is power per unit area, so

$$P = IA = 1000 \times 2.0 = 2000\,\text{W}$$

That is 2.0 kW.

**Step 6: the energy in five hours.** Five hours is 18000 s, so

$$E = Pt = 2000 \times 18000 = 3.6 \times 10^{7}\,\text{J}$$

**Step 7: the lens.** Sunlight arrives as parallel rays, because the Sun is so far
away. A converging lens brings parallel rays to the principal focus, so the image
of the Sun forms 12.0 cm from the lens. That is where the small solar cell is
placed.

When the student puts a small illuminated object 18.0 cm from the lens,

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{12.0} - \frac{1}{18.0} = \frac{3}{36} - \frac{2}{36}
= \frac{1}{36}$$

so $v = 36.0$ cm and

$$m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{36.0}{18.0} = 2.0$$

The image is real, inverted and twice as tall as the object.

**The answer.** The panel has an area of 2.0 m$^2$, receives a power of 2000 W and
an energy of $3.6 \times 10^{7}$ J in five hours. The sunlight took 500 s, or
8.3 minutes, to reach the roof, and the red light has a frequency of
$4.3 \times 10^{14}$ Hz. A prism spreads the sunlight into a spectrum with violet
bent most. The lens forms the image of the Sun at its focus, 12.0 cm away, and an
object at 18.0 cm gives a real, inverted image at 36.0 cm with a magnification of
2.0.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which relationship
applies** and say why. Two of them ask you to choose between the wave equation and
the lens formula, and two come from earlier chapters.

1. List the seven families of the electromagnetic spectrum in order of increasing
   wavelength.
2. A radio wave has a wavelength of 2.0 m. Calculate its frequency. Take the
   speed of light as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.
3. A star is 6.0 light years from the Earth. State how long its light takes to
   reach us, and explain your answer.
4. A solar panel has an area of 4.0 m$^2$. The irradiance of the sunlight is
   500 W/m$^2$. Calculate the power the panel receives.
5. The irradiance 1.0 m from a lamp is 900 W/m$^2$. Calculate the irradiance
   3.0 m from the lamp.
6. Explain why ultraviolet radiation is ionising but visible light is not.
7. A converging lens has a focal length of 10.0 cm. An object is placed 30.0 cm
   from the lens. Calculate the image distance and the magnification.
8. A converging lens has a focal length of 10.0 cm. An object is placed 8.0 cm
   from the lens. Calculate the image distance, and state whether the image is
   real or virtual.
9. Light passes from air into a glass of refractive index 1.50 at an angle of
   incidence of 30.0°. Calculate the angle of refraction. Which chapter does this
   method come from?
10. A wave has a frequency of 200 Hz and a wavelength of 1.7 m. Calculate its
    speed. Which chapter does this method come from?

## What you can now do

- describe an electromagnetic wave, and say why it needs no medium;
- order the seven families of the electromagnetic spectrum, and give a source, a
  use and the ionising ability of each;
- explain why a prism spreads white light into a spectrum, and say which colour is
  bent most;
- state the speed of light in a vacuum and use $c = f\lambda$;
- calculate the time light takes to travel a distance, and explain the light
  year;
- define irradiance and use $I = \dfrac{P}{A}$ to find a power or an energy;
- explain the inverse square law for a point source;
- explain why high-frequency radiation is ionising;
- describe converging and diverging lenses, and their focal points;
- use $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v}$ and $m = \dfrac{v}{u}$ to find
  an image position and size;
- tell a real image from a virtual image, and say which one a screen can catch;
- use $P = \dfrac{1}{f}$ and $P = P_1 + P_2$.

## Thinking back

Return to the moment you chose at the start of the chapter — the rainbow, the
prism, the magnifying glass, the spectacles or the colours on a CD.

You can now name what the light was doing. A rainbow and a prism both use
dispersion: the glass or the raindrop slows violet more than red, so the colours
leave in different directions. A magnifying glass and spectacles use a converging
lens to form an image, and the magnifying glass works because the object sits
inside the focal length, where the image is virtual and upright.

Write down which of the two effects your example uses, and one sentence that
explains it. If your example uses both, say so. Naming the effect, and saying what
the light does, is exactly what the Physics In Practice Investigation asks you to
do with your own measurements.
