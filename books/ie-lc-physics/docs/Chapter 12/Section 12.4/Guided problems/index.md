# 12.4 Lenses and images — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State the unit of the focal length of a lens.
2. A lens brings a beam of parallel rays to a focus 20 cm from the lens. State
   the focal length.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The metre, or a smaller unit such as the centimetre. A distance is a length.
2. The focal length is 20 cm. The focal length is the distance from the centre of
   the lens to the principal focus.

Both ideas are used here. The three distances in the lens formula must be in the
same unit, and the focal length is one of them.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A converging lens has a focal length of 10.0 cm. An object is placed
15.0 cm from the lens. Calculate the image distance.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v}$ and rearrange it for $v$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{10.0} - \dfrac{1}{15.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{10.0} - \frac{1}{15.0} = \frac{3}{30} - \frac{2}{30}
= \frac{1}{30}$$

So $v = 30.0$ cm. The image distance is positive, so the image is real and forms
on the far side of the lens.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** For the lens in question 1, calculate the magnification.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $m = \dfrac{v}{u}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$m = \dfrac{30.0}{15.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{30.0}{15.0} = 2.0$$

The image is twice as tall as the object. The image is real, so it is inverted as
well as magnified.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A converging lens has a focal length of 20.0 cm. An object is placed
60.0 cm from the lens. Calculate the image distance and the magnification.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find $v$ first, then divide by $u$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{20.0} - \dfrac{1}{60.0} = \dfrac{3}{60} - \dfrac{1}{60}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{20.0} - \frac{1}{60.0} = \frac{3}{60} - \frac{1}{60}
= \frac{2}{60} = \frac{1}{30.0}$$

So $v = 30.0$ cm and

$$m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{30.0}{60.0} = 0.50$$

The image is real, inverted and half the size of the object. This is the kind of
image a camera forms of a distant object.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A converging lens has a focal length of 8.0 cm. An object is placed 6.0 cm
from the lens. Calculate the image distance, and state whether the image is real
or virtual.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The object is inside the focal length. Do not be surprised if the answer is
negative.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{8.0} - \dfrac{1}{6.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{8.0} - \frac{1}{6.0} = \frac{3}{24} - \frac{4}{24}
= -\frac{1}{24}$$

So $v = -24$ cm. The negative sign means the image is virtual. It is on the same
side of the lens as the object, and it is upright and magnified. This is how a
magnifying glass works.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A converging lens has a focal length of 0.20 m. Calculate the power of the
lens.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The power is the reciprocal of the focal length, and the focal length must be in
metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$P = \dfrac{1}{0.20}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{0.20} = 5.0\,\text{m}^{-1}$$

That is 5.0 dioptres. A shorter focal length gives a larger power, so a 0.10 m
lens would have a power of $10\,\text{m}^{-1}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain the difference between a real image and a virtual image.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ask whether light really arrives at the image, and whether a screen would show it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A real image forms where the rays meet. A virtual image forms where the rays only
appear to come from.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A real image forms where the rays actually meet, so light really arrives at that
point. It can be caught on a screen, and for a single converging lens it is
inverted.

A virtual image forms where the rays only appear to come from. No light actually
meets there, so it cannot be caught on a screen. For a single converging lens it
is upright. The image you see in a magnifying glass is virtual, which is why you
can see it but a screen placed behind the lens shows nothing.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Describe the difference between a converging lens and a diverging lens.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the shape in the middle, and about what happens to a beam of parallel
rays.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A converging lens is thicker in the middle and brings the rays together. A
diverging lens is thinner in the middle and spreads them apart.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A converging lens is thicker in the middle. It brings a beam of parallel rays to a
focus, and its focal length is positive.

A diverging lens is thinner in the middle. It spreads a beam of parallel rays
apart, as if they came from a focus behind the lens. Its focal length is negative
in the real-is-positive convention.

The focal length is the distance from the centre of the lens to the principal
focus. A shorter focal length means a more strongly curved and more powerful lens.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 10.2

**8.** A ray of light passes from air into a glass block. State which way the ray
bends, and explain why.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the speed of light in air and in glass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Light travels more slowly in glass, so the ray bends towards the normal.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The ray bends towards the normal. Light travels more slowly in glass than in air,
so its speed drops as it crosses the surface. The change of speed changes the
direction of the wavefront, and the ray bends towards the normal.

A lens uses this bending at two curved surfaces. The rays from the object bend at
each surface, and the lens brings them together to form the image.

</details>
</details>
</details>
