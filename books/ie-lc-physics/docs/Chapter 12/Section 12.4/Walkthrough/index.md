# 12.4 Lenses and images — Learn

**Prerequisites:** refraction and the refractive index from Section 10.2.

**Objectives:** describe a converging lens and a diverging lens; define the
principal focus and the focal length; use
$\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v}$ and $m = \dfrac{v}{u}$; tell a real
image from a virtual image; use $P = \dfrac{1}{f}$ for one lens and
$P = P_1 + P_2$ for two thin lenses in contact.

By the end of this section, you can find where a lens forms an image, how big the
image is, and whether it is real or virtual.

## Two kinds of lens

A **lens** is a shaped piece of transparent material that bends light by
refraction. The curved surfaces change the direction of the rays.

- A **converging lens** is thicker in the middle. It brings a beam of parallel
  rays together at a point.
- A **diverging lens** is thinner in the middle. It spreads a beam of parallel
  rays apart, as if they had come from a point.

The point where the parallel rays meet, or appear to meet, is the **principal
focus**. The **focal length** $f$ is the distance from the centre of the lens to
the principal focus. A short focal length means a strongly curved, powerful lens.

## The lens formula

The position of the image is found from the **lens formula**:

$$\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{u} + \frac{1}{v}$$

- $f$ is the focal length;
- $u$ is the distance from the object to the lens;
- $v$ is the distance from the image to the lens.

All three distances are measured from the centre of the lens, and they must be in
the same unit. The book uses the **real-is-positive** convention:

- $f$ is positive for a converging lens and negative for a diverging lens;
- $u$ is positive for a real object;
- $v$ is positive for a real image and negative for a virtual image.

**Example 1.** A converging lens has a focal length of 12.0 cm. An object is
placed 18.0 cm from the lens. Calculate the image distance.

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{f} - \frac{1}{u} = \frac{1}{12.0} - \frac{1}{18.0}
= \frac{3}{36} - \frac{2}{36} = \frac{1}{36}$$

So $v = 36.0$ cm. The image distance is positive, so the image is real. It forms
on the far side of the lens.

## Magnification

The **magnification** tells you how many times bigger the image is than the
object.

$$m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{\text{image height}}{\text{object height}}$$

The magnification has no unit, because it is a ratio of two lengths.

**Example 2.** For the lens in Example 1, calculate the magnification.

$$m = \frac{v}{u} = \frac{36.0}{18.0} = 2.0$$

The image is twice as tall as the object. A real image from a single converging
lens is inverted, so the image appears upside down on the screen.

**Example 3.** A converging lens has a focal length of 15.0 cm. An object is
placed 40.0 cm from the lens. Calculate the image distance and the magnification.

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{15.0} - \frac{1}{40.0} = \frac{8}{120} - \frac{3}{120}
= \frac{5}{120} = \frac{1}{24.0}$$

So $v = 24.0$ cm and

$$m = \frac{24.0}{40.0} = 0.60$$

The image is real, inverted and smaller than the object. This is what a camera
does.

## Real and virtual images

A **real image** forms where the rays actually meet. Light really arrives at that
point, so a real image can be caught on a screen. For a single converging lens a
real image is inverted.

A **virtual image** forms where the rays only appear to come from. No light
actually meets there, so a virtual image cannot be caught on a screen. For a
single converging lens a virtual image is upright.

If the object is closer to a converging lens than the focal length, the image is
virtual. The lens formula gives a negative value of $v$ for this case.

**Example 4.** A converging lens has a focal length of 12.0 cm. An object is
placed 8.0 cm from the lens. Calculate the image distance, and state whether the
image is real or virtual.

$$\frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{12.0} - \frac{1}{8.0} = \frac{2}{24} - \frac{3}{24}
= -\frac{1}{24}$$

So $v = -24$ cm. The image distance is negative, so the image is virtual. It is on
the same side of the lens as the object, and it is upright and magnified. This is
how a magnifying glass works.

## The power of a lens

The **power** of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length.

$$P = \frac{1}{f}$$

The focal length must be in metres, so the unit of power is the inverse metre,
m$^{-1}$, also called the dioptre. A shorter focal length means a larger power.

For two thin lenses in contact, the powers add:

$$P = P_1 + P_2$$

**Example 5.** A converging lens has a focal length of 0.25 m. Calculate its
power.

$$P = \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{0.25} = 4.0\,\text{m}^{-1}$$

That is 4.0 dioptres, the sort of value used for reading spectacles.

## The lens in use

A lens forms an image of whatever it is pointed at. The kind of image depends on
where the object sits.

- An object **beyond twice the focal length** gives a real, inverted, diminished
  image. This is a camera or the eye looking at something far away.
- An object **between one and two focal lengths** gives a real, inverted,
  magnified image. This is a projector.
- An object **inside the focal length** gives a virtual, upright, magnified
  image. This is a magnifying glass.

Light from the Sun arrives as parallel rays, because the Sun is so far away.
Parallel rays are brought to a focus at the principal focus, so the image forms at
a distance $f$ from the lens. That is how the student in the chapter problem
concentrates sunlight onto a small solar cell.

## You should now be able to

- describe a converging lens and a diverging lens, and their focal points;
- define the focal length, and say what a short focal length means;
- use $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v}$ to find an image distance;
- use $m = \dfrac{v}{u}$ to find a magnification;
- tell a real image from a virtual image, and say which can be caught on a
  screen;
- use $P = \dfrac{1}{f}$ and $P = P_1 + P_2$.
