# Chapter 12: Light and electromagnetic radiation

Light is the fastest thing there is, and the only thing you can see. It is one
member of a family of waves that all travel at the same speed and fill the world
with energy. In this chapter you meet the whole family, find out how fast it
travels, see how much energy it carries, and learn how a lens uses it to make an
image.

## The chapter problem

**Sunlight on the school roof.** A school fits a solar panel on its roof. The
panel is 2.0 m long and 1.0 m wide. On a clear day the sunlight arrives with an
irradiance of 1000 W/m$^2$. The Sun is $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ m from the Earth, and
light travels through space at $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

A student investigates the light. She spreads it into a spectrum with a glass
prism, and uses a converging lens of focal length 12.0 cm to focus it.

By the end of the chapter you can order the families of the electromagnetic
spectrum, explain why a prism separates the colours, work out how long sunlight
takes to reach the roof, calculate the power and the energy the panel receives,
and find where the lens forms an image and how big it is.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- the speed of light in a vacuum is $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s;
- the distance from the Sun to the Earth is $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ m;
- the irradiance of the sunlight at the panel is 1000 W/m$^2$;
- the panel is 2.0 m long and 1.0 m wide;
- the lens has a focal length of 12.0 cm.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- describe an electromagnetic wave, and say why it needs no medium;
- put the seven families of the electromagnetic spectrum in order of wavelength
  and frequency;
- give a source, an everyday use and the ionising ability of each family;
- explain why a prism spreads white light into a spectrum;
- state the speed of light in a vacuum and use $c = f\lambda$;
- calculate the time light takes to travel a distance, and explain the light
  year;
- define irradiance and use $I = \dfrac{P}{A}$ to find a power or an energy;
- explain why the irradiance from a point source falls with the square of the
  distance;
- describe converging and diverging lenses and their focal points;
- use $\dfrac{1}{f} = \dfrac{1}{u} + \dfrac{1}{v}$ and $m = \dfrac{v}{u}$ to find
  an image position and size;
- tell a real image from a virtual image.

## Where it is used

Radio and television, wi-fi and mobile phones. Microwave ovens, thermal cameras
and remote controls. Sunlight for warmth, sight and solar panels. Sunscreen and
vitamin D from ultraviolet. Medical X-rays and cancer treatment with gamma rays.
Spectacles, cameras, microscopes and telescopes all use lenses.
