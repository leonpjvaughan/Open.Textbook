# 12.2 The speed of light — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State the unit of frequency.
2. A wave travels at 300 m/s and has a wavelength of 0.60 m. Calculate its
   frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The hertz, Hz. A frequency of 1 Hz means one wave every second.
2. $f = \dfrac{v}{\lambda} = \dfrac{300}{0.60} = 500\,\text{Hz}$.

Both ideas are used here. The wave equation links speed, frequency and
wavelength, and light obeys it with $c$ in place of $v$.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Light travels $3.0 \times 10^{11}$ m from a satellite to a ground station.
Calculate the time it takes. Take the speed of light as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Time is distance divided by speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$t = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{11}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = \frac{d}{c} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{11}}{3.0 \times 10^{8}} = 1000\,\text{s}$$

That is about 17 minutes. A signal to a satellite in a high orbit and back takes a
noticeable time, and the delay is used to measure the distance to the satellite.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Light travels through a vacuum at $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. Calculate its
speed in water of refractive index 1.33.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $n = \dfrac{c}{v}$ and rearrange it for $v$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.33}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \frac{c}{n} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.33} = 2.3 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$$

To two significant figures the speed is $2.3 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. Water slows the
light to about three quarters of its speed in a vacuum.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A radio station broadcasts at 90 MHz. Calculate the wavelength of the
radio waves. Take the speed of light as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change 90 MHz into hertz first. The prefix M means $10^{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$90\,\text{MHz} = 9.0 \times 10^{7}\,\text{Hz}$. Now use
$\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{9.0 \times 10^{7}}
= 3.3\,\text{m}$$

To two significant figures the wavelength is 3.3 m. This is the band used by FM
radio, and the wavelength is a few metres.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Green light has a wavelength of 550 nm. Calculate its frequency. Take the
speed of light as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change 550 nm into metres, then use $c = f\lambda$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$550\,\text{nm} = 5.5 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$. Then
$f = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{5.5 \times 10^{-7}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{5.5 \times 10^{-7}}
= 5.5 \times 10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$$

Every colour of visible light has a frequency of a few hundred trillion hertz.
This is why the frequencies of light are usually written in scientific notation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A light year is the distance light travels in one year. Take one year as
$3.15 \times 10^{7}$ s. Calculate the distance of one light year in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Distance is speed multiplied by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$d = 3.0 \times 10^{8} \times 3.15 \times 10^{7}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$d = ct = 3.0 \times 10^{8} \times 3.15 \times 10^{7} = 9.5 \times 10^{15}\,\text{m}$$

That is about $9.5 \times 10^{12}$ km. A light year is a distance, so an answer
in seconds or years would be wrong.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why the frequency of light does not change when the light passes
from air into glass, even though its speed does change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What sets the frequency of a wave?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The frequency is set by the source. The number of waves arriving each second must
match the number leaving the source.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The frequency is set by the source, which keeps vibrating at the same rate. The
waves that arrive at the glass surface each second are the same waves that left
the source, so the number passing any point each second cannot change.

The speed does change, because light travels more slowly in glass. Since
$c = f\lambda$ must still hold, the wavelength shortens in the same ratio as the
speed. The colour of the light is set by its frequency, so the light stays the
same colour inside the glass.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain what a light year is, and say why astronomers use it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Start with what it measures, not with how long it takes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

It is the distance light travels in one year. The distances between stars are
enormous.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A light year is the distance light travels through a vacuum in one year. It is
about $9.5 \times 10^{15}$ m.

Astronomers use it because the distances between stars are so large that metres
and kilometres become unwieldy. A light year is also convenient, because it tells
you directly how old the light is. A star 4.2 light years away is seen as it was
4.2 years ago.

A light year is a distance, not a time. The name is misleading, so check the unit
in your answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 10.2

**8.** Light of wavelength 600 nm in air enters a glass of refractive index 1.50.
The frequency does not change. Calculate the wavelength of the light in the glass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The wavelength changes in the same ratio as the speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\lambda_2 = \dfrac{6.00 \times 10^{-7}}{1.50}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda_2 = \frac{\lambda_1}{n} = \frac{6.00 \times 10^{-7}}{1.50}
= 4.00 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$$

That is 400 nm. The light is orange in air and stays the same colour in the
glass, because the frequency is unchanged. Only the speed and the wavelength
change.

</details>
</details>
</details>
