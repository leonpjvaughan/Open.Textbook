# 12.3 Energy in electromagnetic radiation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State the unit of power.
2. A machine transfers 3000 J of energy in 6.0 s. Calculate its power.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The watt, W. One watt is one joule each second.
2. $P = \dfrac{E}{t} = \dfrac{3000}{6.0} = 500\,\text{W}$.

Both ideas are used here. Irradiance is a power per unit area, and an energy is a
power multiplied by a time.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A solar panel has an area of 3.0 m$^2$. The irradiance of the sunlight is
1000 W/m$^2$. Calculate the power the panel receives.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Irradiance is power per unit area, so power is irradiance times area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$P = 1000 \times 3.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$P = IA = 1000 \times 3.0 = 3000\,\text{W}$$

That is 3.0 kW. Check the unit: $\text{W/m}^2 \times \text{m}^2 = \text{W}$, so
the area has cancelled and only the watt is left.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A panel receives a power of 500 W for 4.0 hours. Calculate the energy it
receives.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the time into seconds first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$4.0\,\text{hours} = 4.0 \times 3600 = 14400\,\text{s}$. Then use $E = Pt$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = Pt = 500 \times 14400 = 7.2 \times 10^{6}\,\text{J}$$

That is 7.2 MJ. The hours had to become seconds, because a watt is a joule each
second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** The irradiance of the sunlight at the Earth is 800 W/m$^2$. Calculate the
irradiance at a distance twice as far from the Sun.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Doubling the distance divides the irradiance by four.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$I_2 = 800 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{2}\right)^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$I_2 = I_1 \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^2 = 800 \times \left(\frac{1.0}{2.0}\right)^2
= 800 \times \frac{1}{4} = 200\,\text{W/m}^2$$

The same power now spreads over four times the area, so the irradiance is one
quarter of its value.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** The irradiance 1.0 m from a lamp is 40 W/m$^2$. Calculate the irradiance
4.0 m from the lamp.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The distance is four times larger. What happens to the square of the distance?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\left(\dfrac{1.0}{4.0}\right)^2 = \dfrac{1}{16}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$I_2 = I_1 \left(\frac{d_1}{d_2}\right)^2 = 40 \times \left(\frac{1.0}{4.0}\right)^2
= 40 \times \frac{1}{16} = 2.5\,\text{W/m}^2$$

Four times the distance gives one sixteenth of the irradiance. The lamp is a
point source, so the inverse square law applies.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Define irradiance and state its unit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

It is a power, but not the total power. What is it divided by?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Power divided by area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Irradiance is the power of the radiation falling on unit area. It is measured in
watts per square metre, W/m$^2$, and it is calculated as

$$I = \frac{P}{A}$$

A large panel and a small panel in the same sunlight have the same irradiance,
because the irradiance does not depend on the size of the surface.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why ultraviolet radiation can damage living cells, but infrared
radiation does not.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the frequencies, and then the energy in one photon.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Ultraviolet has the higher frequency, so each of its photons carries more energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Ultraviolet has a much higher frequency than infrared, and the energy in one
photon grows with the frequency. So an ultraviolet photon carries more energy than
an infrared photon.

An ultraviolet photon carries enough energy to knock an electron out of an atom,
so ultraviolet is ionising. Ionising atoms inside a cell can damage the cell.
Infrared photons do not carry enough energy to ionise an atom. Infrared warms a
surface instead, because it makes the atoms vibrate more.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A solar panel must receive a power of 5000 W. The irradiance of the
sunlight is 1000 W/m$^2$. Calculate the area the panel needs.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $I = \dfrac{P}{A}$ for the area.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$A = \dfrac{5000}{1000}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$A = \frac{P}{I} = \frac{5000}{1000} = 5.0\,\text{m}^2$$

A larger power at the same irradiance needs a larger area. A real panel also has
an efficiency below 100 per cent, so it would need to be larger still.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**8.** A heater has a power of 2.0 kW. It runs for 30 minutes. Calculate the
energy it transfers.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change the power into watts and the time into seconds.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2.0\,\text{kW} = 2000\,\text{W}$ and $30\,\text{min} = 1800\,\text{s}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = Pt = 2000 \times 1800 = 3.6 \times 10^{6}\,\text{J}$$

This is the same calculation as the energy a solar panel receives from sunlight.
The power and the time are multiplied in both cases, and the hours or minutes must
become seconds first.

</details>
</details>
</details>
