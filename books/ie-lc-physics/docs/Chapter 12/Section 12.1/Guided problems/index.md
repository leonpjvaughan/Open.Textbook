# 12.1 The electromagnetic spectrum — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A wave has a frequency of 250 Hz and a wavelength of 1.2 m. Calculate its
   speed.
2. Name one wave that needs a medium and one wave that does not.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $v = f\lambda = 250 \times 1.2 = 300\,\text{m/s}$.
2. A sound wave needs a medium. A light wave does not need one, because it is an
   electromagnetic wave.

Both ideas are used here. Every electromagnetic wave travels at the same speed in
a vacuum, and the wave equation still links the speed, the frequency and the
wavelength.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Write the wavelength of red light, 700 nm, in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

One nanometre is $1 \times 10^{-9}$ m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Multiply 700 by $1 \times 10^{-9}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$700\,\text{nm} = 700 \times 10^{-9}\,\text{m} = 7.0 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$$

Writing the wavelength in metres matters, because the speed of light is in metres
per second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A microwave oven uses radiation of wavelength 0.12 m. Calculate its
frequency. Take the speed of light as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $c = f\lambda$ and rearrange it for $f$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$f = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{0.12}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{0.12}
= 2.5 \times 10^{9}\,\text{Hz}$$

That is 2500 MHz, which is in the microwave part of the spectrum. The answer is
larger than the frequency of a radio wave, because the wavelength is shorter.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A radio station broadcasts at 100 MHz. Calculate the wavelength of the
radio waves. Take the speed of light as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First change 100 MHz into hertz. The prefix M means $10^{6}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$100\,\text{MHz} = 1.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{Hz}$. Now use
$\lambda = \dfrac{c}{f}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda = \frac{c}{f} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.0 \times 10^{8}}
= 3.0\,\text{m}$$

A wavelength of a few metres is typical of radio and television signals. It is
millions of times longer than the wavelength of visible light.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** State which of these is ionising: visible light, ultraviolet, microwaves,
gamma rays. Explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Ionising means carrying enough energy to remove an electron from an atom. Which
end of the spectrum has the most energy per photon?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The energy per photon grows with frequency. The highest frequencies are at the
short-wavelength end.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Ultraviolet and gamma rays are ionising. Visible light and microwaves are not.

Ionising radiation carries enough energy to knock an electron out of an atom,
which leaves the atom with a charge. The energy of one photon grows with the
frequency, so only the high-frequency, short-wavelength end of the spectrum is
ionising. Gamma rays are strongly ionising, and ultraviolet is ionising but less
strongly.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain why a prism spreads a beam of white light into a spectrum.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed of light in glass depends on the wavelength. Which colour is slowed
most?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Violet is slowed most, so the glass has a larger refractive index for violet and
bends it most.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

White light is a mixture of all the visible wavelengths. In glass the speed of
light depends on the wavelength, so violet travels more slowly than red. The glass
therefore has a slightly larger refractive index for violet than for red, and a
larger refractive index bends the ray more.

The prism bends violet most and red least. The two surfaces of the prism are at
an angle, so the small difference in bending builds up, and the colours leave in
slightly different directions. They land at different places on the screen, so a
spectrum appears.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Give one source and one everyday use for infrared, and one source and one
everyday use for X-rays.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what produces each one, and about what it can pass through.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Infrared comes from anything warm. X-rays come from an X-ray tube and pass through
soft tissue.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Infrared comes from any warm object, including the Sun. It is used for thermal
imaging, for remote controls and for heaters.

X-rays come from an X-ray tube. They are used for medical images and for security
scanners, because they pass through soft tissue but not through bone or metal.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Which has the longer wavelength, an X-ray or a radio wave? Which has the
higher frequency? Explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the order of the table in the section, then use $c = f\lambda$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The radio wave has the longer wavelength. The speed is the same for both, so the
longer wavelength must have the lower frequency.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The radio wave has the longer wavelength. A radio wave can be metres or even
kilometres long, and an X-ray is smaller than a cell.

The two travel at the same speed in a vacuum, and $c = f\lambda$. So the longer
wavelength must have the lower frequency. The X-ray therefore has the higher
frequency, which is why it is ionising and the radio wave is not.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 9.2

**8.** A sound wave travels through air at 340 m/s with a frequency of 500 Hz.
Calculate its wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the wave equation $v = f\lambda$ and rearrange it for $\lambda$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\lambda = \dfrac{340}{500}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{340}{500} = 0.68\,\text{m}$$

This is the same wave equation that light obeys. The difference is that sound
needs a medium, so its speed depends on the air, while every electromagnetic wave
has the same speed in a vacuum.

</details>
</details>
</details>
