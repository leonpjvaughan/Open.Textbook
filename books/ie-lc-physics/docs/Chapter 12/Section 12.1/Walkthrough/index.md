# 12.1 The electromagnetic spectrum — Learn

**Prerequisites:** waves, frequency, wavelength and amplitude from Chapter 9; the
wave equation $v = f\lambda$ from Section 9.2.

**Objectives:** describe an electromagnetic wave; list the seven families of the
electromagnetic spectrum in order of wavelength and frequency; give a source, a
use and the ionising ability of each family; explain why a prism spreads white
light into a spectrum.

By the end of this section, you can place any electromagnetic wave in the
spectrum and say what it is used for.

## A wave that needs no medium

In Chapter 9 you met mechanical waves. A sound wave needs air, and a wave on a
rope needs the rope. An **electromagnetic wave** is different. It travels through
a vacuum, where there is nothing at all.

An electromagnetic wave is made of an oscillating electric field and an
oscillating magnetic field. The two fields are at right angles to each other, and
both are at right angles to the direction the wave travels. So an electromagnetic
wave is a transverse wave.

Every electromagnetic wave travels through a vacuum at the same speed:

$$c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$$

That is the **speed of light**. Light is one member of the family. Radio waves,
microwaves, infrared, ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are the others. They are
all the same kind of wave, and they differ only in their wavelength and
frequency.

## The seven families

The **electromagnetic spectrum** is the whole family, arranged in order of
wavelength. The table gives the order, an approximate wavelength range, a source
and an everyday use.

| Family | Wavelength | Source | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| radio | longer than 0.1 m | a transmitter aerial | broadcasting, wi-fi |
| microwave | 1 mm to 0.1 m | a magnetron, a phone | cooking, mobile phones, radar |
| infrared | 700 nm to 1 mm | any warm object, the Sun | thermal imaging, remote controls, heaters |
| visible | 400 nm to 700 nm | the Sun, a lamp | sight, photography, solar cells |
| ultraviolet | 10 nm to 400 nm | the Sun, a mercury lamp | sterilising, vitamin D, fluorescent lamps |
| X-ray | 0.01 nm to 10 nm | an X-ray tube | medical images, security scanners |
| gamma | shorter than 0.01 nm | a radioactive nucleus | cancer treatment, sterilising |

The table runs from the longest wavelength at the top to the shortest at the
bottom. Because $c = f\lambda$ and $c$ is the same for every one of them, the
frequency runs the other way: radio waves have the lowest frequency and gamma
rays have the highest.

Notice the size of the range. A radio wave can be a kilometre long. A gamma ray
can be smaller than an atomic nucleus. That is a range of more than a hundred
trillion.

## The visible spectrum

The visible range is very narrow. It runs from red light at about 700 nm to
violet light at about 400 nm.

- Red has the longest wavelength in the visible range, so it has the lowest
  frequency.
- Violet has the shortest wavelength, so it has the highest frequency.
- The order in between is red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet.
- White light is a mixture of all the visible wavelengths.

One nanometre is $1 \times 10^{-9}$ m, so 700 nm is $7.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m.

## Dispersion: why a prism makes a spectrum

Shine a beam of white light into a triangular glass prism. A band of colours
appears on the other side, with red at one end and violet at the other. This
spreading is **dispersion**.

Dispersion happens because the speed of light in glass depends on the wavelength.

- In a vacuum every wavelength travels at $c$.
- In glass, violet light travels more slowly than red light.
- So the glass has a slightly larger refractive index for violet than for red.
- A larger refractive index bends the ray more, so violet is bent most and red
  is bent least.

The prism has two surfaces at an angle, so the small difference in bending builds
up. The colours leave the prism travelling in slightly different directions, and
they land at different places on the screen. A rainbow forms in the same way,
with raindrops in place of the prism.

> **Watch out.** Red is bent **least** by a prism, not most. It is tempting to
> remember the order of the colours and then assume that red leads the bending
> too. The reason is the speed: violet is slowed most in the glass, so it is bent
> most. Check your spectrum by asking which end travelled fastest through the
> glass.

**Example 1.** A radio wave has a wavelength of 1.5 m. Calculate its frequency.
Take the speed of light as $3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

$$c = f\lambda \qquad f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{1.5}
= 2.0 \times 10^{8}\,\text{Hz}$$

That is 200 MHz, in the radio part of the spectrum.

**Example 2.** Green light has a wavelength of 550 nm. Write this in metres.

$$550\,\text{nm} = 550 \times 10^{-9}\,\text{m} = 5.5 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$$

**Example 3.** State which of these are ionising: a radio wave, ultraviolet, an
X-ray and infrared.

Ultraviolet, X-rays and gamma rays are ionising. Radio waves and infrared are
not.

## Ionising radiation

**Ionising radiation** carries enough energy to knock an electron out of an atom.
That leaves the atom with a charge, and a charged atom is called an ion. Removing
electrons from the atoms in living cells can damage the cells.

Only the short-wavelength end of the spectrum is ionising.

- **X-rays and gamma rays** are strongly ionising.
- **Ultraviolet** is ionising, but less strongly.
- **Visible, infrared, microwaves and radio waves** are not ionising.

This is why a dentist stands behind a screen during an X-ray, and why you wear
sunscreen. It is also why a radio transmitter is treated differently.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The student shines white sunlight into a glass prism
and sees a band of colours.

1. Name the seven families of the electromagnetic spectrum in order of
   increasing frequency.
2. State which two families in your list are strongly ionising.
3. The student says that red light is bent most because it is at one end of the
   spectrum. Explain why this is wrong, and say which colour is bent most.

  and say why it needs no medium;

- state which families are ionising and explain what ionising means;
- explain dispersion, and say which colour a prism bends most.
