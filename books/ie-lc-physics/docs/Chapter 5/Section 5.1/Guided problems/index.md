# 5.1 Hooke's law — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. What is the unit of force?
2. A spring is 0.20 m long. It is stretched to 0.26 m. What is its extension?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The newton, N. A force of 1 N gives a 1 kg mass an acceleration of
   $1\,\text{m/s}^2$.
2. $0.26 - 0.20 = 0.06\,\text{m}$. The extension is the **change** in length, not the
   new length. If you wrote 0.26 m, that is the length — and this is the mistake that
   costs most marks in this section.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A spring has a spring constant of 25 N/m. Calculate the force needed to stretch
it by 0.20 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which form of $F = kx$ do you need? You are given $k$ and $x$, and asked for $F$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$F = kx = 25 \times 0.20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$F = kx = 25 \times 0.20 = 5.0\,\text{N}$$

The unit works out too: $\text{N/m} \times \text{m} = \text{N}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A force of 18 N stretches a spring by 0.06 m. Calculate its spring constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $F = kx$ for $k$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$k = \dfrac{F}{x} = \dfrac{18}{0.06}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$k = \frac{F}{x} = \frac{18}{0.06} = 300\,\text{N/m}$$

A spring constant of 300 N/m means 300 N would stretch it by a full metre, so this is
a stiff spring.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A spring of spring constant 500 N/m is pulled with a force of 15 N. Calculate its
extension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Now you want $x$, so divide the force by the spring constant.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$x = \dfrac{15}{500}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$x = \frac{F}{k} = \frac{15}{500} = 0.030\,\text{m}$$

That is 30 mm. Check the sense of it: a large spring constant means a small extension
for the same force, and 30 mm is small.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A spring stretches by 0.08 m when a 2.4 N force is applied. Calculate the force
needed to stretch it by 0.20 m, assuming it stays within its limit of proportionality.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two steps, or one ratio. Either find $k$ first, or use the fact that force is
proportional to extension.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$k = \dfrac{2.4}{0.08} = 30\,\text{N/m}$. Then $F = 30 \times 0.20$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$k = \frac{2.4}{0.08} = 30\,\text{N/m} \qquad F = kx = 30 \times 0.20 = 6.0\,\text{N}$$

The ratio way is quicker once you trust it: the extension grew from 0.08 m to 0.20 m,
which is 2.5 times bigger, so the force is 2.5 times bigger: $2.4 \times 2.5 =
6.0\,\text{N}$. Same answer.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A force-extension graph for a spring is a straight line through the origin
passing through the point $(0.05\,\text{m},\ 2.0\,\text{N})$. State the spring constant
and say what the gradient of the line represents.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

$F = kx$ is a straight line through the origin. What is its gradient?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Gradient $= \dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} = \dfrac{2.0}{0.05}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Gradient $= \dfrac{2.0\,\text{N}}{0.05\,\text{m}} = 40\,\text{N/m}$.

Because $F = kx$ is $F = k \times x$, the gradient **is** the spring constant. So
$k = 40\,\text{N/m}$.

That is why the graph is worth drawing: the gradient of the straight part gives $k$
from all the points at once, rather than from one pair that might be misread.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A spring obeys Hooke's law up to an extension of 0.15 m. A student stretches an
identical spring by 0.40 m. Describe the graph of force against extension, and explain
why $F = kx$ cannot be used at 0.40 m.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The limit of proportionality is at 0.15 m. What happens to the line after that?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Straight up to 0.15 m, then bending upwards.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Up to 0.15 m the graph is a straight line through the origin, with gradient equal to
the spring constant. Past 0.15 m it curves upwards: each extra newton gives a larger
extension than the one before.

At 0.40 m the spring is well past its limit of proportionality, so the extension is no
longer proportional to the force. $F = kx$ describes only the straight part, so using
it at 0.40 m gives an answer that is too small.

Stretch it far enough and it passes its elastic limit and does not return to its
natural length at all.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 4

**7.** A box is pushed 3.0 m along a floor by a force of 12 N. Calculate the work done.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Work is force times distance along the direction of the force.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$W = Fd = 12 \times 3.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$W = Fd = 12 \times 3.0 = 36\,\text{J}$$

This matters here: stretching a spring also does work, and Section 5.3 works out where
that energy goes. The difference is that the force changes as the spring stretches, so
you cannot multiply force by extension.

</details>
</details>
</details>
