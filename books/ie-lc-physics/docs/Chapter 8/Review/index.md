# Chapter 8: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The ice-cooled flask.** A vacuum flask holds 1.0 kg of water at 20°C. A student
adds 0.20 kg of ice at 0°C and seals the flask. The specific heat capacity of
water is 4200 J/(kg K), and the specific latent heat of fusion of ice is
$3.34 \times 10^{5}$ J/kg.

In these formulas $m$ is a mass, $L$ the specific latent heat of fusion of ice, $c$
the specific heat capacity of water and $\Delta\theta$ a temperature change. $\theta_f$
is the final temperature of the mixture and $E$ an energy transferred.

**Step 1: the energy needed to melt the ice.** The ice is already at its melting
point, so the energy changes its state and not its temperature.

$$E_{\text{melt}} = mL = 0.20 \times 3.34 \times 10^{5} = 6.68 \times 10^{4}\,\text{J}$$

**Step 2: the energy the warm water can give up.** If the water cooled all the
way to 0°C, it would release

$$E_{\text{cool}} = mc\Delta\theta = 1.0 \times 4200 \times 20 = 8.4 \times 10^{4}\,\text{J}$$

This is more than the $6.68 \times 10^{4}$ J needed to melt the ice. All the ice
can melt, and the final temperature is above 0°C.

**Step 3: the final temperature.** Let the final temperature be $\theta_f$. The
warm water cools from 20°C to $\theta_f$, so it loses

$$E_{\text{lost}} = 1.0 \times 4200 \times (20 - \theta_f)$$

The ice melts and the melted ice warms from 0°C to $\theta_f$, so it gains

$$E_{\text{gained}} = 6.68 \times 10^{4} + 0.20 \times 4200 \times \theta_f$$

The energy lost equals the energy gained:

$$4200(20 - \theta_f) = 66800 + 840\theta_f$$

$$84000 - 4200\theta_f = 66800 + 840\theta_f$$

$$17200 = 5040\theta_f$$

$$\theta_f = \frac{17200}{5040} = 3.4\,°C$$

**The answer.** All the ice melts. The flask holds 1.2 kg of water at about
3.4°C. The final temperature is above 0°C, as Step 2 showed, and below the
starting 20°C.

**Step 4: why the flask slows the transfer.** The vacuum between the walls has no
particles, so it stops conduction and convection. The silvered walls reflect
infrared radiation. The stopper slows conduction and stops convection at the top.
Without these features, energy would flow in from the warmer room and the mixture
would warm up.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide which relationship applies
and say why. Some use $E = mc\Delta\theta$, some use $E = mL$, and some come from
earlier chapters.

1. A room is at 35°C. Write this temperature in kelvin. Take 0°C = 273 K.
2. A 0.40 kg mass of water cools from 90°C to 20°C. Calculate the energy released.
   The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg K).
3. Calculate the energy needed to melt 0.25 kg of ice at 0°C. The specific latent
   heat of fusion of ice is $3.34 \times 10^{5}$ J/kg.
4. A 2.0 kW kettle heats 0.50 kg of water from 20°C to 100°C. Calculate the time
   taken. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg K).
5. Explain why the temperature stays at 100°C while water boils, even though the
   kettle is still heating it.
6. A metal spoon in hot soup becomes hot at the handle. Name the mechanism and
   explain it.
7. A car of mass 1200 kg accelerates from rest to 20 m/s. Calculate its kinetic
   energy. Which chapter does this method come from?
8. A 1.5 kg iron block cools from 200°C to 20°C. Calculate the energy released.
   The specific heat capacity of iron is 450 J/(kg K).
9. A vacuum flask has a vacuum between its walls. Explain how this reduces the
   transfer of energy.

## What you can now do

- explain the difference between temperature and heat, and describe thermal
  equilibrium;
- convert a temperature between the Celsius and kelvin scales;
- use $E = mc\Delta\theta$ to find an energy, a mass, a specific heat capacity or
  a temperature change;
- explain what happens to the particles during a change of state;
- use $E = mL$ for melting, boiling, freezing and condensing;
- work out the total energy when a substance warms and then changes state;
- read a heating curve and explain its flat parts;
- describe conduction, convection and radiation, and compare them;
- explain how a vacuum flask slows the transfer of energy.

## Thinking back

Look at the moment you chose at the start of the chapter, when something heated
up or cooled down.

You can now name the mechanism that carried the energy. If the energy moved
through a solid, the mechanism was conduction. If a fluid moved, it was
convection. If the energy crossed empty space or arrived from the Sun, it was
radiation. You can also say how to slow the transfer, using a poor conductor, a
trapped layer of air, or a shiny surface.

Write down one number for your own choice. It could be a mass, a temperature
change or a time. Then work out the energy transferred, using
$E = mc\Delta\theta$ or $E = mL$. If your number is a rough estimate, write that
down as well. Saying how rough an estimate is belongs in the physics, and it is
what the Physics In Practice Investigation asks you to do.
