# Chapter 8: Heat, temperature and energy transfer

A vacuum flask keeps a hot drink hot and a cold drink cold. It slows the transfer
of energy in both directions. In this chapter you find out what temperature and
heat really are, how much energy a change in temperature needs, how much a change
of state needs, and how energy moves through solids, fluids and empty space.

## The chapter problem: the ice-cooled flask

A vacuum flask holds 1.0 kg of water at 20°C. A student adds 0.20 kg of ice at
0°C and seals the flask. The ice and the water exchange energy until they reach
the same temperature.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- the mass of the water is **1.0 kg**;
- the mass of the ice is **0.20 kg**;
- the water starts at **20°C** and the ice starts at **0°C**;
- the specific heat capacity of water is **4200 J/(kg K)**;
- the specific latent heat of fusion of ice is **3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg**.

By the end of the chapter you can find the energy needed to melt the ice, the
energy the warm water gives up, and the final temperature of the mixture. You can
also explain how the walls of the flask slow each kind of energy transfer.

> **First contact: no working yet.** Predict. The ice is at 0°C and the water is
> at 20°C. Write down whether you think all the ice will melt, and whether the
> final temperature will be above or below 10°C. Give one sentence of reasoning.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- explain the difference between temperature and heat;
- describe what happens when two bodies reach thermal equilibrium;
- convert between the Celsius and kelvin scales;
- use $E = mc\Delta\theta$ to find an energy, a mass, a specific heat capacity or a
  temperature change;
- explain what a change of state is, and why the temperature stays constant;
- use $E = mL$ for melting, boiling, freezing and condensing;
- read a heating curve and explain its flat parts;
- describe conduction, convection and radiation, and compare them;
- explain how insulation slows the transfer of energy.

## Where it is used

A cook heats water and boils it. A fridge moves energy out of the food
compartment by boiling and condensing a refrigerant. A central heating system
carries energy in hot water. A vacuum flask, a sleeping bag and a double-glazed
window all slow the transfer of energy. A heat pump moves energy from the cold
air outside to the warm room inside, and it uses work to do so. In Chapter 9 you
meet waves, and the infrared radiation in this chapter is one member of the
electromagnetic spectrum.
