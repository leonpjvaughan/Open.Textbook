# 8.2 Specific heat capacity — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A room is at 40°C. Write this temperature in kelvin. Take 0°C = 273 K.
2. State what is meant by thermal equilibrium.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $T = 40 + 273 = 313\,\text{K}$.
2. Two bodies are in thermal equilibrium when they are at the same temperature,
   so no net energy flows between them.

Both ideas return here. A temperature change of 1 K is the same as a change of
1°C, and a heating experiment ends when the block and its surroundings reach
thermal equilibrium.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Calculate the energy needed to heat 1.5 kg of water from 20°C to 70°C.
The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg K).

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E = mc\Delta\theta$. Find the temperature change first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\Delta\theta = 70 - 20 = 50\,\text{K}$, then $E = 1.5 \times 4200 \times 50$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = mc\Delta\theta = 1.5 \times 4200 \times 50 = 3.15 \times 10^{5}\,\text{J}$$

The water needs $3.15 \times 10^{5}$ J. To two significant figures that is
$3.2 \times 10^{5}$ J.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A 2.0 kg block gains $9.0 \times 10^{4}$ J of energy, and its temperature
rises by 30 K. Calculate its specific heat capacity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $E = mc\Delta\theta$ to make $c$ the subject.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c = \dfrac{E}{m\Delta\theta} = \dfrac{9.0 \times 10^{4}}{2.0 \times 30}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = \frac{E}{m\Delta\theta} = \frac{9.0 \times 10^{4}}{2.0 \times 30} = 1500\,\text{J/(kg K)}$$

The value is between the values for water and for copper, so the block is not
made of either.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A 0.25 kg copper block is heated by 2340 J. The specific heat capacity of
copper is 390 J/(kg K). Calculate the temperature rise.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $E = mc\Delta\theta$ to make $\Delta\theta$ the subject.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\Delta\theta = \dfrac{E}{mc} = \dfrac{2340}{0.25 \times 390}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\Delta\theta = \frac{E}{mc} = \frac{2340}{0.25 \times 390} = \frac{2340}{97.5} = 24\,\text{K}$$

Copper has a small specific heat capacity, so a small amount of energy gives a
large temperature rise.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A 630 W heater warms 1.5 kg of water for 200 s. Calculate the temperature
rise. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg K).

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Find the energy supplied first, using $E = Pt$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E = 630 \times 200 = 126000\,\text{J}$, then
$\Delta\theta = \dfrac{126000}{1.5 \times 4200}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = Pt = 630 \times 200 = 1.26 \times 10^{5}\,\text{J}$$

$$\Delta\theta = \frac{E}{mc} = \frac{1.26 \times 10^{5}}{1.5 \times 4200} = 20\,\text{K}$$

The water warms by 20 K, which is the same as 20°C.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A 0.40 kg aluminium block cools from 150°C to 50°C. The specific heat
capacity of aluminium is 900 J/(kg K). Calculate the energy released.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The temperature falls, so the block releases energy. Use the size of the
temperature change.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\Delta\theta = 150 - 50 = 100\,\text{K}$, then $E = 0.40 \times 900 \times 100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = mc\Delta\theta = 0.40 \times 900 \times 100 = 3.6 \times 10^{4}\,\text{J}$$

The block releases $3.6 \times 10^{4}$ J to its surroundings as it cools.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Explain why the sea warms more slowly than the land on a sunny day.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Compare the specific heat capacities of water and of rock or soil.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Water has a much larger specific heat capacity than land, so the same energy
gives a smaller temperature rise.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The Sun supplies about the same energy to each square metre of land and sea. The
specific heat capacity of water is about 4200 J/(kg K), which is much larger than
the value for rock or soil.

For the same mass and the same energy, a larger specific heat capacity gives a
smaller temperature rise. The sea therefore warms more slowly than the land, and
it also cools more slowly at night.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** A 500 g iron block is heated from 20°C to 120°C. The specific heat
capacity of iron is 450 J/(kg K). Calculate the energy needed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Write the mass in kilograms before you substitute.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$m = 0.50\,\text{kg}$ and $\Delta\theta = 100\,\text{K}$, so
$E = 0.50 \times 450 \times 100$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = mc\Delta\theta = 0.50 \times 450 \times 100 = 2.25 \times 10^{4}\,\text{J}$$

Using 500 instead of 0.50 would make the answer 1000 times too large.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 8.1

**8.** A beaker of water is at 27°C. Write this temperature in kelvin. Take
0°C = 273 K.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T = \theta + 273$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$T = 27 + 273$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \theta + 273 = 27 + 273 = 300\,\text{K}$$

The beaker is at room temperature. A temperature change in kelvin is the same
size as a change in degrees Celsius, so the value 300 is not used as
$\Delta\theta$ unless the water changes temperature.

</details>
</details>
</details>
