# 8.4 Conduction, convection and radiation — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. State the three ways in which energy can be transferred.
2. State what is meant by thermal equilibrium.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. Energy is transferred by conduction, by convection and by radiation.
2. Two bodies are in thermal equilibrium when they are at the same temperature,
   so no net energy flows between them.

Both ideas return here. Each mechanism moves energy from a hotter place to a
colder place, and each one stops transferring net energy once the two places
reach thermal equilibrium.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Explain how energy is transferred along a metal rod by conduction.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what the particles at the hot end do, and what they pass to their
neighbours.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The particles at the hot end vibrate faster and collide with neighbouring
particles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At the hot end of the rod the particles vibrate faster. They collide with their
neighbours and pass energy along the rod.

The metal also contains free electrons. These electrons move through the metal
and carry energy quickly, so the rod conducts well. The particles themselves stay
in place; only the energy moves along.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Explain why metals are better conductors than wood.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which material has free electrons that can move through it?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Metals have free electrons, and wood does not.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A metal contains free electrons that can move through the metal. These electrons
carry energy quickly from the hot end to the cold end, so metals are good
conductors.

Wood has no free electrons. Its particles pass energy on slowly by collisions, so
wood is a poor conductor. That is why a wooden handle stays cooler than a metal
one.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Explain how a radiator heats a room by convection.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What happens to the density of air when it is warmed?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Warm air expands, becomes less dense and rises. Cooler air moves in to take its
place.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The radiator warms the air next to it. The warm air expands and becomes less
dense, so it rises.

Cooler, denser air moves in to take its place. That air is warmed in turn and
rises as well. The moving air carries energy around the room and forms a
convection current.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A vacuum flask has a vacuum between its two walls. Explain how the vacuum
reduces the transfer of energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which two mechanisms need particles to carry the energy?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Conduction and convection both need particles. A vacuum has no particles.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Conduction transfers energy by particle collisions, and convection transfers
energy by the movement of a fluid. Both mechanisms need particles.

The vacuum between the walls has no particles, so neither mechanism can happen
there. Energy can still cross the vacuum by radiation, so the flask also uses
silvered surfaces to reflect infrared radiation.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain why a dark, matt surface is a better absorber of infrared radiation
than a shiny surface.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about what happens to the radiation at the surface. Is it absorbed or
reflected?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A shiny surface reflects most of the radiation. A dark, matt surface absorbs it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

A shiny, light surface reflects most of the infrared radiation that falls on it,
so little energy is absorbed.

A dark, matt surface reflects very little and absorbs most of the radiation. The
absorbed energy raises the internal energy of the surface, so its temperature
rises. A solar panel is painted dark and matt for that reason.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** Energy is transferred through a wall at a steady rate of 500 W. Calculate
the energy transferred in 5.0 minutes.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Power is the rate of energy transfer, so use $E = Pt$. Convert the time to
seconds first.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$t = 5.0 \times 60 = 300\,\text{s}$, then $E = 500 \times 300$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = Pt = 500 \times 300 = 1.5 \times 10^{5}\,\text{J}$$

The wall transfers $1.5 \times 10^{5}$ J in five minutes. A larger rate or a
longer time transfers more energy.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why it is wrong to say that heat rises.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which mechanism carries energy upwards in a room, and what does it need?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Convection carries the energy upwards, and the fluid rises because it is less
dense.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Energy is carried upwards by a convection current. The warm fluid rises because
it is less dense than the cooler fluid around it. The energy does not rise on its
own.

In a cold room the convection current flows the other way, so the same mechanism
can carry energy downwards. Conduction and radiation have no preferred direction
either. Energy always moves from the hotter place to the colder place.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 8.1

**8.** A window is at 35°C on a hot day. Write this temperature in kelvin. Take
0°C = 273 K.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T = \theta + 273$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$T = 35 + 273$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \theta + 273 = 35 + 273 = 308\,\text{K}$$

The window is warmer than a room at 20°C. Energy would flow from the window to
the cooler room by conduction, convection and radiation.

</details>
</details>
</details>
