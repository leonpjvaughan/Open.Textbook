# 8.4 Conduction, convection and radiation — Learn

**Prerequisites:** temperature and heat from Section 8.1; specific heat capacity
from Section 8.2.

**Objectives:** describe conduction, convection and radiation; explain how each
transfers energy; compare the three; explain how insulation slows energy
transfer.

By the end of this section, you can say how energy moves from place to place, and
how to slow it down.

## Conduction

**Conduction** is the transfer of energy through a solid by particle collisions.
At the hot end, the particles vibrate faster. They collide with their neighbours
and pass energy along. The energy moves through the solid, but the particles
themselves stay in place.

Metals are good conductors. They contain free electrons that move through the
metal and carry energy quickly. Wood, plastic, glass and air are poor conductors,
also called insulators.

**Example 1.** A metal spoon is left in a hot drink. The handle becomes hot.
Explain why.

The particles at the hot end vibrate faster and pass energy along the spoon by
collisions. The spoon also has free electrons that carry energy quickly. The
handle becomes hot by conduction.

## Convection

**Convection** is the transfer of energy by the movement of a fluid. A fluid is a
liquid or a gas.

When a fluid is heated, it expands and becomes less dense. The warm, less dense
fluid rises. Cooler, denser fluid moves in to take its place. The moving fluid
carries energy with it and forms a **convection current**.

Convection happens only in fluids. A solid cannot flow, so it cannot convect.

**Example 2.** A radiator heats a room. Explain how.

The radiator warms the air next to it. That air expands, becomes less dense and
rises. Cooler air moves in, is warmed in turn, and rises. The moving air carries
energy around the room.

## Radiation

**Radiation** is the transfer of energy by infrared electromagnetic waves. It
does not need a medium, so it can travel through a vacuum. It travels at the
speed of light.

Every object emits and absorbs infrared radiation. The hotter an object is, the
more it emits. Dark, matt surfaces absorb and emit infrared well. Shiny, light
surfaces reflect infrared and emit it poorly.

**Example 3.** The Sun warms the Earth through space. Explain how.

Space is almost empty, so there are no particles to carry energy by conduction or
convection. The Sun emits infrared radiation, and the waves cross the vacuum of
space and are absorbed by the Earth.

## Comparing the three

| Mechanism | How energy moves | Needs a medium? |
|---|---|---|
| Conduction | particle collisions | yes, particles in contact |
| Convection | moving fluid | yes, a liquid or a gas |
| Radiation | infrared waves | no |

## Slowing the transfer of energy

Insulation slows the transfer of energy. A **vacuum flask** uses all three ideas.

- The vacuum between the two walls has no particles, so conduction and convection
  cannot happen there.
- The walls are silvered, so they reflect infrared radiation.
- The stopper is a poor conductor and traps air, so it slows conduction and stops
  convection at the top.

A double-glazed window works in a similar way. The trapped air or gas between the
panes is a poor conductor.

> **Watch out.** It is not true that heat rises. Energy is carried upwards by a
> convection current because the warm fluid is less dense, not because heat has a
> direction of its own. In a cold room the convection current flows the other
> way. Conduction and radiation have no preferred direction either. Energy always
> moves from the hotter place to the colder place.
> **Re-check:** ask which mechanism is carrying the energy, and which way the
> fluid is moving.

## A heat pump

A heat pump moves energy from a cold place to a hot place. It uses work to do
this, so the flow is not spontaneous. A fridge is a heat pump: it moves energy
out of the food compartment and into the room. The back of a fridge feels warm
for that reason.
