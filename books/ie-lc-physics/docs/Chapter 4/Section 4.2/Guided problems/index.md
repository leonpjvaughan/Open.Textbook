# 4.2 Kinetic and potential energy — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. A force of 15 N moves an object 2.0 m in the direction of the force. State the work
   done.
2. An object is not moving. State its kinetic energy, and explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $W = Fd = 15 \times 2.0 = 30\,\text{J}$.
2. The kinetic energy is zero. The formula $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ has $v = 0$, so
   $E_k = 0$. An object at rest has no energy stored in its motion, however large its
   mass.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A cyclist and bicycle have a total mass of 80 kg and travel at 5.0 m/s.
Calculate the kinetic energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$. The mass is the total mass of rider and bicycle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E_k = 0.5 \times 80 \times 5.0^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.5 \times 80 \times 25 = 1000\,\text{J}$$

That is $1.0 \times 10^3\,\text{J}$. Check the unit: $\text{kg} \times (\text{m/s})^2
= \text{kg m}^2/\text{s}^2 = \text{J}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A ball of mass 0.40 kg is kicked and moves at 12 m/s. Calculate its kinetic
energy.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Square the speed first, then multiply by the mass and by one half.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E_k = 0.5 \times 0.40 \times 12^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = 0.5 \times 0.40 \times 144 = 28.8\,\text{J}$$

That is about 29 J. A small mass can carry a surprising amount of energy when the speed
is high, because the speed is squared.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A 3.0 kg stone is lifted 2.0 m onto a wall. Calculate the gravitational potential
energy it gains. Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E_p = mgh$, with the height measured from the ground.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E_p = 3.0 \times 9.8 \times 2.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E_p = mgh = 3.0 \times 9.8 \times 2.0 = 58.8\,\text{J}$$

That is about 59 J. The energy is stored in the height of the stone above the ground,
and it is released if the stone falls.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A 1500 kg car has 300000 J of kinetic energy. Calculate its speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ for $v$. Take the square root at the end.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 300000}{1500}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{2E_k}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 300000}{1500}} = \sqrt{400} = 20\,\text{m/s}$$

Check it: $0.5 \times 1500 \times 20^2 = 300000\,\text{J}$, so the answer is right.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball of mass 0.50 kg has 20 J of gravitational potential energy above the
ground. Calculate its height. Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $E_p = mgh$ for $h$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h = \dfrac{20}{0.50 \times 9.8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$h = \frac{E_p}{mg} = \frac{20}{0.50 \times 9.8} = 4.1\,\text{m}$$

To two significant figures the height is 4.1 m. If you used the mass instead of the
weight, $20 \div 0.50$, you would get 40 m, which is ten times too large. Convert the
mass to a weight first.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A runner doubles her speed. Explain what happens to her kinetic energy, and
support your answer with a calculation for a 60 kg runner at 4.0 m/s and at 8.0 m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The speed is squared in $E_k$. Work out both energies and compare them.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

At 4.0 m/s, $E_k = 0.5 \times 60 \times 16$. At 8.0 m/s, $E_k = 0.5 \times 60 \times 64$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

At 4.0 m/s:

$$E_k = 0.5 \times 60 \times 4.0^2 = 480\,\text{J}$$

At 8.0 m/s:

$$E_k = 0.5 \times 60 \times 8.0^2 = 1920\,\text{J}$$

The kinetic energy is four times larger, because doubling the speed multiplies $v^2$ by
$2^2 = 4$. The runner must do four times as much work to reach the higher speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 4.1

**7.** A box of mass 12 kg is lifted onto a shelf, and the work done against gravity is
118 J. Calculate the height of the shelf. Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The work done against gravity becomes the gravitational potential energy. Use
$E_p = mgh$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$h = \dfrac{118}{12 \times 9.8}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The work done lifting the box is stored as gravitational potential energy, so
$mgh = 118\,\text{J}$:

$$h = \frac{E_p}{mg} = \frac{118}{12 \times 9.8} = 1.0\,\text{m}$$

The shelf is about 1.0 m above the floor. Work and stored energy are the same quantity
here, which is the link between Sections 4.1 and 4.2.

</details>
</details>
</details>
