# 4.2 Kinetic and potential energy — Learn

**Prerequisites:** work and energy (Section 4.1).

**Objectives:** calculate kinetic energy with $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$; calculate
gravitational potential energy with $E_p = mgh$; describe how the two change as an
object moves.

By the end of this section, you can calculate the energy stored in motion and in
height, and rearrange both formulas.

## Two energy stores

Energy is the ability to do work. It is measured in joules, the same unit as work, and
it can be stored in different ways.

- An object that is moving has **kinetic energy**, stored in its motion.
- An object that is raised above a chosen level has **gravitational potential
  energy**, stored in its height.

Energy can also be stored in a stretched spring (Chapter 5) or in a hot object
(Chapter 8). This section gives you the two formulas for the movement of everyday
objects, and Section 4.3 shows how energy moves between them.

## Kinetic energy

An object of mass $m$ moving at speed $v$ has kinetic energy

$$E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

- $m$ is the mass, in kilograms (kg);
- $v$ is the speed, in metres per second (m/s);
- $E_k$ is the kinetic energy, in joules (J).

The speed is **squared**. Double the speed and the kinetic energy becomes four times
larger. That is why speed matters so much in a crash.

**Example 1.** A car of mass 1200 kg travels at 15 m/s. Calculate its kinetic energy.

$$
E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

$$
E_k = 0.5 \times 1200 \times 15^2
$$

$$
E_k = 1.35 \times 10^5\,\text{J}
$$

That is about $1.4 \times 10^5\,\text{J}$.

**Example 2.** A ball of mass 0.50 kg has 100 J of kinetic energy. Calculate its speed.

Rearranging $E_k = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$ for $v$:

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2E_k}{m}}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 100}{0.50}}
$$

$$
v = 20\,\text{m/s}
$$

## Gravitational potential energy

Lifting an object does work against gravity. That work is stored as gravitational
potential energy:

$$E_p = mgh$$

- $m$ is the mass, in kilograms (kg);
- $g$ is the gravitational field strength, $9.8\,\text{N/kg}$ near the surface of the
  Earth;
- $h$ is the height above the chosen level, in metres (m).

The height is measured from a level you choose. On a shelf problem the floor is the
natural choice. The energy depends on the height above that level, and not on the
height above the sea.

**Example 3.** A book of mass 2.0 kg is placed on a shelf 1.5 m above the floor.
Calculate its gravitational potential energy relative to the floor.

$$
E_p = mgh
$$

$$
E_p = 2.0 \times 9.8 \times 1.5
$$

$$
E_p = 29.4\,\text{J}
$$

The book stores about 29 J.

**Example 4.** A ball of mass 0.25 kg has 5.0 J of gravitational potential energy above
the ground. Calculate its height.

Rearranging $E_p = mgh$ for $h$:

$$
h = \frac{E_p}{mg}
$$

$$
h = \frac{5.0}{0.25 \times 9.8}
$$

$$
h = 2.0\,\text{m}
$$

> **Watch out.** Two traps. First, $v$ is squared in $E_k$, so doubling the speed
> multiplies the energy by four, not by two. Second, the height $h$ is measured from
> the level you chose as zero, so a change of level changes every value. Write down the
> level before you substitute.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The station raises $4.0 \times 10^5$ kg of water through
300 m.

Calculate the gravitational potential energy stored in that water. Take
$g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$. You should find $1.2 \times 10^9\,\text{J}$.

Now calculate the kinetic energy of the same mass of water if it were moving at 20 m/s.
You should find $8.0 \times 10^7\,\text{J}$.

The two answers are very different sizes, and that difference is the point: the height
stores far more energy than the slow flow. Keep both numbers for Section 4.3.

## You should now be able to

- calculate the kinetic energy of a moving object, and rearrange the formula for speed;
- calculate gravitational potential energy, and say what the height is measured from;
- explain why doubling the speed multiplies the kinetic energy by four;
- state the unit of each quantity in both formulas.
