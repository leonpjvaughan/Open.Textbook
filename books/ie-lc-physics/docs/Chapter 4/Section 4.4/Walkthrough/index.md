# 4.4 Power and efficiency — Learn

**Prerequisites:** work and energy (Section 4.1), and conservation of energy
(Section 4.3).

**Objectives:** define power; use $P = W/t$; calculate energy from power and time;
calculate efficiency as a percentage.

By the end of this section, you can compare machines by how fast they transfer energy,
and say how much of the energy is useful.

## Power is a rate

**Power** is the rate at which work is done, or the rate at which energy is transferred.

$$P = \frac{W}{t}$$

- $P$ is the power, in watts (W);
- $W$ is the work done or the energy transferred, in joules (J);
- $t$ is the time taken, in seconds (s).

One watt is one joule per second. A 100 W lamp transfers 100 J of energy every second.

$$1\,\text{W} = 1\,\text{J/s}$$

**Example 1.** A motor does 600 J of work in 4.0 s. Calculate its power.

$$
P = \frac{W}{t}
$$

$$
P = \frac{600}{4.0}
$$

$$
P = 150\,\text{W}
$$

**Example 2.** A motor lifts a 200 kg load 10 m in 20 s. Calculate the useful power
output. Take $g = 9.8\,\text{N/kg}$.

The useful work is the work done against gravity:

$$
W = mgh = 200 \times 9.8 \times 10 = 19600\,\text{J}
$$

$$
P = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{19600}{20} = 980\,\text{W}
$$

## Energy from power and time

Rearranging $P = W/t$ gives the energy transferred:

$$E = Pt$$

This is useful for an appliance. A 2.0 kW heater running for 5.0 minutes transfers

$$
E = Pt = 2000 \times 300 = 6.0 \times 10^5\,\text{J}
$$

The time must be in seconds, because a watt is a joule per second.

## Efficiency

No machine transfers all the energy it is given. Some is always transferred to the
surroundings, usually as thermal energy. **Efficiency** says how much of the input is
useful.

$$\text{efficiency} = \frac{\text{useful energy output}}{\text{total energy input}} \times 100\%$$

**Example 3.** A motor is supplied with 2450 J of energy and transfers 1960 J usefully.
Calculate its efficiency.

$$
\text{efficiency} = \frac{1960}{2450} \times 100\%
$$

$$
\text{efficiency} = 80\%
$$

The other 20% warmed the motor and the surroundings.

An efficiency can also be written as a decimal between 0 and 1. Here it is 0.80. The
percentage form is the one the examination expects.

> **Watch out.** Power and energy are different quantities. A powerful motor transfers a
> lot of energy quickly; it does not transfer more energy than a weaker motor that runs
> for longer. A 2 kW kettle boils water faster than a 1 kW kettle, but both use about
> the same energy for the same job. Read the question and decide: is it asking for a
> rate, or for a total?

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The station moves $4.0 \times 10^5$ kg of water through a
300 m drop every 60 s.

Calculate the rate at which gravitational potential energy is transferred to the
turbines. You should find about $2.0 \times 10^7\,\text{W}$, which is 20 MW.

The electrical output of the station is 16 MW. Calculate its efficiency while it
generates, as a percentage. You should find about 82%.

Keep both answers. The Review asks why the station still gives back less energy than it
takes in.

## You should now be able to

- define power and state its unit;
- calculate power from work and time, and energy from power and time;
- calculate the efficiency of a machine as a percentage;
- explain the difference between a rate of energy transfer and a total amount of
  energy.
