# 2.3 The equations of motion for constant acceleration — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the unit of acceleration.
2. A car accelerates from rest at 3.0 m/s$^2$ for 2.0 s. Calculate its final velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The unit is m/s$^2$. It is a velocity in m/s divided by a time in s.
2. $v = u + at = 0 + 3.0 \times 2.0 = 6.0\,\text{m/s}$.

Both answers come back in this section. Acceleration carries the unit m/s$^2$, and the
first equation of motion turns an acceleration and a time into a change in velocity.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A motorbike starts from rest and accelerates at 4.0 m/s$^2$ for 5.0 s. Calculate
its final velocity.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You are given $u$, $a$ and $t$, and you want $v$. Which equation leaves out $s$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v = u + at$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = u + at = 0 + 4.0 \times 5.0 = 20\,\text{m/s}$$

The motorbike reaches $20\,\text{m/s}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A trolley starts from rest and accelerates at 3.0 m/s$^2$ for 4.0 s. Calculate the
distance it travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You want $s$, and the time is given. Which equation contains $s$ and $t$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 3.0 \times 4.0^2 = 24\,\text{m}$$

The trolley travels $24\,\text{m}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A car speeds up steadily from 5.0 m/s to 15 m/s in 4.0 s. Calculate the distance
it travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You are given $u$, $v$ and $t$, and you want $s$. Which equation leaves out $a$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $s = \dfrac{u + v}{2}\,t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = \frac{u + v}{2}\,t = \frac{5.0 + 15}{2} \times 4.0 = 40\,\text{m}$$

The average velocity is $10\,\text{m/s}$, and the car keeps it for 4.0 s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A car travelling at 20 m/s brakes to rest with a deceleration of 4.0 m/s$^2$.
Calculate the distance it travels while stopping.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The time is not given. Which equation links $u$, $v$, $a$ and $s$?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$, with $a = -4.0\,\text{m/s}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$

$$0 = 20^2 + 2 \times (-4.0) \times s$$

$$s = \frac{20^2}{2 \times 4.0} = 50\,\text{m}$$

The car travels $50\,\text{m}$ before it stops. The deceleration goes into the equation as
a negative number.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball rolls at 6.0 m/s and accelerates at 2.0 m/s$^2$ over a distance of 16 m.
Calculate its final speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The time is not given, so use the equation without $t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v^2 = u^2 + 2as = 6.0^2 + 2 \times 2.0 \times 16$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v^2 = u^2 + 2as = 6.0^2 + 2 \times 2.0 \times 16 = 36 + 64 = 100$$

$$v = 10\,\text{m/s}$$

The ball speeds up from $6.0\,\text{m/s}$ to $10\,\text{m/s}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A car travelling at 10 m/s accelerates at 2.0 m/s$^2$ for 5.0 s. Calculate its
final velocity and the distance it travels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Two steps. Find $v$ first, then use it to find $s$, or use $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
directly.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = 10 + 2.0 \times 5.0$, then $s = \dfrac{10 + v}{2} \times 5.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = u + at = 10 + 2.0 \times 5.0 = 20\,\text{m/s}$$

$$s = \frac{u + v}{2}\,t = \frac{10 + 20}{2} \times 5.0 = 75\,\text{m}$$

The same distance comes from $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 50 + 25 = 75\,\text{m}$. Both
routes agree, so you can choose whichever is quicker.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem continues

**7.** The drone starts from rest and accelerates upwards at 2.0 m/s$^2$ for 4.0 s.
Calculate the height it rises.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

You want $s$, and you are given $u$, $a$ and $t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Use $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 4.0^2 = 16\,\text{m}$$

The drone rises $16\,\text{m}$ during its acceleration phase. It then climbs a further
$4.0\,\text{m}$ at a steady speed, which brings it to the release height of $20\,\text{m}$.
Section 2.4 follows the parcel from there.

</details>
</details>
</details>
