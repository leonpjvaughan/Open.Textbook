# 2.1 Displacement, velocity and acceleration — Learn

**Prerequisites:** a measurement is a number with a unit (Section 1.1); reading a
straight-line graph (Section 1.5).

**Objectives:** tell a scalar from a vector; calculate an average speed, an average
velocity and an acceleration.

By the end of this section, you can describe any straight-line journey with the right
quantity and the right unit.

## Distance and displacement

Hold a ball at arm's length and walk once around the room. You have travelled a long
way. You finish in the same place you started. The **distance** you walked is how far you
travelled along the path. Your **displacement** is the straight-line distance from the
start to the finish, together with its direction. It is zero, because you came back.

- A **scalar** has a size only. Distance, speed and time are scalars.
- A **vector** has a size and a direction. Displacement, velocity and acceleration are
  vectors.

**Example 1.** A runner completes one lap of a 400 m track in 50 s. State the distance
travelled and the displacement at the finish.

The distance is 400 m. The runner finishes at the starting line, so the displacement is
0 m. The direction is not needed, because the size is zero.

## Speed and velocity

**Speed** is the distance travelled divided by the time taken. **Velocity** is the
displacement divided by the time taken, so it carries a direction.

$$v = \frac{s}{t}$$

- $v$ is the average speed, in metres per second (m/s);
- $s$ is the distance travelled, in metres (m);
- $t$ is the time taken, in seconds (s).

The same equation gives the average velocity when $s$ is the displacement. The direction
of the velocity is the direction of the displacement.

**Example 2.** A cyclist travels 120 m along a straight road in 15 s. Calculate the
average speed.

$$
v = \frac{s}{t}
$$

$$
v = \frac{120}{15}
$$

$$
v = 8.0\,\text{m/s}
$$

**Example 3.** The same cyclist then turns and rides 120 m back to the start in another
15 s. Calculate the average speed and the average velocity for the whole journey.

The distance is $120 + 120 = 240$ m and the time is 30 s, so

$$
v = \frac{240}{30} = 8.0\,\text{m/s}
$$

The cyclist finishes where they started, so the displacement is 0 m. The average velocity
is therefore $0\,\text{m/s}$, even though the average speed is $8.0\,\text{m/s}$.

> **Watch out.** Speed and velocity are not the same, and a lap makes the difference
> clear. A runner who returns to the start has a large average speed and an average
> velocity of zero. Reading "speed" as "velocity" loses the direction, and reading
> "distance" as "displacement" turns the answer into the wrong quantity.

## Acceleration

**Acceleration** is the rate at which velocity changes. It is the change in velocity
divided by the time taken.

$$a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v - u}{t}$$

- $a$ is the acceleration, in metres per second squared (m/s$^2$);
- $u$ is the velocity at the start, in m/s;
- $v$ is the velocity at the end, in m/s;
- $t$ is the time taken, in s.

An acceleration of $3.0$ m/s$^2$ means the velocity changes by 3.0 m/s every second. A
positive acceleration speeds the object up in the direction you chose as positive. A
negative acceleration slows it down, and it is often called a **deceleration**.

**Example 4.** A car speeds up from 8.0 m/s to 20 m/s in 4.0 s. Calculate its
acceleration.

$$
a = \frac{v - u}{t}
$$

$$
a = \frac{20 - 8.0}{4.0}
$$

$$
a = 3.0\,\text{m/s}^2
$$

**Example 5.** A bus slows from 15 m/s to rest in 5.0 s. Taking the forward direction as
positive, calculate its acceleration.

$$
a = \frac{0 - 15}{5.0} = -3.0\,\text{m/s}^2
$$

The negative sign says that the acceleration points backwards, opposite to the motion.
That is what slowing down looks like in the numbers.

> **Watch out.** The sign of the acceleration depends on the direction you chose as
> positive, not on whether the object is speeding up. A car that reverses and speeds up
> has a negative velocity and a negative acceleration, and its speed is increasing. Always
> state the positive direction before you write the sign.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The drone lifts off from rest and reaches 8.0 m/s in 4.0 s.

Write down its acceleration, with its unit. Then write one sentence saying why the
drone's speed and the magnitude of its velocity are equal here. Keep both: Section 2.2
turns this acceleration into the gradient of a graph.

## You should now be able to

- tell a scalar from a vector, and give an example of each;
- tell distance from displacement, and say why a lap gives a displacement of zero;
- calculate an average speed and an average velocity from a distance and a time;
- calculate an acceleration from a change in velocity, with the correct sign.
