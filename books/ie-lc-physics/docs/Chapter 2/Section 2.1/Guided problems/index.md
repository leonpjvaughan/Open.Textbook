# 2.1 Displacement, velocity and acceleration — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. How many metres are in 1 km?
2. A car travels 100 m in 4.0 s. Calculate its average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $1\,\text{km} = 1000\,\text{m}$. The prefix kilo means $10^3$.
2. $v = \dfrac{s}{t} = \dfrac{100}{4.0} = 25\,\text{m/s}$.

Both answers come back in this section. A distance is measured in metres, and a speed is
a distance divided by a time.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A walker walks 400 m in 80 s. Calculate the average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Average speed is distance divided by time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \dfrac{400}{80}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \frac{s}{t} = \frac{400}{80} = 5.0\,\text{m/s}$$

The unit comes from the division: metres divided by seconds gives m/s.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A runner completes one lap of a 300 m track in 60 s. State the distance travelled
and the displacement, then calculate the average speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The runner finishes where they started. What does that make the displacement?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Distance $= 300\,\text{m}$, displacement $= 0\,\text{m}$. Speed is distance over time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The distance is 300 m. The runner returns to the starting line, so the displacement is
0 m.

$$v = \frac{300}{60} = 5.0\,\text{m/s}$$

The average speed is $5.0\,\text{m/s}$. The average velocity is $0\,\text{m/s}$, because
the displacement is zero. Speed and velocity answer different questions.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A train speeds up from 12 m/s to 24 m/s in 6.0 s. Calculate its acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Acceleration is the change in velocity divided by the time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{24 - 12}{6.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{24 - 12}{6.0} = 2.0\,\text{m/s}^2$$

where $a$ is the acceleration, $v$ the final velocity, $u$ the initial velocity and
$t$ the time. The train gains 2.0 m/s of velocity every second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A cyclist slows from 10 m/s to 4.0 m/s in 3.0 s. Taking the forward direction as
positive, calculate the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The final velocity is smaller than the initial velocity. What sign will the answer have?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{4.0 - 10}{3.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{4.0 - 10}{3.0} = -2.0\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The negative sign shows that the acceleration points backwards, so the cyclist is slowing
down. The word for this is deceleration.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A trolley hits a barrier at 3.0 m/s and rebounds along the same line at 2.0 m/s.
The contact lasts 0.10 s. Taking the direction towards the barrier as positive, calculate
the trolley's acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The rebound velocity is negative. Write both velocities with their signs before you
subtract.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{-2.0 - 3.0}{0.10}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{v - u}{t} = \frac{-2.0 - 3.0}{0.10} = -50\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The velocity changes by 5.0 m/s, and the change happens in only 0.10 s, so the
acceleration is large. The negative sign shows that the force from the barrier acts
backwards.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A student says, "The bus has a negative acceleration, so it must be slowing down."
Explain what is wrong with this statement.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The sign of the acceleration depends on the direction you chose as positive.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A bus can move backwards and speed up. What are the signs of its velocity and
acceleration then?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The sign of the acceleration depends on the direction chosen as positive, not on whether
the speed is rising or falling.

If the bus is moving forwards and slowing down, its acceleration is negative. But if the
bus is reversing and speeding up, its velocity is negative and its acceleration is also
negative, so the speed is increasing. The statement is therefore not always true. You
must compare the direction of the acceleration with the direction of the velocity.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem begins here

**7.** The delivery drone lifts off from rest and reaches 8.0 m/s in 4.0 s. Calculate its
acceleration, then write one sentence saying why its speed and the magnitude of its
velocity are equal here.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The drone starts from rest, so its initial velocity is zero.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$a = \dfrac{8.0 - 0}{4.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$a = \frac{8.0 - 0}{4.0} = 2.0\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The drone moves in a straight line and does not turn, so its speed and the magnitude of
its velocity are the same. The direction is straight up, and it does not change during
this phase.

Keep this value. Section 2.2 draws it as the gradient of a velocity-time graph, and
Section 2.3 uses it to find the height.

</details>
</details>
</details>
