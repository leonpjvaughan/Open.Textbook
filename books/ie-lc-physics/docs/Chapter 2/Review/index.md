# Chapter 2: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**The drone delivery.** The drone lifts off from rest, accelerates upwards at
2.0 m/s$^2$ for 4.0 s, then rises at a steady 8.0 m/s for 0.50 s. It releases a parcel
from a height of 20 m, and the parcel falls from rest.

**The acceleration phase.** With $u = 0$, $a = 2.0\,\text{m/s}^2$ and $t = 4.0\,\text{s}$:

$$v = u + at = 0 + 2.0 \times 4.0 = 8.0\,\text{m/s}$$

$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 4.0^2 = 16\,\text{m}$$

where $u$ is the initial velocity, $v$ the final velocity, $a$ the acceleration, $s$
the displacement and $t$ the time.

The drone reaches $8.0\,\text{m/s}$ after rising $16\,\text{m}$.

**The steady phase.** At $8.0\,\text{m/s}$ for $0.50\,\text{s}$:

$$s = vt = 8.0 \times 0.50 = 4.0\,\text{m}$$

The total height is $16 + 4.0 = 20\,\text{m}$, which is the release height.

**The graph.** Plotting velocity against time gives a straight rise from $(0, 0)$ to
$(4.0\,\text{s}, 8.0\,\text{m/s})$, then a horizontal line to $4.5\,\text{s}$. The area
under the line is the displacement:

$$\text{area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4.0 \times 8.0 + 0.50 \times 8.0 = 16 + 4.0 = 20\,\text{m}$$

The graph and the equations give the same height. That is the point of Section 2.5: the
equations come from the graph.

**The parcel.** Released from rest at 20 m, with downwards as positive and
$g = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$:

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 20}{9.8}} = 2.0\,\text{s}$$

$$v = \sqrt{2gs} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 20} = 20\,\text{m/s}$$

where $s$ is the distance fallen, $g$ the acceleration of free fall, $t$ the fall
time and $v$ the landing speed.

The parcel takes about $2.0\,\text{s}$ to fall and lands at about $20\,\text{m/s}$.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which relationship applies** and
say why. Two of them ask you to choose between the graph and the equations.

1. A car travels 240 m in 12 s. Calculate its average speed.
2. A velocity-time graph is a straight line from 5.0 m/s to 17 m/s over 6.0 s. Find the
   acceleration and the displacement.
3. A stone is dropped from rest from a height of 44.1 m. Calculate the time it takes to
   reach the ground.
4. A ball is thrown straight up at 24.5 m/s. Calculate the time to the highest point and
   the maximum height.
5. A train travelling at 25 m/s decelerates steadily to rest in 10 s. Calculate its
   deceleration and its stopping distance.
6. A cyclist starts from rest and accelerates at 1.5 m/s$^2$ for 8.0 s. Calculate the
   final speed and the distance travelled.
7. A ball is dropped from rest and lands at 15 m/s. Calculate the height it fell.
8. A car speeds up from 12 m/s to 30 m/s over a distance of 90 m. Calculate its
   acceleration.
9. You know the initial velocity, the final velocity and the time. State which equation
   gives the distance, and explain why it is the shortest route.
10. A car accelerates from rest at 3.0 m/s$^2$ for 4.0 s, then travels at a constant
    speed for 6.0 s. Calculate the total distance travelled.

## What you can now do

- tell a scalar from a vector, and tell distance from displacement;
- calculate an average speed, an average velocity and an acceleration, with the correct
  sign;
- find a speed from the gradient of a distance-time graph;
- find an acceleration from the gradient of a velocity-time graph, and a displacement
  from the area under it;
- choose an equation of motion by the quantity it leaves out, and use it with units;
- describe free fall, and solve problems about objects dropped or thrown upwards;
- derive the equations of motion from a velocity-time graph.

## Thinking back

Return to the journey you chose at the start of the chapter — the bus, the cycle, the
lift or the basketball.

Sketch its velocity-time graph. Mark the part where it speeds up, the part where it moves
steadily and the part where it slows down. Then read a distance from the area under the
graph, and check that the number makes sense for the journey.

A sketch with the right shape is a real answer. It shows that you know which quantity is
changing, and by how much, at every stage of the journey.
