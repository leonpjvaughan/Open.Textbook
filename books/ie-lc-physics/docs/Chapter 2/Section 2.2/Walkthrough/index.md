# 2.2 Motion graphs — Learn

**Prerequisites:** distance, displacement, speed, velocity and acceleration (Section 2.1);
reading the gradient of a straight line (Section 1.5).

**Objectives:** find a speed from the gradient of a distance-time graph; find an
acceleration from the gradient of a velocity-time graph; find a displacement from the
area under a velocity-time graph.

By the end of this section, you can read a whole journey from its graph.

## Distance-time graphs

A **distance-time graph** puts distance on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal
axis. The shape of the line tells you how the object moves.

- A horizontal line means the object is not moving.
- A straight sloping line means a steady speed.
- A curve means the speed is changing.

The **gradient** of the line is the speed, because gradient is the change in distance
divided by the change in time.

$$\text{speed} = \frac{\Delta d}{\Delta t}$$

**Example 1.** A distance-time graph is a straight line through the origin and the point
(5.0 s, 20 m). Calculate the gradient and state what it represents.

$$
\text{gradient} = \frac{20 - 0}{5.0 - 0} = 4.0
$$

The gradient is $4.0\,\text{m/s}$, so the object moves at a steady speed of
$4.0\,\text{m/s}$.

## Velocity-time graphs

A **velocity-time graph** puts velocity on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal
axis. Two things can be read from it.

- The **gradient** of the line is the acceleration.
- The **area** between the line and the time axis is the displacement.

$$\text{acceleration} = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t}$$

**Example 2.** A velocity-time graph is a straight line from rest to 12 m/s over 6.0 s.
Calculate the gradient and the area under the line, and state what each represents.

$$
\text{gradient} = \frac{12 - 0}{6.0 - 0} = 2.0
$$

The gradient is $2.0\,\text{m/s}^2$, which is the acceleration. The area is a triangle:

$$
\text{area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 6.0 \times 12 = 36
$$

The area is $36\,\text{m}$, which is the displacement.

## Reading the area

The area under a velocity-time graph is the displacement, and its shape decides how you
find it.

- A **rectangle** for a steady velocity: $\text{area} = v \times t$.
- A **triangle** for a change from rest: $\text{area} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times
  \text{height}$.
- A **trapezium** for a change between two speeds:
  $\text{area} = \frac{u + v}{2} \times t$.

**Example 3.** A velocity-time graph is a straight line from 4.0 m/s to 14 m/s over
5.0 s. Calculate the area under the line.

The shape is a trapezium, so

$$
\text{area} = \frac{u + v}{2}\,t = \frac{4.0 + 14}{2} \times 5.0 = 45\,\text{m}
$$

The displacement is $45\,\text{m}$.

> **Watch out.** Keep the two graphs apart. The gradient of a distance-time graph is the
> speed, but the gradient of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration. The area under a
> velocity-time graph is the displacement, but the area under a distance-time graph has no
> physical meaning. Reading the wrong graph gives an answer in the wrong unit, which is
> how the mistake shows up.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The drone rises from rest to 8.0 m/s in 4.0 s, then slows
to rest in a further 2.0 s.

Sketch the velocity-time graph for these two phases. It rises as a straight line, then
falls back to the axis as a second straight line. Find the area under the line, and
state what that area represents.

You should find $24\,\text{m}$. That is the height of the drone when it releases the
parcel. Section 2.3 finds the same height from the equations of motion.

## You should now be able to

- describe the shape of a distance-time graph for a stationary object, a steady speed and
  a changing speed;
- find a speed from the gradient of a distance-time graph;
- find an acceleration from the gradient of a velocity-time graph;
- find a displacement from the area under a velocity-time graph, using a rectangle, a
  triangle or a trapezium.
