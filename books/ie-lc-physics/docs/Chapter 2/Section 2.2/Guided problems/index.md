# 2.2 Motion graphs — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. What does the gradient of a straight line measure?
2. A car moves 60 m in 12 s at a steady speed. Calculate its speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. The gradient is the change in the vertical quantity divided by the change in the
   horizontal quantity. It tells you how steep the line is.
2. $v = \dfrac{60}{12} = 5.0\,\text{m/s}$.

Both answers come back in this section. The gradient of a graph carries a physical
meaning, and here it gives a speed.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A distance-time graph is a straight line through the origin and the point
(4.0 s, 24 m). Calculate the gradient and state what it represents.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Gradient is the change in distance divided by the change in time.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\text{gradient} = \dfrac{24 - 0}{4.0 - 0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{gradient} = \frac{24}{4.0} = 6.0$$

The gradient is $6.0\,\text{m/s}$, so the speed is $6.0\,\text{m/s}$. The gradient of a
distance-time graph is the speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A distance-time graph is a horizontal line at 15 m for 5.0 s. Describe the motion
of the object and explain your answer.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The distance is not changing. What does that mean for the speed?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

A horizontal line has zero gradient.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The object is stationary. Its distance from the start stays at 15 m, so it is not moving.

The gradient of the line is zero, and the gradient is the speed, so the speed is
$0\,\text{m/s}$. The object is at rest 15 m from the starting point.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A velocity-time graph is a straight line from 6.0 m/s to 18 m/s over 4.0 s.
Calculate the gradient and state what it represents.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

For a velocity-time graph, the gradient is the acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\text{gradient} = \dfrac{18 - 6.0}{4.0}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{gradient} = \frac{18 - 6.0}{4.0} = 3.0$$

The gradient is $3.0\,\text{m/s}^2$, so the acceleration is $3.0\,\text{m/s}^2$. The
object gains 3.0 m/s of velocity every second.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A velocity-time graph rises as a straight line from rest to 10 m/s over 5.0 s.
Calculate the area under the line and state what it represents.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The shape is a triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\text{area} = \dfrac{1}{2} \times 5.0 \times 10$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 5.0 \times 10 = 25\,\text{m}$$

The area is the displacement, so the object travels $25\,\text{m}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A velocity-time graph is a horizontal line at 6.0 m/s from 0 s to 4.0 s. Calculate
the area under the line and state what it represents.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

A constant velocity gives a rectangle, not a triangle.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\text{area} = 6.0 \times 4.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = 6.0 \times 4.0 = 24\,\text{m}$$

The area is the displacement, so the object travels $24\,\text{m}$ at a steady
$6.0\,\text{m/s}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A velocity-time graph is a straight line from 2.0 m/s to 8.0 m/s over 6.0 s.
Calculate the area under the line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The shape is a trapezium. The two parallel sides are the two velocities.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\text{area} = \dfrac{2.0 + 8.0}{2} \times 6.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\text{area} = \frac{u + v}{2}\,t = \frac{2.0 + 8.0}{2} \times 6.0 = 30\,\text{m}$$

The displacement is $30\,\text{m}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem continues

**7.** The drone rises from rest to 8.0 m/s in 4.0 s, then slows to rest in a further
2.0 s. Sketch its velocity-time graph, then calculate the area under the line.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The shape is a triangle up and a triangle down, both with base on the time axis.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Triangle up: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times 4.0 \times 8.0$. Triangle down:
$\dfrac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 8.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The graph rises as a straight line from $(0, 0)$ to $(4.0\,\text{s}, 8.0\,\text{m/s})$,
then falls as a straight line to $(6.0\,\text{s}, 0)$.

$$\text{area} = \frac{1}{2} \times 4.0 \times 8.0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 2.0 \times 8.0 = 16 + 8.0 = 24\,\text{m}$$

The area is the displacement, so the drone has risen $24\,\text{m}$. That is the height
from which the parcel is released in the chapter problem.

</details>
</details>
</details>
