# 2.4 Motion under gravity — Learn

**Prerequisites:** the equations of motion for constant acceleration (Section 2.3).

**Objectives:** describe free fall; use $g = 9.8$ m/s$^2$ in the equations of motion;
solve problems about objects that are dropped or thrown upwards.

By the end of this section, you can follow an object through a fall or a throw.

## Free fall

Drop a stone and a sheet of paper side by side. The stone lands first, but that is air
resistance, not gravity. In a vacuum, with no air, they fall together. An object that
moves under gravity alone is in **free fall**.

Near the surface of the Earth, every freely falling object has the same acceleration:
**9.8 m/s$^2$ downwards**. The symbol for this acceleration is $g$.

$$g = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$$

The mass of the object does not appear. A coin and a hammer fall with the same
acceleration when there is no air to slow them.

Because $g$ is a constant acceleration, the equations of motion from Section 2.3 apply.
Replace $a$ by $g$, and take care with the sign.

- If you choose **downwards as positive**, then $a = +9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$.
- If you choose **upwards as positive**, then $a = -9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$.

State your choice before you substitute. The sign then looks after itself.

## Dropping from rest

An object dropped from rest has $u = 0$. With downwards as positive, the equations become

$$v = gt \qquad s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2 \qquad v^2 = 2gs$$

**Example 1.** A stone is dropped from rest from a bridge and takes 2.0 s to reach the
water. Calculate the height of the bridge.

$$
s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times 2.0^2 = 19.6\,\text{m}
$$

The bridge is about $20\,\text{m}$ above the water.

**Example 2.** A stone is dropped from rest from a cliff 44.1 m high. Calculate the time
it takes to reach the bottom.

Rearranging $s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$ for $t$:

$$
t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 44.1}{9.8}} = 3.0\,\text{s}
$$

## Throwing upwards

A ball thrown straight up is still in free fall. The only force on it is gravity, which
pulls downwards the whole time. So its acceleration is always $9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$
downwards, even at the very top of its flight.

With upwards as positive, $a = -9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$. At the highest point the velocity is
momentarily zero.

**Example 3.** A ball is thrown straight up at 19.6 m/s. Calculate the time it takes to
reach the highest point, and the maximum height.

At the top, $v = 0$, so $v = u + at$ gives

$$
t = \frac{u}{g} = \frac{19.6}{9.8} = 2.0\,\text{s}
$$

The height comes from $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ with $v = 0$:

$$
s = \frac{u^2}{2g} = \frac{19.6^2}{2 \times 9.8} = 19.6\,\text{m}
$$

The ball rises for 2.0 s and reaches 19.6 m.

> **Watch out.** Two traps. First, $g$ is an acceleration, not a force, and it is the same
> for every mass, so a heavy ball and a light ball fall with the same acceleration in a
> vacuum. Second, the ball thrown upwards is still accelerating downwards at the top,
> even though its velocity is zero for an instant. A zero velocity with a non-zero
> acceleration is exactly what "at the top" means. Air resistance is ignored throughout
> this section.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The drone releases the parcel from rest at a height of
20 m, and the parcel falls freely.

Write down $u$ and $a$ for the parcel, choosing downwards as positive. Use the equations
of motion to find the time it takes to reach the ground, and then its landing speed. You
should find about $2.0\,\text{s}$ and about $20\,\text{m/s}$.

The Review collects the two answers and finishes the chapter problem.

## You should now be able to

- describe free fall and state the acceleration due to gravity near the Earth's surface;
- explain why the mass of a falling object does not affect its acceleration;
- solve a drop problem with $u = 0$;
- solve a throw problem by taking upwards as positive and using $a = -g$;
- state what the velocity and the acceleration are at the highest point of a throw.
