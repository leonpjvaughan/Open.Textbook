# 2.4 Motion under gravity — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the value of $g$ near the surface of the Earth, with its unit.
2. An object is dropped from rest. What is its initial velocity?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $g = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$ downwards. It is an acceleration, not a force.
2. Its initial velocity is zero, so $u = 0$.

Both answers come back in this section. Every dropped object starts from rest and falls
with the same acceleration.

</details>

:::figure warm-up-gate


## Practise with help

**1.** A stone is dropped from rest and falls for 3.0 s. Calculate its speed just before
it lands.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

It starts from rest, so $v = gt$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = 9.8 \times 3.0$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = u + gt = 0 + 9.8 \times 3.0 = 29.4\,\text{m/s}$$

The stone is travelling at about $29\,\text{m/s}$ when it lands.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** A stone is dropped from rest and takes 1.5 s to land. Calculate the height it fell.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$s = \frac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times 1.5^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 9.8 \times 1.5^2 = 11\,\text{m}$$

The stone fell about $11\,\text{m}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** A stone is dropped from rest from a height of 19.6 m. Calculate the time it takes
to reach the ground.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Rearrange $s = \frac{1}{2}gt^2$ for $t$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 19.6}{9.8}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 19.6}{9.8}} = \sqrt{4.0} = 2.0\,\text{s}$$

The stone takes $2.0\,\text{s}$ to fall.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** A ball is thrown straight up at 14.7 m/s. Calculate the time it takes to reach the
highest point, and the maximum height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

At the top the velocity is zero. Take upwards as positive, so $a = -9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$t = \dfrac{u}{g}$, then $s = \dfrac{u^2}{2g}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$t = \frac{u}{g} = \frac{14.7}{9.8} = 1.5\,\text{s}$$

$$s = \frac{u^2}{2g} = \frac{14.7^2}{2 \times 9.8} = 11\,\text{m}$$

The ball rises for $1.5\,\text{s}$ and reaches about $11\,\text{m}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A ball is thrown straight up at 9.8 m/s. Calculate the total time it is in the air
before it returns to the thrower's hand.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The time up equals the time down, and the ball returns to its starting height.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Time to the top $= \dfrac{9.8}{9.8} = 1.0\,\text{s}$. Double it.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The time to the highest point is

$$t = \frac{u}{g} = \frac{9.8}{9.8} = 1.0\,\text{s}$$

The ball returns to the height it started from, so the time down equals the time up. The
total time in the air is $2.0\,\text{s}$.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** A heavy ball and a light ball are dropped together in a vacuum. Explain why they
land at the same time, and say what happens when the experiment is repeated in air.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What force acts on each ball in a vacuum? Does its effect depend on the mass?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The acceleration due to gravity is the same for every mass.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

In a vacuum the only force on each ball is gravity. The acceleration due to gravity is
$9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$ for every mass, so both balls gain speed at the same rate and land
together.

In air, each ball also meets air resistance. The lighter ball is slowed more by the same
drag, so the heavy ball lands first. The difference comes from the air, not from gravity.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## The chapter problem continues

**7.** The drone releases the parcel from rest at a height of 20 m. Taking downwards as
positive, calculate the time the parcel takes to reach the ground and its landing speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2s}{g}}$ for the time, then $v = gt$ or $v = \sqrt{2gs}$ for the
speed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$t = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 20}{9.8}}$, then $v = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 20}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The parcel is released from rest, so $u = 0$ and $a = 9.8\,\text{m/s}^2$ downwards.

$$t = \sqrt{\frac{2s}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 20}{9.8}} = 2.0\,\text{s}$$

$$v = \sqrt{2gs} = \sqrt{2 \times 9.8 \times 20} = 20\,\text{m/s}$$

The parcel lands about $2.0\,\text{s}$ after it is released, travelling at about
$20\,\text{m/s}$. That finishes the chapter problem.

</details>
</details>
</details>
