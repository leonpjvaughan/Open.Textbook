# Chapter 17: Review

## The chapter problem, solved

**How fast, and how heavy?** A heated filament releases electrons. A potential
difference of 2000 V accelerates them into a beam. The beam enters a magnetic field of
flux density $2.0 \times 10^{-3}$ T at right angles, and the electrons curve along an
arc of radius 7.5 cm.

**Step 1: the speed.** The field does work $eV$ on each electron, and all of it becomes
kinetic energy:

$$eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Rearrange for the speed and substitute the values:

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m}} = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2000}{9.1
\times 10^{-31}}}$$

$$v = \sqrt{7.0 \times 10^{14}} = 2.7 \times 10^7\,\text{m/s}$$

**Step 2: the charge-to-mass ratio.** The magnetic force provides the centripetal
force, so $Bev = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$. Rearranged for the ratio:

$$\frac{e}{m} = \frac{v}{Br} = \frac{2.7 \times 10^7}{2.0 \times 10^{-3} \times 7.5
\times 10^{-2}}$$

$$\frac{e}{m} = \frac{2.7 \times 10^7}{1.5 \times 10^{-4}} = 1.8 \times
10^{11}\,\text{C/kg}$$

**Step 3: a route that removes the speed.** The two equations can be combined so that
the speed never appears. Squaring $\dfrac{e}{m} = \dfrac{v}{Br}$ and substituting into
$eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ gives

$$\frac{e}{m} = \frac{2V}{B^2r^2}$$

Substituting the three measured values:

$$\frac{e}{m} = \frac{2 \times 2000}{(2.0 \times 10^{-3})^2 (7.5 \times 10^{-2})^2} =
\frac{4000}{2.25 \times 10^{-8}} = 1.8 \times 10^{11}\,\text{C/kg}$$

The two routes agree. The accepted value is $1.76 \times 10^{11}$ C/kg, so the
measurement is close.

**What it tells you.** The electrons are negatively charged, because the beam bends
towards the positive plate. Their charge-to-mass ratio is about 1800 times larger than
that of a hydrogen ion, and the charge is the same size, so the electron is far
lighter than any atom. The same value is found for every gas and every cathode, so the
electron is a common constituent of all matter.

## Mixed practice — choose the method first

Work through these without notes. For each one, decide **which relationship applies**
and say why. Two of them ask you to choose between the electric force and the magnetic
force.

1. A beam of cathode rays is deflected by a magnetic field. Explain how the direction
   of the deflection shows that the rays are negatively charged.
2. An electron is accelerated from rest through 400 V. Calculate its kinetic energy in
   joules, and then its speed.
3. A student claims that doubling the accelerating voltage doubles the speed of the
   electrons. Explain why this is wrong, and state the factor by which the speed
   changes.
4. An electron moves at $2.0 \times 10^7$ m/s at right angles to a magnetic field of
   flux density $1.5 \times 10^{-3}$ T. Calculate the magnetic force and the radius of
   the path.
5. In a crossed-field experiment the electric field strength is $2.0 \times 10^4$ V/m
   and the flux density is $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ T. The beam passes through undeflected.
   Calculate the speed of the electrons.
6. Explain why the magnetic force changes the direction of an electron but not its
   speed.
7. An electron gun uses a heated filament. Explain why the filament is in a vacuum, and
   describe what would happen if the tube contained air.
8. A beam of electrons passes between two charged plates. Describe what happens to the
   deflection if the potential difference across the plates is doubled, and why.
9. You must find the charge-to-mass ratio of the electron. Would you use an electric
   field alone, a magnetic field alone, or crossed fields? Say why, and name what each
   method can and cannot give you.
10. Explain why the charge-to-mass ratio of the electron is the same for every gas and
    every cathode, and why that result mattered.

## What you can now do

- describe the discharge-tube experiments that showed cathode rays are negatively
  charged particles;
- state the evidence that the particles are the same in all matter;
- explain thermionic emission, the work function and the role of the vacuum;
- use $eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ to find the speed of an electron from an accelerating
  voltage;
- explain how crossed fields measure the speed of a beam without knowing the charge or
  the mass;
- use $Bev = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ to find the charge-to-mass ratio of the electron;
- combine the two equations into $\dfrac{e}{m} = \dfrac{2V}{B^2r^2}$ and say why the
  speed is not needed.

## Thinking back

Look at the beam you chose at the start of the chapter — a television tube, an X-ray
set, an electron microscope, a particle accelerator.

You can now say how the beam is made and how it is steered. The accelerating voltage
sets the speed, and the magnetic field sets the radius. Changing the field strength is
what aims the beam.

Write down one number from your own choice: a voltage, a field strength or a radius.
Then work out how the beam would move if that number were doubled. If your estimate is
rough, write that down too. Saying how rough an estimate is belongs in the physics, and
it is exactly what the Physics In Practice Investigation asks you to do.
