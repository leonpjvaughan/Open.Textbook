# 17.2 Thermionic emission and the electron gun — Learn

**Prerequisites:** electric fields and potential difference from Chapter 13; work and
energy from Chapter 4; the electron from Section 17.1.

**Objectives:** explain thermionic emission and the work function; describe how an
electron gun makes a beam; use $eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ to find the speed of an
electron.

By the end of this section, you can explain how a beam of electrons is produced and
find the speed the electrons gain from an accelerating voltage.

## Getting electrons out of a metal

A metal conducts because some of its electrons are free to move from atom to atom.
Even so, those electrons are held inside the metal. To escape from the surface, an
electron needs a certain minimum energy.

That minimum energy is the **work function** of the metal. It is measured in joules,
or more conveniently in electronvolts. For most metals it is a few electronvolts.

Heat the metal and its electrons gain thermal energy. Once the metal is hot enough,
the fastest electrons have more energy than the work function, and they escape from
the surface. This release of electrons from a hot metal is **thermionic emission**.

Thermionic emission happens in a vacuum. If air were present, the escaping electrons
would collide with air molecules, lose energy and scatter before they could form a
beam.

## The electron gun

An **electron gun** uses thermionic emission to make a narrow beam of electrons.

- The **cathode** is a small filament. A separate current heats it, and electrons are
  emitted from its surface.
- The **anode** is a metal plate held at a high positive potential, with a small hole
  in the middle.
- The electric field between the cathode and the anode pulls the electrons across the
  gap and accelerates them.
- Most electrons strike the anode. The ones that pass through the hole form a narrow
  beam.

The potential difference between the cathode and the anode is the **accelerating
voltage** $V$. The electric field does work on each electron as it crosses the gap.

## The energy equation

The work done on a charge $q$ when it moves through a potential difference $V$ is
$qV$. For an electron, $q = e$. So the work done on each electron is $eV$.

The electron starts from rest at the cathode and gains kinetic energy as it crosses
the gap. If the small thermal energy from the heating is ignored, all the work becomes
kinetic energy:

$$eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$

- $e$ is the charge on the electron, in coulombs (C);
- $V$ is the accelerating voltage, in volts (V);
- $m$ is the mass of the electron, in kilograms (kg);
- $v$ is the speed of the electron, in metres per second (m/s).

**Example 1.** An electron is accelerated from rest through 500 V. Calculate its
speed.

$$
eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m}}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 500}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}}}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{1.8 \times 10^{14}} = 1.3 \times 10^7\,\text{m/s}
$$

**Example 2.** An electron gun accelerates electrons through 2000 V. Calculate the
speed at which they leave the gun.

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2eV}{m}}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\frac{2 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2000}{9.1 \times 10^{-31}}}
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{7.0 \times 10^{14}}
$$

$$
v = 2.7 \times 10^7\,\text{m/s}
$$

This is the first step of the chapter problem. Keep the value, because Section 17.3
uses it.

**Example 3.** Calculate the kinetic energy gained by an electron accelerated through
2000 V, in joules and in electronvolts.

$$
E = eV = 1.6 \times 10^{-19} \times 2000
$$

$$
E = 3.2 \times 10^{-16}\,\text{J}
$$

Here $E$ is the kinetic energy gained, in joules. In electronvolts the answer is
2000 eV, because one electronvolt is the energy gained by an electron accelerated
through one volt.

> **Watch out.** The speed depends on the **square root** of the voltage. Doubling the
> voltage does not double the speed. It multiplies the speed by $\sqrt{2}$, which is
> about 1.4. The reason is that $v^2$ is proportional to $V$, so $v$ is proportional
> to $\sqrt{V}$.

## Progress check

Back to the chapter problem. The electron gun accelerates the electrons through 2000 V.

Write down the equation $eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ and rearrange it for the speed. Then
substitute the values and state the speed to two significant figures, with its unit.
Keep the equation: Section 17.3 combines it with the magnetic force to find the
charge-to-mass ratio.

## You should now be able to

- explain what thermionic emission is, and what the work function means;
- describe the cathode, the anode and the accelerating voltage of an electron gun;
- explain why the beam must be formed in a vacuum;
- use $eV = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ to find the speed of an electron from an accelerating
  voltage;
- explain why doubling the voltage does not double the speed.
