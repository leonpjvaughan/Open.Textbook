# Chapter 6: Gravity and gravitational fields

A dish on a house points at one spot in the sky and never moves. The satellite it
points at is falling towards the Earth, and it has been falling for years. In this
chapter you find out how gravity holds it there, and why the same force that pulls an
apple down keeps the Moon in its orbit.

## The chapter problem

**The satellite over Ireland.** A communications satellite has a mass of 1200 kg. It
sits in a geostationary orbit, $4.22 \times 10^{7}\,\text{m}$ from the centre of the
Earth. It stays above the same point on the Earth's surface, so a dish on a house can
point at it without moving.

These are the facts to use all the way through the chapter.

- the mass of the satellite is **1200 kg**;
- the mass of the Earth is $5.97 \times 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$;
- the radius of the Earth is $6.37 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}$;
- the gravitational constant is
  $G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}\,\text{N}\,\text{m}^{2}\,\text{kg}^{-2}$;
- the gravitational field strength at the Earth's surface is **9.8 N/kg**;
- the satellite orbits $4.22 \times 10^{7}\,\text{m}$ from the centre of the Earth.

By the end of the chapter you can find the weight of the satellite on the launch pad
and at its orbit, the force between it and the Earth, the energy needed to lift it
there, and you can check that its period is 24 hours.

## By the end of this chapter you can

- tell mass from weight, and use $W = mg$;
- define gravitational field strength and use $g = F/m$;
- use Newton's law of universal gravitation, $F = \dfrac{Gm_1m_2}{d^2}$;
- explain what an inverse-square law is, and find the field strength at the surface
  of a planet;
- find the gravitational potential energy of a mass in a field, and the escape
  velocity from a planet;
- find the speed and the period of a satellite in a circular orbit;
- explain why a geostationary satellite stays above one point on the Earth.

## Where it is used

Satellite television, weather forecasting and satellite navigation all depend on
orbits you can predict. The tides, the Moon and the planets are held by the same law.
Every rocket launch has to beat the escape velocity of the Earth.
