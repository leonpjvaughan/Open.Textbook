# 6.4 Orbits and satellites — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down Newton's law of universal gravitation as an equation.
2. A 1200 kg satellite has a weight of 270 N at its orbit. Calculate the gravitational
   field strength there.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $F = \dfrac{Gm_1m_2}{d^2}$.
2. $g = \dfrac{F}{m} = \dfrac{270}{1200} = 0.22\,\text{N/kg}$.

Both return here. The law gives the force that acts as the centripetal force, and the
field strength is not zero at an orbit.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A satellite orbits the Earth at a distance of $6.77 \times 10^{6}\,\text{m}$ from
the centre. Calculate its orbital speed. Take $M = 5.97 \times 10^{24}\,\text{kg}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $v = \sqrt{\dfrac{GM}{r}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.97 \times 10^{24}}{6.77 \times 10^{6}}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{5.88 \times 10^{7}} = 7.7 \times 10^{3}\,\text{m/s}$$

That is 7.7 km/s. Notice that the mass of the satellite never appears, so a heavier
satellite at the same radius would have the same speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Calculate the period of the satellite in question 1.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $T = \dfrac{2\pi r}{v}$ with $v = 7.7 \times 10^{3}\,\text{m/s}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$T = \dfrac{2\pi \times 6.77 \times 10^{6}}{7.7 \times 10^{3}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$T = \frac{2\pi r}{v} = \frac{4.25 \times 10^{7}}{7.7 \times 10^{3}} = 5.5 \times 10^{3}\,\text{s}$$

That is about 92 minutes, so this satellite circles the Earth about 16 times a day. The
space station orbits at about this height.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Calculate the orbital speed of a satellite at
$r = 4.22 \times 10^{7}\,\text{m}$ from the centre of the Earth.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The same formula, with a much larger radius.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$v = \sqrt{\dfrac{3.98 \times 10^{14}}{4.22 \times 10^{7}}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$v = \sqrt{9.44 \times 10^{6}} = 3.1 \times 10^{3}\,\text{m/s}$$

The speed is **smaller** than in question 1, even though the orbit is much bigger. A
satellite further out moves more slowly.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** One satellite has an orbital radius nine times that of another. Calculate the
ratio of their periods.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Kepler's third law says $T^2 \propto r^3$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\dfrac{T_2}{T_1} = 9^{3/2}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$9^{3/2} = (\sqrt{9})^3 = 3^3 = 27$$

The period of the second satellite is 27 times the period of the first. An answer of 9
comes from forgetting to take the square root.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** A student says a heavier satellite must orbit faster, because it feels a bigger
gravitational force. Explain why the student is wrong.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Look at the two sides of the force equation. Which symbol appears in both?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Write $\dfrac{GMm}{r^2} = \dfrac{mv^2}{r}$ and cancel $m$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The gravitational force is bigger for a heavier satellite, but so is the force needed to
keep it in the circle. The two grow together, so the mass cancels:

$$\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r} \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$

The speed depends only on the radius and on the mass of the Earth. A heavier satellite
at the same radius travels at exactly the same speed.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**6.** State the three conditions for a geostationary orbit.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Think about the period, the latitude and the direction of travel.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The period must match the Earth's rotation, and the orbit must sit over the equator.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

1. The period is exactly 24 hours, so the satellite turns once while the Earth turns
   once.
2. The orbit is above the equator, so the satellite does not drift north or south.
3. The satellite moves in the same direction as the Earth spins, from west to east.

A period of 24 hours fixes the radius at $4.22 \times 10^{7}\,\text{m}$, so every
geostationary satellite is at the same height.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**7.** Explain why an astronaut on the space station floats, even though the
gravitational force on her is about 90% of her weight on the ground.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What are the astronaut and the space station both doing?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both are falling towards the Earth with the same acceleration.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The astronaut and the space station are both in free fall towards the Earth. They fall
together at the same rate, so the floor does not push on her and she does not push on
the floor. Nothing presses on her, so she floats.

Gravity has not gone. The field strength at the space station is about
$8.7\,\text{N/kg}$, so her weight is nearly as large as on the ground. She feels
weightless because she is falling, not because the force has disappeared.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Section 6.3

**8.** A satellite of mass 800 kg is lifted from the Earth's surface to
$r = 4.22 \times 10^{7}\,\text{m}$. Calculate the energy needed.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use the field form, $\Delta E_p = GMm\left(\dfrac{1}{R} - \dfrac{1}{r}\right)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\Delta E_p = 3.98 \times 10^{14} \times 800 \times \left(\dfrac{1}{6.37 \times 10^{6}} - \dfrac{1}{4.22 \times 10^{7}}\right)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$3.98 \times 10^{14} \times 800 = 3.19 \times 10^{17}$$

$$\left(\frac{1}{6.37 \times 10^{6}} - \frac{1}{4.22 \times 10^{7}}\right) = 1.33 \times 10^{-7}$$

$$\Delta E_p = 3.19 \times 10^{17} \times 1.33 \times 10^{-7} = 4.2 \times 10^{10}\,\text{J}$$

The energy is positive, because it is supplied to the satellite. It is a little smaller
than for the 1200 kg satellite, in proportion to the mass.

</details>
</details>
</details>
