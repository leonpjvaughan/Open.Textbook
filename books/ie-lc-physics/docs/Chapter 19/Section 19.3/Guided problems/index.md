# 19.3 Emission and absorption spectra — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the wave equation that connects speed, frequency and wavelength.
2. A photon has a wavelength of 500 nm. Is its energy larger or smaller than a photon
   of wavelength 700 nm? Give a reason.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $c = f\lambda$, where $c$ is the speed, $f$ is the frequency and $\lambda$ is the
   wavelength.
2. Larger. Energy is proportional to frequency, and a shorter wavelength means a higher
   frequency. So the 500 nm photon carries more energy than the 700 nm photon.

The second answer is the key to the colours in a spectrum. Blue light has a shorter
wavelength than red light, so a blue photon carries more energy.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** A discharge tube filled with hydrogen gives bright lines at 486 nm and 656 nm,
and darkness between them. Describe this spectrum and state what the lines show about
the hydrogen atom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Is every wavelength present, or only some?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The spectrum is a set of bright lines. Each line has a fixed wavelength.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

This is an emission line spectrum: bright lines on a dark background.

Each line has its own wavelength, and the atom can emit only those wavelengths. So the
hydrogen atom has a set of fixed energy levels, and each line comes from an electron
moving between two of them.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Calculate the frequency of light of wavelength 410 nm. Take
$c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Change 410 nm to metres first, then use $c = f\lambda$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$410\,\text{nm} = 4.1 \times 10^{-7}$ m, then $f = \dfrac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{4.1 \times
10^{-7}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$f = \frac{c}{\lambda} = \frac{3.0 \times 10^{8}}{4.1 \times 10^{-7}} = 7.3 \times
10^{14}\,\text{Hz}$$

That is a violet line, near the short-wavelength end of the visible spectrum.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Use the frequency from question 2 to calculate the energy of one photon of this
light. Take $h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J s.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E = hf$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E = 6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 7.3 \times 10^{14}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = hf = 6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 7.3 \times 10^{14} = 4.8 \times
10^{-19}\,\text{J}$$

The violet photon carries more energy than the red one, because its wavelength is
shorter and its frequency is higher.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Explain why a cool gas absorbs light only at certain wavelengths.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What must happen for an atom to take in a photon?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The photon energy must equal the gap between two energy levels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An atom can take in a photon only when the photon energy equals the gap between two of
its energy levels. A photon of that energy lifts an electron to the higher level, and
the photon is removed from the beam.

The levels are fixed, so only certain photon energies are absorbed. Those wavelengths
appear as dark lines. A photon of any other energy passes through.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain why the absorption lines of an element are at the same wavelengths as its
emission lines.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Which energy levels are involved in each case?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

Both lines come from the same pair of levels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

Both lines come from the same pair of energy levels in the atom.

In emission the electron falls from the higher level to the lower one, and the atom
gives out a photon. In absorption the electron takes in a photon and moves up. The
energy difference is the same in both cases, so the photon energy and the wavelength
are the same.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 9

**6.** A wave has a frequency of $5.0 \times 10^{14}$ Hz and a wavelength of
$6.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m. Calculate its speed, and state which part of the
electromagnetic spectrum it belongs to.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $c = f\lambda$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$c = 5.0 \times 10^{14} \times 6.0 \times 10^{-7}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$c = f\lambda = 5.0 \times 10^{14} \times 6.0 \times 10^{-7} = 3.0 \times
10^{8}\,\text{m/s}$$

This is the speed of light, as it must be for any electromagnetic wave. A wavelength of
$6.0 \times 10^{-7}$ m is 600 nm, which is in the visible part of the spectrum, close to
the orange-red end.

</details>
</details>
</details>
