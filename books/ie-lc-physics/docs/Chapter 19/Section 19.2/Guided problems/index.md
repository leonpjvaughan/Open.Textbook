# 19.2 The Bohr model and energy levels — Practise with help

## Warm-up: remember

1. Write down the equation that connects the energy of a photon to its frequency.
2. A photon has an energy of 2.0 eV. Calculate its energy in joules. Take
   $1\,\text{eV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ J.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Answers and feedback</summary>

1. $E = hf$, where $h$ is the Planck constant and $f$ is the frequency.
2. $E = 2.0 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 3.2 \times 10^{-19}$ J. Multiply the energy in
   electronvolts by the number of joules in one electronvolt.

The second answer is the link between the two units used in this section. The level
energies are written in electronvolts, and the photon equation gives joules.

</details>

## Practise with help

**1.** Use $E_n = -\dfrac{13.6}{n^2}$ eV to calculate the energy of the $n = 3$ level
of the hydrogen atom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Put $n = 3$ into the formula. Remember that $3^2 = 9$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E_3 = -\dfrac{13.6}{9}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E_3 = -\frac{13.6}{3^2} = -\frac{13.6}{9} = -1.51\,\text{eV}$$

The energy is negative, and it is closer to zero than the $n = 2$ level. The higher the
level, the closer its energy is to zero.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**2.** Calculate the energy of the photon emitted when an electron in a hydrogen atom
moves from the $n = 4$ level to the $n = 2$ level. Take the level energies as $-0.85$
eV and $-3.40$ eV.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

The photon energy is the difference between the two levels.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$\Delta E = E_4 - E_2 = -0.85 - (-3.40)$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$\Delta E = E_4 - E_2 = -0.85 - (-3.40) = 2.55\,\text{eV}$$

The electron loses energy, so the atom gives out a photon of energy 2.55 eV.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**3.** Use the photon energy from question 2 to calculate the wavelength of the emitted
light. Take $h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, $c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s and
$1\,\text{eV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ J.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

First change 2.55 eV to joules, then use $\Delta E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$2.55 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 4.08 \times 10^{-19}$ J, then
$\lambda = \dfrac{6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^{8}}{4.08 \times 10^{-19}}$.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = 2.55 \times 1.6 \times 10^{-19} = 4.08 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$$

$$\lambda = \frac{hc}{E} = \frac{6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^{8}}{4.08
\times 10^{-19}}$$

$$\lambda = 4.9 \times 10^{-7}\,\text{m}$$

That is 490 nm, in the blue-green part of the spectrum.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**4.** Explain why a hydrogen atom in its ground state cannot absorb a photon of energy
1.0 eV.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What is the gap between the ground state and the first excited state?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The gap from $n = 1$ to $n = 2$ is $13.6 - 3.40 = 10.2$ eV.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

An atom can absorb a photon only when the photon energy equals the gap between two
levels. The smallest gap from the ground state is the gap to $n = 2$:

$$13.6 - 3.40 = 10.2\,\text{eV}$$

A photon of 1.0 eV is far too small, so the atom cannot take it in. The photon passes
through.

</details>
</details>
</details>

**5.** Explain why the energy of the ground state of hydrogen is written as a negative
number.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

What is the zero of energy chosen to be?

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

The zero is the electron free of the atom.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

The zero of energy is chosen with the electron free of the atom, at $n = \infty$. An electron bound in the atom has less energy than a free electron, so
its energy is less than zero.

The ground state is the most tightly bound level, so it has the most negative energy,
$-13.6$ eV.

</details>
</details>
</details>

## A question from Chapter 18

**6.** The blue-green line of the hydrogen spectrum has a wavelength of 486 nm.
Calculate the energy of one photon of this light, in joules and in electronvolts. Take
$h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J s, $c = 3.0 \times 10^{8}$ m/s and
$1\,\text{eV} = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ J.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Hint</summary>

Use $E = \dfrac{hc}{\lambda}$, with the wavelength in metres.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Next step</summary>

$E = \dfrac{6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^{8}}{4.86 \times 10^{-7}}$, then
divide by $1.6 \times 10^{-19}$ to change to electronvolts.

<details markdown="1">
<summary>Full working</summary>

$$E = \frac{hc}{\lambda} = \frac{6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 3.0 \times 10^{8}}{4.86
\times 10^{-7}} = 4.1 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{J}$$

$$E = \frac{4.1 \times 10^{-19}}{1.6 \times 10^{-19}} = 2.6\,\text{eV}$$

This is close to the 2.55 eV gap from $n = 4$ to $n = 2$. The blue-green line comes
from that transition.

</details>
</details>
</details>
