# Irish textbook tables of contents — what the market actually teaches, and in what order

**Why this exists.** The four Irish books are being written from the NCCA
specifications. A specification is a list of outcomes; it is not a teaching order,
and it does not say how a chapter is shaped, how many sections it runs to, or what a
publisher puts beside the mathematics. `research/curriculum/a-level-maths-textbooks-and-exams.md`
is the English equivalent. This is the Irish one, and it is the missing quality input
the Irish books were being written without.

**Retrieved:** 2026-10-15. Every list below was read off a publisher's own sample of
the book, not recalled. The method is at the end, so it can be repeated.

**A caution about age.** Two of these books predate the current specifications — the
Physics title is the *old* syllabus (see §5), and the Junior Cycle titles are the
Project Maths books that the 2015 Framework replaced. They are useful for **order,
chapter shape and section granularity**, which change slowly, and not for what the
current specification requires, which is `curriculum.yaml`. Where the two disagree,
the specification wins.

formula, which puts one topic to a chapter and carries it through model → worked →
independent. A chapter here is *narrower and deeper* than an Open.Textbook chapter.

## 5. What this changes, and what it does not

**It does not change the curriculum.** `books/ie-*/curriculum.yaml` is the
specification's outcome list and stays the source of truth. A publisher's chapter
order is one editor's judgement.

**It does change three things in the authoring.**

1. **Section granularity.** A Junior Cycle chapter is 3–6 sections, and the
   collection's `ie-jc-maths` chapters already match. Good.
2. **Proof is content in Ireland.** Two Higher Level chapters — *Theorems* and
   *Methods of Proof*, with *Induction* its own chapter — say that an Irish book has
   to teach proof, and the 1,468 `Show that`/`Prove` occurrences in the corpus agree.
   The English books are lighter on this and their chapters should not be copied over.
3. **Geometry is bigger than one chapter at Junior Cycle.** Four chapters in
   *Destination Maths*, and the collection currently has fewer. Worth revisiting the
   `ie-jc-maths` geometry split before writing those chapters.

## 6. The method, so this can be repeated or extended

The publishers' product pages are reachable but their sample readers are not obvious.
What works, from a publisher's product page:

1. Fetch the page and read the `__NEXT_DATA__` JSON — the sample links are in it.
   Gill's are `https://online.flippingbook.com/view/<id>/`.
2. Load the flippingbook in headless Chromium (Playwright) at a page number:
   `https://online.flippingbook.com/view/<id>/<page>/`. The viewer is a JS app and
   `networkidle` never settles; wait on `domcontentloaded` and then ~6 s.
3. Screenshot at `deviceScaleFactor: 2`, then OCR with `tesseract`. The contents
   pages are 4–8, and the page images are low-resolution but the OCR is good enough
   for a chapter list.

The page *images* behind the viewer are also directly fetchable: the viewer requests
`.../<bookhash>/flash/pages/pageNNNN_w.webp` with a CloudFront signed query string
whose policy covers the whole `<bookhash>/*` path, so the same signature fetches any
page. The `common/pages/html5substrates/pageNNNN_2.webp` files are the text layer and
OCR better than the page images. Neither is stored here — see
`research/sources/ie-lc-maths/papers/README.md` for why fetched sources are not
tracked.
