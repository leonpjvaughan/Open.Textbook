# Physics equations and data provision — what each board puts in front of the reader

Input to `books/gcse-physics/booklet/physics.yaml` and
`books/a-level-physics/booklet/physics.yaml`. `BOOKLETS.md` is the contract; this
document is the evidence it requires, per qualification and board. **Retrieved
2026-10-11**; the two Edexcel documents were re-retrieved on **2026-10-12** and
the Pearson Edexcel sections below record what was found and what changed.

The distinction the booklet must get right is `given` / `question` / `know`
(`BOOKLETS.md` § "The three groups"). In physics this maps to:

- `given` — printed on the board's equations sheet or data booklet;
- `question` — not on the sheet but provided inside the question (values of
  specific heat capacity, density, latent heat, and — on some papers —
  equations the content uses that are not on the sheet);
- `know` — the reader must have it, and the Walkthrough must teach it: units and
  prefixes, the meaning of each symbol, definitions, and the typical values the
  board says must be recalled.

**The single most important fact for both books.** For the current GCSE
specifications, and for A-level, the boards *provide* the equations. That is a
regulatory position with a date on it, not a board habit:

- A-level Edexcel states it directly: "Students need not memorise formulae for
  this qualification. The formulae below will be supplied in each examination.
  Any other formulae that are required will be provided in the question."
- GCSE: DfE confirmed on 26 February 2026 that students do not need to memorise
  equations for GCSE physics or combined science for the lifetime of the current
  specifications, and Ofqual's 5 May 2026 decision requires boards to keep
  providing the sheets and *not* to set questions answerable by transferring
  information from them.

So a physics `know` group of "equations the reader must memorise" would be
wrong. The `know` group is definitions, units, prefixes and typical values.

# GCSE Physics

## AQA — GCSE Physics 8463

**Provision.** AQA supplies a *Physics Equations Sheet* as an insert in every
paper. The current one is titled "Insert: equations sheet - 2026" and says
"FOR USE IN JUNE 2026 ONLY".

- Sheet: `https://cdn.sanity.io/files/p28bar15/green/6b5358e1159b1b6cc2d622fc5eb894320d6fb763.pdf`
- Landing page: `https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/gcse/physics-8463/assessment-resources`
- The sheet repeats yearly (there are 2025 and earlier versions), marked
  `HT` for Higher-tier-only equations.

**What is on it.** It is the same 35 equations as AQA specification
Appendix A, in sheet order. Six are marked `HT` (see below). The full list:

kinetic energy E = ½mv²; elastic potential energy E = ½ke²; gravitational
potential energy E = mgh; change in thermal energy ΔE = mcΔθ;
power = energy transferred / time; power = work done / time;
efficiency = useful output energy transfer / total input energy transfer;
efficiency = useful power output / total power input; charge Q = It;
potential difference V = IR; power P = VI; power P = I²R; energy E = Pt;
energy E = QV; density ρ = m/V; thermal energy for a change of state E = mL;
for gases pV = constant; weight W = mg; work done W = Fs; force F = ke;
moment M = Fd; pressure p = F/A; **HT** pressure p = hρg; distance s = vt;
acceleration a = Δv/t; **HT** momentum p = mv; **HT** force F = mΔv/Δt;
period T = 1/f; wave speed v = fλ; magnification = image height / object
height; **HT** force on a conductor F = BIl; **HT** transformer turns
Vp/Vs = np/ns; **HT** transformer power VpIp = VsIs; (final velocity)² – (initial
velocity)² = 2as; resultant force F = ma.

**What must be recalled rather than given.** The two-tier split in the *spec* is
now historical. AQA 8463 specification v1.1 (30 September 2019), Appendix A,
lists 23 equations as "students should be able to recall and apply" and 12 more
as "select and apply ... from the Physics equation sheet". The 2026 insert
prints **all 35**, so under the current policy the Appendix A recall set is not
a memorisation requirement.

What the reader must still have is:

- **Units, prefixes and symbols** — AQA's Appendix 2 SI unit list and the
  specification's `MS` references.
- **Definitions** stated in the content, e.g. "define weight", "define the
  moment of the force", and the qualitative statements the paper assesses
  (the equation sheet does not carry the definition).
- **Typical values the specification says must be recalled.** AQA 8463 says:
  "Students should be able to recall typical values of speed for a person
  walking, running and cycling as well as the typical values of speed for
  different types of transportation systems", giving walking ≈ 1.5 m/s,
  running ≈ 3 m/s, cycling ≈ 6 m/s, and "A typical value for the speed of sound
  in air is 330 m/s."
- **Equations not on the sheet.** The AQA sheet is exactly Appendix A's 35; any
  content relationship outside that list is either described qualitatively or
  supplied in the question. Do not invent a 36th `given` item.

**Combined science.** AQA's combined-science equation sheet is a *different*
document (the separate-science sheet is not used for 8464). A combined-science
book must use the 8464 sheet.

prints both lists. As at AQA, `know` is units, symbols, definitions and the
typical values; the equations are `given`.

carries a units table, follow the book's board (Edexcel → eight), not the DfE
list.

print a recall/select split and does not mention the booklet by name; the
booklet is the provision. Nothing in the booklet has to be memorised. What
remains `know` is: the *definitions* the content states (e.g. the newton as the
unit of force, emf, stress, strain), SI units and prefixes, the meaning of every
symbol, and the derivations the specification asks for (e.g. AQA requires
derivations of the equations of motion and the kinetic-theory model). `question`
covers data the booklet omits for a specific problem.

**The printed insert, retrieved.** The document students actually receive is the
*List of data, formulae and relationships*, Issue 3, November 2022 (code
P57019RRA), 8 pages:
`https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/A%20Level/Physics/2015/Specification%20and%20sample%20assessments/a-level-physics-data-formulae-relationships.pdf`.
It was found through Pearson's resource search index after the exam-materials

must not be normalised away.

**What the insert does not print, and why the booklet now says so.** Several
core relationships the booklet carries are **not** on the Edexcel insert: the
quadratic formula and the circle and sphere formulae; `hf = E₁ − E₂`; fringe
spacing; the SHM speed and maxima; series and parallel resistors; emf; the
parallel-plate capacitor and the charging curve; the time constant; `pV = nRT`;
magnetic flux and flux linkage; the rotating-coil emf; the transformer
equations; the inverse-square law for gamma radiation; the nuclear radius; and
the gravitational and electric potential-gradient and work equations. Edexcel
says "Any other formulae that are required will be provided in the question", so
the booklet marks those `question` for Edexcel; the two definitions a reader must
hold, `v = Δs/Δt` and `a = Δv/Δt`, are `know`. The AQA and OCR values are
unchanged. This corrects a normalisation that treated the DfE core as if every
board printed all of it. `know` remains the definitions and units; Edexcel's
**Appendix 10: Uncertainties and practical work** is the board's own treatment of
uncertainties and is the right citation for a Chapter on practical skills.

**Still to check: the shared core is not perfectly shared.** Some relationships
the booklet treats as common are not in Edexcel's specification content at all —
`transformer` and `transformer-efficiency`, the rotating-coil emf and the nuclear
radius (`R = R₀A^{1/3}`) have no hit in the Edexcel 9PH0 specification, so for an
Edexcel reader `question` is a placeholder for "not printed here", not a claim
that the topic is examined. A per-board audit of *content* coverage, not just of
what each insert prints, is still outstanding; the booklet's `meta.note` and
reader-visible provenance now say the booklet is the board-neutral core and that
board-only topics are not covered, so the limit is stated even though the audit
is not finished.

# What a data / constants sheet carries

Across the A-level boards the data section is a recognisable, near-shared set,
which is what makes a `tables` section in the booklet possible
(`BOOKLETS.md`: "a table of physical constants").

- **Fundamental constants**: c, h, e, G, Nₐ, R, k, ε₀ (and μ₀ at AQA), σ.
- **Particle data**: electron/proton/neutron rest masses and rest energies;
  at AQA, a full lepton/meson/baryon table and quark/lepton properties.
- **Astronomical and conversion data**: the parsec, light year, astronomical
  unit; sometimes the Sun's and Earth's mass and radius (AQA); AQA also gives a
  Hubble constant.
- **Mathematical data**: the quadratic formula, areas, volumes, trigonometry
  (OCR and AQA); OCR adds small-angle approximations and log rules.
- **A value convention to reproduce**: OCR states "Values are given to three
  significant figures, except where more – or fewer – are useful." Do not
  silently re-round a board's constant.

The GCSE sheets carry **no constants table at all** — only equations. So a GCSE
physics booklet has no `tables` section of constants; the `tables` it might
carry are unit conversions and prefixes (see below).

# The recall question, board by board

| Qualification | Board | Equations sheet / booklet | What it is called | Equations the reader must memorise |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GCSE Physics | AQA 8463 | Yes, insert | Physics Equations Sheet | None (DfE, 26 Feb 2026, for the lifetime of the current spec) |
| GCSE Physics | Edexcel 1PH0 | Yes, insert | Equations List | None (same policy) |
| GCSE Physics | OCR J249 | Yes, insert | Equation Sheet | None (same policy) |
| GCSE Physics | OCR J259 | Yes, insert | Equation Sheet | None (same policy) |
| A-level Physics | AQA 7408 | Yes, insert | Data and formulae | None stated; spec asks for some derivations |
| A-level Physics | Edexcel 9PH0 | Yes, formula + data sheets | Formulae sheet / Data sheet | None — "need not memorise formulae ... supplied in each examination" |
| A-level Physics | OCR H556 | Yes, printed from Appendix 5c | Physics A data sheet | None, but some equations are "also expected to be recalled" (F = ma, W = mg, Ek, Ep, R = V/I) |

Because "no equations must be memorised" is now true at both qualifications,
the booklet's value is not "what you may look up". It is:

1. **`given` marks fidelity** — it reproduces what the board actually prints,
   per board, per tier (AQA prints six HT-only equations — p = hρg, p = mv,
   F = mΔv/Δt, F = BIl, Vp/Vs = np/ns, VpIp = VsIs; Edexcel prints five —
   p = mv, F = Δ(mv)/Δt, F = BIl, the transformer turns equation and p = hρg;
   OCR prints four — p = hρg, p = mv, F = BIl and the transformer turns
   equation).
2. **`question`** — values (c, L, densities, specific heat capacities) and
   occasional equations supplied in the stem.
3. **`know`** — units, prefixes, symbols, definitions, and the typical values
   the board says to recall (AQA's speeds are the only board-stated GCSE
   example found).

# Is a booklet possible, and what must it contain?

**Yes, for both qualifications**, and `BOOKLETS.md` needs no change.

- **GCSE physics booklet** — one item per equation with `provision` per board
  (AQA 8463, Edexcel 1PH0, OCR J249, OCR J259) and a `tier` of `[F, H]` or `[H]`
  matching the board's `HT`/bold marking. It must reproduce each board's
  wording and symbol choice rather than normalising, because the boards differ:
  AQA prints period and magnification, Edexcel prints wave speed = distance ÷
  time and E = I × V × t, OCR prints density explicitly and its efficiency is
  word-only. A `tables` section can carry SI units and prefixes (DfE Appendix 2 /
  board appendices), but there are no constants.
- **A-level physics booklet** — the same, plus a genuine `tables` section
  (constants and particle data) because the boards' data sections are real data.
  The `meta.provenance` must name the board document and the series it applies
  to, because AQA's booklet is versioned ("version 1.5", "from the June 2017
  series onwards") and OCR's is embedded in a specification whose version
  changes (H556 v3.0, March 2026).
- **Time-bound.** Ofqual's decision runs "for the lifetime of the current
  qualifications", and reformed qualifications are intended from 2029–2030.
  Record the basis and re-verify when the boards reissue. When HM Government or
  a board changes a sheet, the booklet changes — the widget is per-book and
  per-board, so this is a data edit, not a code change.

# Fidelity notes to carry into the booklet YAML

- Reproduce each board's own symbol, not a tidied equivalent. AQA writes the
  momentum force as F = mΔv/Δt; the DfE Appendix 1 writes "force = change in
  momentum ÷ time taken"; Edexcel writes F = (mv − mu)/t. Three spellings of
  the same physics — the booklet keeps all three and lets the board selector
  choose.
- Keep `HT` on the item, not just in the tier field: AQA marks HT in the left
  column, OCR with a `HT` key, Edexcel with bold. A reader scanning the sheet
  for the HT marker should see it.
- AQA's sheet title itself is per-series ("FOR USE IN JUNE 2026 ONLY"). The
  booklet should record the series it was transcribed from in `note` or
  `meta`, not pretend to be timeless.
- Never put `$...$` into runtime-populated text; the booklet LaTeX renders to
  MathML at build time.
